Microcrystalline wax (E905)

E905AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Microcrystalline wax is a refined petroleum-based glazing agent used to coat sweets and chewing gum; Clara rates it as a limited-concern additive.

What it is

Microcrystalline wax is a highly refined mineral wax derived from petroleum. Unlike paraffin wax, which has large crystals and can be brittle, this wax contains tiny, intricate crystals that make it remarkably flexible, dense, and elastic. It is a tasteless and odourless substance that behaves as a natural barrier to moisture. While the word petroleum might sound unusual on a food label, this material is purified to a high degree to ensure it meets strict food-grade standards before it ever touches anything edible.

How it's made

The production process begins with the heavy lubricating oil fractions obtained during the refining of crude petroleum. These fractions undergo a de-oiling process to separate the wax from the liquid oil. To make it safe for food use, the crude wax is then subjected to intense purification. This involves chemical treatment and filtration to remove impurities, aromatic compounds, and unwanted colours. The result is a clean, white, and highly stable wax that consists of saturated solid hydrocarbons.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers value this wax for its exceptional moisture-barrier properties and its flexibility. It is primarily used as a glazing agent to give confectionery a appealing, glossy finish and to prevent sweets from sticking together in the packet. Because it does not crack when bent or compressed, it is also an ideal ingredient in chewing gum bases, where it helps to maintain a smooth, pliable texture during chewing. It is often more durable and consistent than plant-based alternatives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects the fact that while the wax is considered non-toxic, EFSA has not established an acceptable daily intake due to a lack of comprehensive long-term data. It is not absorbed by the body and passes through the digestive system unchanged. Because of the limited data, its use is restricted in the European Union to specific applications like chewing gum and the surface treatment of certain confectionery and fruits.

What the science says

European and American regulators have evaluated this wax to ensure its safety in food. EFSA re-evaluated the substance and noted that while it does not accumulate in human tissues, the available scientific database was not sufficient to define a specific numerical daily limit. No toxicological concern has been identified at the levels currently consumed. The FDA permits its use as a synthetic masticatory substance and as a direct food additive, provided it meets strict ultraviolet absorbance limits to guarantee the absence of impurities.

Where you'll find it

You will primarily find this ingredient in chewing gum, jelly sweets, and decorated confectionery, where it acts as a protective coating. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically declared by its name, microcrystalline wax, or by its E-number, E905. On US labels, it is often listed as microcrystalline wax or petroleum wax. It is also sometimes used to coat the skins of certain fresh fruits to help retain their moisture and prolong their shelf life.
