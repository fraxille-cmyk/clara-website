Sodium gluconate (E576)

E576AdditiveNeutral · benign

Sodium gluconate is a benign food additive used to bind metals, helping to preserve the natural colour and texture of packaged foods.

What it is

Sodium gluconate is the sodium salt of gluconic acid, an organic compound that occurs naturally in fruit, honey, and wine. In the food industry, it is used as a sequestrant, which is a substance that binds to trace metal ions in food. By locking up these metals, it keeps them from reacting with other ingredients, which helps to maintain the quality and appearance of packaged products over time.

How it's made

To make this ingredient on a commercial scale, manufacturers typically start with glucose, a simple sugar derived from corn or starch. They ferment this sugar using a common, non-pathogenic fungus called Aspergillus niger, which converts the glucose into gluconic acid. This acid is then neutralised with a sodium source, such as sodium hydroxide or sodium carbonate, to yield sodium gluconate crystals, which are purified and dried into a white powder.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this ingredient because it is an exceptionally efficient chelating agent, meaning it binds tightly to metals like iron and copper even in neutral or alkaline conditions. This binding action prevents these metals from catalysing oxidation reactions, which would otherwise cause fats to go rancid, flavours to degrade, and vibrant colours to fade. It is a highly stable and cost-effective alternative to other synthetic preservatives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is processed by the body in the same way as naturally occurring sugars and organic acids, it is considered highly safe. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the substance and determined that no acceptable daily intake limit is required, meaning it can be used freely in food production without raising safety concerns for consumers.

What the science says

Scientific panels have thoroughly assessed sodium gluconate and found no evidence of accumulation or unwanted activity in the body. It is metabolised easily, breaking down into harmless compounds that the body uses for energy. Major regulatory bodies, including the European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration, agree that it presents no toxicological concern and requires no restrictive upper limits in our daily diet.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of processed foods, including canned vegetables, processed meats, dairy products, and some soft drinks. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its plain name, sodium gluconate, or by its European code, E576. On US labels, it is often listed as sodium gluconate and is recognised as a safe substance for preserving food stability.
