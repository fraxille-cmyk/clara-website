Sodium gluconate (E576)

E576AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Sodium gluconate is a synthetic salt used to preserve food quality by binding metal ions, and regulators consider it a benign additive with no set daily limit.

What it is

Sodium gluconate is the sodium salt of gluconic acid, an organic compound that occurs naturally in fruit, honey, and wine. In the food industry, it is used as a sequestrant, which means it acts like a chemical claw to grab and bind metal ions. By locking up these metals, it prevents them from reacting with other ingredients in the food. It is closely related to the natural sugars found in plants, but it is produced in larger quantities for industrial use to help maintain the stability and appearance of packaged foods.

How it's made

To make sodium gluconate on a commercial scale, manufacturers start with simple glucose, which is a sugar typically derived from corn or starch. This glucose undergoes a fermentation process using a common, non-harmful fungus or bacterium that oxidises the sugar into gluconic acid. Once this acid is formed, it is neutralised by adding a sodium compound, such as sodium hydroxide or sodium carbonate. The resulting mixture is then crystallised, filtered, and dried into a fine, white, highly soluble powder that is ready for use in food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on sodium gluconate because it is an exceptionally efficient chelating agent, meaning it binds metal ions like iron and copper even in alkaline conditions. These metals naturally speed up oxidation, which leads to discolouration, off-flavours, and rancidity. By neutralising the metals, sodium gluconate keeps food looking fresh and tasting good for longer. It is highly valued because it performs this job without altering the texture of the food, and it is much more stable at high temperatures than many organic alternatives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This synthetic salt binds metal ions to preserve food quality, and the European Food Safety Authority has not specified an acceptable daily intake limit. This means the regulator considers it so safe that there is no need to restrict how much of it you consume in your daily diet. Because it is metabolised by the body in the same way as natural carbohydrates, it does not accumulate in your tissues and has no known adverse effects when consumed as part of a normal diet.

What the science says

The safety of sodium gluconate has been thoroughly evaluated by major international food safety authorities. In its comprehensive re-evaluation, the European Food Safety Authority concluded that there is no safety concern and therefore did not establish a numerical acceptable daily intake. The US Food and Drug Administration also recognises it as generally safe for use in food. Because it breaks down into simple sodium and gluconic acid, which the body processes easily as energy, scientific assessments have consistently found no evidence of unwanted side effects or accumulation.

Where you'll find it

You will find sodium gluconate in a wide variety of processed foods, including dairy products, processed meats, canned vegetables, and bakery goods. It is also common in dietary supplements and sports drinks where mineral stability is important. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically listed by its name, sodium gluconate, or by its European code, E576. On US labels, you will see it listed plainly as sodium gluconate, where it is permitted for use as a sequestrant.
