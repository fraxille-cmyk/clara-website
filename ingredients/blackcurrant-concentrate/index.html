Blackcurrant concentrate

Processed ingredientNeutral

A concentrated fruit juice made from real blackcurrants, used to add natural colour and a tart, fruity flavour to foods and drinks.

What it is

Blackcurrant concentrate is simply blackcurrant juice that has had most of its natural water content removed. Blackcurrants are small, dark purple berries known for their deep colour and sharp, tangy taste. By removing the water, the natural sugars, acids, and vibrant pigments of the fruit become highly concentrated, creating a thick, intensely flavoured liquid that is easy to store and transport. It is a real food ingredient rather than a synthetic additive, retaining the characteristic taste and rich purple hue of the fresh berries.

How it's made

The process begins with harvesting ripe blackcurrants, which are washed, crushed, and pressed to extract their raw juice. This juice is filtered to remove the seeds and skins. To concentrate the liquid, the juice is heated under a vacuum, which allows the water to evaporate at a lower temperature, preserving the delicate flavours and natural colours of the fruit. The resulting thick syrup is pasteurised to ensure it stays fresh, then packaged for food manufacturers to use in their recipes.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this concentrate because it is an efficient, natural way to add both flavour and colour to products. It provides a rich, dark purple shade and a bright, tart berry taste without the need for synthetic dyes or artificial flavourings. Because the water has been removed, it takes up much less space in storage and is cheaper to transport than fresh juice. It also has a longer shelf life, making it a highly practical ingredient for keeping recipes consistent.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. Because it is a concentrated fruit juice rather than a chemical additive, it does not carry the risks associated with synthetic colours or artificial sweeteners. It is digested just like regular fruit juice, containing natural fruit sugars. No daily intake limit is established for this ingredient because it is classified as a food rather than a food additive. It is a simple, familiar ingredient that poses no safety concerns for the general population.

What the science says

Regulatory bodies view blackcurrant concentrate as a standard food ingredient rather than an additive. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration do not subject fruit concentrates to the same restrictive daily limits as synthetic additives, as they are metabolised by the body in the same way as whole fruits. Assessments focus on ensuring the concentrate is prepared hygienically and remains free from contaminants, confirming it is entirely safe for regular consumption as part of a balanced diet.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of products, including fruit juices, smoothies, yoghurts, jellies, ice creams, and baked goods. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed simply as blackcurrant concentrate, blackcurrant juice concentrate, or concentrated blackcurrant juice. Because it is a food ingredient and not a food additive, it does not have an E-number and is listed by its plain, recognisable name.
