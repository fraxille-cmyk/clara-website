Glycerol esters of wood rosins (E445)

E445AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Glycerol esters of wood rosins is a plant-derived stabiliser used to keep citrus oils mixed in drinks, with a daily limit of 12.5 mg per kg of body weight.

What it is

Glycerol esters of wood rosins is a food additive used to keep ingredients from separating. It is a complex mixture of substances derived from the natural resin found in old pine stumps. Because raw pine resin is too sticky and acidic to be used in food directly, it is combined with glycerol, a simple alcohol, to create a stable, neutral substance that can mix easily with oils and water.

How it's made

The production process begins by harvesting the resinous stumps of longleaf and slash pine trees. This raw wood rosin is extracted using solvents and then purified to remove volatile compounds. To make it suitable for food, manufacturers heat the purified rosin with glycerol under a vacuum. This chemical modification creates a neutral ester that is further purified, often using steam, to ensure it is free from unwanted tree residues.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this ingredient primarily as a stabiliser and density adjuster in citrus-flavoured beverages. Citrus oils are naturally lighter than water and tend to float to the top of a bottle, creating an unappealing ring of oil. By bonding with these oils, this additive increases their density so they remain evenly suspended throughout the liquid, ensuring a consistent flavour and appearance from the first sip to the last.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. It is processed by the body without accumulating in tissues, and most of it passes through unabsorbed. EFSA keeps its daily limit of 12.5 mg per kg of body weight, which is a generous threshold that is very difficult to exceed through normal food consumption. There are no major safety concerns or open questions regarding its use in food products.

What the science says

This ingredient has been thoroughly evaluated by international food safety authorities. EFSA completed a full re-evaluation of the additive and confirmed that it does not pose a safety concern at current exposure levels. The FDA also permits its use in food under specific regulations. Because it is highly purified, the final substance does not contain the proteins or compounds that typically trigger pine allergies, making it well-tolerated.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in cloudy soft drinks, sports beverages, and fruit-flavoured waters. On a label, it is usually listed by its full name, glycerol esters of wood rosins, or by its European code, E445. On US labels it is listed as glycerol ester of wood rosin, and it may also appear in some chewing gum bases to provide a smooth, elastic texture.
