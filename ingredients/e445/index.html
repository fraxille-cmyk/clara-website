Glycerol esters of wood rosins (E445)

E445AdditiveAvoid · limited concern

A wood-derived stabiliser used to keep citrus oils suspended in drinks, which Clara rates to avoid because it is a marker of ultra-processed foods.

What it is

Glycerol esters of wood rosins are complex mixtures of substances derived from the natural resin of pine trees. This resin is chemically combined with glycerol, a simple alcohol that occurs naturally in fats, to create a stable compound. It acts as an emulsifier and stabiliser, helping oil-based and water-based ingredients stay mixed together. In the food industry, it is primarily used to keep flavouring oils suspended evenly throughout liquids, preventing them from separating and floating to the top.

How it's made

The production process begins with harvesting the resinous stumps of longleaf pine trees. These stumps are cleared, cleaned, and refined to extract the raw wood rosin. This rosin is then purified through distillation and reacted with glycerol under high temperatures to form the esters. To make the final ingredient suitable for food use, the mixture is washed and steam-stripped to remove volatile impurities, resulting in a pale, hard resin that can be easily dissolved into food-grade oils.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this ingredient because it has a very specific density that matches the density of water. When citrus oils are added to beverages, they naturally want to float to the surface. By dissolving these oils in glycerol esters of wood rosins, manufacturers can adjust the density of the oil droplets so they remain suspended throughout the drink. This keeps the beverage looking uniformly cloudy and ensures that every sip has the same level of flavour, without requiring the consumer to shake the bottle.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a limited-concern additive. While it is not considered highly toxic, it is a marker of ultra-processed foods that are best kept to a minimum in a balanced diet. EFSA has evaluated this additive and established an acceptable daily intake of 12.5 mg per kg of body weight. The regulator notes that the substance is poorly absorbed by the body, with most of it passing through unchanged, meaning it does not accumulate in tissues or cause systemic disruption when consumed within the established limit.

What the science says

The safety of this ingredient has been thoroughly assessed by international food safety bodies. JECFA and EFSA have both reviewed the toxicological data, concluding that the substance does not pose a risk of genetic damage or reproductive issues. Because the raw material comes from pine trees, early evaluations looked closely at potential allergic reactions, but refined food-grade esters have been shown to lack the specific compounds that trigger pine resin allergies. No long-term health consequences have been demonstrated at normal dietary exposure levels.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in cloudy citrus-flavoured soft drinks, sports beverages, and energy drinks. It is also occasionally used in the surface coatings of citrus fruits to help preserve freshness. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its full name as glycerol esters of wood rosins, or simply as E445, and it may sometimes be grouped under the general term of stabiliser or emulsifier.
