Hydrochloric acid (E507)

E507AdditiveNeutral · benign

Hydrochloric acid is a strong mineral acid used to adjust acidity in food processing; Clara rates it NEUTRAL as it poses no risk in the amounts used.

What it is

Hydrochloric acid is a strong mineral acid that is highly acidic. While it sounds intimidating because it is a powerful industrial chemical, it is actually the very same acid that your stomach produces naturally to digest your food. In the food industry, it is used in highly diluted forms to control acidity, neutralise alkaline substances, and assist in the breakdown of complex food components during processing.

How it's made

To make food-grade hydrochloric acid, manufacturers typically start by reacting hydrogen gas with chlorine gas to produce hydrogen chloride gas. This gas is then dissolved in purified water to create a highly concentrated acid solution. This solution is carefully purified to remove any industrial impurities, ensuring it meets strict food-grade standards before it is diluted and shipped to food production facilities.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this acid primarily as an acidity regulator and processing aid. It is highly effective at adjusting the pH of a food mixture, which is crucial for controlling texture, gel formation, and preservation. It is also used to hydrolyse or break down proteins and starches, helping to create ingredients like hydrolysed vegetable protein or corn syrup more efficiently than weaker organic acids can.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Although concentrated hydrochloric acid is highly corrosive, the tiny amounts used in food processing are completely neutralised or diluted to safe levels before the final product reaches the shelf. Because it is neutralised into simple salts and water during manufacturing, it poses no risk to your body, and no limit is established for its daily intake.

What the science says

The safety of this ingredient has been thoroughly evaluated by major regulatory bodies. The Scientific Committee on Food established an unspecified acceptable daily intake, which is a category reserved for substances of very low risk. Additionally, IARC classifies hydrochloric acid as Group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans, confirming that its use in food does not pose a hazard.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient listed on labels as hydrochloric acid or by its European code, E507. It is commonly used in the production of modified starches, hydrolysed proteins, syrups, and some canned vegetables or cheeses where precise pH control is required. Because it is often used as a processing aid and neutralised during manufacturing, it sometimes does not even appear on the final ingredient list.
