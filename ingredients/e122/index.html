Azorubine (carmoisine) (E122)

E122AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Azorubine is a synthetic red food dye that requires a warning in Europe because it may affect activity and attention in children.

What it is

Azorubine, also known as carmoisine, is an artificial red food dye. It belongs to the azo dye family, which is a group of synthetic colourants known for producing intense, stable shades that do not fade easily. Unlike natural red colours derived from beetroot or insects, this substance is entirely man-made and does not exist in nature. It is used to give foods a bright red, crimson, or purple hue, helping products look more appealing on the shelf.

How it's made

This colourant is manufactured through chemical synthesis in a laboratory. It is produced by coupling two organic coal-tar derivatives, specifically sodium salts, in a multi-step chemical reaction. The raw materials are derived from petroleum products. After the reaction is complete, the mixture is purified, crystallised, and dried into a fine, water-soluble powder or granule that can be easily mixed into liquid and solid foods during manufacturing.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose this synthetic dye because it is highly stable, cheap, and incredibly efficient. Natural red colours often degrade when exposed to heat, light, or changes in acidity during processing and storage. This synthetic alternative remains vibrant under harsh factory conditions and has a long shelf life. It allows companies to achieve a consistent, bright red colour across large production batches using only tiny amounts of the substance, which keeps production costs very low.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. The main concern is its potential impact on young consumers, as European regulations require foods containing it to carry a warning label stating that it may have an adverse effect on activity and attention in children. While EFSA keeps its daily limit of 4 mg per kg of body weight, the dye is not permitted for food use in the United States. IARC classifies carmoisine as Group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.

What the science says

This additive has been evaluated by several major food authorities over the decades. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the dye and established an acceptable daily intake to limit exposure, particularly for children who might consume multiple coloured products. While some international bodies find the evidence regarding behavioural changes to be mixed, the European Union took a precautionary approach by mandating warning labels on packaging. No other major safety concerns or open chemical risks are currently highlighted by European regulators.

Where you'll find it

You will find this colourant in highly processed foods that require a bright red or purple appearance. It is commonly added to sweets, jellies, soft drinks, flavoured yoghurts, ice creams, cake decorations, and packaged sauces. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically listed by its name as azorubine (carmoisine) or simply as E 122. It does not appear on US food labels as it is not permitted for food use there.
