Brown HT (E155)

E155AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Brown HT is a synthetic coal tar dye used to colour food brown. Clara recommends avoiding it because it belongs to the controversial azo dye family.

What it is

Brown HT is a synthetic food colouring that gives products a rich, chocolate-brown shade. It is a coal tar dye, meaning it is derived from petroleum products rather than natural plant or mineral sources. Chemically, it belongs to a group of compounds known as azo dyes, which are characterised by a specific nitrogen bond that makes the colour exceptionally bright and highly stable under heat and light.

How it's made

This additive is manufactured entirely in a laboratory through a chemical synthesis process. It is produced by coupling diazotised amines, which are derived from petroleum or coal tar, with other organic compounds. The resulting chemical structure is then purified, dried, and processed into a fine reddish-brown powder or granules that dissolve easily in water to create a deep brown liquid.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose this synthetic dye because it is incredibly stable, cheap, and highly effective. Natural brown alternatives, like caramel or cocoa, can sometimes alter the taste of a product or fade when exposed to high baking temperatures and bright supermarket lighting. This synthetic option ensures a consistent, appealing colour throughout a long shelf life without adding any unwanted flavours to the recipe.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. This rating is given because it is a synthetic azo colour, which is the exact dye family of five of the six colours that must carry the European Union warning regarding activity and attention in children. While it does not carry that specific warning label itself, its chemical relationship to those restricted dyes makes it an ingredient that is best kept out of your shopping basket.

What the science says

EFSA re-evaluated this additive and established an acceptable daily intake of 1.5 mg per kg of body weight. While the European Union authorises its use within these strict limits, the United States FDA has never authorised it for use in food. Scientific assessments focus on how the body metabolises azo compounds, as some individuals may experience sensitivity or mild allergic-like reactions after consuming synthetic coal tar dyes.

Where you'll find it

You will find this colour in chocolate cakes, biscuits, puddings, ice creams, and soft drinks, where it is used to mimic or enhance the appearance of cocoa. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically declared by its name, Brown HT, or by its European number, E 155. Because it is not permitted in food in the United States, you will not see it on American imports.
