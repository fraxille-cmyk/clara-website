Safflower oil

Processed ingredientNeutral

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Safflower oil is a refined vegetable oil pressed from safflower seeds, used as a neutral cooking fat and texturiser in processed foods.

What it is

Safflower oil is a highly refined vegetable oil extracted from the seeds of the safflower plant, a thistle-like annual with bright yellow or orange flowers. Historically cultivated for its vibrant orange dye, the plant is now grown primarily for its oil-rich seeds. Chemically, it consists of triglycerides rich in unsaturated fatty acids, particularly oleic or linoleic acid depending on the plant variety. It is a clear, pale yellow liquid with a very mild, neutral taste, making it highly versatile in cooking and food formulation. It behaves much like sunflower or canola oil in everyday kitchen use.

How it's made

The production of safflower oil begins with harvesting and cleaning the seeds of the safflower plant. These seeds are dehulled to separate the oil-rich kernels from the tough outer shells. The kernels are then mechanically pressed to extract the bulk of the oil. To maximise yield, the remaining seed cake often undergoes solvent extraction using food-grade hexane. The crude oil is then refined, bleached, and deodorised to remove free fatty acids, pigments, and volatile compounds. This refining process yields a highly stable, neutral oil with a high smoke point, ready for industrial food formulation.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose safflower oil because of its exceptional stability and neutral sensory profile. High-oleic varieties of safflower oil are particularly prized because they resist oxidation under high heat, extending the shelf life of fried snacks and baked goods. Because it does not carry a strong flavour of its own, it allows the intended flavours of spices and seasonings to stand out. Additionally, it remains liquid at low temperatures, which makes it an ideal base for refrigerated dressings and mayonnaise, preventing the product from solidifying or separating in the cold.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. Safflower oil is a refined vegetable oil that serves as a source of dietary fats. It is not classified as an additive, so no daily intake limit is established for its consumption. While it is a concentrated source of energy, it consists primarily of unsaturated fats, which are generally preferred over saturated fats in a balanced diet. Because it undergoes thorough refining, the proteins that could trigger allergic reactions are typically removed during processing, making it a well-tolerated ingredient in everyday food products.

What the science says

Safflower oil has been evaluated by major food authorities as a standard food ingredient rather than an additive. The FDA recognises safflower oil as a safe, edible vegetable oil suitable for general use in food. Joint evaluations by FAO and WHO treat refined vegetable oils as macronutrients that contribute to daily fat intake. Scientific assessments focus on the balance of fatty acids, noting that high-oleic safflower oil provides monounsaturated fats that remain stable during storage. No safety concerns or adverse physiological effects are associated with its normal dietary consumption.

Where you'll find it

You will find safflower oil in a wide variety of packaged foods, particularly in the condiment and snack aisles. It is a common ingredient in commercial salad dressings, mayonnaise, margarines, and spreads. It is also used for frying potato crisps, roasting nuts, and preparing baked goods like crackers and biscuits. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared simply as safflower oil, high-oleic safflower oil, or sometimes grouped under the general term vegetable oil, depending on local labelling regulations.
