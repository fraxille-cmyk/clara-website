Dried apricots

Whole foodGood

Dried apricots are simply the whole fruit with the water removed, making them a nutritious and natural whole-food ingredient.

What it is

Dried apricots are the sweet, orange fruits of the apricot tree that have had most of their natural water content removed. They are a traditional whole food, concentrated in flavour and natural sugars, and they retain the dietary fibre, vitamins, and minerals of the fresh fruit. Unlike processed additives, they are a simple, recognisable ingredient that people have enjoyed for thousands of years. They offer a chewy texture and a deep, sweet-tart taste that comes entirely from the fruit itself.

How it's made

The journey begins in the orchard where fresh apricots are harvested at peak ripeness. The fruits are washed, cut in half, and the stones are removed. To dry them, producers either lay them out in the sun on large trays for several days or place them in specialised warm-air dehydrators. As the water evaporates, the fruit shrinks, darkens to a rich brown or deep orange, and becomes shelf-stable. No chemical processing or synthetic alteration is needed to turn the fresh fruit into this durable pantry staple.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose dried apricots to add natural sweetness, a pleasant chewiness, and a vibrant fruit flavour to their products. They are an excellent way to sweeten foods without relying on refined sugars or artificial sweeteners. Because the drying process concentrates the natural sugars, these fruits act as a natural binder in snack bars and provide a burst of flavour in breakfast cereals. They also offer a long shelf life, which helps keep packaged foods fresh without the need for synthetic preservatives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. This is a single-ingredient product that represents the whole food itself, with nothing added. Because it is not an additive, there are no synthetic chemicals or industrial processes to worry about. It is a wholesome source of dietary fibre and essential nutrients. Some commercial dried apricots are treated with sulphur dioxide to preserve their bright orange colour, but plain, untreated dried apricots are entirely natural and present no health concerns for the general population.

What the science says

Regulators and food safety authorities view dried apricots as a standard, wholesome agricultural product rather than a food additive. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration recognise dried fruits as nutritious components of a balanced diet. Because no limit is established for the consumption of whole fruits, they can be enjoyed freely. Scientific assessments focus on their positive nutritional profile, noting that the drying process preserves most of the beneficial dietary fibre and minerals found in fresh apricots.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in the baking aisle, snack sections, and breakfast aisles. It is a common component in muesli, granola, fruit-and-nut bars, and festive baked goods. On a label, it is usually listed simply as dried apricots, but you might also see it under its European names such as soft dried apricots, orejones, albicocche secche, or getrocknete aprikosen depending on where the product was packaged.
