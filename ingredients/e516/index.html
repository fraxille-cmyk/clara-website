Calcium sulphate (E516)

E516AdditiveNeutral · benign

Calcium sulphate is a natural mineral used to firm tofu and canned vegetables, rated neutral by Clara with an excellent safety profile.

What it is

Calcium sulphate is a naturally occurring mineral that you might recognise in its everyday form as gypsum or plaster of Paris. In the food world, it is a clean, odourless white powder that provides a rich source of both calcium and sulphur. It is a traditional ingredient with a long history of use, particularly in Asian culinary traditions where it has been used for centuries to turn liquid soy milk into firm, sliceable blocks of tofu.

How it's made

This ingredient is sourced directly from natural mineral deposits found in the earth. The raw gypsum rock is mined and then thoroughly washed to remove any impurities. After cleaning, the mineral is heated to dehydrate it, ground into an extremely fine powder, and carefully purified to ensure it meets strict food-grade standards. Because it is a simple mineral, it does not undergo any complex chemical modification during this manufacturing process.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers rely on this mineral primarily as a firming agent and dough conditioner. In tofu production, it acts as a coagulant, binding the soy proteins together into a smooth, stable curd. In commercial baking, it feeds yeast and improves dough texture, which helps bread rise evenly. It also helps canned vegetables, like tomatoes, retain their structural integrity and crispness during the high-heat sterilisation process, ensuring they do not turn mushy in the tin.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. This mineral has an excellent safety profile, and because it is processed by the body in the same way as dietary calcium from other sources, regulators have not felt the need to set a restrictive limit on how much you can consume. It is a simple, non-toxic substance that poses no health concerns for the general population when eaten in normal dietary amounts.

What the science says

Scientific panels have thoroughly evaluated this mineral and found no safety concerns. Because the body easily breaks it down into calcium and sulphate ions, which are both essential nutrients we use every day, it is treated as a normal part of our mineral intake. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the substance and confirmed that it does not accumulate in the body or cause adverse effects, meaning no numerical upper intake limit is established for its use as an additive.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient most commonly in packaged tofu, where it is the traditional coagulant of choice. It also appears frequently in sliced breads, baked goods, canned vegetables, and fortified breakfast cereals. On ingredient labels, it is usually listed plainly as calcium sulphate, though you may also see it written as E516, and on US labels it is listed as calcium sulfate.
