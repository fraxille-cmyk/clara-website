Sodium stearoyl-2-lactylate is a synthetic emulsifier used to improve the texture of baked goods, with a daily limit set by European regulators.

What it is

Sodium stearoyl-2-lactylate is a synthetic food additive that acts as an emulsifier and dough conditioner. It is a combination of lactic acid and stearic acid, which is a fatty acid found naturally in both animal fats and vegetable oils. While its components are inspired by nature, the substance itself is chemically modified to create a highly effective compound that helps oil and water mix smoothly, ensuring a uniform texture in processed foods.

How it's made

To manufacture this ingredient, producers chemically react stearic acid with lactic acid in a controlled industrial process. This reaction is typically neutralised with sodium hydroxide or sodium carbonate to form a stable sodium salt. The stearic acid used in this process can be derived from either animal fats or plant-based oils, such as palm oil, meaning the final additive is a highly processed, synthetic compound by the time it reaches the factory floor.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on this additive primarily in commercial baking because it strengthens dough and increases its volume. It binds tightly to gluten proteins and starches, which prevents the dough from collapsing during mechanical mixing and baking. This results in a softer crumb structure, a more resilient loaf, and a significantly longer shelf life, as it prevents bread from going stale quickly. It is far more stable and cost-effective than natural alternatives like egg yolks.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects its synthetic origin and the fact that regulators have established a specific limit for its daily consumption. EFSA keeps its daily limit of 22 mg per kg of body weight, which was established during a comprehensive safety re-evaluation. Within this limit, the additive is processed by the body much like ordinary dietary fats and lactic acid, presenting no safety concerns for the general population.

What the science says

Scientific assessments show that the body easily breaks down this substance into its original components, stearic acid and lactic acid, which are then metabolised normally. EFSA evaluated the additive and concluded there is no concern regarding its safety at typical dietary exposure levels. The FDA also permits its use as a multipurpose food additive. No major safety gaps or adverse effects have been identified in modern reviews, and it does not accumulate in the body.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in packaged bakery products, including sliced bread, buns, tortillas, and pastries. It also appears in dehydrated potatoes, gravies, and creamers. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its full name, sodium stearoyl-2-lactylate, or by its European code, E481, and sometimes simply as e 481 in international products.
