Basic methacrylate copolymer (E1205)

E1205AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

A synthetic glazing agent used to coat food supplements and mask unpleasant tastes, with a daily intake limit established by European regulators.

What it is

Basic methacrylate copolymer is a synthetic polymer, which means it is a large molecule made of repeating chemical units linked together. It belongs to the acrylic family, similar to the materials used in some medical devices and specialised coatings. In the food industry, it functions as a glazing agent, forming a thin, protective barrier over solid items. It is designed to remain stable in neutral environments like the mouth but dissolve quickly when it reaches the acidic environment of the stomach.

How it's made

This ingredient is entirely synthetic and is produced in a chemical laboratory or factory. Manufacturers create it by polymerising three specific monomer compounds: methyl methacrylate, butyl methacrylate, and dimethylaminoethyl methacrylate. These raw chemical building blocks are reacted together under controlled temperatures and pressures, using catalysts to link them into long chains. The resulting polymer is then purified, washed, dried, and ground into a fine white powder ready for use in manufacturing.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this polymer primarily as a protective coating on solid products. Its main commercial advantage is its ability to mask unpleasant tastes and odours, which is highly useful for ingredients that have a bitter or metallic flavour. Because it does not dissolve in saliva but breaks down rapidly in stomach acid, it ensures the consumer does not taste the active ingredients while still allowing the body to absorb them quickly. It also protects sensitive ingredients from moisture and light, extending their shelf life.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is given because it is a synthetic substance with a numeric acceptable daily intake of 20 mg per kg of body weight, established by EFSA in 2010. It is not absorbed by the body in any significant amount; instead, it passes through the digestive system largely intact. Because it is used in very small quantities as a thin coating, typical consumption levels remain well below the established safety limit, and there is no indication of accumulation in the body.

What the science says

Both European and American regulators have thoroughly evaluated this polymer. EFSA assessed its safety in 2010 and concluded that it is of no safety concern at the intended levels of use, setting a clear daily limit of 20 mg per kg of body weight. The FDA also reviewed its safety profile and filed a GRAS notice in 2009 with no objections. Scientific evaluations show that the polymer does not break down into harmful components in the digestive tract and is excreted from the body without causing adverse physiological changes.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient almost exclusively in solid food supplements, such as vitamin tablets, mineral capsules, and herbal formulations, as well as solid foods intended for special medical purposes. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its full name, basic methacrylate copolymer, or by its European additive number, E1205. Because it is only permitted in specific product categories, you will not find it in everyday groceries like bread, dairy, or confectionery.
