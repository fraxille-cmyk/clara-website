Propane-1,2-diol (propylene glycol) (E1520)

E1520AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Propane-1,2-diol is a synthetic liquid carrier used to dissolve food colours and flavours, with a safe daily limit established by regulators.

What it is

Propane-1,2-diol, commonly known as propylene glycol, is a synthetic, odourless, and colourless liquid. It has a slightly sweet taste and a syrupy consistency. Chemically, it belongs to the alcohol family and is highly effective at absorbing moisture and dissolving other substances. While it is entirely synthetic, it behaves similarly to glycerine, a substance found naturally in fats, making it highly compatible with many different food ingredients.

How it's made

This ingredient is produced through a synthetic chemical process. Manufacturers start with propylene, a gas derived from refining petroleum. This gas is converted into propylene oxide, which is then reacted with water under high pressure and temperature to yield propane-1,2-diol. The resulting liquid is carefully purified and distilled to meet strict food-grade standards, ensuring that no unwanted chemical residues remain in the final ingredient.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this substance primarily as a carrier and solvent. Many concentrated flavourings, colours, and nutrients do not dissolve easily in water or oil. This liquid dissolves these compounds beautifully, allowing them to be distributed evenly throughout a food product. It also acts as a humectant, which means it holds onto moisture, helping to keep baked goods soft, preventing clumping in powdered mixes, and maintaining a consistent texture over time.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. It is processed efficiently by the body, which breaks it down into lactic acid and pyruvic acid, two completely normal compounds that your body uses for energy. EFSA keeps its daily limit of 25 mg per kg of body weight. Because it is mostly used in tiny amounts to carry flavours and colours, typical daily intake for most people remains well below this established safety threshold.

What the science says

This ingredient has been thoroughly evaluated by major food safety authorities. EFSA completed a full re-evaluation of the substance, confirming that it does not accumulate in the body and showing no safety concerns at typical dietary levels. The FDA also recognises it as safe for use in food under specific limits. Scientists have found no evidence of harm to the reproductive system or development at standard exposure levels, and no toxicological concern exists.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of highly flavoured or coloured foods, such as soft drinks, confectionery, baked goods, salad dressings, and liquid seasonings. On a label, it is typically listed as propane-1,2-diol (propylene glycol), e 1520, or simply E1520. On US labels it is listed as propylene glycol, where it is often used to keep packaged shredded cheese from sticking together or to keep cake mixes moist.
