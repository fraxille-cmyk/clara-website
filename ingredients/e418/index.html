Gellan gum (E418)

E418AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Gellan gum is a plant-based gelling agent made by fermenting bacteria, rated neutral by Clara because it is a safe, soluble fibre with no daily limit.

What it is

Gellan gum is a soluble fibre that acts as a powerful gelling agent and thickener. It is a complex carbohydrate produced by a specific type of bacterium, which makes it a popular plant-based alternative to animal-derived gelatin. In the kitchen, it behaves much like other natural gums, dissolving in hot liquids and forming a firm, stable gel as it cools. Because it is a soluble fibre, it passes through our digestive system without being absorbed, acting as bulk in the gut.

How it's made

This ingredient is made through a natural fermentation process. Manufacturers feed a pure culture of the bacterium Sphingomonas elodea on a nutrient-rich broth containing simple sugars. As the bacteria consume the sugar, they produce the gum as a protective outer layer. Once the fermentation is complete, the mixture is treated with isopropyl alcohol to precipitate the gum out of the liquid. The resulting solid is then separated, washed, dried, and ground into a fine, off-white powder.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose gellan gum because it creates incredibly stable gels at very low concentrations. It is highly versatile, allowing manufacturers to create either firm, brittle gels or fluid, pourable liquids that still hold suspended particles in place. It remains stable under high heat, which is essential for products that undergo pasteurisation or sterilisation. It also provides a clean, pleasant mouthfeel without masking the natural flavours of the food, making it more efficient than starch or gelatin.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects its status as a benign, non-toxic soluble fibre that is processed naturally by our gut bacteria. Because of its excellent safety profile, EFSA has not specified an acceptable daily intake limit, meaning there is no restrictive threshold for its daily consumption. It is well-tolerated by the human body, and even in large amounts, it behaves simply like dietary fibre, occasionally causing mild digestive softening in sensitive individuals but posing no chemical risk.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have evaluated this ingredient thoroughly and found no safety concerns. EFSA re-evaluated it in 2018 and confirmed that its use as a food additive does not raise any safety issues, maintaining that a numerical limit is unnecessary. JECFA reached a similar conclusion in 1990, noting that the substance is not absorbed by the body and is partially broken down by normal gut microbes. No adverse effects have been observed in dietary studies, confirming its role as a stable food ingredient.

Where you'll find it

You will frequently find this ingredient in dairy alternatives like oat, almond, and soy milks, where it keeps added calcium and cocoa suspended evenly. It is also common in vegan jellies, gummy sweets, fillings, and low-sugar jams. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared as gellan gum or by its European code, E418. On US labels, it is listed as gellan gum, and it is favoured in gluten-free baking to help retain moisture and structure in the absence of gluten.
