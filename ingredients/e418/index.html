Gellan gum (E418)

E418AdditiveNeutral · benign

Gellan gum is a plant-friendly thickening agent made by fermenting sugar, rated neutral by Clara because it is a benign fibre with no daily limit.

What it is

Gellan gum is a soluble fibre that dissolves in water to create a gel-like structure. It is a natural substance produced by a specific type of bacterium, and it behaves much like other plant-based thickeners you might have in your kitchen, such as cornstarch or gelatin, though it is completely vegan. In the food world, it is highly valued for its ability to create firm, clean-cutting gels or to keep liquids thick and stable without changing how they taste or feel in the mouth.

How it's made

To make gellan gum, manufacturers feed simple sugars to a bacterium called Sphingomonas elodea. This natural fermentation process is very similar to how beer, yoghurt, or vinegar is made. As the bacteria consume the sugar, they produce a complex carbohydrate coating around themselves. This substance is then separated from the mixture, purified with alcohol, dried, and ground into a fine, off-white powder that is ready to be mixed into food products.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers love gellan gum because it is incredibly efficient and versatile. It can create strong gels at very low concentrations, which saves money and keeps the ingredient list light. Unlike gelatin, it remains stable at high temperatures, meaning products can be pasteurised or baked without losing their structure. It is also excellent at keeping cocoa particles suspended in chocolate milk or calcium distributed evenly in plant-based milks, ensuring an even texture from the first sip to the last.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is a soluble fibre, your body does not absorb it, and it simply passes through your digestive system. EFSA re-evaluated it and confirmed there is no safety concern, meaning no acceptable daily intake limit is established. It is considered safe for the general population, and even at very high doses in animal studies, it showed no adverse effects on how the body processes nutrients or maintains its natural balance.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly assessed gellan gum and found it to be exceptionally safe. JECFA evaluated the substance and established that no numerical daily limit was necessary, a conclusion that EFSA supported in its comprehensive review. Because it is a complex carbohydrate, it can act as a dietary fibre in the gut. Human studies have shown that eating relatively large amounts of it does not cause digestive discomfort or alter blood chemistry, confirming its status as a gentle, non-disruptive ingredient.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in dairy alternatives like almond, soy, and oat milks, where it keeps the added calcium and vitamins from settling at the bottom of the carton. It also appears in jams, jellies, gummy sweets, and low-calorie spreads. On ingredient lists, it is usually declared plainly as gellan gum, though on European labels you might see it listed by its official additive number, E418, or as E 418.
