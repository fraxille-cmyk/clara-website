Sodium hydroxide (E524)

E524AdditiveNeutral · benign

Sodium hydroxide is a strong alkaline mineral used to adjust acidity and glaze pretzels; it is safe in food and Clara rates it neutral.

What it is

Sodium hydroxide is a highly alkaline mineral compound that is also known as lye or caustic soda. In its pure, concentrated form, it is a strong corrosive substance, but in the food industry, it is used in tiny, highly diluted amounts to balance acidity or alter the surface of dough. It is the chemical opposite of an acid, and when it reacts with acidic ingredients, it neutralises them, turning into simple water and table salt.

How it's made

This ingredient is produced through a synthetic manufacturing process known as the chloralkali process. Manufacturers pass an electric current through a solution of sodium chloride, which is common salt water. This electrolysis splits the salt and water molecules apart, generating chlorine gas, hydrogen gas, and a solution of sodium hydroxide. The resulting liquid is then purified, concentrated, and often dried into solid flakes or pellets for food-grade use.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use this compound primarily as an acidity regulator to adjust and maintain the pH levels of foods and drinks. It is also famous for its role in making traditional German-style pretzels. Dipping the raw dough into a diluted solution of this ingredient before baking gelatinises the starch on the surface. This chemical reaction creates the characteristic deep brown colour, glossy finish, and unique chewy texture that water alone cannot produce.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. While the raw chemical is highly corrosive and must be handled with extreme care in factories, it is completely neutralised during food processing and baking. Once it reacts with the air and other ingredients, it ceases to be corrosive, leaving behind no harmful residues. EFSA evaluated the substance and determined that there is no safety concern when it is used properly in food products.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly assessed this compound and found no reason for concern in dietary amounts. Because it breaks down into sodium and water, it is processed by the body just like the sodium from regular table salt. EFSA re-evaluated the additive and concluded that establishing a numerical acceptable daily intake is unnecessary. The FDA also affirms its status as generally recognised as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practices.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient listed on labels under its common name, sodium hydroxide, or as E524. It is most commonly found in bakery products like pretzels, rolls, and buns. It also appears in cocoa powder processing to reduce bitterness, in black olives to soften them and remove their natural bitter compounds, and in some canned fruits and vegetables to help peel the skins cleanly during processing.
