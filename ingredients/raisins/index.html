Raisins

Whole foodGood

Raisins are simply dried grapes, offering a natural way to sweeten foods along with some dietary fibre and essential minerals.

What it is

Raisins are dried grapes that have lost most of their water content, leaving behind a concentrated, sweet fruit. They are a familiar whole food that people have enjoyed for thousands of years. Because the water is gone, the natural sugars, dietary fibre, and minerals like potassium are much more concentrated than they are in fresh grapes. They are a simple, single-ingredient food that brings natural sweetness and a chewy texture to many different dishes without any artificial additives.

How it's made

The journey of a raisin begins in the vineyard, where specific grape varieties are grown until they reach peak sweetness. Once harvested, the grapes are laid out on paper trays between the vine rows to dry naturally in the sun for several weeks. During this drying process, the grapes lose about four-fifths of their moisture and turn a deep brown colour. After drying, they are gathered, cleaned, stemmed, and sorted by size before being packaged for the shops. Some varieties may be treated with warm water to restore a tiny amount of moisture for a softer bite.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose raisins because they are a versatile, whole-food ingredient that adds both natural sweetness and texture. They help manufacturers sweeten products like breakfast cereals, baked goods, and snack bars without relying solely on refined white sugar. Raisins also hold onto moisture well, which helps keep baked items from drying out on the shelf. Because they are a recognisable, traditional ingredient, they help create a clean label that appeals to shoppers looking for simple, minimally processed foods.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As a whole food rather than an additive, raisins are a nutritious ingredient that provides natural energy, dietary fibre, and antioxidants. They do contain a high concentration of natural sugars, so it is sensible to enjoy them as part of a balanced diet, but they do not carry the health concerns associated with ultra-processed sweeteners or synthetic additives. No daily intake limit is established for this traditional dried fruit, and it is widely recognised as a wholesome addition to your daily meals.

What the science says

Major food authorities view raisins as a standard agricultural product rather than a food additive. The FDA classifies grapes and raisins as safe, traditional whole foods under general dietary guidelines. Because they are a natural fruit, they have not been subjected to the additive evaluation processes of organisations like EFSA or JECFA, which focus on synthetic substances. Nutritional science highlights that drying grapes concentrates their beneficial plant compounds, such as polyphenols, which help support the body's natural defences, alongside dietary fibre that assists with healthy digestion.

Where you'll find it

You will find raisins in a wide variety of everyday foods, especially in the baking and breakfast aisles. They are a staple in muesli, granolas, bran flakes, porridge pots, fruit loaves, scones, and cookies. They also appear in savoury dishes like tagines, couscous mixes, and stuffing, or simply packed on their own as a convenient snack. On food labels, they are usually listed plainly as raisins or dried grapes, with no E-number or complex chemical names to look out for.
