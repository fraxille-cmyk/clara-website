Silicon dioxide (E551)

E551AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Silicon dioxide is a mineral powder used to stop dry ingredients from clumping, rated neutral because regulators cannot set a daily limit due to sparse data.

What it is

Silicon dioxide is a natural compound made of silicon and oxygen, two of the most abundant elements in the earth crust. In nature, it is found as quartz or sand. In the food industry, it is used as a very fine, lightweight white powder. While it has the same chemical formula as sand, the food-grade version is manufactured to be highly purified and extremely fine, allowing it to coat other powders evenly.

How it's made

To make the food-grade ingredient, manufacturers do not simply crush sand. Instead, they produce it synthetically through a chemical process. They start with silicon tetrachloride or sodium silicate, which are reacted with oxygen or acids under highly controlled, high-temperature conditions. This chemical reaction precipitates out pure, amorphous silica. The resulting powder is washed, dried, and milled to ensure the particles are of a uniform, microscopic size suitable for mixing into food.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this powder as an anti-caking agent to keep dry foods flowing freely. Powdered ingredients like spices, salt, creamer, and soup mixes naturally absorb moisture from the air, which makes them sticky and causes them to clump together into hard lumps. By coating the individual particles of these powders, silicon dioxide acts as a physical barrier that repels moisture and prevents clumping. This keeps the product easy to pour, measure, and pack, extending its shelf life.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is given because the European Food Safety Authority could not establish an acceptable daily intake limit, noting that the available scientific data are insufficient to set a specific safe daily amount. It is not known to accumulate in the body, and much of it passes through the digestive system unchanged. IARC classifies silica as Group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.

What the science says

European and American regulators have evaluated this mineral compound several times. The European Food Safety Authority completed a major re-evaluation in 2018, raising no immediate safety concerns but urging manufacturers to provide more precise data on particle sizes, particularly regarding the presence of extremely small nanoparticles. The US Food and Drug Administration permits its use with a strict limit, restricting it to a tiny fraction of the total weight of the food product.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of dry, powdered, and granulated foods. It is common in garlic powder, onion powder, table salt, powdered soups, cake mixes, cocoa powder, and non-dairy coffee creamers. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed as silicon dioxide, or by its European code E551, and on US labels it is listed as silicon dioxide.
