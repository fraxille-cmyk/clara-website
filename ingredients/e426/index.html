Soybean hemicellulose (E426)

E426AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Soybean hemicellulose is a plant-derived fibre used to stabilise food mixtures, but it may trigger allergic reactions in people sensitive to soy.

What it is

Soybean hemicellulose is a soluble dietary fibre that comes from the outer hulls of soybeans. Chemically, it is a complex carbohydrate made of various sugar units linked together, similar to the natural pectins found in fruits. In the kitchen, you can think of it as a plant-based thickener and binder. It behaves much like other seed gums and starches, helping to hold ingredients together and keep liquids from separating in processed foods.

How it's made

The production process begins with the hulls of soybeans, which are a natural by-product of soy oil extraction. These hulls are washed and treated with hot water under pressure to dissolve the soluble fibres. The mixture is then filtered to remove any solid plant matter. The liquid extract is purified, concentrated, and dried into a fine, off-white powder. No chemical modification is performed on the fibre structure itself, meaning the final ingredient remains a natural plant polysaccharide.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this ingredient primarily as an emulsifier and stabiliser. It is highly effective at keeping oil and water droplets evenly mixed, which prevents separation in dressings and sauces. It also coats starch particles to stop them from sticking together, which helps maintain a smooth texture in noodles and rice dishes. Because it performs well in acidic conditions and at high temperatures, it is often chosen over other starches that might break down during pasteurisation.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. While the fibre itself is not toxic, the European Food Safety Authority warns that soybean hemicellulose may trigger allergic reactions in individuals sensitive to soy. Because it is derived directly from soybean hulls, it can carry trace proteins that cause an immune response in sensitive people. For anyone with a soy allergy, this ingredient is one to watch out for on packaging, though it poses no known risk to those without this specific sensitivity.

What the science says

Regulators have thoroughly evaluated this fibre and found no general safety concerns beyond its allergen potential. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the additive in 2017 and concluded that no numerical daily limit is established because it behaves like other dietary fibres in the digestive tract. It passes through the stomach and is broken down by friendly bacteria in the large intestine. The US Food and Drug Administration also reviewed its safety, filing it under its generally recognised as safe notification system.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of processed foods, including pre-packaged noodles, rice dishes, salad dressings, and flavoured milk drinks. On food labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically listed by its name, soybean hemicellulose, or by its E-number, E426. On products imported from the United States, it may sometimes be declared as soluble soybean polysaccharide, reflecting its chemical structure as a complex plant sugar.
