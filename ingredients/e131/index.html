Patent blue V (E131)

E131AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Patent blue V is a synthetic coal tar dye used to colour foods blue, with a strict daily intake limit set by European regulators.

What it is

Patent blue V is a synthetic food colour that imparts a deep, vibrant blue hue to foods and drinks. It is a coal tar dye, meaning its chemical structure belongs to a family of synthetic compounds originally derived from coal processing. Unlike natural pigments, this substance does not occur in nature and is valued for its intense, stable shade that does not fade easily under light or heat.

How it's made

This colour is manufactured entirely in a laboratory through a complex chemical synthesis. It is produced by condensing specific aromatic organic compounds, namely diethylaminobenzaldehyde and diethylaniline, using strong acids. The resulting compound is then oxidised and converted into a stable sodium or calcium salt, which is purified to remove unwanted chemical byproducts before it is approved for food use.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this dye to restore colour that might be lost during high-heat processing, or to add an appealing blue or green shade to products that are naturally colourless. It is highly soluble in water and incredibly potent, meaning only a tiny amount is needed to achieve the desired shade. This makes it a highly cost-effective alternative to natural blue pigments, which are often unstable and expensive.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. While it is synthetic, major food safety authorities have evaluated its safety thoroughly. EFSA keeps its daily limit of 5 mg per kg of body weight, which was re-confirmed during a detailed review. There is no toxicological concern when consumption remains within this established daily limit, and it is not classified by IARC.

What the science says

European food safety experts have evaluated the scientific evidence on how this dye behaves in the body. Studies show that the vast majority of the ingested colour passes through the digestive system without being absorbed, meaning very little of it enters the bloodstream. Because it is mostly excreted unchanged, researchers concluded that it does not accumulate in tissues or cause adverse systemic effects at normal dietary levels.

Where you'll find it

You will find this colour in a variety of processed foods, particularly in vivid blue or green confectionery, gelatin desserts, ice creams, lollies, and certain liqueurs or carbonated drinks. On ingredient lists in the UK and Europe, it is typically declared by its name, patent blue v, or by its official code, E131. It is not authorised for food use in the United States, so you will not find it on US labels.
