Onions are a whole, single-ingredient food that Clara rates as good, providing natural flavour and nutrients with no additives.

What it is

Onions are the edible bulbs of a plant in the allium family, which also includes garlic, leeks, and chives. They are one of the oldest cultivated vegetables in human history, prized for their pungent aroma when raw and their deep sweetness when cooked. Onions contain natural sugars, dietary fibre, and various sulphur compounds that give them their characteristic sharp taste and make your eyes water when you slice them. They are a fundamental building block of savoury cooking in almost every culinary tradition around the globe.

How it's made

Onions are grown directly in the ground as a root crop. Farmers plant seeds or small immature bulbs, called sets, in well-drained soil. As the plant grows, it develops green leafy shoots above the ground while the bulb swells below. Once the leaves turn yellow and fall over, the bulbs are harvested from the earth. They are then dried or cured in the sun or in ventilated sheds to allow the outer papery skins to harden, which protects the moist layers inside during storage and transport.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use onions to build a savoury foundation in processed foods, mimicking the traditional scratch-cooking process. They provide a complex depth of flavour that balances sweetness and acidity in soups, sauces, ready meals, and savoury snacks. Using real onions adds texture and visual appeal to a product, making it look more appealing and homemade. Commercially, they are a highly cost-effective way to deliver robust, familiar flavours that consumers expect in savoury dishes without relying solely on artificial flavourings.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. Since this is a single-ingredient whole food with nothing added, there are no industrial additives or synthetic chemicals to worry about. Onions are a natural, nourishing part of a balanced diet that humans have consumed safely for thousands of years. No regulatory authority has established an upper limit for their consumption, as they pose no toxicological concern. The only consideration is for individuals with a specific personal intolerance to alliums, who may experience mild digestive discomfort after eating them.

What the science says

Food safety authorities view onions as a standard agricultural commodity rather than a food additive, meaning they require no special chemical evaluations or safety limits. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration accept onions as a wholesome ingredient with a long history of safe use. Scientific assessments focus on their natural nutritional profile, noting that they are rich in vitamin C, potassium, and protective organosulphur compounds. These natural substances are metabolised easily by the body, supporting general wellbeing and digestive health.

Where you'll find it

You will find onions in an incredibly wide range of savoury products, including ready-made pasta sauces, canned soups, frozen pizzas, ready meals, and savoury tarts. On a ingredient label, they are usually listed simply as onions, onion, or by their specific variety such as red onions or brown onions. On imported European products, you might spot them under their translated names, such as oignons, cebollas, cipolle, or zwiebeln, but they always represent the same simple, whole vegetable.
