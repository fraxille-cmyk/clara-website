Plain caramel (E150a)

E150aAdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Plain caramel is a brown food colour made by heating sugars, and Clara rates it as a neutral, limited-concern additive.

What it is

Plain caramel is a dark brown food colour used to give foods and drinks a warm, rich appearance. It is the oldest and most widely used type of caramel colour. While it shares a name with the sweet caramel sauce you might pour over ice cream, this ingredient is processed specifically for its colouring properties rather than its sweet flavour. It is a complex mixture of compounds that dissolves easily in water, helping to standardise the appearance of many everyday products.

How it's made

This ingredient is made through a controlled thermal treatment of carbohydrates. Manufacturers take food-grade starches or sugars, such as glucose, fructose, or sucrose, and heat them to high temperatures. Unlike other caramel colours, plain caramel is produced without any ammonium or sulphite compounds. Sometimes, food-grade acids or alkalis are added to help speed up the caramelisation process, resulting in a chemically modified dark liquid or powder that is then purified and standardised for food use.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use plain caramel to ensure colour consistency across different batches of food, which can naturally vary due to seasonal ingredients or processing temperatures. It provides a stable, appealing brown hue that does not fade when exposed to light or heat during storage. It is also highly cost-effective compared to natural fruit or vegetable extracts, and it disperses evenly in liquid formulations without altering the texture or adding unwanted sweetness to the final product.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is based on its synthetic origin and the fact that it has a numeric acceptable daily intake. EFSA established a group acceptable daily intake of 300 mg per kg of body weight in 2011 for caramel colours, though this specific plain variety is considered the least processed of the four caramel types. There are no open safety concerns or warnings associated with its use, and it is widely accepted as a benign option for colouring foods.

What the science says

The safety of plain caramel has been thoroughly evaluated by major food safety authorities. EFSA re-evaluated the additive in 2011 and confirmed its safety, setting a clear daily limit for consumption. Because it is manufactured without the use of ammonium or sulphites, it does not contain the reaction by-products that sometimes raise questions in other caramel classes. JECFA and the FDA have also reviewed the scientific evidence and concluded that the ingredient presents no toxicological concern when used in standard commercial quantities.

Where you'll find it

You will find plain caramel in a wide variety of products, including dark beers, whiskies, soft drinks, vinegar, gravy granules, and baked goods. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed as plain caramel or by its European code, E150a. On US labels, it is simply listed as caramel colour. It is often chosen for products where a clean, simple label is preferred, as it does not carry the allergen associations of sulphite-containing additives.
