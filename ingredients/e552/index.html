Calcium silicate (E552)

E552AdditiveNeutral · benign

Calcium silicate is a safe, mineral-based powder used to stop dry foods from clumping together, with no daily limit needed.

What it is

Calcium silicate is a fine, white, free-flowing powder that is chemically a compound of calcium oxide and silica. Silica is the very same mineral that makes up quartz and sand, while calcium is a mineral we naturally find in dairy products and bones. When combined, they form a highly absorbent substance that is excellent at managing moisture. It is a synthetic mineral compound, meaning that while its components are found in nature, the specific structure used in food is created in a controlled environment to ensure purity.

How it's made

To make calcium silicate, manufacturers react lime, which is calcium oxide, with diatomaceous earth or silica sand in water under high pressure and temperature. This hydrothermal reaction causes the calcium and silica to bond together into a hydrated compound. The resulting mixture is then filtered, thoroughly washed to remove any impurities, dried into a fine powder, and milled to achieve an even particle size. This process ensures a clean, consistent substance that meets strict food-grade standards.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use calcium silicate primarily as an anti-caking agent to keep powdered and granulated foods flowing smoothly. Because it can absorb up to two and a half times its own weight in water without losing its powdery texture, it acts like a tiny sponge. It coats other food particles, preventing them from sticking together or forming hard lumps when exposed to humidity. This makes it incredibly useful for keeping products easy to pour, measure, and mix, which is why it beats alternatives that might clump under damp conditions.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. It is not absorbed by the body in any significant way, passing through the digestive tract without causing harm. Because it is so safe, the European Food Safety Authority evaluated it in 2018 and determined that no limit is established for its daily intake. It does not accumulate in the body, and there are no known health concerns associated with consuming it in food. It is widely accepted as a safe ingredient for the general population.

What the science says

The science on calcium silicate is well-established, with multiple major food authorities confirming its safety. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the substance alongside other silicates in 2018 and concluded that it does not pose a safety concern at the levels currently used in food. The Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives has also reviewed it and assigned it an acceptable daily intake of not specified, which is a designation reserved for substances of very low toxicity. No open safety concerns or adverse effects have been identified in these comprehensive reviews.

Where you'll find it

You will find calcium silicate in a variety of dry, powdered products that need to remain free-flowing. It is commonly added to table salt, baking powder, spice blends, powdered milk, grated cheese, and dehydrated soups. On ingredient lists, it is usually declared plainly as calcium silicate, but you might also see it listed under its European code, E552. On US labels, it is listed as calcium silicate, where it is recognised as a safe substance for food use.
