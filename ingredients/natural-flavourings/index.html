Natural flavourings

FlavouringNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Natural flavourings are taste-enhancing extracts derived from real plants or animals, though their exact chemical recipes are kept secret by manufacturers.

What it is

Natural flavourings are concentrated extracts used to give food a specific taste or smell. Unlike artificial alternatives, these substances must come from a natural starting material such as a plant, herb, spice, fruit, vegetable, yeast, meat, or seafood. They are complex mixtures of volatile molecules that mimic the natural essence of familiar foods, helping to restore or enhance the sensory experience of what we eat. While they originate in nature, they are highly processed by the time they reach the final product.

How it's made

The journey begins with a natural source, such as citrus peels, vanilla pods, or savoury yeasts. Manufacturers use physical, enzymatic, or microbiological processes to isolate the flavour molecules. This involves heating, roasting, distilling, or using solvents to extract the essential oils and aromatic compounds. Once extracted, these concentrated flavours are often standardised by blending them with carriers like starch, maltodextrin, or vegetable oils so they can be easily mixed into food formulations.

Why manufacturers use it

Food processing, heating, and freezing can dull the natural taste of ingredients. Manufacturers use these extracts to restore lost flavours and ensure that every batch of food tastes exactly the same, regardless of seasonal variations in agricultural crops. They are incredibly potent, meaning a tiny amount can replace expensive whole ingredients. Using them also allows companies to print the appealing word natural on the front of the packaging, which shoppers find more reassuring than synthetic alternatives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. By law, these substances must originate from natural sources, but the exact chemical blend is kept as a trade secret, which is a sign of industrial processing rather than a direct health concern. Because the precise ingredients are not disclosed on the label, individuals with highly specific food sensitivities or uncommon allergies may find it difficult to know exactly what they are consuming. No regulatory body has established an upper limit for general natural flavourings as a single category.

What the science says

Food safety authorities evaluate the individual flavouring substances that make up these mixtures rather than the proprietary blends themselves. The European Food Safety Authority and the Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives systematically categorise and assess these chemical components to ensure they do not present safety concerns at the levels consumed. Because these mixtures are highly purified, they do not carry the nutritional benefits of the original whole foods, serving purely a sensory purpose in our diet.

Where you'll find it

You will find these ingredients in almost every category of processed food, including yoghurts, fizzy drinks, baked goods, savoury snacks, and ready meals. On the packaging, they are declared under various names depending on the country, such as natural flavouring, natural flavour, arôme naturel, aroma naturale, or natural flavours. They do not have an E-number because they are classified as flavourings rather than food additives under European labelling laws.
