Ponceau 4R (E124)

E124AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Ponceau 4R is a synthetic red dye that Clara rates AVOID because it requires a warning in Europe regarding its effects on activity and attention in children.

What it is

Ponceau 4R is a synthetic red dye used to give foods a bright, stable strawberry-red shade. It belongs to the azo dye family, a group of human-made colourings known for their intense pigment. Unlike natural red colours derived from beetroot or insects, this substance is entirely artificial and does not occur in nature. It is highly soluble in water, making it incredibly easy to blend into liquid foods and wet mixtures where natural alternatives might clump or fade.

How it's made

This colouring is manufactured through a chemical synthesis process starting with petroleum derivatives. Specifically, coal tar or petroleum by-products are reacted to form aromatic hydrocarbons, which then undergo a process called diazotisation. This chemical reaction links the molecules together to create the characteristic azo bond that gives the dye its intense red colour. The resulting powder is then purified, standardised, and prepared as a sodium salt for food manufacturing.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose this synthetic dye because it is exceptionally stable, cheap, and reliable. Natural red colourings often degrade when exposed to heat during baking, light on supermarket shelves, or acids in soft drinks. This artificial dye resists all of these factors, ensuring the product keeps its bright red appearance for months. It is also highly concentrated, meaning manufacturers only need a tiny amount to achieve a vivid colour, which keeps production costs very low.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. This rating is driven by the fact that foods containing this dye in Europe must carry a warning label. Under European rules, any product with this colouring must state that it may have an adverse effect on activity and attention in children. Because of these concerns, the United States has never authorised the substance for food use, although the European Union still permits it within strict maximum limits.

What the science says

Regulators have evaluated this dye several times to assess its safety. EFSA re-evaluated the substance in 2009 and established a daily limit of 0.7 mg per kg of body weight, lowering it from the previous limit due to concerns over sensitivity. IARC classifies Ponceau 4R as Group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans. While there is no established evidence of long-term cellular damage in humans, the potential impact on childhood behaviour remains the primary reason for regulatory caution and warning labels.

Where you'll find it

You will find this colour in highly processed foods that require a bright red appearance, such as tinned cherries, strawberry fillings, soft drinks, sweets, and dessert gels. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically listed by its name, Ponceau 4R, or by its E-number, E124. It is not found in American food products as the US authorities do not permit its use in food.
