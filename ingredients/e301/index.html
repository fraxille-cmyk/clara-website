Sodium ascorbate (E301)

E301AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Sodium ascorbate is a form of vitamin C used to keep food fresh and stop colour from fading, which Clara rates as a neutral, limited-concern additive.

What it is

Sodium ascorbate is a mineral salt of ascorbic acid, which you probably know better as vitamin C. It is a white, odourless crystalline powder that dissolves easily in water. Unlike ascorbic acid, which is quite sour and acidic, this sodium-bound version is much milder on the palate. In the body, it is broken down into sodium and ascorbate, providing a source of vitamin C that our cells can readily use just like the vitamin found naturally in citrus fruits.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers start with ascorbic acid, which is typically produced through a multi-step fermentation process of glucose derived from corn or wheat starch. This acid is then dissolved in water and mixed with a sodium source, such as sodium bicarbonate or sodium hydroxide. The reaction neutralises the acid, creating sodium ascorbate. The solution is then carefully filtered, crystallised, and dried into a fine powder, ready to be added to food products.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use this ingredient primarily as an antioxidant to stop oxygen from spoiling food. When exposed to air, fats can turn rancid and natural pigments can fade, making food look unappealing. By reacting with oxygen before the food does, this additive preserves the natural colour of cured meats, keeps sliced fruits from turning brown, and extends the shelf life of packaged goods. It is often preferred over pure ascorbic acid because its neutral acidity does not alter the intended flavour profile of the recipe.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Because it is simply a combination of sodium and vitamin C, the body metabolises it easily and safely. The only minor consideration is that it contributes a small amount of dietary sodium to your food, which is why it carries a limited-concern rating for those who need to monitor their overall salt intake. No upper limit is established for its use in general food applications, as it is considered highly compatible with human biology.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this substance and found no safety concerns. JECFA has assessed the ingredient and established that there is no need to set a specific numerical limit on daily intake. EFSA re-evaluated the additive and confirmed that its use in food does not pose a safety risk, as any excess vitamin C is simply excreted by the body. The FDA also recognises it as generally safe for use as a chemical preservative and dietary nutrient.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of processed foods, particularly cured meats like bacon, ham, and sausages, where it helps maintain a fresh pink colour. It also appears in canned vegetables, fruit juices, bakery products, and some infant foods. On packaging, it is typically declared by its full name as sodium ascorbate, or simply as E301 in European ingredient lists.
