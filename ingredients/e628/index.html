Dipotassium guanylate (E628)

E628AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Dipotassium guanylate is a savoury flavour enhancer made from yeast or starch that is generally considered safe and has no established daily limit.

What it is

Dipotassium guanylate is a potassium salt of guanylic acid, a natural compound found in yeast, mushrooms, and many plants. It belongs to a family of ingredients known as nucleotides, which are the building blocks of genetic material in all living things. In food, these compounds are responsible for the rich, savoury taste often called umami. It is closely related to disodium guanylate, but it uses potassium instead of sodium to stabilise the flavour-enhancing molecule.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers start with a natural source rich in nucleic acids, such as yeast cells or starch from corn or sugar cane. Microorganisms are used to ferment these raw materials, breaking down the complex structures into simpler nucleotides. Once the guanylic acid is isolated from the fermentation broth, it is neutralised with potassium hydroxide. This chemical reaction yields dipotassium guanylate, which is then purified, crystallised, and dried into a white, odourless powder.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this ingredient to intensify savoury flavours in processed foods. It is an incredibly potent flavour enhancer that works synergistically with glutamates, meaning that when it is combined with ingredients like yeast extract or monosodium glutamate, the savoury effect is multiplied. This allows food companies to achieve a deep, meaty, or brothy taste while using much less salt, helping them reformulate products to meet lower sodium targets without sacrificing the appealing taste.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. It is processed by the body in the same way as the natural nucleotides found in everyday foods. Because it is metabolised into uric acid, people who need to manage their uric acid levels are sometimes advised to be mindful of foods containing these additives, but for the general population, it is considered benign. EFSA evaluated the additive and determined that no numerical acceptable daily intake limit needs to be specified, reflecting its low concern.

What the science says

The safety of this compound has been thoroughly assessed by major food safety authorities. EFSA re-evaluated guanylic acid and its salts in 2020 and concluded that they do not pose a safety concern at the levels currently used in food. Because these substances are natural components of human cells and are consumed daily through a normal diet, regulators have not found any reason to restrict their use. No safety concerns or adverse effects have been identified in scientific evaluations.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in highly savoury, processed foods such as instant noodles, canned soups, gravy granules, potato crisps, seasoned snack mixes, and ready meals. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically declared by its name as dipotassium guanylate or by its European code, E628. On US labels, it is listed as dipotassium guanylate, where it is permitted for use as a flavour enhancer under federal regulations.
