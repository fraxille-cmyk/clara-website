Garlic is a natural whole food used to add a rich, savoury flavour to dishes, and Clara rates it as a positive addition to your basket.

What it is

Garlic is a pungent bulb from a plant closely related to onions, leeks, and chives. It has been grown and eaten for thousands of years across the globe, prized both for its culinary qualities and its traditional role in supporting general wellbeing. Inside each clove is a complex mix of sulphur compounds, which are released when the plant tissue is crushed or chopped. These compounds give garlic its characteristic sharp aroma and warm, savoury taste, making it a staple in almost every culinary tradition.

How it's made

Garlic is grown naturally in fields, harvested when the leaves begin to yellow, and then dried or cured to preserve the bulbs. For food manufacturing, the cloves are peeled and processed into various convenient formats. This can involve simple dehydration and grinding to create garlic powder or granules, mincing the fresh cloves into a paste with a little oil or salt, or extracting the essential oils to create concentrated garlic flavourings. No synthetic chemicals are needed to produce these ingredients, keeping the process close to the farm.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers rely on garlic to build deep, complex savoury flavours that are difficult to replicate with synthetic additives. It acts as a natural flavour enhancer, bringing out the richness of meats, vegetables, and grains. Using dried or powdered garlic is often preferred in commercial kitchens because it distributes evenly through dry spice blends, sauces, and batters without adding extra moisture. It also has a much longer shelf life than fresh cloves, ensuring that packaged foods maintain their intended taste profile over time.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As a whole food, garlic is a wholesome ingredient that poses no safety concerns for the vast majority of people. It is a natural part of a balanced diet, providing both culinary pleasure and beneficial plant compounds. No daily intake limit is established for garlic when consumed as a food ingredient. While eating very large amounts of raw garlic can sometimes cause mild digestive warmth or temporary breath odour, it is entirely supportive of a healthy lifestyle when found in your everyday grocery items.

What the science says

Regulators and food safety authorities view garlic as a standard, wholesome food ingredient rather than an additive. The FDA classifies garlic as generally recognised as safe, meaning it has a long history of common use in food without safety concerns. Scientific evaluations focus on its active sulphur compounds, which are known to support healthy circulation and natural defences in the body. Because it is a traditional agricultural crop, it does not require the intensive chemical testing or daily limit evaluations associated with synthetic food additives.

Where you'll find it

You will find garlic in an incredibly wide range of savoury foods, including pasta sauces, ready meals, marinades, crisps, soups, and seasoned meats. On food labels, it is typically listed simply as garlic, garlic powder, minced garlic, or dehydrated garlic. Because it is a whole food rather than a regulated additive, it does not have an E-number. It is sometimes grouped under the general term of spices or natural flavourings, but manufacturers usually prefer to name it proudly on the ingredient list.
