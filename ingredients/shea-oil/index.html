Shea oil

Processed ingredientNeutral

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Shea oil is a natural plant fat pressed from the nuts of the African shea tree, used to give foods a smooth, rich texture.

What it is

Shea oil is a liquid fraction of the natural fat extracted from the kernels of the shea tree, which is native to the dry savannahs of West Africa. While many people know shea butter as a rich cosmetic cream, the oil is simply a lighter, more liquid version of this same plant fat. It consists of natural fatty acids, particularly oleic and stearic acids, which are very similar to the fats found in olive oil and cocoa butter, making it a familiar substance to our bodies.

How it's made

The journey starts with the collection of wild shea fruits. The fleshy outer pulp is removed, and the hard nuts inside are boiled, cracked, and dried in the sun. The kernels are then crushed, roasted, and ground into a thick paste, which is mixed with water to separate the valuable fats. To make the oil, this raw fat undergoes a physical fractionation process where it is cooled gently so that the harder fats crystallise and can be filtered out, leaving behind a clear, liquid oil.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers value shea oil because it behaves beautifully at room temperature, offering a smooth mouthfeel without needing chemical modification. It is an excellent plant-based alternative to dairy fats and expensive cocoa butter. The oil helps to keep chocolate smooth, stops fat from separating in spreads, and ensures that fillings remain soft and creamy over a long shelf life. It is also highly stable, meaning it resists going rancid when exposed to air.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. As a natural vegetable fat, it is processed by the body in the same way as other dietary oils. It does not carry any chemical additive status, and no daily intake limit is established because it is consumed as a standard food ingredient rather than a chemical modifier. It is a wholesome source of plant lipids that has been eaten safely for generations in West Africa and is widely accepted by food authorities worldwide.

What the science says

Regulatory bodies have evaluated shea fats as standard food ingredients rather than additives. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration recognise shea oil as a safe source of dietary fat. Because it is a natural oil, scientific assessments focus on its fatty acid profile, which is rich in monounsaturated and saturated fats. No safety concerns have been raised by regulators, and no daily limit is established for its consumption in food products.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient listed on labels simply as shea oil, shea fat, or sometimes grouped under vegetable oil or vegetable fat. It is a common sight in chocolate confectionery, biscuit fillings, margarines, vegan spreads, and baked goods where a creamy texture is needed. Because it has no E-number, it always appears under its plain English name in the ingredient list.
