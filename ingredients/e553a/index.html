Magnesium silicate (E553a)

E553aAdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Magnesium silicate is a synthetic mineral powder used to keep dry foods flowing smoothly. Clara rates it as a benign additive with no safety concerns.

What it is

Magnesium silicate is a fine, white, odourless powder that is chemically composed of magnesium, silicon, and oxygen. While it shares a similar chemical family with natural clay minerals, the version used in our food is a highly purified, synthetic compound. It is insoluble in water and alcohol, meaning it does not dissolve when mixed into liquids, but instead disperses evenly to coat other particles. You can think of it as a microscopic barrier that keeps dry ingredients from sticking together.

How it's made

To manufacture food-grade magnesium silicate, producers combine soluble sodium silicate with a water-soluble magnesium salt, such as magnesium sulphate or magnesium chloride. This chemical reaction causes magnesium silicate to precipitate out of the liquid as a solid. The resulting precipitate is carefully filtered, washed with water to remove any remaining salts, dried, and then milled into an extremely fine powder. This synthetic route ensures the final substance is highly pure and entirely free from any natural mineral impurities.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on magnesium silicate primarily as an anti-caking agent. In powdered products, moisture from the air can easily cause particles to stick together, forming annoying lumps or solid blocks. By coating the individual grains of a powder, this substance absorbs excess moisture and acts as a physical spacer, allowing the powder to flow freely and pour smoothly. It is a highly efficient and cost-effective solution that outperforms many natural alternatives by remaining stable under a wide range of temperatures and processing conditions.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is not absorbed by the human digestive tract, it simply passes through the body without causing any known adverse effects. EFSA re-evaluated the substance in 2018 and determined that there was no need to establish a numerical acceptable daily intake limit, meaning it is considered safe for consumption at the levels typically found in food. No limit is established for its daily intake, and there are no reports of sensitivity or intolerance associated with it.

What the science says

Scientific panels have thoroughly evaluated magnesium silicate and found no evidence of accumulation or toxicity in the body. When consumed, it does not break down into harmful components, and the body does not metabolise it. EFSA published a comprehensive re-evaluation in 2018, confirming its safety as a food additive alongside similar silicate compounds. The US FDA also maintains its status as a generally recognised as safe substance, reflecting a broad scientific consensus that this mineral compound poses no risk to human health.

Where you'll find it

You will find magnesium silicate in a variety of dry, powdered, and granulated foods. It is commonly added to table salt, powdered spices, dried herbs, icing sugar, non-dairy coffee creamers, and dehydrated soup mixes to prevent clumping. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is often listed by its name or as E553a. On US labels, it is typically declared as magnesium silicate, where it is permitted for use under federal regulations.
