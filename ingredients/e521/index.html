Aluminium sodium sulphate (E521)

E521AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Aluminium sodium sulphate is a synthetic firming agent that Clara rates AVOID because it adds to our overall dietary intake of aluminium.

What it is

Aluminium sodium sulphate is a synthetic mineral salt that functions as a firming agent in food. It is a double sulphate of aluminium and sodium, which means it contains both metals bound to sulphate groups. In its natural state, it is related to alum minerals, but the version used in food is a highly purified powder. It is used to keep plant tissues firm and crisp during processing, preventing them from turning mushy when heated or stored.

How it's made

This ingredient is manufactured chemically by combining aluminium sulphate and sodium sulphate in water. The mixture is heated and then cooled to allow the compound to crystallise into a solid form. Once the crystals form, they are dried, milled into a fine white powder, and packaged for food manufacturers. It is a synthetic process that does not involve any genetic modification or biological fermentation, resulting in a highly stable mineral salt.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this compound primarily to maintain the structure of delicate foods. When processing fruits or egg whites, the cell walls can easily break down, leading to a loss of texture. This mineral salt interacts with the natural proteins and pectins in the food to strengthen their physical structure. It is a highly reliable and inexpensive firming agent that performs consistently under different temperatures, making it a preferred choice over less stable alternatives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. The main issue is that it contributes to our total intake of aluminium, which can accumulate in the body over time. EFSA established a tolerable weekly intake of 1 mg of aluminium per kg of body weight in 2008, and noted that dietary exposure to aluminium from food additives already exceeds this limit in a significant part of the population. Because of this, it is best to minimise your intake of foods containing this additive.

What the science says

Regulators have closely evaluated the safety of aluminium compounds in food. EFSA re-evaluated aluminium dietary intake in 2008 and concluded that many people already consume more than the recommended weekly limit of 1 mg per kg of body weight. The FDA considers it generally recognised as safe under specific conditions, but European regulators have restricted its use to a very small number of food categories to help limit public exposure to aluminium. No toxicological concern is raised for occasional low exposure, but regular consumption is discouraged.

Where you'll find it

You will find this additive in a limited range of products, particularly candied, crystallised, and glacé fruits, as well as some processed egg white products. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its full name as aluminium sodium sulphate, or by its European code E521. On US labels, it may be listed as aluminium sodium sulfate, reflecting the American spelling, but it performs the same firming role wherever it is used.
