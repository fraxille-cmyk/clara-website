Carboxymethyl cellulose (E466)

E466AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Carboxymethyl cellulose is a plant-derived thickener and stabiliser used to improve texture, rated neutral by Clara as a limited-concern additive.

What it is

Carboxymethyl cellulose is a modified plant fibre that helps foods hold their shape and texture. It is derived from the natural cellulose found in the cell walls of plants, such as wood pulp or cotton fibres. By chemically attaching small acid groups to this natural cellulose, chemists make it highly soluble in water. In the kitchen, it behaves much like natural starches or gums, absorbing water to create a smooth, thick consistency in liquids and semi-solids without adding any flavour of its own.

How it's made

The production process begins with harvesting natural cellulose from sustainable plant sources like wood pulp or cotton linters. This raw cellulose is treated with an alkaline solution, usually sodium hydroxide, which swells the fibres and makes them more reactive. Manufacturers then introduce monochloroacetic acid, which reacts with the cellulose to attach carboxymethyl groups to the molecular chain. After this reaction, the mixture is thoroughly washed, purified to remove any by-products, dried, and ground into a fine white or off-white powder ready for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this ingredient because it is an exceptionally efficient thickener and stabiliser that performs reliably under various temperatures. It prevents ice crystals from forming in frozen desserts, keeps oil and water from separating in salad dressings, and gives low-fat foods a rich, creamy mouthfeel. Because it binds water so tightly, it also helps packaged baked goods retain their moisture and stay soft for longer. It is highly cost-effective, requiring only tiny amounts to achieve the desired thickness compared to natural starches.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Because it is a modified form of dietary fibre, your body does not digest or absorb it; instead, it passes straight through your digestive system. It has no nutritional value and does not add calories to your diet. No limit is established for its daily intake because regulators consider it exceptionally low in risk. Some studies on animals suggest that consuming very large quantities might alter the balance of microbes in the gut, but normal dietary levels are generally well tolerated.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this ingredient and confirmed its safety for the general public. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated it and concluded there is no safety concern at the levels currently found in food. Because it behaves like other non-digestible carbohydrates, consuming extremely large amounts could lead to mild digestive changes, such as temporary bloating or a laxative effect, but these do not occur at normal dietary levels. No regulatory body has found any reason to restrict its use in standard food products.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of processed foods, particularly ice cream, low-fat spreads, ready-made sauces, gluten-free bread, and cake mixes. On ingredient lists, it is commonly declared as carboxymethyl cellulose, sodium carboxymethyl cellulose, cellulose gum, or simply by its European code, E466. On US labels, it is often listed as carboxymethylcellulose sodium or cellulose gum, where it serves the same texturising purpose.
