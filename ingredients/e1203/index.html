Polyvinyl alcohol (E1203)

E1203AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Polyvinyl alcohol is a synthetic glazing agent used to coat food supplements, rated NEUTRAL by Clara as it is considered safe within established limits.

What it is

Polyvinyl alcohol is a synthetic polymer, which means it is a large molecule made of repeating chemical units linked together. While it shares a name with the alcohol in drinks, it behaves entirely differently in the body and does not cause intoxication. It is a water-soluble, odourless, and tasteless substance that forms an exceptionally strong, flexible, and clear film when dried, making it highly useful for creating protective barriers around delicate ingredients.

How it's made

This ingredient is entirely synthetic and does not exist in nature. Manufacturers produce it through a multi-step chemical process. First, they polymerise vinyl acetate to create polyvinyl acetate, which is the main ingredient in common household wood glue. They then chemically modify this polymer by reacting it with an alcohol, such as methanol, in a process called hydrolysis. This reaction replaces the acetate groups with hydroxyl groups, transforming the water-insoluble glue base into a highly water-soluble polymer ready for food use.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use polyvinyl alcohol primarily as a glazing agent and protective coating. Its main commercial advantage is its superb ability to block moisture and oxygen, which prevents the active ingredients inside a tablet or capsule from degrading. It dissolves quickly once swallowed, ensuring the nutrients are released at the right time. Because it is highly flexible and adheres well to surfaces, it creates a smooth, professional finish on tablets without cracking, beating out traditional natural alternatives that can be brittle or sensitive to humidity.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. It is not absorbed well by the human digestive system, and most of what you consume passes straight through your body without change. EFSA keeps its daily limit of 50 mg per kg of body weight, a level that is very difficult to reach through normal consumption of coated tablets. IARC classifies polyvinyl alcohol as Group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans, meaning there is no evidence that it poses such a risk.

What the science says

The safety of this polymer has been thoroughly analysed by international food authorities. EFSA re-evaluated the additive in 2020 and confirmed that it does not raise safety concerns at current exposure levels, maintaining the established acceptable daily intake. Animal studies show that the substance has very low toxicity and does not accumulate in the organs. Because its use is strictly limited to specific applications like supplement coatings, the amount a person consumes on a daily basis remains extremely low and well within safe boundaries.

Where you'll find it

You will primarily find this ingredient in food supplements, vitamins, and mineral tablets, where it is used to coat the capsules. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically declared by its functional class and name or its E-number, appearing as glazing agent polyvinyl alcohol or glazing agent E1203. On US labels, you may see it listed simply as polyvinyl alcohol, where it is also permitted in certain food packaging materials and adhesive coatings.
