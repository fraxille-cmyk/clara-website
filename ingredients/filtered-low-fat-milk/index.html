Filtered low fat milk

Processed ingredientGood

Diets

✕ Not vegan✕ Not low-FODMAP
  • Filtered low fat milk is not vegan.
  • Not low-FODMAP — filtered low fat milk contains lactose.

Filtered low fat milk is cow milk that has been run through fine filters to concentrate the protein and reduce the natural sugars and fat.

What it is

This ingredient is simply cow milk that has been passed through a series of very fine membranes. This physical separation process allows some parts of the milk to pass through while holding others back. The result is a dairy liquid that has a different balance of nutrients than standard milk, typically containing more protein and calcium but less of the natural milk sugar known as lactose, all while keeping the fat content low.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, fresh skimmed or low-fat milk is chilled and pumped under pressure through specialised polymer or ceramic filters. This process, known as ultrafiltration, does not use chemicals. The filters have pores so tiny that they let water and some dissolved sugars pass through, while retaining the larger milk proteins and minerals. The concentrated milk protein liquid is then standardised with low-fat milk to reach the exact nutritional profile desired before being pasteurised.

Why manufacturers use it

Food and drink makers use this ingredient to boost the protein content of their products naturally without relying on isolated protein powders. It provides a rich, creamy texture and a clean dairy flavour even with very little fat. Because some of the natural sugars are filtered out, it also helps manufacturers keep the sugar count low on the nutrition panel while maintaining a appealing mouthfeel in dairy drinks, shakes, and yoghurts.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. Since it is a minimally processed dairy product rather than an additive, it does not carry a tier rating. It is a nutritious ingredient that offers a concentrated source of calcium and high-quality dairy protein. For most shoppers, it is a wholesome choice, though anyone with a milk allergy or severe lactose intolerance should treat it with the same caution as standard dairy products.

What the science says

Regulators view filtered milk as a standard food ingredient rather than a food additive, so it does not require a complex additive safety evaluation. The filtration process is a physical separation technique that does not alter the safety of the milk proteins or minerals. European and American food authorities recognise the process as a clean and safe way to standardise dairy components, and no limit is established for its consumption.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in high-protein milk drinks, ready-to-drink breakfast shakes, recovery beverages, and light yoghurts. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared plainly as filtered low fat milk, filtered low-fat milk, or ultrafiltered low fat milk. Because it is a whole food ingredient rather than a chemical additive, it does not have an E-number and is listed by its common name.
