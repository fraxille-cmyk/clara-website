Sodium aluminium silicate (E554)

E554AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Sodium aluminium silicate is a synthetic mineral used to stop powders from clumping, but its food uses are now highly restricted in Europe.

What it is

Sodium aluminium silicate is a synthetic mineral compound made from sodium, aluminium, silicon, and oxygen. In nature, similar minerals exist as feldspars, which make up a large portion of the crust of the earth. In the food industry, however, manufacturers use a highly purified, manufactured version. It is a fine, white, odourless powder that does not dissolve in water. Its primary characteristic is its ability to absorb moisture and keep dry ingredients flowing freely without forming hard lumps.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers combine water-soluble sodium silicate with an aluminium salt, such as aluminium sulphate, in an aqueous solution. This chemical reaction causes a solid precipitate to form. The resulting white precipitate is carefully filtered out of the liquid, thoroughly washed with water to remove any leftover salts, dried at high temperatures, and then milled into an extremely fine powder. This process ensures a consistent particle size that is ideal for coating other dry substances.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this compound as an anti-caking agent to keep powdered foods flowing smoothly through processing machinery and packaging lines. It acts as a physical barrier between particles, absorbing excess moisture and preventing them from sticking together. Without it, powdered ingredients would absorb humidity from the air, forming sticky clumps that clog factory equipment and make the final product difficult for consumers to pour or measure. It is a highly efficient and cost-effective choice for maintaining a long shelf life.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This synthetic mineral compound is used as an anti-caking agent to prevent clumping, but the data are currently insufficient to rate it higher. No daily limit is established by EFSA, which noted in its re-evaluation that the available database was too limited to set a specific acceptable daily intake. Because of concerns regarding overall dietary exposure to aluminium, the European Union has heavily restricted its use, though the FDA still permits it under US regulations.

What the science says

The safety of this additive has been evaluated by major food authorities, focusing primarily on the aluminium content. EFSA published a re-evaluation in 2020 and concluded that the scientific evidence was insufficient to establish a numerical acceptable daily intake. While some shoppers worry about aluminium accumulation in the body, regulators have not found evidence of harm at typical dietary levels, though they have chosen to limit its use to minimise overall exposure. No toxicological concern has been proven at current restricted levels.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient primarily in dry, powdered foods and specialised dairy products. In the European Union, its use is strictly limited to salt intended for the surface treatment of ripened cheese. In other regions, it may appear in powdered milk, non-dairy creamers, dried soup mixes, and table salt. On ingredient labels, it is listed as sodium aluminium silicate, E554, or e 554, and on US labels it is often declared as sodium aluminosilicate.
