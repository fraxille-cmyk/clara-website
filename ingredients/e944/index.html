Propane (E944)

E944AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Propane is a synthetic gas used as a propellant to push foods out of aerosol cans, and it is considered a limited-concern additive.

What it is

Propane is a colourless, odourless gas that you might already know as a fuel for camping stoves or heating. In the food world, however, it serves a very different and highly purified purpose. It is a simple hydrocarbon gas that does not mix with water, meaning it can sit inside a pressurised container alongside food without changing the food itself. When it is refined to food-grade standards, it is incredibly pure and free from the impurities found in industrial fuels.

How it's made

To make food-grade propane, manufacturers extract raw natural gas from deep underground or isolate it during the refining of crude oil. This raw gas undergoes a series of intensive purification and distillation steps to separate the propane from other hydrocarbons. For food use, the gas must be exceptionally clean, so it is scrubbed of any moisture, sulphur compounds, and odourants, resulting in a highly purified propellant that meets strict regulatory standards.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use propane as a propellant to push liquid foods out of aerosol containers, such as whipped cream cans or non-stick cooking sprays. It is an excellent choice because it provides a steady, reliable pressure that helps dispense the food in a smooth, even spray or foam. Because it is highly volatile, the gas evaporates almost instantly into the air the moment it leaves the nozzle, leaving only the food behind on your plate or pan without altering the flavour.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Because propane is a gas that evaporates immediately upon spraying, virtually none of it remains in the food you actually consume. The Scientific Committee on Food has evaluated its safety and decided that no acceptable daily intake limit is established, as the residue in food is negligible. It is authorised for food use in both Europe and the United States, where it is considered to pose no toxicological concern for shoppers.

What the science says

Scientific panels have thoroughly assessed propane to ensure it is safe for use in aerosol foods. The European Scientific Committee on Food reviewed the gas alongside other propellants and concluded that its use does not present a risk to human health because exposure is so minimal. The United States Food and Drug Administration has also affirmed its status as generally recognised as safe. Because it does not accumulate in the body or remain in the food, researchers have found no reason to set a restrictive daily limit.

Where you'll find it

You will primarily find this ingredient in aerosol products, most notably in cans of whipped cream, dessert toppings, and various non-stick cooking oil sprays. On a food label, it is usually listed by its name as propane, or under its European designation E944. It is often used in combination with other authorised propellant gases like butane or isobutane to achieve the perfect pressure inside the can.

Sources

  • Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 16 December 2008 on food additives
  • FDA 21 CFR 184.1655, Direct food substances affirmed as Generally Recognized As Safe (GRAS) for Propane
