Argon (E938)

E938AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Argon is a natural, inert gas used to displace oxygen in packaging to keep food fresh, and it is completely harmless to consume.

What it is

Argon is a completely natural gas that makes up about one percent of the atmosphere we breathe every single day. It belongs to a family of elements known as noble gases, which are famous for being chemically lazy, meaning they do not like to react or bond with other substances. In the food world, it is used as a protective blanket inside packaging to keep oxygen away from sensitive ingredients, helping to preserve their natural qualities without changing their taste or texture.

How it's made

Because argon is already present in the air around us, manufacturers do not need to synthesise or build it from scratch. Instead, they capture ordinary air and cool it down to extremely low temperatures until it turns into a liquid. Through a process called fractional distillation, the different gases in the liquid air are separated based on their boiling points. The argon is collected in its pure, gaseous form, ready to be used in food packaging without any chemical modification.

Why manufacturers use it

Oxygen is the enemy of freshness, as it causes fats to go rancid, colours to fade, and fresh foods to spoil. Manufacturers use argon to flush out the oxygen from bags and containers before sealing them. Argon is heavier than nitrogen and oxygen, which makes it incredibly efficient at sinking into a container and displacing the air. Because it is chemically inert, it does not react with the food, preserving the original flavour, aroma, and crispness of the product for much longer.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because argon is a natural mineral gas that we breathe constantly and which does not react with our bodies, it poses no toxicological concern. EFSA has re-evaluated the gas and determined that there is no need to establish an acceptable daily intake limit. It simply passes out of the packaging and into the air when you open the product, leaving no residue behind for your body to process.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly assessed argon and found it to be entirely safe for its intended use. The European Food Safety Authority evaluated it alongside other inert gases and confirmed its safety due to its natural origin and lack of chemical reactivity. In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration has affirmed its status as generally recognised as safe. There are no open safety concerns, and no negative effects on human biology have ever been reported.

Where you'll find it

You will typically find argon used in the packaging of delicate foods that are highly sensitive to oxygen, such as bags of potato crisps, roasted nuts, ground coffee, and pre-packaged salads. It is also frequently used in bottled wines to prevent oxidation after the cork is drawn. On a food label, it is usually listed simply as argon or by its European code, E938, though because it is a packaging gas that escapes upon opening, it is not always required to be declared.
