Fumaric acid (E297)

E297AdditiveNeutral · benign

Fumaric acid is a benign, synthetic version of a natural organic acid used to control acidity in food, with a long history of safe use.

What it is

Fumaric acid is a naturally occurring organic acid that plants, fungi, and even human skin produce when exposed to sunlight. It has a sharp, fruit-like taste, similar to malic acid found in apples. In the human body, it plays a key role in how our cells generate energy from food. Although it exists widely in nature, the version you see on ingredient lists is typically made in a laboratory to ensure purity and consistency for food production.

How it's made

To make this ingredient at a commercial scale, manufacturers do not extract it from plants, as that would be highly inefficient. Instead, they produce it synthetically through a chemical process. This involves heating maleic acid, which is derived from petroleum products, in the presence of a catalyst to rearrange its molecular structure. This reaction transforms it into fumaric acid, which is then purified, crystallised into a fine white powder, and washed to ensure it is completely free of impurities.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use this ingredient primarily to control acidity and add a lasting, sour flavour. It is highly valued because it does not absorb moisture from the air, which helps dry powdered mixes, like gelatins and instant drinks, stay free-flowing without clumping. It also releases its sourness slowly when dissolved, extending the tart taste in chewing gum and fruit-flavoured sweets. Additionally, it helps to regulate acidity in baked goods, improving the texture of dough.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. It is a normal part of human metabolism and is processed by the body without any difficulty. EFSA established an acceptable daily intake of 12.5 mg per kg of body weight in 2016, noting that typical dietary exposure is well below this limit. There are no safety concerns or adverse effects associated with the amounts normally consumed in food, and it has a long history of use as a natural acid.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this substance and confirmed its safety. EFSA re-evaluated it in 2016 and found no safety concerns, confirming that the body metabolises it efficiently. The FDA also permits its use as a direct food additive. Because it is a natural intermediate in our own cellular energy cycle, it does not accumulate in the body and is broken down into carbon dioxide and water, leaving no harmful residues.

Where you'll find it

You will frequently find this ingredient in powdered desserts, fruit-flavoured drinks, pie fillings, chewing gum, and packaged tortillas. On food labels, it is typically listed by its plain name as fumaric acid, or by its European code, E297. On US labels, you will see it listed as fumaric acid, where it is permitted for use in various processed foods to control acidity and enhance flavour stability.
