Almonds

Whole foodGood

Almonds are a nutritious whole food, packed with healthy fats, fibre, and protein, making them an excellent choice for a balanced diet.

What it is

Almonds are the edible seeds of the almond tree, a close relative of peaches and plums. Though we often call them nuts, they are botanically classified as drupes, which are fruits with a hard outer shell protecting a seed inside. They have been cherished for thousands of years across the Mediterranean and the Middle East, prized for their rich, buttery flavour and satisfying crunch. Today, they are enjoyed worldwide as a versatile staple that fits easily into a modern pantry.

How it's made

The journey of an almond begins in the orchard, where the trees bloom with pink and white flowers before developing green, fuzzy hulls. As summer ends, these hulls split open to reveal the hard shell inside. Mechanical shakers gently vibrate the trees so the almonds fall to the ground to dry. Once harvested, they are cleaned and de-hulled. Depending on how they will be sold, they may be left whole, blanched in hot water to slip off their brown skins, sliced into flakes, or ground into a fine meal.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose almonds because they add a wonderful texture, rich flavour, and natural nutrition to products. They provide a satisfying crunch in granolas and cereal bars, or a smooth, creamy base when blended into dairy-free milks and spreads. Ground almonds are also a popular alternative to wheat flour, offering a moist texture and structure to gluten-free baked goods. Because they are naturally stable, they help extend the shelf life of snacks without the need for artificial preservatives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As a whole food, almonds are highly nutritious and present no health concerns for the vast majority of people. They are packed with beneficial monounsaturated fats, plant protein, and dietary fibre, which support overall well-being. The only caution is for individuals with a tree nut allergy, who must avoid them to prevent an allergic reaction. For everyone else, they are a wholesome, nourishing ingredient that can be enjoyed freely as part of a balanced diet.

What the science says

Major food authorities have thoroughly evaluated almonds as a whole food and recognise their positive role in human nutrition. The European Food Safety Authority has reviewed health claims regarding dietary fibre and unsaturated fats, confirming their role in maintaining normal blood cholesterol levels. Because they are a natural agricultural product rather than a food additive, regulators do not set an upper limit for their consumption. Instead, dietary guidelines worldwide encourage their inclusion in daily meals as a source of essential nutrients.

Where you'll find it

You will spot this ingredient in a wide variety of products, from breakfast cereals and muesli to chocolate bars, baked goods, and dairy alternatives. On packaging, they might be listed simply as almonds, or more specifically as blanched almonds, flaked almonds, or ground almonds depending on how they were prepared. On imported European labels, you might also see them listed under their traditional names such as amandes, mandeln, mandorle, or almendras.
