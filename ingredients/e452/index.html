Polyphosphates (E452)

E452AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Polyphosphates are added mineral salts used to keep food moist and stable, but Clara rates them AVOID because modern diets often contain too much phosphate.

What it is

Polyphosphates are a group of mineral salts made of linked phosphate units. In nature, phosphorus is a vital element found in every living cell, helping to build bones and manage energy. In the food industry, these synthetic salts are used to control acidity, bind water, and keep ingredients from separating. They behave differently to the organic phosphorus naturally present in whole foods, as our bodies absorb these added inorganic forms much more efficiently.

How it's made

These additives are produced through a controlled chemical process. Manufacturers start with phosphoric acid, which is typically derived from mined phosphate rock. This acid is neutralised with alkaline sodium, potassium, calcium, or ammonium compounds. The resulting mixture is then heated to high temperatures, which drives off water and forces the individual phosphate molecules to link together into long chains, creating the final white powder.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on these salts because they are incredibly effective at holding moisture. In processed meats and seafood, they prevent water from leaking out during storage and cooking, which keeps the product juicy and maintains its weight. In processed cheese, they act as emulsifiers to stop the fat and water from separating when melted. They also help stabilise batters, prevent grittiness in powdered drinks, and stop canned vegetables from discolouring.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. While phosphorus is an essential nutrient, the inorganic phosphates added to processed foods are absorbed very rapidly by the body. EFSA has raised concerns that our total daily intake of phosphates from all food sources can easily exceed safe levels. High levels of phosphate in the blood can place a strain on the kidneys and have a negative impact on the cardiovascular system, particularly when consumed regularly in modern processed diets.

What the science says

Regulators have closely scrutinised our total exposure to these additives. EFSA established a group acceptable daily intake of forty milligrams per kilogram of body weight for phosphates, expressed as phosphorus. However, their dietary exposure assessments showed that many people, especially children and those with phosphate-rich diets, regularly exceed this limit. JECFA also monitors these substances, noting that while they are not stored in the body, high intakes alter the delicate balance of minerals in our circulation.

Where you'll find it

You will find these additives in a wide variety of highly processed foods, including sausages, chicken nuggets, tinned seafood, processed cheese slices, instant noodles, and bakery mixes. On the label, they are usually declared by their specific function and name as polyphosphates, sodium polyphosphate, potassium polyphosphate, calcium polyphosphate, or simply by the E-number, E452.
