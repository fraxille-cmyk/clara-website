Potassium metabisulphite (E224)

E224AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

A synthetic preservative used to keep food fresh, which Clara rates AVOID due to temporary safety limits and data gaps regarding its effects on the body.

What it is

Potassium metabisulphite is a synthetic inorganic salt that belongs to the sulphite family. It is a white, crystalline powder with a pungent sulphur smell that acts as a powerful preservative and antioxidant. In the kitchen, you can think of it as a chemical shield that keeps food from spoiling and stops fresh produce from turning an unappealing brown when exposed to the air. It is closely related to other sulphur-based preservatives commonly used in winemaking and food preservation.

How it's made

This ingredient is manufactured through a precise chemical process rather than being extracted from nature. It is produced by reacting potassium hydroxide, which is a strong alkaline substance, with sulphur dioxide gas. This chemical reaction causes the crystals of potassium metabisulphite to form. The resulting powder is then purified, dried, and packaged for use in food and beverage processing, ensuring a highly stable and consistent additive.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on this additive because it performs multiple technical jobs at a very low cost. It is highly effective at stopping the growth of unwanted wild yeasts, bacteria, and moulds, which dramatically extends the shelf life of packaged foods. Additionally, it prevents oxidation, meaning it stops fruits, vegetables, and juices from losing their vibrant natural colours. Its ability to release sulphur dioxide under acidic conditions makes it far more efficient and reliable than natural alternatives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. While regulators permit its use, EFSA established a temporary group acceptable daily intake of 0.7 mg per kg of body weight in 2016 because of lingering uncertainties and data gaps regarding its long-term effects on the body. Some consumers may easily exceed this limit due to high dietary exposure across many everyday foods. IARC classifies sulphur dioxide and sulphites as Group 3, not classifiable as to their carcinogenicity to humans.

What the science says

The safety of this substance has been evaluated by major international food authorities. EFSA re-evaluated sulphites in 2016 and highlighted that more data is needed to fully understand how they behave in the body, particularly concerning potential sensitivity reactions. The FDA considers it generally recognised as safe but restricts its use on raw fruits and vegetables, and in foods that are major sources of vitamin B1, because sulphites are known to neutralise this essential nutrient in the body.

Where you'll find it

You will frequently find this preservative in dried fruits, dehydrated vegetables, fruit juices, syrups, and vinegar. It is also widely used in winemaking and cider production to control fermentation. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its full name, potassium metabisulphite, or by its European code, E224. On US labels it is listed as potassium metabisulfite, and it must always be clearly declared because some individuals have a high sensitivity to sulphites.
