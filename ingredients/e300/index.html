Ascorbic acid (E300)

E300AdditiveGood · benign

Ascorbic acid is simply vitamin C, used as a benign antioxidant to keep food fresh and colourful.

What it is

Ascorbic acid is the scientific name for vitamin C, a water-soluble nutrient that occurs naturally in many fruits and vegetables. In the food industry, it is used as an antioxidant to protect packaged foods from the damaging effects of oxygen. While it is identical to the vitamin C found in fresh oranges, its presence on an ingredient list usually means it has been added to help preserve the product rather than to fortify it, though it still acts as a nutrient once consumed.

How it's made

Most commercial ascorbic acid is produced through a multi-step process that starts with natural plant sugars, typically glucose derived from corn or wheat starch. This sugar undergoes a fermentation process using specialised micro-organisms to convert it into an intermediate compound. This intermediate is then chemically purified, oxidised, and crystallised into a fine white powder. The resulting substance is chemically identical to the vitamin C that forms naturally in citrus fruits.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use ascorbic acid to keep food looking and tasting fresh. When food is exposed to air, oxygen reacts with the natural fats, pigments, and enzymes, causing fats to turn rancid and fruits to turn brown. Ascorbic acid acts as a shield, reacting with the oxygen first to stop these changes. It is a highly effective, cost-efficient alternative to synthetic chemical preservatives, allowing products to maintain an appealing colour and flavour during their time on the supermarket shelf.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. Because it is an essential nutrient that our bodies require daily, it is a benign additive that poses no safety concerns for the general population. The body naturally metabolises what it needs and easily excretes any excess. It is approved for use in food by major regulatory bodies worldwide, including EFSA and the FDA, with no daily intake limit established because of its exceptionally high safety profile.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have evaluated ascorbic acid extensively and consistently confirm its safety. EFSA re-evaluated the additive and confirmed that its use in food does not raise safety concerns, noting that it is a well-understood nutrient. JECFA has also reviewed the substance and declined to set a numerical limit on daily intake, classifying it as safe for general use. The consensus across all major food safety authorities is that it is a helpful, non-toxic addition to the food supply.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of products, including fruit juices, canned vegetables, cured meats, sliced fruits, and baked goods. On food labels, it may be listed by its common name, ascorbic acid, or as vitamin C, vitamine c, vitamina c, or its European code, E300. Its presence is a sign that the manufacturer is using a familiar nutrient to keep the product fresh.
