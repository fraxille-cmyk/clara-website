Calcium alginate (E404)

E404AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Calcium alginate is a natural thickener extracted from brown seaweed that is used to create gels and stable textures in processed foods.

What it is

Calcium alginate is a natural substance derived from the cell walls of brown seaweed. It belongs to a family of soluble fibres known as alginates, which are celebrated for their remarkable ability to absorb water and form thick, jelly-like structures. While it shares some culinary properties with other seaweed extracts, it is entirely distinct from carrageenan. In its pure form, it is a white to yellowish fibrous powder that dissolves slowly in water to create a firm, heat-stable gel, making it a highly versatile ingredient in modern food preparation.

How it's made

The journey of this ingredient begins with the harvesting of brown algae from coastal waters. The harvested seaweed is washed and treated with an alkali solution to extract the soluble alginic acid. This acid is then neutralised and reacted with calcium salts, such as calcium chloride, which causes the alginate to precipitate out of the liquid as a solid. The resulting calcium alginate is carefully washed, dried, and milled into a fine, uniform powder that is ready for food manufacturing, requiring no chemical modification of its natural polymer structure.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this ingredient primarily for its exceptional gelling, thickening, and stabilising properties. Unlike many other gelling agents that melt when heated, the gel formed by this substance remains stable at high temperatures. This makes it incredibly useful for restructured foods, such as synthetic cherries, onion rings, and reformed meat products. It also helps to maintain a smooth texture in ice creams, prevents ice crystals from forming, and stabilises the foam in beverages, offering a highly reliable and cost-effective alternative to animal-derived gelatin.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. This ingredient has a long-standing record of safety and is processed by the body much like any other dietary fibre, passing through the digestive system largely unchanged. Because it is not absorbed into the bloodstream and shows no adverse effects even at high intake levels, EFSA has established that no numerical daily limit is necessary. It is a clean, carrageenan-free option for those looking to avoid specific seaweed-derived additives while still enjoying well-textured foods.

What the science says

Extensive scientific evaluations have confirmed that this substance behaves as a non-digestible carbohydrate. When consumed, it is partially fermented by the natural bacteria in the large intestine, while the remainder is excreted. Major food safety authorities have reviewed its safety profile thoroughly. The evidence shows no accumulation in the body and no interference with the absorption of essential minerals. Because of this excellent safety profile, regulators have concluded that its use in food poses no safety concerns for the general population.

Where you'll find it

You will frequently find this ingredient in structured fruit pieces, pie fillings, puddings, ice cream, and processed meat or fish products. On ingredient labels, it is typically declared by its plain name as calcium alginate or under its European code, E404. In the United States, it is permitted as a generally recognised as safe substance, where it is listed on US labels as calcium alginate. It is also common in low-sugar jams and jellies, where it helps to create a firm set without requiring large amounts of sugar.
