Creatine monohydrate

IngredientGood

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

A natural compound that helps supply energy to cells, widely used to support muscle strength and physical performance.

What it is

Creatine monohydrate is a naturally occurring compound that our bodies produce in small amounts, mainly in the liver and kidneys, and that we also get from eating meat and fish. Chemically, it is a combination of three amino acids, which are the basic building blocks of proteins. In the body, it is stored mostly in skeletal muscle, where it plays a vital role in regenerating the primary energy currency of our cells during quick bursts of physical activity. The monohydrate form simply means it is bound with a single water molecule, which makes it highly stable and easy for the body to absorb.

How it's made

While creatine is found naturally in animal tissues, the creatine monohydrate used in supplements and fortified foods is made in a laboratory to ensure purity and consistency. Manufacturers synthesise it by reacting two specific organic compounds, cyanamide and sodium sarcosinate, in a water-based environment. This chemical reaction yields a highly pure creatine slurry, which is then heated, crystallised, and thoroughly dried into a fine white powder. This synthetic route ensures that the final ingredient is completely free of animal-derived contaminants, making it suitable for vegetarians and vegans.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers and sports nutrition brands add this ingredient to products to enhance their functional value. It is the gold standard for supporting muscle performance, helping to increase physical power and strength during successive bursts of short-term, high-intensity exercise. Creatine monohydrate is favoured over other forms of creatine because it is exceptionally stable in dry powder mixes, has a neutral flavour, dissolves easily in liquids, and is highly cost-effective to produce, making it the most reliable choice for fortifying protein powders, energy bars, and recovery drinks.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. This ingredient is not classified as an additive, and extensive evaluations show that it is highly compatible with human biology when consumed in standard dietary or supplemental amounts. The European Food Safety Authority has evaluated creatine and established that a daily intake of three grams is completely safe for healthy adults. Because it is a natural compound that the body already produces and metabolises daily, it does not accumulate in a harmful way, and no upper limit of concern has been established for general consumption beyond standard serving guidelines.

What the science says

This compound is one of the most thoroughly researched dietary substances in the world. Numerous scientific panels have analysed its behaviour in the human body, concluding that it effectively increases muscle creatine stores and supports physical performance. EFSA has authorised specific health claims for creatine, confirming its role in increasing physical performance during short, high-intensity exercise. Researchers have monitored its long-term use in athletes and found no adverse effects on kidney or liver function in healthy individuals, making it a highly trusted ingredient with a clear, well-documented mechanism of action.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in sports nutrition products, including pre-workout powders, protein shakes, meal replacement bars, and specialised recovery drinks. On packaging, it is typically listed clearly under its full name, creatine monohydrate, as it does not have an E-number. It may also appear in fortified functional foods aimed at active individuals or older adults looking to support muscle maintenance, where it is easily identified by its standard English name.
