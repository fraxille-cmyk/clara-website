Ginger root

Whole foodGood

Ginger root is a natural, aromatic spice used to add a warm, fiery flavour and soothing qualities to foods and drinks.

What it is

Ginger root is the thick, underground stem of a flowering plant native to warm parts of Asia. It belongs to the same botanical family as turmeric and cardamom. When you slice or grate it, it releases a sharp, woody aroma and a warm, spicy heat. People have used this knobbly root in kitchens and traditional wellness practices for thousands of years, valuing it both as a culinary staple and a natural way to soothe the stomach.

How it's made

This ingredient is a whole food that requires very little processing to reach your kitchen. Farmers grow the ginger plant in rich, warm soil until the leaves wither, indicating the underground stems are mature. Workers dig up these roots, wash them thoroughly to remove soil, and trim away the green shoots. The ginger is then either sold fresh as whole roots, dried and ground into a fine pale yellow powder, or pressed to extract its natural juices and oils.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose ginger root because it delivers a complex, fiery flavour that synthetic alternatives cannot easily replicate. It contains natural oils that give gingerbread, biscuits, and soft drinks their characteristic bite. Beyond its taste, ginger acts as a natural preservative by slowing down the oxidation of fats in baked goods. It also helps to mask bitter notes in herbal teas and provides a warming sensation in throat lozenges and wellness shots.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As a whole food and a traditional spice, ginger root is a nourishing ingredient with an excellent safety profile. It is metabolised easily by the body and has been consumed for generations without concern. No daily intake limit is established for ginger root when used as a food ingredient. It is widely recognised as a wholesome addition to a balanced diet, bringing natural warmth and flavour without any synthetic additives.

What the science says

Regulators have thoroughly evaluated ginger root and consider it entirely safe for general consumption. The FDA classifies ginger as generally recognised as safe under 21 CFR section 182.10. Researchers have focused on the active compounds in the root, particularly gingerols, which give the spice its heat. Studies show these compounds can help soothe the digestive tract and support comfortable digestion. Because it is a natural food rather than a chemical additive, it does not carry the safety warnings associated with synthetic flavourings.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of products, from spicy ginger beers and herbal teas to biscuits, cakes, stir-fry sauces, and ready meals. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared simply as ginger, ginger root, ground ginger, or ginger extract. Because it is a whole food rather than a food additive, it does not have an E-number, making it a very clean and straightforward label entry to spot in the supermarket aisle.

Sources
