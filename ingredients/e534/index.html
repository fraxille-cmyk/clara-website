Iron tartrate (E534)

E534AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Iron tartrate is a synthetic anti-caking agent used in salt with a low daily intake limit, rated neutral by Clara.

What it is

Iron tartrate is a synthetic compound made by combining iron with tartaric acid, an organic acid found naturally in grapes and other fruits. In the food industry, it is classified as an anti-caking agent. It is a specialised additive designed to keep fine grains from sticking together, ensuring that free-flowing crystals remain loose and easy to pour even when exposed to moisture in the air.

How it's made

This ingredient is produced in a controlled factory setting rather than being extracted directly from nature. Manufacturers synthesise it by reacting a soluble iron salt, such as iron chloride, with tartaric acid or its sodium salt in an aqueous solution. The resulting mixture is carefully filtered, purified, and dried into a fine powder that is ready to be blended into commercial salt mixtures.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use iron tartrate because it is an exceptionally efficient anti-caking agent for salt. Salt naturally absorbs moisture from the atmosphere, which causes the crystals to dissolve slightly and fuse into hard clumps. Iron tartrate prevents this clumping process at very low concentrations. It is highly valued because it performs this technical job reliably without altering the clean white appearance, taste, or odour of the final product.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is due to its synthetic origin and a low numeric acceptable daily intake of 0.06 mg per kg of body weight. EFSA established this strict limit in 2015 to ensure that consumers do not ingest excessive amounts of the compound. Because it is only permitted in tiny quantities in a single food category, typical daily exposure remains well below this safety threshold.

What the science says

The safety of this additive has been thoroughly evaluated by European authorities. EFSA assessed the compound and concluded that it does not pose a safety concern at the proposed use levels, establishing a numeric acceptable daily intake of 0.06 mg per kg of body weight. There are no open toxicological concerns or safety gaps identified by European regulators, though the substance is not authorised for food use in the United States.

Where you'll find it

You will find this additive almost exclusively in table salt, sea salt, and various salt substitutes. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its name, iron tartrate, or by its European code, E534. Because its authorisation is strictly limited, you will not find it in other processed foods, spice blends, or baked goods.

Sources
