Glucose syrup

Processed ingredientAvoid

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Glucose syrup is a highly concentrated liquid sugar made from starch that adds intense sweetness and improves texture but offers no nutritional value.

What it is

Glucose syrup is a sweet, thick liquid made up of simple sugar molecules. Unlike table sugar, which is sucrose, this syrup consists mainly of glucose, the primary sugar our bodies use for energy. It is a highly refined carbohydrate that behaves much like other liquid sweeteners, such as corn syrup, which is simply a specific variety of glucose syrup. While it occurs naturally in many fruits and plants in small amounts, the version we find in packaged foods is a concentrated ingredient designed to deliver rapid sweetness and body to processed products.

How it's made

To make this syrup, manufacturers start with a cheap, abundant starch source, most commonly corn, wheat, potatoes, or barley. This starch is mixed with water and treated with acids or specific enzymes, which break down the long, complex carbohydrate chains into simple glucose molecules. The liquid is then filtered to remove any remaining plant proteins or minerals, clarified to make it completely transparent, and evaporated until it reaches a thick, sticky consistency. The final product is a highly purified sugar solution with almost all other plant nutrients removed.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose this syrup because it is incredibly versatile and cheaper than cane sugar. It does not crystallise easily, which keeps sweets smooth and glossy rather than grainy. It also retains moisture, which stops baked goods from drying out and extends their shelf life. In frozen desserts, it lowers the freezing point to keep ice cream scoopable. Because it blends easily with liquids, it is the ideal choice for sweetening beverages, sauces, and condiments where solid sugar crystals would struggle to dissolve completely.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID. While it is approved for use by food authorities, it is a concentrated form of sugar that provides empty calories and contributes to high blood sugar levels. Because it is a dominant ingredient in this product, consuming it regularly can lead to rapid rises in blood glucose. The body processes these simple sugars very quickly, which can disrupt natural energy levels and lead to a constant demand for more sweet foods, offering no beneficial vitamins, minerals, or fibre to balance its high energy content.

What the science says

Food authorities have evaluated glucose syrup as a standard food ingredient rather than an additive. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration have not set a specific daily limit for its intake, meaning no limit is established, but both organisations emphasise the importance of limiting free sugars in the diet. Scientific consensus shows that consuming large amounts of refined sugars contributes to an imbalance in daily energy intake. Because it is absorbed rapidly into the bloodstream, researchers focus on how it affects insulin response and overall metabolic balance.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a vast range of sweet and savoury packaged foods, including confectionery, biscuits, cakes, ice cream, sports drinks, and sweetened yoghurts. It also appears in savoury items like barbecue sauces, salad dressings, and ready meals. On a packaging label, it is typically listed simply as glucose syrup, glucose-fructose syrup, or wheat glucose syrup, and because it is classified as a food ingredient rather than a food additive, it does not carry an E-number.
