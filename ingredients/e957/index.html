Thaumatin (E957)

E957SweetenerNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Thaumatin is an extremely sweet natural protein extracted from a West African fruit, used in tiny amounts to sweeten and enhance flavours.

What it is

Thaumatin is a natural, highly intense sweetener and flavour enhancer. It is actually a mixture of closely related proteins rather than a sugar or a synthetic chemical. In nature, these proteins are found in the velvety fruit of a West African shrub called Thaumatococcus daniellii. It is famous for being incredibly sweet, thousands of times sweeter than table sugar, which means manufacturers only need to use a microscopic amount to achieve the desired taste in food.

How it's made

The journey of this ingredient begins with the harvest of the wild fruit in West Africa. The seeds are removed, and the fleshy part of the fruit is extracted using clean water. This liquid extract contains the sweet proteins, which are then purified through a process called ultrafiltration to separate them from other plant materials. The purified proteins are crystallised and dried into a fine, cream-coloured powder. This process is purely physical extraction and purification, meaning the protein is not chemically modified.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers choose this ingredient because it is an exceptionally powerful sweetener that also works as a flavour modifier. It helps to mask bitter tastes, smooth out sharp edges in citrus flavours, and enhance the overall mouthfeel of a product. Because it is so potent, it can replace large amounts of sugar without adding calories or bulk. It is also highly stable during pasteurisation and baking, making it a versatile tool for creating low-sugar foods that still taste satisfying.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Because it is a simple plant protein, the body digests and metabolises it just like any other dietary protein. Regulators have found no safety concerns even at high levels of consumption. No limit is established for daily intake because the substance is so intensely sweet that manufacturers cannot physically add enough of it to a product to cause any harm before the food becomes unpalatably sweet.

What the science says

The safety of this protein has been thoroughly evaluated by major food authorities worldwide. EFSA re-evaluated the substance and confirmed its safety, noting that there is no need to set a numerical acceptable daily intake. The FDA has also reviewed its use and filed multiple notices classifying it as generally recognised as safe. Because it is digested into standard amino acids, science views it as a benign dietary protein with no toxicological concerns or adverse effects on human biology.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of low-calorie and sugar-free products, including flavoured milks, yoghurts, chewing gum, table-top sweeteners, and dietary supplements. On a shopping label, it is usually listed by its plain name as thaumatin, or by its European code, E957. It is sometimes grouped with other sweeteners or natural flavourings depending on how the manufacturer uses its taste-modifying properties in the recipe.
