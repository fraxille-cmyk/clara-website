Butane (E943a)

E943aAdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Butane is a synthetic propellant gas used to spray food from aerosol cans, and regulators consider it of limited concern because it evaporates instantly.

What it is

Butane is a colourless, odourless gas that belongs to a family of simple hydrocarbons. You might recognise its name because it is commonly used as a fuel for pocket lighters and camping stoves. In the food world, however, it does not serve as a fuel or an ingredient you eat. Instead, it acts as a propellant, which is a gas used to push liquid foods out of their containers in a fine, even mist.

How it's made

This gas is a synthetic substance obtained during the refining of crude oil and the processing of natural gas. Raw petroleum is heated and separated into various fractions based on boiling points. The butane fraction is captured, isolated, and then subjected to a rigorous purification process. This ensures that any impurities are completely removed, leaving behind a highly purified, food-grade gas that meets strict regulatory standards for contact with what we eat.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on this gas because it is highly effective at creating a consistent spray. When packed under pressure inside an aerosol can, it liquefies and mixes with the food, such as vegetable oil. When you press the nozzle, the pressure drops, causing the liquid gas to expand instantly back into a vapour. This rapid expansion breaks the food into tiny droplets, allowing you to coat a baking pan evenly with a very small amount of oil.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Because it is a gas that boils at a temperature well below freezing, it vaporises into the air the moment it leaves the nozzle, leaving virtually no residue on your food. The European Commission Scientific Committee on Food evaluated its safety and decided that no acceptable daily intake limit needs to be specified, as the amount of gas actually consumed from sprayed food is negligible.

What the science says

Scientific panels have thoroughly assessed this propellant to ensure it poses no risk to consumers. Because it is highly volatile, it does not accumulate in the body or interact with human biochemistry when used in aerosol sprays. Both European and American authorities have reviewed the safety data regarding inhalation and ingestion of the tiny residual amounts. They concluded that the gas is safe for its intended technical use, as it dissipates almost instantly upon spraying.

Where you'll find it

You will primarily find this ingredient in aerosol cooking sprays, baking pan releases, and some whipped toppings that come in pressurised cans. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its plain name as butane, or sometimes under its European code, which is E943a. Because it is used as a processing aid to expel the product, it is sometimes omitted from the main ingredient list depending on local labelling laws, but it is often listed on cooking sprays.
