Anthocyanins (E163)

E163AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Anthocyanins are natural plant pigments that give foods a vibrant red, purple, or blue colour, and they are generally considered safe to consume.

What it is

Anthocyanins are natural pigments that belong to the flavonoid family. They are the very compounds responsible for the rich reds, deep purples, and bright blues you see in autumn leaves, ripe blueberries, red cabbage, and dark grapes. In the plant world, these pigments help attract pollinators and protect plant tissues from light damage. When used as a food additive, they bring those same natural, water-soluble colours to our plates, acting as a direct alternative to synthetic dyes.

How it's made

To make this colour on a commercial scale, manufacturers extract the pigments from natural plant sources. The most common raw materials are grape skins, black carrots, red cabbage, and elderberries. These materials undergo a physical extraction process, usually involving water or a mild alcohol solution, to draw the colour out. The resulting liquid is then filtered, concentrated, and sometimes dried into a powder. Because it comes directly from edible fruits and vegetables, the production route is straightforward and avoids heavy chemical synthesis.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use these pigments to restore colour that might be lost during high-heat processing, or to make a product look more appealing. They are highly valued because they allow brands to achieve bright, attractive shades of red, purple, and blue while keeping a clean label that appeals to shoppers who prefer to avoid artificial dyes. They perform beautifully in acidic foods, where the colour remains stable and vibrant, making them a popular choice for colouring fruit-based products.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because these pigments are natural components of a normal diet, they are processed by the body in the same way as the fruits and vegetables we eat every day. Regulators have evaluated their safety thoroughly and found no reason for concern. No daily limit is established for anthocyanins extracted from grape skins or blackcurrants, as the evidence shows they are processed without any adverse effects on our biology.

What the science says

The scientific consensus on these natural colours is highly reassuring. Major food safety authorities have reviewed the safety data multiple times. EFSA re-evaluated these pigments and confirmed that their use as food colours does not pose a safety concern, noting that the intake from food additives is typically much lower than what we naturally consume from a diet rich in berries and vegetables. JECFA has also evaluated these substances and supports their safety, finding no evidence of accumulation or unwanted activity in the body.

Where you'll find it

You will find these colours in a wide variety of supermarket items, especially those with fruit flavours. They are common in soft drinks, yoghurts, sweets, jellies, ice creams, and fruit preparations. On a food label, they might be listed plainly as anthocyanins, grape skin extract, or by their European code, E163. They are sometimes grouped under the name copper complexes of chlorophylls when used in specific colour formulations, though they primarily provide red and purple hues.
