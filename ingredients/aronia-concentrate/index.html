Aronia concentrate

Processed ingredientNeutral

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Aronia concentrate is a natural, concentrated fruit juice made from aronia berries, used to add a rich purple colour and a tart flavour to foods.

What it is

Aronia concentrate is a thick, dark liquid made by removing water from the juice of aronia berries, which are also known as black chokeberries. These small, dark-purple berries grow on hardy shrubs native to North America but are now widely cultivated in Europe. The concentrate is naturally rich in deep pigments and has a sharp, astringent taste that is similar to a dry red wine or a tart cranberry. It is a whole-food ingredient rather than a purified chemical additive, meaning it retains the natural characteristics of the original fruit.

How it's made

The journey starts with harvesting ripe aronia berries, which are washed and crushed to release their deep purple juice. This juice is pressed to separate the liquid from the skins and seeds. To turn the juice into a concentrate, it is gently heated under a vacuum, which allows the water to evaporate at a lower temperature to preserve the natural colour and flavour. The resulting thick liquid is pasteurised to ensure it stays fresh, then packaged for food manufacturers to use as a natural colouring and flavouring ingredient.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose aronia concentrate primarily for its intense, stable purple hue and its distinctive tartness. It serves as an excellent natural alternative to synthetic red and purple food dyes, which helps brands maintain a clean label. Because it is highly concentrated, a very small amount goes a long way in colouring a product, making it a cost-effective choice. It also adds a complex, fruity acidity that balances sweetness in beverages and fruit preparations without the need for added artificial flavourings.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. As a concentrated fruit juice rather than a synthetic chemical additive, it does not carry the safety concerns associated with artificial colourings. No daily intake limit is established for this ingredient because it is consumed as a normal part of a balanced diet. It is processed by the body in the same way as any other fruit juice, providing natural fruit sugars and plant pigments. It is widely accepted as a wholesome food ingredient that poses no safety concerns for the general population.

What the science says

Because aronia concentrate is classified as a food ingredient rather than a food additive, it is not subjected to the same restrictive daily limits as synthetic substances. EFSA and the FDA recognise concentrated fruit juices as safe food components. Scientific evaluations focus on the rich concentration of natural plant pigments, known as anthocyanins, which give the berry its dark colour. These compounds are well studied for how they interact with the body, and researchers consistently find them to be highly compatible with human digestion and metabolic processes.

Where you'll find it

You will find aronia concentrate in a variety of colourful products, including fruit juices, smoothies, yoghurts, jellies, and fruit gums. It is also popular in herbal teas and berry-flavoured soft drinks. On food labels, it is typically listed plainly as aronia concentrate, aronia berry concentrate, concentrated aronia juice, or simply concentrated chokeberry juice. Because it is a food ingredient rather than an additive, it does not have an E-number and is listed alongside other fruit ingredients in the ingredient list.
