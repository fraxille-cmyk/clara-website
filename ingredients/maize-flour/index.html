Maize flour

Processed ingredientNeutral

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Maize flour is a simple, gluten-free flour made from ground dried corn kernels, used to thicken sauces and give structure to baked goods.

What it is

Maize flour is a fine powder made by milling whole dried corn kernels. It is a staple food across the globe, known for its bright yellow or pale white colour depending on the variety of corn used. Unlike cornstarch, which is just the isolated starch of the grain, maize flour contains the protein, fibre, and nutrients of the whole kernel. It is naturally free from gluten, making it a popular alternative to wheat flour for people who prefer to avoid gluten in their daily diet.

How it's made

The journey of maize flour begins in the fields where corn is grown and allowed to dry on the stalk. Once harvested, the kernels are cleaned to remove any debris and then milled. In traditional milling, large stones grind the whole kernel. In modern industrial milling, rollers break down the kernel, sometimes removing the tough outer hull and the oily germ to extend the shelf life of the flour, before grinding the remaining portion into a uniform, fine powder that is ready for packaging.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose maize flour because it provides structure, bulk, and a pleasant golden colour to baked goods. Since it does not contain gluten, it behaves differently to wheat flour, helping to create a tender, crumbly texture in biscuits and cakes rather than a chewy one. It is also an excellent thickener for soups, sauces, and gravies because it absorbs liquid efficiently when heated, and it serves as a cost-effective, neutral-tasting base for many extruded breakfast cereals and snacks.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. As a whole-food ingredient rather than a chemical additive, it is digested by the body just like any other complex carbohydrate. It provides energy through starches and contains small amounts of dietary fibre and protein. There are no known safety concerns or negative health effects associated with consuming maize flour as part of a balanced diet, and no daily intake limit is established by food safety authorities.

What the science says

Because maize flour is a traditional food ingredient rather than an additive, it does not undergo the same chemical safety evaluations as preservatives or artificial colours. Major food authorities view it as a standard dietary component. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration recognise it as a safe source of carbohydrates. Scientific assessments focus primarily on its nutritional profile, confirming that it is easily metabolised by the human digestive system to provide energy.

Where you'll find it

You will find maize flour in a wide variety of supermarket items, including gluten-free breads, tortillas, corn chips, crackers, and breakfast cereals. It is also frequently used as a coating for fried foods to create a crispy texture. On ingredient lists, it is usually declared simply as maize flour or corn flour, and because it is a standard food ingredient rather than a regulated additive, it does not have an E-number.
