Elderberry concentrate

Processed ingredientNeutral

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Elderberry concentrate is a natural fruit extract used to add rich purple colour and a sweet, tart berry flavour to foods and drinks.

What it is

Elderberry concentrate is a thick, dark liquid made by removing water from the juice of ripe elderberries. These small, dark purple berries grow on the elder tree, which is common across Europe and North America. It is a familiar fruit ingredient, much like concentrated blackberry or blackcurrant juice, and is valued for its deep, natural pigmentation and characteristic tart taste. Unlike isolated food dyes, it remains a whole food ingredient that carries the natural characteristics of the fruit itself.

How it's made

The process begins with harvesting ripe elderberries, which are washed and crushed to release their juice. This juice is pressed to separate the liquid from the seeds and skins. To turn the juice into a concentrate, it is gently heated under a vacuum, which allows the water to evaporate at a lower temperature. This careful evaporation preserves the delicate flavours, natural sugars, and vibrant pigments. The resulting thick syrup is then filtered and pasteurised to ensure it stays fresh before being packaged.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose elderberry concentrate primarily as a natural colouring agent and flavour enhancer. It provides a beautiful, deep red to purple hue that helps products look appealing without the need for synthetic dyes. Because it is a fruit concentrate, it also adds a pleasant, fruity sweetness and tartness. It is highly favoured by manufacturers who want to keep their labels clean and simple, as it allows them to colour and flavour their products using a recognisable, plant-based ingredient.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. As a concentrated fruit juice rather than a chemical additive, it is treated as a standard food ingredient. No daily intake limit is established because it is consumed as part of a normal diet. It is a benign ingredient that does not carry the safety concerns often associated with synthetic food colourings, making it a straightforward choice for those looking to avoid artificial additives in their daily shopping.

What the science says

Regulators view elderberry concentrate as a food ingredient rather than an additive, meaning it is subject to general food safety standards rather than specific additive evaluations. The European Commission permits its use freely as a colouring foodstuff under Regulation EC No 1333/2008. The FDA recognises concentrated fruit juices of this type as safe for general consumption under 21 CFR regulations. Because it is a natural fruit product, scientific assessments focus on standard food hygiene and quality control rather than chemical safety limits.

Where you'll find it

You will find elderberry concentrate in a variety of products, including fruit juices, smoothies, yoghurts, jellies, and fruit snacks. It is also a popular ingredient in herbal teas and wellness syrups. On a food label, it is typically listed plainly as elderberry concentrate, elderberry juice concentrate, or concentrated elderberry juice. Because it is a whole food ingredient rather than a food additive, it does not have an E-number.

Sources
  • FDA Regulation 21 CFR Section 73.250, Fruit Juice for Colouring, 2023
  • Regulation EC No 1333/2008 of the European Parliament and of the Council on Food Additives, 2008
