Salmon

Whole foodGood

Salmon is a highly nutritious whole food that Clara rates as GOOD, providing an excellent source of protein and essential fats with no added ingredients.

What it is

Salmon is a popular oily fish known for its rich flavour and distinctive pink-orange flesh. It is a whole food rather than an additive, prized for its high content of protein, vitamin D, and essential omega-3 fatty acids. In the culinary world, it is celebrated for its versatile texture, which remains tender and moist whether it is baked, grilled, poached, or cured. For shoppers looking to eat minimally processed foods, this fish represents a nutrient-dense option in its purest form.

How it's made

This ingredient is not manufactured or chemically synthesised. It is harvested either from wild fisheries in cold northern waters or raised in carefully managed aquaculture farms. Once caught or harvested, the fish is cleaned, scaled, and typically filleted or portioned. It is then chilled or flash-frozen immediately to preserve its freshness and nutritional quality before being packaged for the supermarket shelf, ensuring that no artificial preservatives or extra processing steps are required.

Why manufacturers use it

Food companies use salmon as a premium, protein-rich centrepiece in ready meals, frozen entrees, and canned products. It is chosen because its natural oils help it retain a moist texture and rich flavour even after freezing or canning, where leaner fish might become dry and tasteless. Its appealing natural colour also enhances the visual presentation of packaged meals, making them look more appetising to shoppers without the need for any added colourings or artificial enhancers.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. Because this is a single-ingredient whole food with nothing added, there are no additive-related safety concerns to address. No limit is established for its consumption, and it does not carry any regulatory warnings. It is a natural source of essential nutrients that support general physical wellbeing, making it an excellent choice for a balanced diet. Clara always celebrates whole foods that appear on labels without hidden extra substances.

What the science says

Food safety authorities worldwide have thoroughly evaluated fish consumption. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration have published joint advice highlighting the nutritional benefits of eating oily fish, particularly for its role in supporting normal body functions and development. These evaluations focus on balancing the rich nutrient profile against environmental factors, concluding that regular consumption of fish like salmon is highly beneficial for the general population.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in the fresh fish aisle, the freezer section, and the canned food shelf. On ingredient lists, it is usually declared simply as salmon, salmon fillet, or salmon fillets. On imported products, you might spot its European names such as saumon, salmón, salmone, lachs, or laks. It also appears as the main component in prepared seafood salads, fish cakes, and pre-packaged sushi rolls.
