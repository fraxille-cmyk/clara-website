Rice flour

Processed ingredientNeutral

Rice flour is a simple, gluten-free flour made from ground rice grains, used to thicken sauces, add crunch to bakes, and bind ingredients together.

What it is

Rice flour is a mild-tasting flour made from finely milled rice grains. It is a natural source of carbohydrates and is completely free from gluten, making it a familiar alternative to wheat flour. Depending on whether the outer husk is kept or removed before milling, it can be made from brown rice, which has a slightly nuttier flavour, or white rice, which is neutral and light. It behaves similarly to other grain starches but has its own unique texture when cooked.

How it's made

The journey from paddy field to flour is straightforward. Harvested rice grains are first cleaned to remove any debris. For white rice flour, the outer husk and bran layers are gently milled away. The remaining grain is then ground down using mechanical rollers or stone mills until it reaches a fine, powdery consistency. This powder is sifted to ensure an even particle size before being packaged, with no chemical alterations or synthetic additives needed during the process.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers value rice flour because it is incredibly versatile and does not alter the intended flavour of a recipe. It acts as an excellent thickening agent in soups and gravies, and it keeps moisture from separating in chilled foods. In baking, it creates a delicate, crispy texture in biscuits and crackers. Because it lacks gluten, it is the go-to base for creating gluten-free breads and pastas, offering a reliable structure without relying on complex synthetic binders.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. As a simple, minimally processed food ingredient rather than a chemical additive, it is a benign component of a balanced diet. It is digested by the body just like whole rice, breaking down into glucose to provide energy. For the vast majority of people, it is incredibly gentle on the digestive system, though like any carbohydrate-rich flour, it is best enjoyed as part of a varied diet that includes plenty of fibre and protein.

What the science says

Because rice flour is a traditional food rather than a novel additive, regulatory bodies do not subject it to the same restrictive limits as synthetic chemicals. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration recognise it as a standard food ingredient with no established daily intake limit. Scientific evaluations focus primarily on its nutritional profile as a source of energy, confirming it is easily metabolised by the body and poses no safety concerns for general consumption.

Where you'll find it

You will spot this ingredient on a wide variety of labels, particularly in gluten-free breads, crackers, biscuits, and breakfast cereals. It is also frequently used as a dusting agent to keep dough from sticking to machinery, and as a thickener in ready meals, baby foods, sauces, and canned soups. On packaging, it is simply listed as rice flour, brown rice flour, or white rice flour, with no E-number since it is classified as a food ingredient.
