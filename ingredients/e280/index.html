Propionic acid (E280)

E280AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Propionic acid is a synthetic preservative used to prevent mould in bakery products, evaluated as safe by regulators with no daily limit needed.

What it is

Propionic acid is a naturally occurring organic acid that has a sharp, pungent smell. In nature, it is produced by certain beneficial bacteria during fermentation, which is why it is found in small amounts in Swiss cheese and other fermented foods. In the food industry, it is used as a preservative to keep food fresh. It is chemically similar to acetic acid, which is the main component of vinegar, but it has its own distinct properties that make it highly effective at keeping bakery products free from spoilage.

How it's made

While bacteria produce this acid naturally, the version used in packaged food is manufactured synthetically to ensure purity and consistency. It is typically produced through the chemical reaction of ethylene, carbon monoxide, and water, or by the oxidation of propionaldehyde. This industrial process yields a highly concentrated, pure liquid that can be easily measured and mixed into large batches of dough or batter. The final synthetic ingredient is chemically identical to the acid produced by natural fermentation.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this ingredient primarily to prevent the growth of mould and certain bacteria in baked goods. Bread and cakes are moist and nutrient-rich, making them ideal breeding grounds for spores. By adding a small amount of this acid, producers can significantly extend the shelf life of their products, reducing food waste and ensuring the items remain appealing during transport and storage. It is a highly cost-effective choice because it works at very low concentrations without altering the texture of the bread.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects its status as a synthetic preservative that regulators consider exceptionally safe. EFSA evaluated the substance and concluded that there is no need to establish a numerical acceptable daily intake, meaning it does not pose a safety concern at the levels currently used in food. It is metabolised by the human body in the same way as other common fatty acids, breaking down naturally into harmless components without accumulating in your tissues.

What the science says

The safety of this preservative has been thoroughly assessed by major food safety authorities. EFSA re-evaluated the substance in 2019 and confirmed that it is of no safety concern for the general population. The FDA also affirms its safety, designating it as generally recognised as safe for direct addition to human food. Because the body processes it as a normal nutrient, scientific panels have found no evidence of adverse effects from the amounts typically consumed in a standard diet, and no limit is established for its daily intake.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in pre-packaged sliced bread, tortillas, buns, rolls, and various baked goods that need to stay fresh on supermarket shelves for more than a few days. On European labels, it may be declared by its name or as E280. On US labels, it is listed as propionic acid. It is also sometimes used in processed cheese products and puddings to maintain freshness and prevent spoilage during distribution.
