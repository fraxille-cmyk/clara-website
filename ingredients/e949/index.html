Hydrogen (E949)

E949AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Hydrogen is a harmless packaging gas used to keep food fresh in its packet, and Clara rates it as a neutral, limited-concern additive.

What it is

Hydrogen is the simplest and lightest chemical element in the universe. While we usually think of it as a highly flammable gas or as one of the building blocks of water, it also serves a very quiet, protective role in the food industry. In packaged foods, it is used as a packaging gas, which means it fills the empty space inside a container to protect the food from the surrounding air. It is completely colourless, odourless, and tasteless, so it does not alter the sensory experience of your food.

How it's made

To produce food-grade hydrogen, manufacturers typically use a process called steam reforming, where natural gas is reacted with steam at high temperatures to isolate the hydrogen. Another common method is the electrolysis of water, which uses an electric current to split water molecules into hydrogen and oxygen gases. Once isolated, the gas is highly purified to remove any trace impurities, ensuring it meets the strict safety standards required for direct contact with food before it is compressed into cylinders for packaging facilities.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use hydrogen to create a modified atmosphere inside food packaging. When food is exposed to oxygen, it can oxidise, which leads to stale flavours, loss of nutrients, and rapid spoilage. By flushing the packaging with hydrogen, manufacturers displace the oxygen, which prevents oxidation and keeps the food fresh for much longer. It is a highly efficient and cost-effective choice for preserving the quality of delicate products without needing to add chemical preservatives directly to the food itself.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Because it is a gas that quickly dissipates into the air as soon as you open the packet, you do not actually consume any meaningful amount of it. EFSA has evaluated its safety and determined that no acceptable daily intake limit needs to be specified, as it poses no toxicological concern. The FDA also affirms its safety, meaning it is widely recognised as safe for use in food packaging by major global food authorities.

What the science says

Scientific assessments of hydrogen as a food additive show that it is biologically inert when used in packaging. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the gas and confirmed that its use does not raise any safety concerns for consumers. Because it does not react with the food and escapes immediately upon opening, there is no accumulation in the body and no risk of exposure. No international regulator has established an upper intake limit, as the substance is considered entirely benign in this application.

Where you'll find it

You will find hydrogen used in various sealed food products that require a protective atmosphere to stay fresh, such as crisps, snacks, pre-prepared salads, and processed meats. On a food label, it is typically listed by its name or as E949, and it may be accompanied by a statement indicating that the product was packaged in a protective atmosphere. On US labels, it is listed simply as hydrogen, where it is permitted for direct use under federal regulations.
