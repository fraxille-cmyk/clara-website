Hydrogen (E949)

E949AdditiveNeutral · benign

Hydrogen is a harmless packaging gas used to push oxygen out of food packets, keeping the contents fresh without any known health risks.

What it is

Hydrogen is the simplest and lightest chemical element in the universe. In its everyday form, it is a colourless, odourless, and tasteless gas. While we often think of it as a clean fuel or a basic building block of water, it also serves a very specific and quiet role in the food industry as a packaging gas. It is not an ingredient you eat directly, but rather an invisible helper that sits in the empty space inside a sealed packet to protect the food from the air.

How it's made

To make food-grade hydrogen, manufacturers typically split water molecules using electricity, a process known as electrolysis, or they extract it from natural gas through a thermal process called steam reforming. Once the gas is isolated, it undergoes intensive purification and filtration to remove any trace moisture, carbon compounds, or other gases. This ensures that only pure, food-grade hydrogen gas is compressed into cylinders, ready to be introduced into food packaging systems.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use hydrogen to create a protective atmosphere inside sealed containers. When food is exposed to oxygen, it begins to oxidise, which makes fats go rancid, destroys delicate vitamins, and causes colours to fade. By flushing the packaging with hydrogen, manufacturers displace the oxygen and seal the freshness inside. Hydrogen is highly effective because it is completely inert under normal storage conditions, meaning it will not react with the food or alter its natural flavour, texture, or aroma.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is a gas that dissipates into the air as soon as you open the packet, you do not actually consume it, and it poses no risk to your body. EFSA evaluated the gas and concluded that there is no safety concern, meaning no acceptable daily intake limit is established. It is considered entirely safe for its intended packaging use, and there are no reports of adverse reactions or sensitivities associated with it.

What the science says

Scientific panels have thoroughly assessed hydrogen and found no evidence of accumulation or activity in the human body. When we open a packet, the tiny amount of gas simply mixes with the surrounding atmosphere. Both European and American food safety authorities have reviewed its use in detail. The FDA affirms it as generally recognised as safe, and the EFSA re-evaluation confirmed that its use as a packaging gas does not raise any toxicological concerns, as it does not remain in the food.

Where you'll find it

You will find this gas used in the sealed packaging of delicate, oxidation-sensitive products like crisps, nuts, dried fruits, and prepared salads. Because it is a processing aid that escapes into the room when the container is opened, it is often not listed on the ingredient panel at all. When it is declared, it appears under its official name as hydrogen, or by its European code, E949, and is often accompanied by a note on the packet stating that the product was packaged in a protective atmosphere.
