Thermally oxidised soya bean oil interacted with mono- and diglycerides (E479b)

E479bAdditiveNeutral · limited concern

A synthetic emulsifier made from soya bean oil and fats, used to stop frying margarine from splattering, with a moderate daily limit.

What it is

This ingredient is a complex, chemically modified food additive. It is a mixture of refined soya bean oil that has been heated and exposed to oxygen, which is then reacted with mono- and diglycerides, which are common fats. The result is a specialised substance that helps oil and water stay blended under intense heat. While it originates from familiar plant oils and dietary fats, the final product is a highly processed, synthetic compound that does not occur in nature.

How it's made

The production process involves two distinct chemical stages. First, refined soya bean oil is heated to high temperatures, up to two hundred degrees Celsius, while air is blown through it to oxidise the oil. This thermal oxidation alters the structure of the fatty acids. Second, this oxidised oil is reacted under vacuum conditions with mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids. The mixture is then cooled and purified to create a consistent, functional additive ready for food formulation.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this additive primarily as an emulsifier in high-heat applications. Its main technical job is to prevent splattering and spitting when frying fats are heated in a pan. It achieves this by stabilising the tiny water droplets suspended within the fat, ensuring they evaporate smoothly rather than bursting violently. It is highly valued because it remains stable and effective at frying temperatures where simpler, natural emulsifiers would break down and fail.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects its synthetic origin and the fact that regulators have set a specific limit on how much you should consume. EFSA keeps its daily limit of 30 mg per kg of body weight. Because it is highly processed, it is not treated as a simple food, but evaluations show it is metabolised safely by the body when consumed within this established limit. There is no toxicological concern at normal dietary exposure levels.

What the science says

This additive has been thoroughly evaluated by European food safety authorities to ensure its safety in the food supply. EFSA published a full re-evaluation of the substance, confirming that the compound is broken down in the digestive tract into ordinary fat components that the body uses for energy. The assessment concluded that the current dietary exposure does not exceed the acceptable daily intake, and no safety concerns or data gaps were identified during the review.

Where you'll find it

You will almost exclusively find this ingredient in fat emulsions used for frying, such as liquid margarines, baking fats, and specialised frying oils. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically declared by its full technical name, thermally oxidised soya bean oil interacted with mono- and diglycerides, or simply by its European code, E479b.
