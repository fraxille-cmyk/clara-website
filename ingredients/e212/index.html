Potassium benzoate (E212)

E212AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern preservative used to stop mould and yeast growing, which can exceed daily limits for children.

What it is

Potassium benzoate is a synthetic preservative that stops micro-organisms from spoiling food. Chemically, it is the potassium salt of benzoic acid, a compound found naturally in some berries, plums, and spices. While the natural version exists in tiny amounts in fruit, the ingredient added to packaged food is manufactured in a laboratory to ensure a highly concentrated and consistent powder that dissolves easily in liquids.

How it's made

This ingredient is produced through a chemical reaction. Manufacturers start with benzoic acid, which is typically synthesised from toluene, a petroleum derivative. This acid is then neutralised with potassium hydroxide. The resulting chemical reaction produces potassium benzoate, which is crystallised, dried, and ground into a fine white powder ready for industrial food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use this additive primarily to extend the shelf life of acidic products. It is highly effective at preventing the growth of yeasts, moulds, and certain bacteria that thrive in low-pH environments. It is often chosen over sodium benzoate when manufacturers want to reduce the sodium content of their products while maintaining the same strong preservation qualities, helping drinks and sauces stay fresh on supermarket shelves for months.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. While the human body can process and excrete small amounts of this substance, EFSA established a daily limit of 6 mg per kg of body weight in 2016. The regulator found that toddlers and children who consume high amounts of foods containing this additive can easily exceed this limit. Because of this exposure risk for young children, Clara advises avoiding it where possible.

What the science says

Regulators have evaluated this preservative to understand how it behaves in the body. Once consumed, it is absorbed in the gut, metabolised in the liver, and excreted in urine. EFSA re-evaluated the substance in 2016 and lowered the temporary group acceptable daily intake. The main concern highlighted by scientific panels is the cumulative exposure from multiple dietary sources, which can push the daily intake of younger, lighter individuals above the recommended threshold.

Where you'll find it

You will commonly find this preservative in liquid products that need to last a long time without fermenting. It is a frequent ingredient in diet soft drinks, fruit juices, squashes, salad dressings, and condiments. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed as potassium benzoate or by its European code, E212, and on US labels it is often declared as potassium benzoate.
