Coconut water from concentrate

Processed ingredientNeutral

Diets

✕ Not low-FODMAP
  • Not low-FODMAP — coconut water from concentrate is high in excess fructose.

Coconut water that has been evaporated for easier transport and then rehydrated before bottling, offering a natural source of hydrating minerals.

What it is

Coconut water from concentrate is the clear liquid found inside young green coconuts that has undergone a two-step process. First, the natural water is evaporated to create a thick, space-saving syrup for shipping. Later, clean water is added back to restore the liquid to its original strength. It is chemically identical to fresh coconut water, containing natural sugars, vitamins, and minerals, but it has travelled a longer journey before reaching the carton. It is a familiar way to package juices and plant waters to keep transport costs down.

How it's made

The process begins in tropical regions where green coconuts are harvested and cracked open to collect their liquid. This liquid is filtered and then heated under a vacuum, which allows the water to evaporate at a lower temperature to protect the delicate flavour. The resulting concentrated syrup is pasteurised and frozen or chilled for bulk transport. Once it arrives at its packaging destination, clean water is added back to the concentrate in the exact proportion that was removed, and the liquid is pasteurised once more before bottling.

Why manufacturers use it

Shipping fresh coconut water across oceans is incredibly expensive and carbon-heavy because water is heavy and bulky. By evaporating the water at the source, manufacturers can ship a fraction of the weight and volume, making the process much more economical and environmentally friendly. This method also helps standardise the taste and sweetness of the final drink, which can vary naturally from harvest to harvest, ensuring that every carton tastes consistent to the shopper. It also extends the shelf life of the raw ingredient before final packaging.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. This is a simple food ingredient rather than a chemical additive, so no daily intake limits are established. The process of concentration and rehydration does not introduce any risky substances, though the heat used during pasteurisation and evaporation can slightly reduce the levels of heat-sensitive vitamins compared to fresh, raw coconut water. It remains a natural source of potassium and other minerals that help the body maintain its fluid balance, making it a benign choice for daily hydration.

What the science says

Food safety authorities view coconut water from concentrate as a standard food ingredient rather than an additive. The European Commission regulates fruit juices and similar concentrated products to ensure that the amount of water added back matches the original composition of the juice. Because it is classified as a food, it has not been subjected to the same additive risk assessments as preservatives or colourings. The main scientific consensus is that it provides a rich source of electrolytes, particularly potassium, which supports normal muscle function and hydration.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in the drinks aisle, packaged in cartons, cans, or bottles as a standalone hydrating beverage. It is also frequently used as a natural, sweet base for fruit smoothies, protein shakes, and sports drinks. On the packaging, it is typically listed simply as coconut water from concentrate, and because it is a whole food ingredient rather than a food additive, it does not carry an E-number or require a specific additive functional class on the label.
