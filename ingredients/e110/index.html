Sunset yellow FCF (E110)

E110AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Sunset yellow FCF is a synthetic orange-yellow food dye that carries a mandatory European warning regarding its potential impact on attention in children.

What it is

Sunset yellow FCF is a synthetic dye used to give foods a bright, appealing orange-yellow hue. It is an azo dye, which means it belongs to a family of chemical compounds known for their intense and stable colouring properties. Unlike natural pigments derived from plants or spices, this substance is entirely man-made and does not occur in nature. It is highly soluble in water, making it incredibly easy to blend into a wide variety of liquid and solid foods.

How it's made

This colour is manufactured through a chemical synthesis process. It starts with petroleum-derived hydrocarbons, which undergo a reaction called diazotisation. During this process, aromatic chemical compounds are coupled together to form the characteristic azo bond that gives the dye its vibrant orange shade. The resulting mixture is then purified, crystallised, and dried into a fine powder or granules, ensuring that no unwanted chemical by-products remain in the final food-grade additive.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose this synthetic dye because it is highly stable, cheap, and incredibly effective. Natural orange colours, like those from carrots or annatto, can fade quickly when exposed to light, heat, or changes in acidity during storage. This synthetic alternative maintains its bright shade over a long shelf life. It allows companies to standardise the appearance of their products cheaply, ensuring that every batch looks exactly the same to the shopper.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. This rating is due to a mandatory European Union warning that foods containing this dye must carry, stating that it may have an adverse effect on activity and attention in children. While it is permitted for use, this warning highlights why many shoppers prefer to keep it out of their baskets. IARC classifies sunset yellow FCF as Group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans.

What the science says

Regulators have evaluated this colour extensively over the years. EFSA established an acceptable daily intake of 4 mg per kg of body weight in 2014, concluding that exposure below this limit is of no toxicological concern. However, concerns regarding behavioural changes and attention levels in children prompted European authorities to mandate precautionary labelling. While some international bodies consider the evidence on behaviour to be inconclusive, the warning remains in place across Europe to help parents make informed choices.

Where you'll find it

You will find this colour in highly processed foods, including soft drinks, sweets, ice creams, packet soups, and decorated baked goods. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically declared as sunset yellow FCF or by its E-number, E110. On US labels, it is listed as FD&C Yellow No. 6. It is often blended with other synthetic dyes to create custom shades of brown, green, or deep red.
