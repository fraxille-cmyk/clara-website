Niacinamide

FortificantNeutral · benign

Niacinamide is a form of vitamin B3 added to foods to boost their nutritional value, and it is entirely harmless at the levels found on food labels.

What it is

Niacinamide, also known as nicotinamide, is a water-soluble form of vitamin B3. It is an essential nutrient that our bodies need to convert food into energy and to keep our nervous system and skin working properly. Unlike some other forms of vitamin B3, it does not cause the skin to flush or redden when you consume it. It is a natural substance that we normally get from eating meat, fish, nuts, and grains, but it is also produced in a pure form to be added directly to packaged foods.

How it's made

To make this ingredient on a commercial scale, manufacturers start with a compound called 3-methylpyridine, which is sourced from chemical starting materials. This compound undergoes a chemical reaction with oxygen and ammonia to create nicotinonitrile. Through a final step of controlled chemical hydrolysis, where water is used to break down the compound, it is converted into pure, white, crystalline niacinamide powder that is easily dissolved in liquids and mixed into food formulations.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this ingredient primarily as a fortificant to restore vitamins lost during heavy processing or to enrich foods that are naturally low in nutrients. It is highly favoured over other forms of vitamin B3 because it is incredibly stable. It does not break down when exposed to heat, light, or air during manufacturing and storage, and it does not alter the colour, flavour, or texture of the final food product, making it a seamless addition to many recipes.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. It is a vital nutrient that is harmless at the levels typically added to food products. While consuming extremely large quantities of vitamin B3 can sometimes lead to temporary liver strain, the small amounts used to fortify everyday foods are well within what is considered safe for daily consumption. No limit is established for its use as a fortificant in general food products, as the levels are self-limiting due to the nature of food enrichment.

What the science says

The safety of this nutrient has been thoroughly evaluated by major food safety authorities. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the addition of vitamins to food and confirmed that niacinamide is a highly bioavailable source of vitamin B3 that the body can easily metabolise and use. The US Food and Drug Administration recognises it as generally recognised as safe for use as a nutrient supplement in foods, with no safety concerns when used in accordance with good manufacturing practice.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in breakfast cereals, energy bars, fortified flour, infant formulas, and sports drinks. On the ingredient list, it might be declared under its common name niacinamide, or as niacin, nicotinamida, nicotinamide, nicotinammide, or nikotinamid. Because it is a nutrient rather than a technological food additive, it does not have an E-number assigned to it.
