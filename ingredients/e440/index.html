Pectins (E440)

E440AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Pectin is a natural gelling fibre extracted from fruit peels, used to thicken jams and yoghurts, with an excellent safety record.

What it is

Pectins are natural complex carbohydrates found in the cell walls of most land plants. They act like a cellular glue, giving structure and strength to plant tissues. You are already familiar with them if you have ever made jam at home, as they are the very substance that makes fruit juices set. In the kitchen and the food industry alike, they are valued for their remarkable ability to turn liquids into smooth, stable gels when combined with sugar and a little acid.

How it's made

Commercial pectin is typically extracted from citrus peels, such as lemons, limes, and oranges, or from apple pomace, which is the solid residue left after pressing apples for juice. These raw materials are heated in a mild acid bath to release the soluble fibres from the cell walls. The resulting liquid is filtered, concentrated, and then mixed with alcohol to make the pectin precipitate out as a solid. This solid is washed, dried, and ground into a fine, uniform powder.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this ingredient to create a desirable texture in a wide variety of spreads, dairy products, and baked goods. It is the premier choice for gelling fruit spreads because it mimics the natural setting process of fruit. It beats synthetic alternatives because it is highly stable under heat, releases flavour beautifully without masking it, and carries a very clean label that shoppers recognise and trust. It also helps keep fruit pieces evenly suspended in yoghurts and fillings.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. This ingredient is a soluble dietary fibre that passes through our upper digestive system largely unchanged before being broken down by our natural gut bacteria. It has a long history of consumption and is considered entirely non-toxic. Because it is a normal part of a balanced diet rich in fruits and vegetables, regulators have not found any reason to set a daily intake limit, meaning it can be used generously to achieve the desired texture.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have evaluated these plant fibres thoroughly and consistently confirm their safety. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated them and concluded that there is no safety concern at the levels currently used in food, meaning no numerical daily limit is established. Joint FAO/WHO expert committees have also reviewed the evidence and confirmed that these fibres are processed by the body just like any other dietary fibre, posing no risk to human health.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in jams, jellies, marmalades, fruit preparations for yoghurts, gummy sweets, and bakery fillings. On the label, it might be listed simply as pectin, pectins, pectine, pectina, pektin, or by its European code, E440. It is a common sight in both organic and conventional products, valued for its ability to deliver a perfect spreadable texture without the need for synthetic chemicals.
