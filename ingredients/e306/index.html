Tocopherol-rich extract (E306)

E306AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

A natural plant extract rich in vitamin E that acts as an antioxidant to keep fats and oils in your food from spoiling.

What it is

Tocopherol-rich extract is a natural substance packed with different forms of vitamin E. In nature, these compounds are found in vegetable oils, nuts, and green leafy vegetables, where they protect the plant from environmental stress. When used in packaged food, this extract acts as a powerful antioxidant, shielding delicate ingredients from the damaging effects of oxygen. It is a fat-soluble substance, meaning it blends beautifully with oils and fats to keep them stable and fresh without changing the texture of the food.

How it's made

This extract is obtained directly from natural plant sources, most commonly from edible vegetable oils like soy, sunflower, or rapeseed oil. The crude oil undergoes a physical separation process called vacuum distillation, which isolates the tocopherols from the fatty acids. Because it is a physical extraction rather than a chemical synthesis, the molecules remain in their natural, highly active forms. The resulting concentrated liquid is then standardised to ensure a consistent level of vitamin E activity before it is added to food products.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this extract to stop fats and oils from turning rancid. When oxygen reacts with fats, it creates unpleasant off-flavours and stale smells, which ruins the product. Tocopherol-rich extract is highly valued because it is incredibly effective at low concentrations and remains stable during high-temperature processing like baking or frying. It offers a clean-label alternative to synthetic chemical antioxidants, allowing brands to maintain a long shelf life while keeping their ingredient lists appealing to shoppers who prefer plant-derived options.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Because it is a natural form of vitamin E, your body recognises and processes it easily. EFSA has established a numeric acceptable daily intake of 2 mg per kg of body weight for this extract. This limit is in place to ensure that total intake from all food sources remains within a balanced range, as extremely high doses of any concentrated vitamin can alter how the body processes other nutrients. There are no safety concerns or adverse effects associated with the normal amounts found in everyday groceries.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated tocopherols and recognise their role as essential nutrients. EFSA re-evaluated the extract as a food additive and confirmed its safety, noting that it is absorbed and metabolised in the same way as natural dietary vitamin E. The FDA also recognises tocopherols as generally safe for use as preservatives. Because it is a concentrated source, regulators established the daily limit to guide manufacturers, but normal dietary exposure remains well within these guidelines, and no toxicological concerns have been identified in any major safety reviews.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in fat-rich foods like vegetable oils, margarine, salad dressings, breakfast cereals, and processed meats. It is also common in baby foods and infant formulae where fat stability is vital. On a label, it is usually listed as tocopherol-rich extract, tocopherol-rich tocopherols, or by its European code, E306. On US labels, you might see it declared as tocopherols used as a chemical preservative or simply listed as natural vitamin E to highlight its plant origin.
