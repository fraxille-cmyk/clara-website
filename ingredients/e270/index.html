Lactic acid (E270)

E270AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Lactic acid is a natural organic acid used to control acidity and keep food fresh, which Clara rates as a neutral, limited-concern additive.

What it is

Lactic acid is a naturally occurring organic acid that you might already know from fermented foods like yoghurt, sourdough bread, and kimchi. It is the very substance that gives these foods their characteristic tangy, pleasant sourness. In its pure form, it is a clear, syrupy liquid or a white powder that dissolves easily in water. Our own bodies produce it during exercise, and it is found widely in nature wherever beneficial bacteria ferment sugars. On food labels, it serves as a reliable way to manage acidity.

How it's made

While lactic acid can be synthesised chemically, almost all of the lactic acid used in the food industry today is produced through fermentation. Manufacturers feed simple sugars, usually derived from corn starch, sugar beets, or sugarcane, to specialised strains of lactic acid bacteria. These bacteria consume the sugars and convert them into lactic acid. The resulting broth is then neutralised, filtered, purified to remove any bacterial cells or remaining sugars, and concentrated into the final liquid or powder used in food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on lactic acid primarily to control the acidity of their products, which helps to keep spoilage organisms from growing and extends shelf life. It has a milder, more pleasant sour taste than citric acid, making it excellent for rounding out flavours in delicate foods. It also helps to regulate pH levels during processing, which is crucial for the texture of cheeses and the stability of emulsified sauces. Because it is highly soluble and stable under heat, it is much easier to work with than many alternative acids.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Lactic acid is a normal part of human metabolism and is processed easily by the body. No upper limit is established for its daily intake because it is considered highly safe for the general population. The only minor caution is for very young infants, whose immature digestive systems may struggle to metabolise large amounts of certain forms of the acid, though the levels found in standard retail foods are generally of no toxicological concern.

What the science says

Regulators worldwide have thoroughly evaluated lactic acid and confirmed its safety. JECFA has reviewed its use and concluded that it does not require a limited acceptable daily intake, meaning it can be used safely in accordance with good manufacturing practices. EFSA has also re-evaluated lactic acid as a food additive, confirming that it poses no safety concerns for the general public. Because it is a natural metabolite that our cells handle daily, scientific consensus views it as one of the most benign preservatives available.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of supermarket items, including yoghurts, cheeses, salad dressings, pickled vegetables, olives, and bakery products. On packaged goods, it is typically listed by its plain name as lactic acid or under its European code, E270. It is also frequently found in ready meals and soft drinks where a gentle, tangy flavour profile and stable acidity are needed to keep the product fresh and appealing on the shelf.
