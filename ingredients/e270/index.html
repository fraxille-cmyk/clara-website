Lactic acid (E270)

E270AdditiveNeutral · benign

Lactic acid is a natural organic acid produced by fermentation that regulates acidity and has a long history of safe use as a preservative.

What it is

Lactic acid is a natural organic acid that you might already know from sour milk, yoghurt, or even the temporary ache in your muscles after a hard run. It is a simple compound that plays a fundamental role in biology, helping to give fermented foods their characteristic tangy bite. In the kitchen and the food industry, it serves as a reliable way to manage acidity and keep food fresh for longer.

How it's made

To make lactic acid on a commercial scale, manufacturers turn to microscopic helpers. They ferment simple sugars, usually sourced from corn starch, sugar beets, or cane sugar, using specialised lactic acid bacteria. This is the very same biological process that turns milk into cheese. Once the bacteria have finished fermenting the sugars, the resulting liquid is filtered, purified, and concentrated into a clear liquid or a white powder ready for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers value lactic acid because it is an exceptionally versatile tool for controlling acidity. By lowering the pH of a food product, it prevents the growth of unwanted bacteria and moulds, which extends shelf life naturally. It also acts as a pleasant flavouring agent, delivering a smooth, mild sourness that is less sharp than citric acid. This makes it ideal for balancing flavours in delicate sauces, dressings, and confectionery.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is a normal product of human metabolism and is easily processed by the body, it does not pose health concerns for the general population. No limit is established for its daily intake because regulators find it to be exceptionally well tolerated. EFSA re-evaluated the substance and confirmed that it presents no safety concerns when used in food.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly reviewed lactic acid and its salts. EFSA evaluated the additive in 2017 and concluded that there was no need to set a numerical acceptable daily intake, as it is a natural substance that the body metabolises efficiently. The FDA also recognises it as generally safe for use in food. There are no open concerns regarding its safety, and it has a long history of uneventful use in a vast range of dietary products.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide array of everyday items, including sourdough breads, yoghurts, salad dressings, olives, sweets, and baby foods. On food packaging, it is typically listed by its plain name as lactic acid, or by its European code, E270. On US labels, it is listed as lactic acid, where it is permitted under federal regulations as a direct food substance.
