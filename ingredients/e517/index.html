Ammonium sulphate (E517)

E517AdditiveNeutral · benign

Ammonium sulphate is a synthetic salt used as a yeast food in baking; Clara rates it neutral because regulators find no safety concerns.

What it is

Ammonium sulphate is an inorganic salt that consists of nitrogen and sulphur, two elements that are essential to life. In the kitchen, it behaves much like ordinary table salt but with a slightly bitter, salty taste. While it occurs naturally in rare volcanic minerals, the version you find in food is always made in a laboratory. It is closely related to other mineral salts used to nourish yeast and regulate acidity in everyday baking.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers combine ammonia with sulphuric acid. Ammonia is typically produced by combining nitrogen from the air with hydrogen gas, while sulphuric acid is made from elemental sulphur. When these two strong chemicals react, they neutralise each other and form a stable, white crystalline salt. This salt is then purified, filtered, and dried into a fine powder that is ready for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Bakers rely on this salt because it acts as an excellent yeast nutrient. Yeast needs nitrogen to grow and multiply, and this ingredient provides an easily digestible source of it, which helps bread dough rise more quickly and reliably. It also serves as a dough strengthener and a flour treating agent, helping to create a uniform texture in commercial loaves. Because it is highly stable and inexpensive, it is a preferred choice for large-scale bakeries.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. It is processed by the body into ammonium and sulphate ions, both of which are normal parts of our daily metabolism. The Scientific Committee on Food has evaluated its safety and decided that no acceptable daily intake limit is required, meaning it is considered safe at the levels typically used in food. There are no known health concerns or accumulation risks associated with eating foods containing this mineral salt.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly assessed this substance and found no toxicological concerns. The Scientific Committee on Food established that no limit is specified for its daily intake in its 1990 evaluation. In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration recognises it as generally recognised as safe for use in baking. Because it breaks down into simple, familiar nutrients that the body uses and excretes easily, researchers have not identified any adverse effects from its dietary use.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in bakery products, including sliced breads, rolls, buns, and pre-made doughs. It is also used in some brewing processes to feed the yeast. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its plain name as ammonium sulphate, though on European labels it may sometimes be identified by its E-number, E517, and on US labels it is often listed as ammonium sulfate.
