Magnesium chloride (E511)

E511AdditiveNeutral · benign

A natural mineral salt used to firm tofu and preserve texture, considered safe with no established daily limit.

What it is

Magnesium chloride is a naturally occurring mineral salt made of magnesium and chlorine. In nature, it is found abundantly in seawater, salt lakes, and ancient underground mineral deposits. You might already be familiar with it as a traditional coagulant used to turn soy milk into firm tofu, or as a mineral supplement. It dissolves easily in water and has a clean, salty, and slightly bitter taste.

How it's made

To make the food-grade ingredient, manufacturers typically harvest natural brine from deep underground wells or solar salt pans filled with seawater. This brine undergoes a series of purification steps to remove other minerals and impurities. The liquid is then concentrated and crystallised into white flakes or powder. Because it is a simple mineral extraction, the substance is not chemically modified during this production process.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers rely on this mineral primarily as a firming agent and stabilizer. In tofu production, it binds soy proteins together to create a smooth, firm texture. In canned vegetables and sliced fruits, it helps the plant cells hold their structure so the food does not turn mushy during processing and storage. It also serves as a source of dietary magnesium in fortified foods and can help reduce the amount of sodium needed in a recipe.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. This mineral has an excellent safety record, and EFSA has established no upper limit on daily intake because it is metabolised just like the magnesium we get from our regular diet. The body easily processes and excretes any excess. There are no known adverse effects from the amounts typically used in food, making it a very low-risk ingredient for the general population.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this mineral and found no safety concerns. EFSA re-evaluated magnesium salts as food additives and concluded that they do not pose a safety risk at current exposure levels. The FDA also recognises it as a substance that is generally recognised as safe. Because it is a basic nutrient that our bodies require daily to function, scientific consensus focuses on its nutritional benefits rather than any dietary risks.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in tofu, canned vegetables, bottled drinking water, and sports drinks. On ingredient lists, it is usually declared plainly as magnesium chloride, but you might also see it listed by its European code, E511. In traditional Asian foods, it is sometimes referred to as nigari, which is the Japanese term for the natural mineral concentrate.
