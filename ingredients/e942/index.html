Nitrous oxide (E942)

E942AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Nitrous oxide is a synthetic propellant gas used to dispense whipped cream and other aerosols, rated neutral by Clara because it leaves no residue.

What it is

Nitrous oxide is a colourless gas with a slightly sweet odour and taste, often known in other contexts as laughing gas. In the food world, it serves as an efficient propellant and packaging gas. It dissolves exceptionally well in fats under pressure, which makes it the perfect partner for dairy products. When the pressure is released, the gas expands rapidly to create a light, airy foam. It is a synthetic substance that does not occur naturally in the foods we package with it.

How it's made

To manufacture this gas for food use, producers carefully heat ammonium nitrate, a common mineral compound, to a temperature where it decomposes into nitrous oxide and water vapour. The resulting gas is then cooled, thoroughly purified to remove any unwanted chemical byproducts, and compressed into a liquid state. This purified liquid is filled into pressurised metal canisters or chargers, ready to be used in commercial food production or in hand-held kitchen dispensers.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this gas because it is incredibly effective at whipping cream instantly without requiring manual beating. When whipped cream is dispensed from a can, the dissolved gas expands and whips the cream as it exits the nozzle, creating a stable foam with an even texture. It also displaces oxygen inside the packaging, which prevents the fats in the cream from oxidising and turning rancid, thereby extending the shelf life of the product without changing its delicate flavour.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Because it is a gas that escapes into the air almost immediately when a product is dispensed, you consume virtually none of it when you eat the food. EFSA evaluated the gas and concluded that its use as a food additive is of no safety concern, meaning no numerical limit is established for daily intake. It does not linger in the body or interact with your digestive system, making it a benign presence in packaged foods.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this propellant and found no reason for concern in dietary exposures. EFSA re-evaluated the substance and confirmed its safety, noting that it is rapidly eliminated from the body through exhalation when inhaled in tiny amounts during consumption. The FDA also recognises it as generally safe for its intended technical uses. Because it is an inert gas that does not accumulate in human tissues, scientists have not found any adverse dietary effects.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in aerosol cans of whipped cream, dessert toppings, and sprayable oil emulsions. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its functional name as nitrous oxide or by its European code, E 942. It is also used in specialised coffee dispensers to create a creamy head on cold-brew coffee. On US labels it is listed as nitrous oxide, where it is permitted under federal regulations as a propellant and aerating agent for various dairy and fat-based products.
