Polyglycitol syrup (E964)

E964SweetenerNeutral · limited concern

Polyglycitol syrup is a sugar-free sweetener and texturiser made from starch, rated neutral by Clara as it has no established daily intake limit.

What it is

Polyglycitol syrup is a sugar-free sweetener and texturiser that belongs to the polyol family, which includes other sugar alcohols like maltitol and sorbitol. It is a clear, odourless, and highly viscous liquid that is less sweet than table sugar. Because it does not behave like regular sugar in the body, it provides fewer calories per gram and does not cause the rapid rise in blood glucose levels that ordinary sugars do, making it a common choice for sugar-free formulations.

How it's made

The production of polyglycitol syrup begins with natural starches, typically obtained from corn, wheat, or potato. These starches are broken down using enzymes or acids to create a mixture of glucose and maltose syrups. This mixture then undergoes a chemical modification process called catalytic hydrogenation, where hydrogen gas is introduced under high pressure. This reaction transforms the reactive sugars into stable sugar alcohols, resulting in a syrup that is purified, filtered, and concentrated to the desired thickness.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers value polyglycitol syrup because it does much more than just sweeten. It acts as a bulk sweetener, bodying agent, and humectant, which means it helps retain moisture and prevents foods from drying out. It provides a rich, smooth mouthfeel and texture in sugar-free products that would otherwise feel thin or watery. Additionally, it is highly stable under heat and does not caramelise or brown during baking, making it an ideal structural replacement for sugar in processed foods.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. It is generally well tolerated by the body, although like all sugar alcohols, consuming very large quantities can have a mild laxative effect because it is only partially digested in the small intestine. EFSA has evaluated its safety and established no specified acceptable daily intake limit, meaning it can be consumed as part of a normal diet without safety concerns. It does not promote the acid production that damages tooth enamel.

What the science says

Scientific assessments show that polyglycitol syrup is processed by the body in a similar way to other hydrogenated starch hydrolysates. Because it is only partially absorbed, it has a low glycaemic response, which has been confirmed by multiple food safety authorities. EFSA evaluated the additive in 2009 and concluded that its use in foods does not raise safety concerns. In the United States, the FDA reviewed its safety under the GRAS notification program in 2004 and permitted its use as a sweetener and texturiser.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of sugar-free and calorie-reduced products, particularly hard candies, chewing gum, throat lozenges, baked goods, and frozen desserts. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is listed as polyglycitol syrup or by its European code, E964. On US labels, it is typically declared as polyglycitol syrup or hydrogenated starch hydrolysate, where it helps maintain the structure and chewiness of confectionery.
