Pecorino Romano PDO cheese

Processed ingredientGood

Pecorino Romano PDO is a traditional, hard Italian cheese made from sheep milk that provides a rich source of protein and calcium.

What it is

Pecorino Romano PDO is a traditional, hard, salty Italian cheese made entirely from sheep milk. The letters PDO stand for Protected Designation of Origin, which means this cheese can only be produced in specific regions of Italy, primarily Sardinia, Lazio, and the province of Grosseto, using traditional methods. It has a crumbly texture and a sharp, robust flavour that has been celebrated since the days of the Roman Empire, when it was a staple in the rations of marching soldiers.

How it's made

The journey of this cheese begins with raw or pasteurised whole sheep milk from local herds. This milk is heated and curdled using natural lamb rennet. Once the curds form, they are broken down into tiny grains, pressed into large cylindrical moulds, and drained of excess whey. The cheese rounds are then salted repeatedly by hand over several months. Finally, the wheels are left to age in temperature-controlled cellars for at least five months for table cheese, or eight months for grating cheese.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers and chefs use this cheese to deliver an intense, savoury depth of flavour that ordinary cheeses cannot match. Its high concentration of natural glutamates provides a powerful umami punch, which enhances other ingredients in a recipe. Because it is aged and dry, it grates beautifully and melts smoothly into sauces without clumping. It also acts as a natural seasoning, reducing the need for added table salt in processed meals while providing a desirable, authentic Italian character.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As a traditional whole food rather than an additive, it is a nutritious ingredient that provides a rich source of high-quality protein and easily absorbed calcium. Because it is aged for many months, most of the lactose is naturally broken down during fermentation, making it much easier to digest for people who are sensitive to dairy. No daily intake limit is established for this traditional food, though its natural saltiness means it is best enjoyed in moderation as part of a balanced diet.

What the science says

Regulators view traditional cheeses as standard agricultural foods rather than food additives, meaning they do not require E-numbers or complex safety evaluations. The European Commission regulates its production strictly under the Protected Designation of Origin framework to guarantee its authenticity and traditional processing. Scientific assessments of aged sheep cheeses show they are rich in beneficial fatty acids and essential minerals. Because it is a traditional food, no regulator has established any safety concerns or set an upper limit on its consumption.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in premium Italian ready meals, pesto sauces, pasta bakes, and grated cheese blends. On a food label, it is typically listed clearly as Pecorino Romano PDO, Pecorino Romano cheese, or simply Pecorino Romano. Because it is a whole food and not a food additive, it does not have an E-number, and manufacturers are proud to list its full name to highlight the quality of their ingredients.
