Calcium guanylate (E629)

E629AdditiveNeutral · benign

Calcium guanylate is a savoury flavour enhancer derived from fermentation, rated neutral by Clara because regulators find no safety concerns.

What it is

Calcium guanylate is a calcium salt of guanylic acid, which is a natural compound found in yeast, mushrooms, and many plants. It belongs to a family of ingredients known as nucleotides, which are the building blocks of genetic material in all living things. In food, these compounds are responsible for creating a deep, savoury taste. It is closely related to monosodium glutamate but works in a slightly different way to make savoury foods taste richer and more satisfying.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers start with a natural fermentation process. Microorganisms, usually specific strains of bacteria or yeast, are grown in a nutrient-rich broth where they produce guanylic acid. Once the fermentation is complete, the mixture is filtered and purified. The guanylic acid is then neutralised with calcium hydroxide or calcium carbonate, which causes calcium guanylate to crystallise out of the liquid. These crystals are washed, dried, and ground into a fine white powder ready for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use calcium guanylate because it is an incredibly efficient flavour enhancer, especially when paired with glutamates. On its own, it has a mild taste, but when it meets other savoury compounds in a recipe, it multiplies the perception of savoury and meaty flavours. This synergistic effect allows food companies to use much less salt and fewer expensive meat extracts while still achieving a robust, savoury profile. It is particularly valued in dry mixes and processed foods where flavours can fade during shelf life.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Regulators have evaluated this substance thoroughly and found no reason to restrict its use. Because our bodies metabolise it in the exact same way they process the natural nucleotides found in everyday foods like meat and vegetables, it does not accumulate or cause harm. EFSA has reviewed the safety data and concluded that there is no need to establish a numerical acceptable daily intake, meaning it is considered safe for the general population at the levels used in food.

What the science says

Scientific panels have analysed calcium guanylate to ensure it behaves safely in the human body. When we consume it, our digestive system breaks it down into calcium and guanosine, which are normal nutrients that our cells use every day. Because it is processed so naturally, animal studies show no adverse effects even at very high dietary concentrations. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the additive and confirmed that it poses no safety concerns, noting that no limit is established for its daily intake.

Where you'll find it

You will typically find this ingredient in highly savoury, processed foods such as instant noodles, potato crisps, savoury snacks, tinned soups, gravy granules, and seasoning blends. On food labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is usually declared by its name as calcium guanylate or by its European code, E 629. It is often listed alongside other flavour enhancers or yeast extracts on the ingredient panel to help create a balanced, savoury taste profile.
