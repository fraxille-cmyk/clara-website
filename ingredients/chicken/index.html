This is simply chicken, a whole food and an excellent source of high-quality protein with no added ingredients.

What it is

This is the meat of the domestic chicken, a familiar whole food that has been a staple of human diets for thousands of years. It is a lean source of complete protein, meaning it provides all the essential amino acids your body needs to build and repair its own tissues. Unlike ultra-processed ingredients or complex additives, this is a natural food in its original form, containing only the moisture, fats, and proteins native to the animal.

How it's made

This ingredient is produced through standard agricultural practices. After the birds are raised, they are prepared and butchered into familiar cuts such as breasts, fillets, or thighs. The meat is cleaned, chilled, and packaged without any chemical processing, synthetic preservatives, or artificial injections. It is a straightforward agricultural product that goes from the farm to the shop with minimal handling to ensure freshness and quality.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use chicken as the primary, nutrient-dense foundation of ready meals, soups, and pre-packaged foods. It is selected because it is a popular, versatile protein that absorbs flavours beautifully and maintains a pleasant, tender texture when cooked. Using real chicken meat rather than processed substitutes provides shoppers with a familiar, satisfying, and naturally nutritious centrepiece for their meals.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. Because this is a single-ingredient whole food with nothing added, it does not carry the risks associated with ultra-processed additives or synthetic preservatives. It is a clean, natural source of nourishment that fits well into a balanced diet. As with any raw meat, the only standard precaution is to handle and cook it thoroughly to ensure it is completely safe to eat.

What the science says

Nutritional authorities worldwide recognise chicken as a valuable source of lean protein, essential B vitamins, and minerals like selenium and phosphorus. Because it is a whole food rather than an additive, it has not been subjected to the additive evaluation processes of regulators like EFSA or the FDA. Instead, dietary guidelines from organisations such as the WHO consistently recommend lean poultry as a beneficial component of a balanced, health-supporting diet.

Where you'll find it

You will find this listed on labels in the fresh meat aisle, in frozen meals, and in prepared salads or sandwiches. It may appear simply as chicken, chicken breast, chicken fillet, or chicken thighs. On imported European products, you might spot it under its continental names, such as poulet, pollo, hähnchen, or kylling, all of which simply mean chicken.
