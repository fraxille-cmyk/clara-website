Caramel natural colour

IngredientNeutral

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

A traditional brown colouring made by heating natural sugars, used to give foods a warm, familiar golden-brown appearance.

What it is

Caramel natural colour is a colouring agent derived from the controlled heating of carbohydrates. Unlike industrial caramel colours which are processed with ammonium or sulphite compounds, this is a simpler form of caramelisation. It is essentially the same substance you create in a home kitchen when you gently melt and brown table sugar in a saucepan, resulting in a rich, complex mixture of sweet-smelling molecules that carry a deep amber hue.

How it's made

To produce this colour on a commercial scale, manufacturers heat food-grade carbohydrates, such as sucrose, glucose, or fructose, derived from corn, wheat, or sugar beets. The sugars are heated under controlled temperatures and pressures without the addition of chemical catalysts like ammonium or sulphite. This thermal process causes the sugar molecules to lose water and combine into larger, complex structures that reflect a warm brown light, which are then cooled and filtered into a liquid or powder.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this ingredient to restore or standardise the natural brown colour of foods that might be lost during high-temperature processing. It provides a highly stable, water-soluble colour that does not fade under light or heat. It is a cost-effective way to make products look more appealing, rich, and consistent from batch to batch, mimicking the natural golden-brown appearance of slow-cooked or baked goods without altering the texture of the final product.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. Because it is made by simple thermal caramelisation of ordinary food sugars without chemical additives, it does not carry the same safety questions as chemically modified caramel colours. No daily intake limit is established for this simple caramelised sugar when used as a natural colouring ingredient. It is processed by the body in the same manner as other caramelised food components that we consume in a normal, balanced diet.

What the science says

Regulators have evaluated caramelised sugars and simple caramel colours to ensure they are safe for general consumption. JECFA and EFSA have reviewed the safety of caramel colours, distinguishing simple caramelised sugars from those processed with ammonia or sulphites. The consensus is that simple caramelisation of food-grade sugars presents no safety concerns. Because it is a natural food component rather than a synthetic additive, it is treated as a benign ingredient with no established upper limit of intake.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of packaged foods, including baked goods, biscuits, gravies, sauces, confectionery, and some soft drinks. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared plainly as caramel natural colour, natural caramel colour, or simply caramel. Because it is not classified as a food additive in the same category as chemically modified caramels, it does not carry an E-number on the label.
