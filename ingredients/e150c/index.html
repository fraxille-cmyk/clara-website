Ammonia caramel (E150c)

E150cAdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Ammonia caramel is a synthetic brown food colour made by heating sugars with ammonium compounds. Clara rates it NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive.

What it is

Ammonia caramel is a water-soluble food colour used to give products a rich brown shade. Unlike the simple caramel you might make in a saucepan at home by melting sugar, this is a highly controlled industrial substance. It is one of four distinct classes of caramel colouring used in the food industry. It is specifically formulated to remain stable in mildly acidic environments, meaning it will not clump or separate when mixed into complex food mixtures. It has a slightly bitter taste and a mild burnt sugar aroma, though it is used in such small quantities that it rarely affects the final flavour of a product.

How it's made

To manufacture ammonia caramel, producers heat food-grade carbohydrates, such as glucose syrup, sucrose, or invert sugar, under highly controlled temperature and pressure conditions. During this heating process, they introduce ammonium compounds, which act as chemical catalysts to promote caramelisation and browning. No sulphite compounds are used in this specific class of caramel. Once the desired deep brown colour and chemical stability are achieved, the mixture is cooled, filtered, and standardised into either a thick liquid or a fine, free-flowing powder ready for food manufacturing.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose ammonia caramel because it is an exceptionally stable and cost-effective brown colourant. It resists fading when exposed to light, heat, and oxygen, ensuring that packaged foods maintain an appealing, consistent appearance throughout their entire shelf life. It is particularly valued for its positive ionic charge, which prevents it from reacting with proteins or other negatively charged ingredients in certain foods. This prevents cloudiness or sedimentation, making it far more reliable than natural colour alternatives or homemade caramel syrups.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. While it is a chemically modified synthetic colour, extensive safety evaluations show it is safe when consumed within established limits. EFSA keeps a numeric acceptable daily intake of 300 milligrams per kilogram of body weight for this colour, which is a very generous limit that a typical shopper is highly unlikely to exceed. There is no toxicological concern at normal dietary levels, and regulators do not require any special warning labels on foods containing it.

What the science says

This colour has been thoroughly evaluated by major food safety authorities worldwide. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated ammonia caramel and confirmed its safety profile, establishing a clear daily limit for human consumption. During the manufacturing process, a minor by-product called 4-methylimidazole can form in small amounts. Because of this by-product, IARC classifies 4-methylimidazole as Group 2B, possibly carcinogenic to humans. However, international food regulators have set strict maximum limits on this by-product within the food colour itself to ensure that the final ingredient remains entirely safe for the general public.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of dark-coloured foods and drinks. It commonly appears in dark beers, stouts, soy sauces, gravies, malt breads, biscuits, and chocolate-flavoured confectionery. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared as ammonia caramel, plain caramel, or by its European code, E 150c. On US labels, it is simply declared as caramel colour.
