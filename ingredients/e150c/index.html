Ammonia caramel (E150c)

E150cAdditiveAvoid · limited concern

Ammonia caramel is a dark brown food colour made by heating sugars with ammonium compounds, which Clara rates as an additive to avoid.

What it is

Ammonia caramel is a complex mixture of dark brown compounds used to colour foods and drinks. It is one of four distinct classes of caramel colourings used in the food industry. Unlike the simple caramelised sugar you might make in a saucepan at home, this industrial version is chemically modified to ensure it remains stable and soluble in highly acidic or salty environments, allowing it to blend smoothly into a wide variety of processed foods without separating or settling.

How it's made

To manufacture this colouring, producers heat food-grade carbohydrates, such as glucose syrup or sucrose, under controlled temperature and pressure conditions. During this heating process, they introduce ammonium compounds to promote specific chemical reactions that darken the mixture. This chemical reaction, known as caramelisation, is accelerated and altered by the ammonia, resulting in a deep brown liquid or powder that is subsequently purified, filtered, and standardised for strength and stability.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this colouring because it provides a highly stable, deep brown shade that does not fade when exposed to light, heat, or acids. It is highly soluble in water and binds well with proteins, making it particularly useful in savoury products. It is a highly cost-effective alternative to natural colouring agents, allowing companies to maintain a consistent, appealing appearance in their products across different production batches and during long periods on supermarket shelves.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a limited-concern additive. While it is approved for use, EFSA classifies its safety with an established acceptable daily intake of 300 mg per kg of body weight, which applies to the group of caramel colours. A specific concern with this class of caramel is the presence of a manufacturing by-product called 2-acetyl-4-tetrahydroxybutylimidazole, which can affect the immune system at high doses, leading regulators to set strict limits on its concentration in the final colouring.

What the science says

The safety of this colouring has been evaluated by several international bodies. EFSA re-evaluated the additive and confirmed its group acceptable daily intake, while noting that the by-product of its ammonia processing can reduce white blood cell counts in animal studies. JECFA also evaluated the substance and established a daily limit to ensure that human exposure remains well below levels that cause these physiological changes. Scientists continue to monitor dietary exposure levels to ensure consumers do not exceed these established safety thresholds.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide range of processed foods, including dark beers, soy sauces, gravies, soups, brown sauces, and certain confectionery items. On ingredient labels, it is typically declared by its name, ammonia caramel, its E-number, E150c, or occasionally as plain caramel, depending on the region and the specific labelling regulations of the country where the product is sold.
