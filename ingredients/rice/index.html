Rice

Whole foodGood

Rice is a wholesome, single-ingredient grain that serves as a staple food worldwide, offering a clean source of energy with nothing added.

What it is

Rice is the seed of a semi-aquatic grass plant that has sustained human civilisations for thousands of years. It is a staple grain, much like wheat or oats, and comes in many familiar varieties such as basmati, jasmine, and arborio. In its whole form, known as brown rice, it retains its outer bran and germ layers. When these layers are polished away, we get white rice, which has a milder flavour and a softer texture when cooked.

How it's made

The journey of rice begins in flooded fields, often called paddies, where the grass is grown and harvested. Once cut, the plants are threshed to separate the grain from the stalk. The outer, inedible hull is then removed to produce brown rice. To make white rice, the grain undergoes a milling and polishing process to rub away the bran and germ. Some rice is parboiled, which means it is soaked and steamed in its husk to lock nutrients into the centre before milling.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use rice because it is incredibly versatile, naturally gluten-free, and acts as an excellent base for other flavours. It provides structure and bulk in ready meals, soups, and baby foods. Because it is easy to dry and store, it has an exceptional shelf life, making it a highly reliable and cost-effective ingredient for packaged foods. It also absorbs liquids beautifully, which helps maintain the desired texture in complex, multi-ingredient dishes.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. This is because rice is a single-ingredient product and a whole food itself, with absolutely nothing added to it. It is a natural source of carbohydrates that your body uses for daily energy. There are no synthetic chemicals, artificial preservatives, or processing aids to worry about here. It is a clean, traditional food that has been eaten safely by billions of people across the globe for generations.

What the science says

Major food authorities view rice as a fundamental dietary staple rather than an additive. The FDA recognises whole and milled rice as safe, standard agricultural products, while the WHO highlights its role in global nutrition. Because it is a whole food, regulators do not set an upper intake limit for it. Scientific evaluations focus on its nutritional value, noting that brown rice retains more dietary fibre and micronutrients, whereas white rice is highly digestible and gentle on the stomach.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a vast range of products, from simple microwave pouches to ready meals, cereals, and baby foods. On ingredient lists, it might appear simply as rice, brown rice, white rice, or under specific variety names like basmati rice, jasmine rice, or arborio rice. On European imports, you might spot its translated names such as riz, riso, arroz, ris, rijst, or reis.
