Sodium acetates (E262)

E262AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Sodium acetates are vinegar-derived salts used to control acidity, preserve freshness, and add a tangy, salt-and-vinegar flavour to processed foods.

What it is

Sodium acetates are the sodium salts of acetic acid, which is the natural compound that gives vinegar its characteristic sharp smell and sour taste. In the food world, you will find them used as a powder or as crystals that dissolve easily in water. They exist in two main forms, either as simple sodium acetate or as sodium diacetate, which is a molecular compound of sodium acetate and acetic acid that delivers a stronger vinegar kick. They occur naturally in some fermenting fruits and plants, but the versions used in packaged foods are made in a controlled environment.

How it's made

To make this ingredient on a commercial scale, manufacturers combine acetic acid with a sodium source, typically sodium carbonate or sodium hydroxide. This is essentially a highly controlled version of the classic kitchen science experiment where you mix vinegar and baking soda. The reaction neutralises the acid and creates a liquid solution of sodium acetate. This liquid is then concentrated, crystallised, and dried into a fine white powder. The process is straightforward, highly efficient, and yields a pure, stable substance that is easy to transport and mix into dry food formulations.

Why manufacturers use it

Food producers rely on sodium acetates for their dual ability to regulate acidity and enhance flavour. They act as a buffer, which means they keep the pH level of a food stable during storage. This stability slows down the growth of unwanted micro-organisms, keeping packaged bread and meats fresh for longer. Commercially, they are the secret behind the dry salt-and-vinegar seasoning on potato crisps, as they deliver that sharp, tangy taste without making the snack soggy. They are cheaper, easier to handle, and much more stable than liquid vinegar in dry seasoning blends.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Because our bodies metabolise sodium acetate just like the natural vinegar we eat in salad dressings, it does not pose a safety concern for the general population. The European Food Safety Authority has evaluated it and determined that no daily intake limit is established because it is so well tolerated. The only minor consideration is that it contains sodium, which contributes to your overall daily salt intake, though the amounts used in food preservation are typically very small.

What the science says

The safety of sodium acetates has been thoroughly reviewed by international food safety bodies. The Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives and the European Food Safety Authority have both assessed the substance and confirmed its safety for use in food. Because acetic acid and its salts are normal components of human metabolism and are broken down naturally by our cells, regulators have not found any reason to set a numerical limit on daily consumption. It is authorised for use in a wide variety of foods, including baby foods, where strict safety standards apply.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide range of savoury foods, especially salt-and-vinegar flavoured crisps, snacks, crackers, and pretzels. It is also commonly added to pre-packed sliced bread, bakery products, processed meats, and ready-made salads to keep them fresh. On food labels, it may be listed by its name as sodium acetate, sodium diacetate, sodium hydrogen diacetate, or simply under its European code, E262.
