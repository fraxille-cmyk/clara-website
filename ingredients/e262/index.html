Sodium acetates (E262)

E262AdditiveNeutral · benign

Sodium acetates are synthetic salts of vinegar that regulate acidity and preserve food, rated neutral by Clara with no safety concerns.

What it is

Sodium acetates are the sodium salts of acetic acid, which is the very same acid that gives vinegar its characteristic sharp taste and aroma. In the food world, they exist as either sodium acetate or sodium diacetate. While they are chemically identical to the compounds that form when vinegar reacts with a sodium source, the versions you see on food labels are prepared in a controlled environment to ensure purity and consistency. They act as a familiar, gentle acid salt that helps manage acidity and freshness in packaged foods.

How it's made

To make these ingredients, manufacturers combine acetic acid with a basic sodium compound, such as sodium carbonate or sodium hydroxide, in water. This acid-base reaction neutralises the liquid and creates a salt solution. The mixture is then concentrated, cooled, and crystallised into a clean, white powder or granular substance. Because it is produced through this chemical synthesis rather than direct extraction from vinegar, it is classified as a synthetic food additive, even though the final molecule behaves exactly like its natural counterpart.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on these salts for two main reasons: flavouring and preservation. In salt and vinegar crisps, they deliver that sharp, tangy kick without making the snack soggy, which liquid vinegar would do. Beyond flavour, they act as excellent acidity regulators and buffering agents, keeping the pH of a food stable. This controlled acidity prevents the growth of unwanted micro-organisms, extending the shelf life of baked goods, meat products, and salads without altering the texture or requiring harsher chemical preservatives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. These salts have an exceptionally long and clean safety record, behaving in the body just like the natural acids found in everyday foods. Because the body metabolises them easily as simple acetate and sodium, they do not accumulate or cause adverse reactions. EFSA re-evaluated these substances and confirmed that no daily limit is established, meaning they are considered safe for the general population at the levels typically used in food production.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly reviewed these compounds and found no reason for concern. EFSA concluded in its re-evaluation that there is no need to set a numerical acceptable daily intake because the substances are natural metabolites in the human body. The FDA also recognises them as generally recognised as safe, permitting their use under specific federal regulations. Because they break down into simple, familiar components during digestion, researchers agree that they pose no risk to human health when consumed in normal dietary amounts.

Where you'll find it

You will frequently spot this ingredient in savoury snacks, particularly salt and vinegar flavoured crisps, crackers, and pretzels. It is also common in sliced bread, ready-to-eat salads, processed meats, and sauces, where it helps maintain freshness. On a label, it might be listed as sodium acetate, sodium diacetate, sodium acetates, or simply by its European code, E262. On US labels, it is often declared as sodium acetate or sodium diacetate to comply with local naming regulations.
