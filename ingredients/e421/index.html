Mannitol (E421)

E421SweetenerNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✕ Not low-FODMAP
  • Not low-FODMAP — mannitol is a polyol.

A low-calorie sugar alcohol used to sweeten and texturise foods, which is generally fine but can cause mild digestive discomfort in large amounts.

What it is

Mannitol is a sugar alcohol, or polyol, which is a type of carbohydrate that occurs naturally in many plants, including seaweed, mushrooms, and celery. It looks and tastes much like regular table sugar but has about half the calories and a distinct cooling sensation on the tongue. Because our bodies absorb it much slower than regular sugars, it does not cause the same rapid rise in blood glucose levels, making it a popular alternative sweetener.

How it's made

While it exists in nature, commercial mannitol is typically produced through industrial processes to ensure a steady and pure supply. Manufacturers usually make it by hydrogenating fructose, a simple sugar derived from corn starch or sugar beet. This chemical process adds hydrogen atoms to the sugar molecules, transforming them into a stable sugar alcohol. The resulting substance is then crystallised, washed, and dried into a fine white powder.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose mannitol because it does not absorb moisture easily, making it an excellent dusting agent for chewing gum to prevent it from sticking to machinery and packaging. It also provides bulk, texture, and a pleasant sweetness without the calories of sugar. Its chemical stability means it does not break down under high heat, which is ideal for baked goods, and it helps to extend the shelf life of confectionery by controlling moisture.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This sugar alcohol is used as a low-calorie sweetener and texturiser, but consuming polyols can cause digestive discomfort when eaten in large quantities. Because it is poorly absorbed in the small intestine, it travels to the large intestine where it draws in water and is fermented by gut bacteria, which can lead to bloating or a laxative effect. No limit is established for daily intake, as regulators consider it safe for general consumption, though moderation is sensible.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated mannitol and confirmed its safety in food. EFSA re-evaluated the additive and established that no numerical daily limit is necessary because it does not pose a safety concern at typical dietary levels. The FDA also affirms its status as a permitted food ingredient. Researchers note that while it is highly effective for reducing sugar content, individual tolerance varies, and some people may experience mild digestive changes at lower amounts than others.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of sugar-free and reduced-calorie products, including chewing gum, hard candies, chocolate, breath mints, and baked goods. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its plain name as mannitol, or by its European code, E421. It may also appear on labels as E 421 or occasionally as mannitol syrup when used in a liquid form.
