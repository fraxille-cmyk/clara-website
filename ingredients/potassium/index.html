Potassium

FortificantGood

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Potassium is an essential mineral that your body needs to keep its fluids balanced and its nerves communicating properly.

What it is

Potassium is a vital mineral and electrolyte that your body cannot make on its own. In nature, it is a highly reactive metal, so it is always found bound to other elements as a salt. It is one of the most abundant elements in the human body, sitting inside your cells where it helps maintain the delicate balance of water and electricity that keeps you alive. You naturally get it from foods like bananas, potatoes, and leafy greens, but it is also added to processed foods to boost their nutritional profile.

How it's made

To make the potassium compounds used in food fortification, manufacturers start with natural potash ore, which is mined from ancient underground lake beds. This raw salt is dissolved in water and purified to remove impurities like clay and sodium chloride. Through chemical crystallisation, the potassium is combined with other elements to create stable, food-grade powders such as potassium chloride or potassium phosphate. These purified mineral salts are easily dissolved and absorbed when added to food and drinks.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use potassium primarily to fortify products, restoring minerals lost during processing or boosting the nutritional value of everyday foods. It is a popular addition to sports drinks and rehydration formulas because it replaces the electrolytes lost through sweat. In some recipes, manufacturers use potassium salts as a direct replacement for regular table salt. This allows them to lower the overall sodium content of a product while keeping a similar salty flavour and preserving the shelf life of the food.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As an essential nutrient, potassium is highly beneficial for your body, helping to manage fluid balance and support nerve signals. For the vast majority of people, consuming potassium in food is entirely beneficial because the kidneys naturally filter out any excess. No upper limit is established for healthy individuals by major food authorities, though people with specific kidney conditions may need to monitor their intake. It is a supportive mineral that helps balance the high amounts of sodium in modern diets.

What the science says

Scientific bodies worldwide have thoroughly evaluated potassium and recognise its critical role in human health. EFSA has published dietary reference values confirming that potassium contributes to normal muscle function and the maintenance of normal blood pressure. The FDA recognises potassium as an essential nutrient and permits health claims on foods containing sufficient levels of this mineral. Because it is a necessary nutrient rather than a synthetic additive, regulators focus on ensuring people get enough of it rather than setting restrictive safety limits.

Where you'll find it

You will find potassium added to sports drinks, electrolyte powders, infant formulas, and fortified plant milks. It is also common in low-sodium salts, processed meats, and packaged soups where it replaces regular sodium. On a food label, it does not have an E-number because it is classified as a nutrient rather than a food additive. Instead, it is typically listed in the ingredients list under plain names such as potassium chloride, potassium phosphate, or potassium citrate, and its total amount is often highlighted in the nutritional information panel.
