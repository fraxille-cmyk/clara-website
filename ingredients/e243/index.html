Ethyl lauroyl arginate (E243)

E243AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

A synthetic preservative used in processed meats to stop microbes from growing, with a strict daily limit of 0.5 mg per kg of body weight.

What it is

Ethyl lauroyl arginate is a synthetic preservative designed to keep food fresh by stopping the growth of microbes. Chemically, it is made by combining three distinct parts: lauric acid, which is a natural fatty acid found in coconut oil; L-arginine, a common amino acid found in many protein-rich foods; and ethanol, which is ordinary alcohol. Although its building blocks are inspired by nature, the final compound is entirely man-made and does not exist in this form in nature.

How it's made

This additive is produced in a chemical factory through a multi-step synthesis process. First, the amino acid L-arginine is reacted with ethanol to create an ethyl ester. This intermediate compound is then reacted with lauryl chloride, which is derived from lauric acid. The resulting substance is purified, crystallised, and typically sold as a white powder known as ethyl lauroyl arginate hydrochloride, which dissolves easily in water and can be added directly to food mixtures.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this compound because it is a highly effective antimicrobial agent. It works by disrupting the cell membranes of bacteria, yeasts, and moulds, which stops them from multiplying. This action helps to extend the shelf life of products and maintains food quality during storage. It is particularly valued in the meat industry because it works well at low temperatures and does not alter the flavour, colour, or texture of the final product.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. While it is synthetic, it is broken down quickly by the body into normal dietary components, namely arginine, lauric acid, and ethanol. EFSA has established a numeric acceptable daily intake of 0.5 mg per kg of body weight. Because of this strict limit, regulators restrict its use to specific food categories to ensure that shoppers do not consume too much of it in their daily diet.

What the science says

Both European and American regulators have thoroughly evaluated this preservative. EFSA analysed its safety and metabolic pathway, concluding that the body processes it efficiently without leaving harmful residues. The FDA has also reviewed its safety profile and accepted its status as a preservative. Because it is used in very small amounts and is restricted to specific food categories, scientific panels agree that it poses no safety concerns when consumed within the established daily limit.

Where you'll find it

You will primarily find this ingredient in processed and heat-treated meat products, such as sliced hams, bacon, and poultry products, though it is excluded from some traditional items like liver paste and smoked sausages. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its full name, ethyl lauroyl arginate, or by its European code, E243. On US labels, it may sometimes appear as ethyl lauroyl arginate HCl.
