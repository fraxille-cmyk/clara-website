Potassium aluminium silicate (E555)

E555AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Potassium aluminium silicate is a mineral carrier used to disperse food colours; Clara rates it AVOID due to concerns over aluminium accumulation.

What it is

Potassium aluminium silicate is a naturally occurring mineral compound, commonly known in the geological world as mica. It is a silicate mineral that forms thin, glittering sheets in rocks. In the food industry, it is processed into a very fine, shimmering powder. While it does not dissolve in water, its unique structure allows it to act as a physical support for other substances, helping to spread them evenly throughout a mixture.

How it's made

This ingredient is sourced directly from natural mineral deposits. The raw mica is mined from the earth and then undergoes a series of physical purification steps. It is ground down into an extremely fine powder, washed thoroughly to remove any unwanted geological impurities, and dried. Because the mineral structure is kept intact, the final powder is chemically identical to the natural stone from which it was mined.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this mineral primarily as a carrier for other additives, especially pearlescent food colours. It acts as a tiny physical platform, allowing colours to disperse evenly and create a shimmering, metallic, or glittering effect on cake decorations, sweets, and chocolates. It also prevents clumping and ensures that powdered ingredients flow smoothly through factory machinery during the packaging process.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. The primary concern is that this compound contains aluminium, which can accumulate in the body over time. In 2020, EFSA evaluated the substance and decided not to establish a safe daily limit because there was not enough data to prove how much aluminium is absorbed from it. To keep your overall aluminium intake as low as possible, Clara recommends avoiding foods that contain this carrier.

What the science says

European regulators have closely scrutinised this mineral due to the presence of aluminium. EFSA published a scientific opinion in 2020 stating that a safe daily intake level could not be established because of gaps in the available toxicological data. While the mineral is considered highly stable, scientists want more research to confirm exactly how much aluminium might be released and absorbed by the human body during digestion.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in highly decorated foods, such as cake dusts, edible glitters, chocolates, chewing gum, and confectionery. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically listed as potassium aluminium silicate or by its E-number, E555. On US labels it is listed as potassium aluminium silicate, where it is permitted for use as an anticaking agent.
