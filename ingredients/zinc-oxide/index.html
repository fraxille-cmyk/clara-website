Zinc oxide

FortificantNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Zinc oxide is a mineral compound used to fortify foods with zinc, an essential nutrient that helps our bodies function properly.

What it is

Zinc oxide is a simple mineral compound made of zinc and oxygen. In the natural world, it exists as a rare mineral called zincite, but the version we see in our food is created in a controlled environment to ensure purity. Zinc itself is an essential trace element, meaning our bodies need a small, regular supply of it to keep our immune systems working and to help our cells grow and repair themselves. Because our bodies cannot make zinc on their own, we must get it from what we eat.

How it's made

To make the food-grade ingredient, manufacturers start with metallic zinc, which is purified and heated to a very high temperature until it vaporises. This zinc vapour is then introduced to oxygen in the air, causing a natural reaction that forms a fine, bright white powder. This powder is carefully collected, washed, and milled to ensure the particles are of an even size and completely free from impurities before it is added to food products. This thermal process ensures the final mineral is highly stable and clean.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this ingredient primarily as a nutrient fortificant to boost the mineral content of everyday foods. It is highly favoured over other zinc compounds because it is incredibly stable, does not break down easily when exposed to heat or light during processing, and has a neutral flavour that does not alter the taste of the food. It also has a bright white colour, which can help maintain a clean look in powdered products without affecting the final appearance of the prepared food.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. This mineral nutrient is harmless at the small amounts typically found on food labels. The body processes it just like the zinc naturally present in whole foods, absorbing what it needs and safely excreting the rest. No limit is established for its use as a fortificant in standard food products, as the amounts added are self-limiting and designed to help consumers meet their daily recommended intake of this essential mineral without any risk of overexposure.

What the science says

The safety of this mineral has been thoroughly evaluated by major food authorities worldwide. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration have both assessed its use as a source of zinc in food supplements and fortified foods, concluding that it is a highly bioavailable and safe way to deliver this essential nutrient. Because it is a simple mineral that the body recognises and metabolises naturally, scientific consensus focuses on its beneficial role in supporting daily nutrition rather than any safety concerns.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in fortified breakfast cereals, nutritional shakes, infant formulas, and meal replacement bars. On the packaging, it is usually listed plainly as zinc oxide, but you might also spot it under its European and international names such as zinkoxid, zinkoxide, oxyde de zinc, ossido di zinco, or óxido de zinc depending on where the product was packaged. It does not have an assigned E-number because it is classified as a nutrient rather than a technological food additive.
