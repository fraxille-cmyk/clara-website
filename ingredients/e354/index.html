Calcium tartrate (E354)

E354AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Calcium tartrate is a benign food additive derived from grapes, used to control acidity and keep packaged foods stable.

What it is

Calcium tartrate is a natural calcium salt of tartaric acid, which is an organic acid found abundantly in many fruits, especially grapes. If you have ever noticed tiny, harmless crystals at the bottom of a wine bottle, you have seen tartrates in their natural habitat. In food manufacturing, this substance behaves as a mild acid regulator and stabiliser, helping to manage the delicate balance of acidity in packaged goods.

How it's made

This ingredient is typically obtained as a natural by-product of the winemaking industry. During the fermentation and aging of wine, tartrates naturally precipitate out of the liquid and settle on the walls of the wine casks. Manufacturers collect these crude tartrate deposits, dissolve them, and neutralise them with calcium hydroxide. The resulting calcium tartrate is then purified, crystallised, and dried into a fine white powder ready for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use calcium tartrate primarily to control the acidity of their products, which helps to preserve flavour and maintain a consistent texture over time. It also acts as a firming agent, helping to keep canned fruits and vegetables from turning mushy. Because it is highly stable and does not easily absorb moisture from the air, it is an excellent choice for dry mixes and powders where clumping must be prevented.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. It has a long history of safe use in food and is metabolised by the body much like the natural acids found in fresh fruit. EFSA established a group acceptable daily intake of 240 mg per kg of body weight for tartaric acid and its salts in 2020, which is a generous limit that is very difficult to exceed through a normal diet. There are no known health concerns or adverse effects associated with its consumption.

What the science says

The safety of this substance has been thoroughly evaluated by major food authorities worldwide. EFSA re-evaluated calcium tartrate alongside other tartrates in 2020 and confirmed that it does not pose a safety concern at current exposure levels. The FDA also recognises it as generally safe for use in food. Because it is a simple mineral salt of a common fruit acid, it is easily processed by the human digestive system without accumulation.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of processed foods, including canned fruits, vegetables, jams, jellies, and bakery products. It is also common in some confectionery and self-rising flour mixes. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically listed by its name, calcium tartrate, or by its European code, E354, while on US labels it is listed as calcium tartrate.
