Quillaia extract (E999)

E999AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Quillaia extract is a natural foaming agent from tree bark that is limited to a daily intake of 3 mg per kg of body weight.

What it is

Quillaia extract is a natural substance obtained from the inner bark of the soapbark tree, which is native to the warm regions of South America. It contains natural compounds called saponins, which have a unique molecular structure that allows them to interact with both water and oil. This natural property makes the extract highly effective at creating and holding bubbles, much like a natural soap, which is how the tree originally earned its common name.

How it's made

The production process begins with harvesting the bark of the soapbark tree. This bark is milled into small pieces and treated with hot water to extract the active compounds. The resulting liquid is filtered to remove any solid plant matter and then concentrated. To ensure it is stable and easy for food producers to handle, the concentrated liquid is often spray-dried into a fine, light brown powder before being packaged.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this extract primarily for its exceptional ability to create a thick, stable head of foam on beverages. It is highly valued in the soft drink industry because it keeps bubbles suspended for a long time, even in acidic conditions where other foaming agents might fail. Because it is highly concentrated, manufacturers only need to use a tiny amount to achieve the desired frothy texture, making it a highly efficient choice for fizzy drinks.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is given because it is a plant-derived substance that has a numeric acceptable daily intake of 3 mg per kg of body weight. EFSA keeps this daily limit to ensure that consumers do not ingest excessive amounts of saponins, which can irritate the digestive tract in very high doses. At the low levels actually used in drinks, it is considered safe by food safety authorities.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated this extract to ensure its safety in beverages. EFSA completed a full re-evaluation of the additive and confirmed that the current limit of 3 mg per kg of body weight protects consumers. Because its use is restricted to specific beverage categories, dietary exposure assessments show that even people who drink large amounts of soft drinks are highly unlikely to exceed this safe limit.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient almost exclusively in carbonated soft drinks, root beers, ginger beers, and cider, where a long-lasting frothy head is desired. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically declared by its functional class and name or number, appearing as foaming agent quillaia extract or simply E999. On US labels, it is often listed as quillaia extract or under its botanical name.
