Riboflavin (vitamin B2) (E101)

E101FortificantNeutral · benign

Riboflavin is vitamin B2, a natural yellow substance used both to add a bright colour to food and to boost its nutritional value.

What it is

Riboflavin is simply vitamin B2, a water-soluble nutrient that our bodies need every day to help turn food into energy. In its pure form, it is an intense orange-yellow powder. Because of this natural pigment, it serves a dual purpose in the food industry. It can be added to foods to restore vitamins lost during processing, or it can be used as a natural colouring agent to give products a warm, appealing yellow hue. It is the very same vitamin found naturally in green vegetables, eggs, milk, and almonds.

How it's made

While riboflavin can be extracted from natural sources, manufacturers almost always produce it through fermentation. Special strains of yeast or bacteria are grown in large vats containing sugar-rich broths, which they naturally metabolise into high yields of vitamin B2. Once the fermentation is complete, the mixture is heated to kill the micro-organisms, and the riboflavin is filtered out, purified, and dried into a fine powder. This biological process is highly efficient and avoids the need for harsh chemical synthesis.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use riboflavin because it is a highly stable, natural way to colour and fortify foods. It resists heat and acid very well, meaning it keeps its yellow colour even after baking or pasteurisation. Commercially, it is an excellent alternative to synthetic yellow dyes, allowing brands to keep their ingredient lists clean and appealing to shoppers. Additionally, adding it allows manufacturers to claim a product is fortified with essential vitamins, which increases its appeal to health-conscious buyers.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is a water-soluble vitamin, any excess that your body does not need is simply flushed out in your urine, which might turn a bright, harmless yellow. The European Food Safety Authority has evaluated riboflavin and confirmed it is of no safety concern when used as a food additive, meaning no daily limit is established. It is a nutrient that the body recognises and processes easily, making it entirely safe at the levels typically found in food.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated riboflavin as both a nutrient and a food additive. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated its safety as a food colour and concluded that the substance is not a safety concern and does not require a numerical acceptable daily intake. Joint FAO/WHO expert meetings have also reviewed its use, finding no evidence of unwanted effects even at high supplemental doses. Because it is an essential nutrient, the scientific consensus focuses on its beneficial role in cellular energy production rather than any dietary risk.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of everyday foods, including breakfast cereals, energy drinks, baby foods, sauces, and instant noodles. On the packaging, it might be listed under its E-number, E101, or by its common names like riboflavin, vitamin B2, riboflavina, or vitamine b2. It is also frequently added to plant-based milk alternatives to match the nutritional profile of dairy milk, where it serves as both a fortificant and a subtle colour enhancer.
