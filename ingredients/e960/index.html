Steviol glycosides (E960)

E960SweetenerNeutral · limited concern

A plant-derived sweetener that replaces sugar without adding calories, considered safe but usually found in highly formulated foods.

What it is

Steviol glycosides are the sweet-tasting compounds found naturally in the leaves of the stevia plant, a shrub native to South America. These compounds are incredibly potent, tasting hundreds of times sweeter than regular table sugar. Unlike sugar, they do not provide calories or raise glucose levels in the blood because the body does not metabolise them for energy. Instead, they pass through the digestive system, offering a way to sweeten foods and drinks without the bulk or energy density of traditional sugars.

How it's made

The journey from plant to powder begins by harvesting the stevia leaves and drying them. These dried leaves are steeped in hot water to release the sweet compounds, much like brewing tea. This liquid extract is then filtered and purified using water or food-grade alcohol to isolate the specific sweet molecules, mainly rebaudioside A and stevioside. The purified liquid is concentrated and spray-dried into a fine white powder, leaving behind the bitter green elements of the original leaf.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose this ingredient because it delivers intense sweetness at a fraction of the weight of sugar, which helps lower the calorie count of their products. It is highly stable under heat and across a wide range of acidity levels, making it ideal for baked goods, pasteurised beverages, and dairy products. Because it is so potent, only a tiny amount is needed, which reduces shipping and storage costs compared to bulky sugar, though manufacturers often must add bulkers to recreate the texture of sugar.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. It is not a health concern at the levels normally found in food, but its presence is a clear sign of a highly formulated product. EFSA has established an acceptable daily intake of 4 mg per kg of body weight for these glycosides. While this limit is difficult to exceed through normal eating habits, keeping an eye on your overall intake of highly sweetened products is always a sensible approach to building balanced eating habits.

What the science says

The safety of these compounds has been thoroughly evaluated by major international food authorities. JECFA and EFSA have both reviewed the scientific evidence and concluded that they do not pose safety concerns when consumed within the established daily limits. Research shows that the microbes in our lower digestive tract break down the glycosides into steviol, which is then absorbed, processed by the liver, and harmlessly excreted in urine. No accumulation occurs in the body, and studies confirm they do not affect blood sugar regulation.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in low-calorie yoghurts, diet soft drinks, protein powders, sugar-free confectionery, and tabletop sweeteners. On the ingredient list, it might be declared by its E-number, E960, or under names like stevia extract, stevia leaf extract, rebaudioside A, rebaudioside M, or simply steviol glycosides. It is often paired with other bulking agents or sweeteners to mask a slight liquorice-like aftertaste that can sometimes occur at higher concentrations.
