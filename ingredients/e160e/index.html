Beta-apo-8'-carotenal (E160e)

E160eAdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Clara rates this orange-red colour as AVOID because refined exposure estimates for toddlers and children can exceed the acceptable daily intake.

What it is

Beta-apo-8'-carotenal is a deep orange-to-red pigment that belongs to the carotenoid family. Carotenoids are the natural compounds that give carrots, oranges, and autumn leaves their warm, vibrant hues. While some carotenoids are extracted directly from plants, this particular version is a synthetic counterpart designed to mimic those natural shades. It is highly stable and provides a reliable, uniform colour that does not fade easily during food processing, making it a popular choice for creating an appealing appearance in everyday groceries.

How it's made

Although it exists in nature in citrus fruits and green vegetables, the version used in processed food is manufactured in a laboratory. It is synthesised through a series of controlled chemical reactions that build the complex carotenoid structure from simpler organic compounds. The process is carefully managed to ensure the final crystalline powder is highly pure and free from unwanted chemical residues. This synthetic route is favoured because extracting the pigment from natural sources in commercial quantities is inefficient and expensive.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this additive primarily as a colour. It imparts a warm, rich orange or red hue to foods that might otherwise look pale or unappealing after being heated, sterilised, or stored on shelves. It is highly valued because it resists fading when exposed to light and heat, outperforming many natural plant extracts. By using a small amount of this stable pigment, companies can ensure that their products maintain a consistent, fresh-looking appearance from the factory floor to the kitchen table.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. While it is not considered toxic, EFSA raised concerns during its re-evaluation because refined exposure estimates for toddlers and children at the high level exceeded the acceptable daily intake of 0.05 mg per kg of body weight. Because younger children eat more food relative to their small body weight, they can easily consume more than the recommended limit when eating products containing this colour. No other major safety issues or long-term risks have been established by regulators.

What the science says

European and American regulators have evaluated this colour to establish clear limits for its use. EFSA reviewed the additive in 2012 and set a strict acceptable daily intake of 0.05 mg per kg of body weight. The main focus of scientific discussion is how much of this colour children consume, as dietary models show that high consumers in younger age groups can exceed this threshold. The FDA permits its use in the United States without requiring batch certification, classifying it as a colour additive exempt from certification.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of processed foods, including processed cheeses, fats and oils, confectionery, fine bakery wares, and certain soft drinks. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically listed by its name, beta-apo-8'-carotenal, or by its E-number, E160e. On US labels, it is listed as beta-Apo-8'-carotenal. It is often used alongside other colours to achieve precise shades of yellow, orange, or red in packaged goods.
