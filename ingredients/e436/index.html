Polysorbate 65 (E436)

E436AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Polysorbate 65 is a synthetic emulsifier used to blend oil and water, but high consumption can easily push people over the recommended daily limit.

What it is

Polysorbate 65 is a synthetic food additive that belongs to a family of compounds known as polyoxyethylene sorbitan esters. It is a complex, chemically modified substance designed to help ingredients that normally repel each other, such as oil and water, mix together smoothly. While it shares some structural similarities with natural fats, it is entirely a product of laboratory synthesis and does not occur in nature.

How it's made

To manufacture this ingredient, producers start with sorbitol, a sugar alcohol derived from corn or wheat. This sorbitol is chemically reacted with ethylene oxide, a synthetic gas, to create a polyoxyethylene chain. Finally, this intermediate compound is joined with stearic acid, a fatty acid typically obtained from animal fats or vegetable oils, resulting in the final emulsifier.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this substance because it is an exceptionally stable emulsifier. It prevents oil and water from separating over long periods on supermarket shelves, maintaining a uniform texture. It is particularly valued in frozen desserts because it controls how ice crystals grow and prevents fat from clumping, ensuring a smooth, creamy texture that lasts throughout distribution.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. While the ingredient itself has no immediate toxicological effects, EFSA established a group acceptable daily intake of 25 mg per kg of body weight in 2015. During their safety review, the panel noted that high-level consumers in several population groups, especially children, exceeded this daily limit, raising concerns about cumulative exposure.

What the science says

European and American regulators have evaluated this compound thoroughly. EFSA re-evaluated the entire polysorbate group in 2015 and confirmed that while they do not pose an immediate risk, the total intake from various processed foods can easily surpass the recommended safety threshold. The FDA permits its direct use in food under specific limits to ensure consumers do not ingest excessive amounts.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of processed foods, including ice cream, frozen desserts, cakes, cake mixes, and whipped toppings. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its plain name as polysorbate 65 or by its European code, E436, and on US labels it is listed as polysorbate 65.
