Polydextrose (E1200)

E1200AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Polydextrose is a low-calorie, synthetic soluble fibre used to add bulk and texture to sugar-free and reduced-fat foods without adding sweetness.

What it is

Polydextrose is a complex carbohydrate made of randomly bonded glucose units. It behaves like a soluble dietary fibre in the human body, meaning it passes through the upper digestive tract mostly intact and is only partially fermented by the bacteria in the large intestine. Unlike regular sugar or starch, it provides very little energy, making it a popular tool for reducing calories in processed foods. It has a neutral taste and does not sweeten foods, which distinguishes it from other sugar alternatives.

How it's made

To manufacture polydextrose, producers heat a mixture of glucose, sorbitol, and a small amount of citric acid or phosphoric acid at high temperatures under a vacuum. This process causes the glucose molecules to link together in highly branched, random chains that digestive enzymes cannot easily break down. The resulting polymer is then refined, neutralised, and purified to remove any unreacted starting materials, leaving a highly soluble, cream-coloured powder that dissolves easily in water.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use polydextrose as a bulking agent to replace the physical volume and mouthfeel of sugar or fat in reformulated recipes. When sugar is removed from a product, the food loses its structure, weight, and texture. Polydextrose mimics these physical properties beautifully, helping to maintain a satisfying thickness in yoghurts, a tender crumb in baked goods, and a smooth melt in frozen desserts, all while keeping the calorie count low and the moisture locked in.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. It is generally well tolerated, but because it is a soluble fibre that undergoes fermentation in the lower gut, consuming very large amounts can lead to temporary digestive discomfort, such as bloating or gas. No daily intake limit is established by regulators because it is considered to have low toxicity, though some countries require a warning label on foods if a single serving contains a high amount of the ingredient.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated polydextrose and confirmed its safety for the general public. JECFA established that no numerical daily limit is necessary, a position shared by the European Food Safety Authority. Because the human body cannot digest the complex bonds, it does not raise blood glucose levels. Research focuses primarily on its prebiotic potential, as it serves as a food source for beneficial microbes in the gut, though excessive consumption simply leads to mild laxative effects.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of calorie-controlled, sugar-free, and high-fibre products. It is common in diet yoghurts, sugar-free syrups, meal-replacement bars, low-calorie ice creams, and sugar-reduced confectionery. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its plain name as polydextrose, but you may also see it listed under its European additive code, E1200, or occasionally as E 1200. It is a staple ingredient in modern light and keto-friendly packaged foods.
