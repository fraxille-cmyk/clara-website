Polydextrose (E1200)

E1200AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Polydextrose is a synthetic, low-calorie carbohydrate used to add bulk and fibre to foods, and EFSA did not specify an acceptable daily intake in 2020.

What it is

Polydextrose is a synthetic carbohydrate that acts as a soluble dietary fibre. It is a complex molecule made of randomly bonded glucose units, which means your digestive enzymes cannot easily break it down. Because it passes through the upper digestive tract mostly intact, it provides very few calories compared to regular sugars and starches. It is a versatile ingredient that mimics the texture, mouthfeel, and bulk of sugar and fat without the sweetness, making it a popular choice in modern food formulation.

How it's made

To make polydextrose, manufacturers heat glucose in the presence of sorbitol and a small amount of citric acid or phosphoric acid as a catalyst. This process causes the glucose molecules to polymerise, linking together in highly branched, random chains. The resulting mixture is then purified to remove any unreacted starting materials and by-products. The final ingredient is a highly soluble, white to off-white powder that dissolves easily in water to form a clear, stable solution.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use polydextrose primarily as a bulking agent and texturiser. When sugar or fat is removed from a recipe to lower the calorie count, the food loses its physical structure and satisfying mouthfeel. Polydextrose steps in to replace that lost volume and weight, ensuring that low-calorie foods still feel substantial and satisfying to eat. It also helps retain moisture, prevents foods from drying out, and functions as a soluble fibre to improve the nutritional profile of processed foods.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. It is generally well tolerated by the human body because it behaves like natural dietary fibre. Because it is not fully digested, consuming very large amounts in a single sitting can sometimes lead to mild digestive effects like bloating or gas, but this is typical of any high-fibre food. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the substance in 2020 and concluded that there is no safety concern, meaning no upper limit is established for daily intake.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated polydextrose and confirmed its safety. The Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives reviewed it and established that it does not pose a risk to human health. In 2020, the European Food Safety Authority confirmed that an acceptable daily intake does not need to be specified because the substance is of such low concern. Researchers have shown that it passes to the large intestine where it is partially fermented by beneficial gut bacteria, behaving just like natural soluble fibres found in fruit and vegetables.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide range of reduced-calorie, sugar-free, and high-fibre products. It is common in light yoghurts, sugar-free ice creams, diet desserts, protein bars, breakfast cereals, and baked goods. On ingredient lists, it is usually declared plainly as polydextrose, though it may also appear under its European code E1200 or simply as E 1200. In the United States, it is permitted as a multipurpose food additive and is listed on labels as polydextrose.
