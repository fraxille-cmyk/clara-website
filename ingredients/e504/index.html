Magnesium carbonates (E504)

E504AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Magnesium carbonates are benign mineral additives used to regulate acidity and prevent clumping in dry foods, with no established daily limit.

What it is

Magnesium carbonates are naturally occurring mineral compounds that you might recognise in other forms, such as the chalk used by gymnasts and rock climbers to keep their hands dry. In the food world, they exist as a fine, white, odourless powder. They are closely related to other common mineral carbonates, like calcium carbonate, which is found in chalk and limestone. Because they are simple mineral salts, they dissolve easily in the stomach, where they break down into magnesium and carbonate ions, both of which are already abundant in our bodies.

How it's made

While these minerals exist in nature as magnesite, the food-grade version is manufactured to ensure high purity. It is typically produced by reacting soluble magnesium salts, such as magnesium chloride, with sodium carbonate in an aqueous solution. This reaction causes the magnesium carbonate to precipitate out as a solid. The resulting powder is then thoroughly filtered, washed with water to remove any leftover salts, dried, and milled into an extremely fine powder that is ready for use in food processing.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this ingredient for two main technical reasons. First, it is an excellent anti-caking agent. It absorbs moisture and coats individual particles, which prevents powders from clumping together and ensures they flow smoothly out of packets. Second, it acts as an acidity regulator. It helps control the pH level of foods, which is crucial for maintaining a consistent flavour, stabilising colours, and ensuring that other ingredients perform as expected during baking or processing.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. This mineral compound is used to control acidity and prevent caking, and has no specified acceptable daily intake due to its safe profile. Because our bodies readily absorb and utilise magnesium, and because any excess is naturally excreted, regulators have found no reason to set a restrictive daily limit. It behaves just like the magnesium we get from eating leafy greens, nuts, and seeds, posing no safety concerns for the general population when consumed in normal dietary amounts.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated this mineral and found no toxicological concerns. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the additive and confirmed that there is no need to establish a numerical acceptable daily intake. The US Food and Drug Administration also recognises it as generally safe for use in food. Because magnesium is an essential nutrient that our bodies require for hundreds of daily biochemical reactions, the small amounts added to processed foods are processed safely by our normal metabolic pathways.

Where you'll find it

You will frequently find this ingredient in dry, powdered products that need to remain free-flowing, such as table salt, icing sugar, milk powders, and dry soup mixes. It is also common in cocoa products, canned peas, and various bakery items. On ingredient lists in the UK and Europe, it is typically declared by its name or as E504, whilst on US labels it is often listed as magnesium carbonate.
