Succinic acid (E363)

E363AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Succinic acid is a natural organic acid used to control acidity in food, which regulators consider entirely safe with no specified daily intake limit.

What it is

Succinic acid is a naturally occurring organic acid that plays a fundamental role in how living cells generate energy. It is found in almost all plants and animals, and it occurs naturally in common foods like broccoli, rhubarb, cheese, and fermented beverages. In the food industry, it is used primarily to control acidity and to add a mild, slightly salty and sour taste that enhances other flavours in a recipe.

How it's made

While succinic acid can be extracted from natural sources or produced through the fermentation of sugars by specific bacteria, the version used in processed foods is typically synthetic. It is manufactured through the chemical hydrogenation of maleic acid or maleic anhydride, which are derived from petroleum. This chemical process yields a highly purified, crystalline white powder that is identical to the acid found in nature.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use succinic acid because it is an exceptionally stable acidifier that does not easily absorb moisture from the air. This makes it ideal for dry powder mixes, such as instant desserts, powdered drinks, and cake mixes, where other acids might cause clumping. It also acts as a flavour enhancer, smoothing out harsh tastes and helping to blend different flavour notes together more harmoniously than citric or malic acid can on their own.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because succinic acid is a normal substance that your body already produces and metabolises every day, it is processed easily and safely. EFSA evaluated its safety and concluded that it poses no safety concerns, establishing no specified daily intake limit. The FDA also affirms its safety, meaning it can be used freely in foods without a restrictive upper limit.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated succinic acid and found no evidence of adverse effects from its use in food. Because it is an intermediate molecule in the citric acid cycle, which is the primary energy-producing pathway in human cells, the body handles dietary succinic acid using existing, highly efficient metabolic pathways. No safety concerns or toxicological issues have been identified by international food safety authorities.

Where you'll find it

You will find succinic acid in dry powder mixes, instant soups, dessert toppings, bakery products, and some savoury snacks. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its name as succinic acid or by its European code, E363. On US labels, it is listed as succinic acid, where it is recognised as a safe substance for direct addition to food.
