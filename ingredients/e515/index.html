Potassium sulphates (E515)

E515AdditiveNeutral · benign

Potassium sulphates are mineral salts used to control acidity in food, rated neutral by Clara as they are considered safe with no daily limit.

What it is

Potassium sulphates are mineral salts that occur naturally in volcanic rocks and salt lakes. In the food world, they are used to manage acidity and provide essential minerals. They are closely related to the common potassium salts found in mineral waters and everyday table salt substitutes, offering a simple way to balance the mineral profile of processed foods.

How it's made

These salts are typically produced by reacting potassium chloride, a natural mineral salt, with sulphuric acid. This reaction yields a highly purified crystalline powder. Alternatively, they can be harvested directly from natural salt deposits and purified through a process of crystallisation, ensuring that no chemical modifications are made to the mineral structure itself.

Why manufacturers use it

Food producers rely on this ingredient to control acidity and stabilise the texture of packaged foods. It acts as an efficient acidity regulator, keeping the pH of a product stable over its shelf life. It also serves as a yeast food in brewing and baking, and can replace sodium in low-sodium products without sacrificing the structural integrity of the food.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. This mineral salt is used to control acidity in processed foods, and EFSA has not specified an acceptable daily intake due to its safe mineral origin. Because our bodies are well-equipped to process potassium and sulphate ions from our regular diet, regulators have found no reason to set a restrictive daily limit on its consumption.

What the science says

The safety of this mineral has been thoroughly evaluated by major food authorities. EFSA reviewed the substance and concluded that it poses no safety concerns, deciding that establishing a numerical acceptable daily intake was unnecessary. The FDA also recognises it as generally recognised as safe, noting that the body metabolises these minerals in the same way it handles potassium and sulphate from natural dietary sources.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of packaged goods, including bottled waters, beer, bakery products, and low-sodium salt alternatives. On a label, it is typically listed as potassium sulphates, potassium sulphate, potassium hydrogen sulphate, or by its European code, E515. On US labels it is listed as potassium sulfate.
