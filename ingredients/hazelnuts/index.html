Hazelnuts

Whole foodGood

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Hazelnuts are a nutritious whole food, packed with healthy fats, fibre, and essential minerals, making them an excellent addition to your diet.

What it is

Hazelnuts are the edible seeds of the hazel tree, which belongs to the birch family. These small, round, brown-skinned nuts have a rich, buttery flavour and a satisfying crunch. They are a traditional staple of temperate forests across Europe and Western Asia. In the kitchen, they are enjoyed either raw, roasted, or ground into a paste, and they are celebrated for their natural abundance of beneficial fats, dietary fibre, and essential micronutrients like vitamin E and manganese.

How it's made

Hazelnuts are harvested in the autumn when the nuts fall naturally from the trees. Growers gather the fallen nuts from the orchard floor and clean them to remove leaves, twigs, and soil. The nuts are then dried to reduce their moisture content, which helps preserve their quality and prevents spoilage. Depending on how they will be sold, the hard outer shells are cracked open to release the kernels, which may then be blanched to remove their bitter skins, roasted to enhance their flavour, or left entirely natural.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose hazelnuts because they provide a rich, distinctive flavour and a pleasant texture that is difficult to replicate with artificial ingredients. They add a premium appeal to products and pair exceptionally well with chocolate and dairy. Beyond their sensory qualities, hazelnuts are highly valued for their natural oil content, which helps create smooth, spreadable textures in nut butters and confectionery fillings without the need for hydrogenated vegetable oils or chemical emulsifiers.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As a whole food, this ingredient is entirely natural and contains no added chemicals or artificial preservatives. It is a wholesome source of nutrients that supports a balanced diet. However, because hazelnuts are tree nuts, they are a major allergen, and individuals with a tree nut allergy must avoid them completely to prevent severe allergic reactions.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have evaluated hazelnuts as a whole food rather than a food additive. The European Food Safety Authority has reviewed the nutritional profile of nuts, noting their high content of unsaturated fatty acids and dietary fibre. No regulatory body has established an upper limit for their consumption, as they are a traditional food with a long history of safe use. Researchers continue to study how the natural fats in nuts interact with our metabolism, consistently finding that they support cardiovascular health when eaten as part of a balanced diet.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of sweet products, including chocolate bars, praline fillings, chocolate-nut spreads, muesli, and granola. On ingredient labels, they may be listed simply as hazelnuts, hazelnut, or blanched hazelnuts. Depending on where the product was packaged, you might also spot them under their European names, such as noisettes, nocciole, haselnüsse, or avellanas.
