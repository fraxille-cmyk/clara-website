Fatty acid esters of ascorbic acid (ascorbyl palmitate) (E304)

E304AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

A fat-soluble form of vitamin C used as an antioxidant to stop oils from spoiling, rated NEUTRAL by Clara as a limited-concern additive.

What it is

Fatty acid esters of ascorbic acid, commonly known as ascorbyl palmitate, are a clever combination of vitamin C and a fat. While normal vitamin C dissolves easily in water, it cannot dissolve in fats and oils where oxidation is a constant threat. By attaching a fatty acid to the vitamin molecule, chemists created a fat-soluble version. This allows the protective antioxidant powers of vitamin C to work directly inside oily foods, keeping them fresh and preventing them from turning rancid when exposed to air.

How it's made

This ingredient is synthetic and chemically modified, meaning it does not exist in this exact form in nature. It is produced in a laboratory or industrial facility by joining ascorbic acid, which is vitamin C, with palmitic acid, a common fatty acid typically derived from vegetable oils. This chemical reaction, known as esterification, bonds the two molecules together. The resulting substance is then purified, crystallised, and dried into a white or yellowish powder that can be easily blended into oils and fats.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this ingredient because it is an exceptionally effective antioxidant for fats and oils. When oxygen reacts with fats, it causes them to break down, leading to unpleasant off-flavours and stale smells. Because this additive dissolves perfectly in lipids, it acts as a shield, absorbing oxygen before it can damage the food. It is highly valued because it remains stable during high-temperature processing, outperforming natural vitamin C and allowing products to stay on supermarket shelves much longer without spoiling.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is based on its synthetic origin and its established numeric acceptable daily intake of eighty milligrams per kilogram of body weight. Once you eat it, your body easily breaks it down into its original parts, which are vitamin C and a common dietary fat, both of which are processed naturally by your digestive system. Because it behaves so much like normal nutrients in the body, it is considered a very low-risk option for preserving food.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this substance and found no safety concerns at normal consumption levels. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the additive and established a clear daily limit of eighty milligrams per kilogram of body weight. In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration recognises it as generally safe for use in food. Because it breaks down into harmless, everyday nutrients during digestion, scientists agree that it does not accumulate in the body or cause adverse reactions when consumed within the recommended limits.

Where you'll find it

You will frequently spot this ingredient in oil-heavy foods such as margarine, vegetable oils, salad dressings, and infant formulas. It is also common in potato crisps, instant noodle packets, and various baked goods where fats need protection from going stale. On ingredient lists, it can appear under several names, including fatty acid esters of ascorbic acid, ascorbyl palmitate, or simply as E304. On US labels, it is often listed as ascorbyl palmitate, while European packaging may use its E-number or its full chemical name to indicate its presence.
