Propane-1,2-diol esters of fatty acids (E477)

E477AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

A synthetic emulsifier that helps oil and water blend in processed foods, with a safe daily limit established by European food safety authorities.

What it is

Propane-1,2-diol esters of fatty acids is a synthetic food additive used to blend ingredients that would naturally separate. It is a combination of propylene glycol, which is a clear and slightly sweet liquid, and fatty acids that typically come from vegetable oils or animal fats. By joining these two components, chemists create a substance that can hold oil and water together in a smooth, uniform mixture. It is similar in function to natural emulsifiers like lecithin but is tailored in a laboratory to perform under specific manufacturing conditions.

How it's made

This ingredient is produced through a chemical reaction between propane-1,2-diol, also known as propylene glycol, and natural fatty acids. These fatty acids are usually derived from edible fats and oils, such as palm oil, coconut oil, or soybean oil. The mixture is heated under controlled conditions, often in the presence of a catalyst, to encourage the molecules to bond. After the reaction is complete, the resulting mixture is refined, neutralised, and purified to remove any unreacted starting materials, leaving behind a consistent, waxy substance ready for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on this additive because it is an exceptionally stable emulsifier, particularly in whipped products. It helps to aerate batters and creams, ensuring they rise evenly and maintain their light, fluffy texture over a long shelf life. In baking, it improves the volume of cakes and keeps the crumb soft. It is highly valued because it performs consistently across a wide range of temperatures and processing conditions, making it more reliable and cost-effective for large-scale industrial food production than many natural alternatives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This synthetic emulsifier helps ingredients blend together, and it has been assigned a numeric acceptable daily intake of eighty milligrams per kilogram of body weight. EFSA established this limit in its comprehensive review, noting that the substance is broken down in the body into normal dietary fats and propylene glycol, which the body then processes and excretes without difficulty. There is no indication of accumulation in the body, and no toxicological concern has been identified when it is consumed within the established daily limit.

What the science says

The safety of this additive has been thoroughly evaluated by major food safety authorities. EFSA re-evaluated the substance in 2018 and confirmed its safety profile, setting the acceptable daily intake at eighty milligrams per kilogram of body weight. The evaluation concluded that the compound is efficiently metabolised by the body. In the United States, the FDA permits its use as a direct food additive under specific regulations. Scientists have found no evidence of adverse effects from normal dietary exposure, and there are no open safety concerns or outstanding data gaps in its regulatory assessments.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of processed foods, particularly whipped toppings, cake mixes, commercial margarines, instant desserts, and bakery products. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically listed as propane-1,2-diol esters of fatty acids or by its E-number, E477. On US labels, it is often declared as propylene glycol mono- and diesters of fats and fatty acids. It is frequently used alongside other emulsifiers to achieve the perfect texture in convenience foods.
