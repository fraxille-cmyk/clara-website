Quinoa

Whole foodGood

Quinoa is a highly nutritious, gluten-free seed eaten like a grain, offering a complete profile of essential amino acids and dietary fibre.

What it is

Quinoa is a flowering plant in the amaranth family, grown primarily for its edible seeds. Though often referred to as a grain because of how we cook and eat it, it is actually a pseudocereal, meaning it is a seed that behaves like a cereal grain. It has been cultivated in the Andean region of South America for thousands of years. It comes in several varieties, including white, red, and black, and is naturally gluten-free, making it a popular alternative to wheat and barley.

How it's made

Quinoa is harvested from the fields once the plants reach maturity and the seeds dry on the stalk. The seeds are threshed from the plant heads and cleaned to remove debris. Crucially, the seeds must undergo a thorough washing or mechanical polishing process to remove saponins, which are naturally occurring bitter compounds on the outer coating that protect the plant from pests. Once dried again, the clean seeds are packaged as a whole food or milled into flour.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use quinoa to add texture, colour, and a mild, nutty flavour to products. It is highly valued in gluten-free formulations because it provides structure and moisture retention that other gluten-free starches lack. It also boosts the nutritional profile of packaged foods, allowing manufacturers to offer products with a higher protein and fibre content. Its ability to absorb water and swell makes it an excellent base for ready-to-eat meals, salads, and breakfast cereals.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As a whole, single-ingredient food, quinoa is a highly nutritious addition to your diet with no safety concerns or additive classifications. No limit is established for its consumption because it is a natural food rather than a chemical additive. The natural bitter saponins on the seed coat are thoroughly washed away during commercial processing, ensuring the final product is gentle on your digestive system and completely wholesome to eat.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have evaluated quinoa as a nutrient-dense food source rather than an additive. It is notable for containing all nine essential amino acids, which the human body cannot synthesise on its own, making it a complete protein. Researchers have confirmed its high digestibility and its abundance of dietary fibre, minerals, and antioxidants. Because it is a whole food, regulators do not set acceptable daily intakes, and its consumption is widely encouraged as part of a balanced diet.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in the grain aisle as whole white quinoa, red quinoa, or tricolour quinoa. It is also a common ingredient in gluten-free pastas, crackers, breads, and baby foods. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared simply as quinoa, quinua, or quinoa flour, with no E-number associated with it because it is a whole food rather than a food additive.
