Potassium phosphates (E340)

E340AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Potassium phosphates are synthetic mineral salts used to stabilise food texture, but some people may consume more than the recommended daily limit.

What it is

Potassium phosphates are a family of synthetic mineral salts that combine potassium and phosphorus. In nature, phosphorus is an essential mineral found in many foods, but these manufactured versions are highly concentrated and behave differently in processed products. They exist in three main forms, known as monopotassium, dipotassium, and tripotassium phosphate, which differ slightly in their chemical structure but perform similar roles in food. You will often find them in processed items where they help maintain a consistent texture and appearance.

How it's made

These mineral salts are produced through a controlled chemical reaction in an industrial setting. Manufacturers neutralise phosphoric acid, which is typically derived from mined phosphate rock, using a potassium source such as potassium hydroxide. This reaction creates a liquid solution that is then purified, crystallised, and dried into a fine white powder. The resulting substance is highly soluble in water and ready to be blended into various food formulations to manage acidity and moisture.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on these salts because they are incredibly versatile and cost-effective. They act as excellent acidity regulators, keeping the pH of a food product stable so it does not spoil or change colour prematurely. In dairy alternatives and powdered drinks, they prevent proteins from clumping together and settling at the bottom, ensuring a smooth and appealing texture. They also help processed meats retain water, which keeps them juicy during storage and transport, outperforming natural alternatives in both stability and price.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. While the human body needs phosphorus, these added synthetic phosphates are absorbed much more efficiently than natural ones, which can easily lead to an excess. EFSA established a group acceptable daily intake of 40 mg per kg of body weight in 2019, but the authority warned that some population groups may exceed this limit through their normal diet. Because of this potential for overconsumption, Clara advises choosing products without these added salts where possible.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated these additives. In 2019, EFSA published a comprehensive re-evaluation of phosphates, noting that total dietary exposure might exceed the safe threshold for highly exposed populations. Because of this, the panel recommended introducing maximum limits for total phosphorus in food supplements. In the United States, the FDA permits their use and considers them generally recognised as safe, but researchers continue to monitor how high cumulative intake of inorganic phosphorus affects mineral balance in the human body.

Where you'll find it

You will frequently spot these additives in ultra-processed foods, including instant noodle packets, powdered soups, non-dairy coffee creamers, processed cheeses, and sports drinks. On a label, they are usually listed by their specific names, such as monopotassium phosphate, dipotassium phosphate, or tripotassium phosphate. They may also appear under the collective name potassium phosphates or simply as the additive code E340.
