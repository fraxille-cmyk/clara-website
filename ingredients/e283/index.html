Potassium propionate (E283)

E283AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Potassium propionate is a synthetic preservative used to prevent mould in bread and baked goods, with no established daily intake limit.

What it is

Potassium propionate is a synthetic salt created from propionic acid, which is a naturally occurring organic acid. In nature, propionic acid is produced in small amounts during the fermentation of certain foods, such as Swiss cheese, and even by the friendly bacteria living in our own digestive systems. In the food industry, however, this substance is produced in a laboratory setting to create a highly stable, consistent powder that can be easily mixed into large batches of dough.

How it's made

To manufacture this ingredient, chemical producers neutralise propionic acid using potassium hydroxide. The propionic acid itself is typically synthesised from petrochemical starting materials, such as ethylene and carbon monoxide, through a series of industrial chemical reactions. Once the acid and the potassium compound react, the resulting solution is purified, crystallised, and dried into a fine white powder that is ready to be shipped to commercial bakeries.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this ingredient primarily as a preservative to extend the shelf life of baked goods. It is highly effective at stopping the growth of mould and certain bacteria that cause bread to spoil or become stringy. Unlike some other preservatives, it does not interfere with the yeast needed to make bread rise, allowing bakers to produce soft, long-lasting loaves on an industrial scale without altering the rising process or the final texture of the loaf.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects its status as a synthetic preservative that the body processes easily, much like the natural acids produced during normal digestion. Because it is highly soluble and metabolised efficiently, regulators have not found a reason to restrict its use. No limit is established for its daily intake, meaning it is considered safe to consume in the quantities typically found in everyday food products.

What the science says

European and American food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this compound and its sister propionates. EFSA re-evaluated the additive and confirmed that it behaves just like natural fatty acids in the human body, being broken down and used for energy without leaving harmful residues. The FDA also recognises it as generally safe for its intended use. There are no outstanding safety concerns or open questions regarding its use in human food.

Where you'll find it

You will find this preservative almost exclusively in bakery products, including sliced bread, tortillas, buns, pastries, and pre-packaged cakes. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its full name as potassium propionate, or by its European code, E283. In some countries, it might be grouped under the general term preservatives, followed by its name or number in brackets, helping to keep packaged baked goods fresh on supermarket shelves for days.
