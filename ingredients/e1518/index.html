Glyceryl triacetate (triacetin) (E1518)

E1518AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Glyceryl triacetate, or triacetin, is a synthetic liquid used to dissolve and carry food flavours, rated neutral by Clara with no safety concerns.

What it is

Glyceryl triacetate, commonly known as triacetin, is a clear, odourless, and oily liquid. It is a compound made by combining glycerol, which is a natural alcohol found in fats, with acetic acid, the main component of vinegar. While these building blocks exist in nature, the ingredient itself is a synthetic creation designed to help other ingredients mix smoothly into food. It behaves much like a very light, stable oil that does not easily spoil or go rancid when exposed to air.

How it's made

To manufacture this ingredient, chemical producers react high-purity glycerol with acetic acid or acetic anhydride. This process, known as esterification, links three acetic acid units to a single glycerol molecule. The mixture is then heated and refined through distillation to remove any leftover acids, water, or impurities. The result is a highly purified, synthetic liquid that is completely uniform in quality, free from natural variations, and ready to be used as a stable carrier in food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this liquid primarily as a carrier and solvent for concentrated flavourings and colourings. Many intense flavours do not dissolve well in water or standard vegetable oils. This ingredient bridges the gap, holding the flavour molecules in a stable solution so they disperse evenly throughout a batch of food. It also acts as a humectant, which helps keep moist foods from drying out, and it prevents ingredients from sticking together during processing, making it highly valued in industrial baking and confectionery.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects its status as a synthetic compound that the body processes easily and safely. When consumed, your digestive enzymes break it down into glycerol and acetate, two completely normal substances that your body uses for energy every day. Because it is processed so naturally and possesses no toxicological concern, EFSA has evaluated it as safe and determined that no upper limit is established for its daily intake.

What the science says

Scientific panels have thoroughly evaluated this compound and found no reason for concern. The European Food Safety Authority completed a full re-evaluation of the substance, confirming its safety and maintaining that an acceptable daily intake limit does not need to be specified. In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration has long designated it as generally recognised as safe. Because it breaks down into simple, everyday nutrients in the digestive tract, researchers have found no evidence of accumulation or unwanted activity in the body.

Where you'll find it

You will most often find this ingredient in highly flavoured processed foods, such as chewing gum, sweets, baked goods, and soft drinks, where it helps distribute the flavouring agents. On an ingredient list, it can appear under several names, including glyceryl triacetate, triacetin, or its European code, E1518. On US labels it is listed as triacetin or glyceryl triacetate, and it is frequently used behind the scenes in flavour mixtures without appearing on the final ingredient panel.
