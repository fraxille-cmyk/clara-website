Xanthan gum (E415)

E415AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Xanthan gum is a soluble fibre made by bacteria, used to thicken and stabilise foods; Clara rates it neutral as it is considered safe with no daily limit.

What it is

Xanthan gum is a soluble fibre that acts as a powerful thickening and stabilising agent in food. In its natural state, it is a complex carbohydrate, similar to the starches and fibres found in plants. It is named after the specific bacterium used to create it, which is the same organism responsible for creating the dark spots on broccoli and cauliflower. When mixed with liquid, it quickly disperses and creates a viscous, smooth texture that helps hold ingredients together.

How it's made

This ingredient is produced through a natural fermentation process. Manufacturers feed simple sugars, usually derived from corn, wheat, or soy, to the bacterium Xanthomonas campestris. The bacteria consume the sugars and ferment them, producing a sticky, fibrous substance. This liquid broth is then pasteurised to kill the bacteria, and the fibrous material is precipitated out using an alcohol. Finally, it is washed, dried, and ground into a fine, cream-coloured powder that dissolves easily in liquids.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on this ingredient because it is incredibly efficient at small doses. It prevents oil and water from separating, which keeps salad dressings creamy and emulsified. It also suspends solid particles, ensuring that spices in a sauce do not settle to the bottom of the bottle. In gluten-free baking, it plays a crucial role by mimicking the elastic texture of gluten, trapping air bubbles so that breads and cakes can rise and hold their structure without crumbling.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is a soluble fibre, your body does not digest or absorb it; instead, it passes through your digestive system largely unchanged. EFSA evaluated xanthan gum in 2017 and determined that no limit is established for its daily intake, meaning it is considered safe for the general public even in large amounts. It has no toxicological concerns, though eating very large quantities might occasionally lead to temporary bloating or mild digestive gas.

What the science says

Scientific panels have thoroughly assessed this substance over several decades. EFSA re-evaluated the ingredient in 2017 and confirmed that it does not pose any safety concerns as a food additive, noting that it behaves like other dietary fibres in the gut. The FDA also permits its direct use in food without restrictive limits. Because it is not absorbed into the bloodstream, researchers agree it does not interfere with internal organs, and the main area of study remains its gentle, fibre-like behaviour in the digestive tract.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a vast range of supermarket products, especially gluten-free baked goods, salad dressings, sauces, ice creams, and low-fat spreads. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its plain name as xanthan gum, but you may also see it listed as E415 or E 415 on European packaging. It is highly valued in liquid products because it maintains its thickness across a wide range of temperatures, from freezing in the freezer to hot on the stove.
