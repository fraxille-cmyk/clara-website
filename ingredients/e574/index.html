Gluconic acid (E574)

E574AdditiveNeutral · benign

Gluconic acid is a mild, naturally occurring organic acid used to control acidity in foods, rated neutral by Clara as it is considered highly safe.

What it is

Gluconic acid is a mild organic acid that occurs naturally in honey, fruit, and wine. It is closely related to glucose, the simple sugar your body uses for energy, and is actually formed when glucose is oxidised. In food, it acts as a gentle acidity regulator, helping to balance flavours and keep minerals dissolved in liquid rather than settling out as sediment. Because it has a very clean, refreshing taste that is less sharp than citric acid, it is a useful tool for food makers who want to adjust tartness without overpowering other delicate flavours.

How it's made

To make gluconic acid on a commercial scale, manufacturers mimic a natural fermentation process. They start with a glucose sugar solution, which is typically derived from corn starch. They then introduce a specific, safe strain of microbe, such as the fungus Aspergillus niger, which naturally produces enzymes that oxidise the glucose into gluconic acid. After the fermentation is complete, the mixture is carefully filtered, purified, and concentrated into a clear, syrupy liquid or crystallised into a white powder, ready to be added to food.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use gluconic acid primarily to manage acidity and maintain a stable pH in their products. It is highly valued because it has a unique ability to bind with metal ions, which prevents mineral precipitation and stops cloudy sediment from forming in bottled drinks. It also acts as a gentle leavening agent and helps to preserve the texture of processed fruits and vegetables. Because its sourness is slow to develop and has no bitter aftertaste, it helps create a smooth, balanced flavour profile that is difficult to achieve with stronger acids.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. This ingredient is processed by the body in much the same way as ordinary carbohydrates, and it is considered exceptionally safe. Because it is so easily metabolised and has no known unwanted effects, the Scientific Committee on Food established no specified acceptable daily intake limit for it in 1990. It is simply treated as a normal, non-toxic food component that your body can use for energy without any special difficulty or accumulation.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated gluconic acid and its salts, concluding that they pose no safety concerns. The Scientific Committee on Food assessed its safety and determined that no numerical daily limit is necessary. JECFA, the joint expert committee of the FAO and WHO, also reviewed the substance and confirmed it is safe for use in food. Because it is a natural metabolite in human carbohydrate chemistry, the body easily breaks it down into water and carbon dioxide, leaving no residues or cause for concern.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of processed foods, including baked goods, dairy products, canned vegetables, and soft drinks. On food labels, it is usually listed by its plain name as gluconic acid, but it may also appear under its European code, E574, or simply E 574. In the United States, it is recognised as a safe substance and is often declared on ingredient lists as gluconic acid in accordance with federal regulations.
