Acerola cherry powder

Whole foodGood

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Acerola cherry powder is a natural, nutrient-rich whole food used to boost vitamin C and keep packaged foods looking fresh.

What it is

Acerola cherry powder comes from the bright red fruit of a shrub native to tropical regions of the Americas. Often called the Barbados cherry or West Indian cherry, this fruit is famous for its exceptionally high concentration of natural vitamin C, which far exceeds that of oranges. Because the fresh cherries are delicate and spoil quickly after harvest, they are commonly turned into a fine, pale pink or yellow powder. This allows the fruit to be stored easily while preserving its rich nutritional profile, making it a popular choice for clean-label products.

How it's made

The journey starts in tropical orchards where the cherries are harvested while still slightly green, as this is when their vitamin C content peaks. The fruits are washed, pressed to extract the juice, and then concentrated. To turn this liquid into a stable powder, it is typically spray-dried, a process where the juice is misted into a heated chamber to evaporate the water instantly. Sometimes a natural carrier like manioc starch is added to keep the powder flowing freely and stop it from clumping together into a solid block during storage.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers love this powder because it does two jobs at once. First, it acts as a natural antioxidant, which stops oxygen from spoiling fats and keeps vibrant colours from fading on the shelf. Second, it allows brands to enrich their products with vitamin C without using synthetic additives. By using a whole-food ingredient, manufacturers can appeal to shoppers looking for simple, recognisable ingredients on the back of the pack, replacing chemical preservatives with a familiar fruit.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As a simple, dried fruit powder, it is considered a wholesome food ingredient rather than a chemical additive. There are no safety concerns or artificial limits associated with its use in everyday food products. Because it is a natural source of vitamin C, the body recognises and metabolises it just like eating fresh fruit. It is an excellent option for those who want to steer clear of synthetic preservatives and artificial fortifying agents.

What the science says

Regulators view this ingredient as a traditional food rather than a regulated food additive. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration accept it as a safe, natural source of nutrients. Scientific evaluations focus on its high antioxidant activity, which helps protect cells from oxidative stress. Because it is a whole food, no daily intake limit is established, and research shows that the natural vitamin C in the powder is highly bioavailable, meaning the body absorbs and uses it efficiently.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of products, especially organic fruit juices, smoothies, baby foods, and breakfast cereals. It is also common in gluten-free breads and baked goods, where it helps improve the rise of the dough. On packaging, it is usually listed simply as acerola cherry powder, acerola extract, or acerola juice powder. Since it is a whole food rather than a synthetic additive, it does not have an E-number, making it a favourite choice for clean-label brands that want to keep their ingredient lists simple and appealing.
