Carrageenan (E407)

E407AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Carrageenan is a seaweed-derived thickener that Clara rates AVOID due to concerns about gut irritation and inflammation.

What it is

Carrageenan is a natural substance extracted from red seaweeds, which are often called Irish moss. It has been used for centuries in traditional cooking to thicken puddings and broths. Chemically, it is a complex mixture of carbohydrates that forms a gel when mixed with water or milk. It behaves similarly to gelatin but is entirely plant-based, making it a popular choice for vegetarian and vegan products that need a smooth, stable texture.

How it's made

To make carrageenan, red seaweed is harvested, washed, and boiled in an alkaline solution. This process breaks down the seaweed cells and releases the soluble carbohydrates. The mixture is then filtered to remove any solid plant debris. The dissolved carrageenan is concentrated, dried into sheets or powder, and milled into a fine, cream-coloured flour that dissolves easily in warm liquids during food manufacturing.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use carrageenan because it is an incredibly efficient thickener and stabiliser. It binds tightly with milk proteins, which makes it perfect for keeping chocolate milk from separating or giving dairy-free milks a creamy mouthfeel. It also prevents ice crystals from forming in frozen desserts and helps processed meats retain water so they stay juicy. It is cheap, highly effective in small amounts, and performs better than many starch-based alternatives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. While regulators consider it acceptable for general use, it is under ongoing scrutiny because some studies suggest it can cause irritation and inflammation in the gut lining. Because it is easy to avoid and adds no nutritional value to your food, choosing products without it is a sensible step if you want to support your digestive comfort.

What the science says

The safety of carrageenan has been evaluated by several international bodies. EFSA re-evaluated the additive and noted that while it is authorised, there are gaps in the data regarding how it behaves in the human digestive tract and whether it can trigger inflammatory responses. JECFA has also reviewed its safety, particularly concerning its use in infant formula, and maintains a cautious approach. No daily intake limit has been established, but researchers continue to investigate its impact on the gut barrier.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in dairy products like whipping cream, chocolate milk, cottage cheese, and ice cream, as well as plant-based alternatives like soy milk, almond milk, and coconut milk. It is also common in deli meats, canned soups, and ready meals. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared as carrageenan, carrageenan gum, or by its European code, E407.
