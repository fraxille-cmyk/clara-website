Isomalt (E953)

E953SweetenerNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✕ Not low-FODMAP
  • Not low-FODMAP — isomalt is a polyol.

Isomalt is a sugar-free sweetener and bulking agent made from sugar beet that is generally fine but can cause mild digestive discomfort in large amounts.

What it is

Isomalt is a sugar alcohol, or polyol, which is a type of carbohydrate used to replace sugar. It looks and behaves very much like regular table sugar, but it has only half the calories and does not raise blood glucose levels in the same way. While it occurs naturally in tiny quantities in sugar cane and sugar beet, the version we find in packaged foods is a modified substance designed to provide sweetness without the rapid energy spike of traditional sugars.

How it's made

The journey to making this ingredient begins with natural sucrose, which is typically extracted from sugar beet. Manufacturers use enzymes to reorganise the chemical bonds of this sugar, turning it into an intermediate substance called isomaltulose. This intermediate is then treated with hydrogen in the presence of a metal catalyst. This process of hydrogenation transforms the molecules into a highly stable mixture of sugar alcohols, which is then crystallised into a white, odourless powder.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose this ingredient because it mimics the physical bulk, texture, and mouthfeel of sugar without the moisture-absorbing properties. It does not caramelise or brown under high heat, which makes it incredibly popular for hard candies, pulled sugar sculptures, and baked goods that need to stay crisp. It also provides a clean, sweet taste that is about half as sweet as sugar, allowing it to mask the bitter aftertaste of stronger artificial sweeteners.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. It is not absorbed well by the small intestine, meaning most of it passes into the large intestine where resident bacteria ferment it. This fermentation process can lead to gas, bloating, and a laxative effect if you consume too much of it at once. No limit is established for daily intake because regulators consider it to have a very low potential for harm, but sensitive individuals may want to monitor how much they eat.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated this sweetener and found no toxicological concern. The Scientific Committee on Food established that no daily limit is specified, meaning it can be used freely in foods according to good manufacturing practice. Because our bodies do not metabolise it like regular sugar, it does not promote the acid production by oral bacteria that damages tooth enamel, making it a popular choice for supporting dental health.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of sugar-free and calorie-reduced products, especially hard boiled sweets, chewing gum, chocolates, baked goods, and throat lozenges. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically declared by its name as isomalt or under its additive code E953, while on US labels it is simply listed as isomalt.
