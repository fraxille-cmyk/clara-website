Isomalt (E953)

E953SweetenerAvoid · moderate concern

Isomalt is a sugar-free sweetener made from beet sugar that can cause digestive discomfort and laxative effects when eaten in large amounts.

What it is

Isomalt is a sugar alcohol, also known as a polyol, which is used to replace sugar in food. It is a white, crystalline substance that looks and tastes very much like regular sugar, but it has only about half the calories. Unlike sugar, it does not raise blood glucose levels quickly, and it does not feed the bacteria in the mouth that damage teeth. It provides a clean, sweet taste without any bitter aftertaste, making it a popular choice for sugar-free products.

How it's made

Although it originates from natural sugar beets, isomalt is a highly processed, synthetic ingredient. Manufacturers first extract sucrose from the beets and convert it enzymatically into isomaltulose, a different type of sugar. This intermediate substance is then hydrogenated, a chemical process that adds hydrogen atoms to the molecules using a catalyst. This chemical modification transforms the sugar into a stable sugar alcohol that the human body cannot fully digest.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose isomalt because it behaves exactly like sugar during cooking and baking. It resists moisture, meaning it does not absorb water from the air, which prevents hard candies from becoming sticky and extends their shelf life. It also has a high thermal stability, so it does not caramelise or turn brown when heated. This makes it the premier choice for decorative confectionery, hard-boiled sweets, and baked goods that require a sturdy, clear structure.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. Because the human body only partially absorbs sugar alcohols, the undigested portion passes into the large intestine where it draws in water and is fermented by gut bacteria. This process can lead to gas, bloating, and abdominal discomfort. Under European law, any food containing more than ten per cent added polyols like isomalt must carry a mandatory warning label stating that excessive consumption may produce laxative effects.

What the science says

Regulators have evaluated isomalt thoroughly and found no toxicological concerns, meaning no numerical upper limit is established for daily intake. The Scientific Committee on Food and JECFA both concluded that the sweetener is acceptable for food use, though they noted its digestive effects at high doses. Because individual tolerance varies widely, some people experience digestive changes after eating only a small amount, while others can tolerate larger portions before experiencing any laxative effect.

Where you'll find it

You will find this sweetener in sugar-free hard candies, cough drops, chewing gum, chocolates, and baked decorations. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its plain name as isomalt, or by its European additive number, E953. In the United States, it is often listed simply as isomalt, and the product packaging will usually display the total sugar alcohol content in the nutrition facts panel.
