Skimmed milk powder

Processed ingredientGood

Skimmed milk powder is simply liquid skimmed milk with the water removed, providing a concentrated source of dairy protein and calcium.

What it is

Skimmed milk powder is a concentrated dairy product made by removing nearly all the water and fat from fresh cow milk. It retains the natural proteins, carbohydrates, and minerals found in fresh milk, particularly calcium and phosphorus, but in a highly stable, dry form. To a shopper, it is essentially the same as the skimmed milk you buy in a carton, just without the liquid. It has a mild, slightly sweet dairy flavour and dissolves easily when mixed with water.

How it's made

The production starts with fresh raw milk, which is spun in a separator to skim off the cream, leaving behind liquid skimmed milk. This liquid is pasteurised to ensure it is clean and stable. Next, it goes through an evaporator where heat and vacuum pressure remove most of the water, turning it into a thick concentrate. Finally, this concentrate is sprayed as a fine mist into a heated chamber, where the remaining moisture evaporates instantly, leaving behind a fine, free-flowing powder.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use this powder because it is incredibly versatile and far easier to store and transport than fresh liquid milk. It does not require refrigeration and has a long shelf life. In recipes, it adds creamy texture, improves the structure of baked goods, and helps chocolates and sauces achieve a smooth consistency. It is also a highly cost-effective way to standardise the protein and solid content in dairy products like yoghurt, ensuring a consistent texture in every batch.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. Because it is a whole food ingredient rather than a chemical additive, it is not classified as an additive and has no associated safety limits. It is a nutritious source of dairy protein and calcium with almost no fat. The only consideration is for individuals who cannot tolerate lactose or have a dairy allergy, as it contains the same milk sugars and proteins as fresh milk. For everyone else, it is a straightforward, wholesome ingredient.

What the science says

Regulators view skimmed milk powder as a standard food ingredient rather than a regulated additive. The FDA recognises it as a traditional food substance, and EU regulations treat it as a common agricultural ingredient. Because it is simply dehydrated milk, scientific assessments focus on its nutritional value, confirming it delivers high-quality proteins like casein and whey, alongside essential minerals. No regulatory body has established an upper intake limit, as it is metabolised by the body in the exact same way as fresh skimmed milk.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of packaged foods, including milk chocolates, biscuits, cake mixes, instant soups, and processed meats. It is also a staple in infant formulas and protein powders. On packaging, it is typically listed as skimmed milk powder, nonfat dry milk, or by its French name, lait écrémé en poudre. Because it is a natural food ingredient and not an additive, it does not have an E-number.
