Potassium citrates (E332)

E332AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Potassium citrates are benign acidity regulators made from citric acid that help control the tartness and preserve the texture of processed foods.

What it is

Potassium citrates are the potassium salts of citric acid, the very same natural acid that gives lemons and limes their sharp, refreshing bite. In the kitchen and the food industry, they exist as a fine, white crystalline powder that dissolves easily in water. They are closely related to other common food acids and are used primarily to manage how acidic or alkaline a food is, helping to balance flavours and keep ingredients stable.

How it's made

To make potassium citrates, manufacturers start with citric acid, which is typically produced by fermenting simple sugars using a common, food-grade fungus. This citric acid is then neutralised by adding potassium carbonate or potassium hydroxide. The chemical reaction that follows creates potassium citrate molecules, which are then carefully crystallised out of the liquid, filtered, washed, and dried into a clean, uniform powder ready for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers rely on potassium citrates because they are exceptionally good at regulating acidity, which keeps flavours consistent over time. They also act as stabilisers, preventing dairy proteins from separating in canned milks and helping gels form smoothly in jams and jellies. By binding to certain minerals, they stop foods from discolouring and help preserve a fresh appearance. They are often chosen over sodium-based alternatives to keep the sodium content of a product low.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. There is no credible mechanism of harm associated with potassium citrates at the levels normally found in our food. Because our bodies are entirely accustomed to processing both potassium and citric acid from our regular diet, this additive is handled easily by our normal metabolic pathways. No daily intake limit is established by regulators because the substance is considered highly safe for the general population.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated potassium citrates and found no reason for concern. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated citric acid and its salts, confirming their safety in food. Because potassium is an essential nutrient that the body regulates naturally, and citric acid is a normal part of cellular energy production, the substance is broken down and utilised without any accumulation. No regulator has found any adverse effects from its use as a food additive.

Where you'll find it

You will spot this ingredient on labels under names like potassium citrate, monopotassium citrate, tripotassium citrate, or its European code, E332. It is a common sight in carbonated soft drinks, sports beverages, processed cheeses, condensed milk, jams, and infant formulas. On European or imported packaging, you might also see it listed in other languages as citrate de potassium, kaliumcitrat, or citrato de potasio.
