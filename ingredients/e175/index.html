Gold (E175)

E175AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Gold is a metallic food colour used for decoration that Clara rates AVOID because regulators lack the safety data to set a daily limit.

What it is

Gold is a precious metal that has been used for centuries to decorate food. In the food industry, it is known as a mineral-based colourant that adds a metallic sheen to the surface of luxury items. Unlike nutrients or starches, it is not digested or absorbed as food by the body. Instead, it is an inert substance that passes through the digestive tract unchanged, serving a purely aesthetic purpose rather than adding any nutritional value or flavour.

How it's made

The production of food-grade gold begins with mining natural gold ore from the earth. This raw metal is refined to an extremely high purity level, typically around ninety-four per cent or higher, often alloyed with a tiny amount of silver to achieve the correct structural properties. The refined metal is then mechanically beaten, rolled, and pressed into incredibly thin sheets known as gold leaf, or ground into a fine powder that can be applied to food surfaces.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this ingredient solely for its visual appeal. It provides a bright, reflective, and luxurious metallic finish that cannot be replicated by synthetic yellow dyes. Because it is highly stable, it does not react with moisture, oxygen, or light, meaning it will not tarnish, fade, or change colour over time. This makes it highly valued for high-end confectionery, chocolates, and celebratory beverages where a premium appearance is desired.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. This rating is due to a lack of safety data rather than a proven hazard. EFSA evaluated the colourant and concluded that there was not enough toxicity information available to establish an acceptable daily intake. Additionally, there is insufficient information regarding the presence and behaviour of gold nanoparticles, which are tiny particles that can behave differently in the body compared to larger pieces of metal.

What the science says

Scientific evaluations of this colourant highlight significant gaps in our understanding of how it behaves at a microscopic level. When EFSA re-evaluated the substance, the panel noted that the database on its absorption, distribution, and elimination from the body is extremely limited. Because modern analytical methods show that metallic additives can contain nanoparticles, researchers require more detailed studies on how these minute particles interact with human tissues before the substance can be declared fully understood.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient on luxury chocolates, wedding cakes, external coatings of confectionery, and in some high-end liqueurs. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its name as gold, or by its European code E175. In the United States, it is permitted under specific regulations and may be listed on US labels as gold, where its use is strictly limited to decorative coatings on confectionery and other specific celebratory items.
