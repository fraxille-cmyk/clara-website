Soybean oil

Processed ingredientAvoid

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Soybean oil is a highly refined vegetable oil used widely in processed foods, but Clara advises avoiding it due to its high omega-six fat content.

What it is

Soybean oil is a vegetable oil extracted from the seeds of the soybean plant. It is one of the most widely consumed cooking oils in the world, often appearing in kitchens and packaged foods under the generic name of vegetable oil. In its natural state, the oil contains a mixture of polyunsaturated, monounsaturated, and saturated fats. Most of the soybean oil we eat has been highly refined, a process that strips away its natural colour, strong aroma, and beneficial nutrients to leave a pale, neutral liquid that blends easily into almost any recipe.

How it's made

To make this oil, harvested soybeans are cracked, heated, and rolled into thin flakes. A chemical solvent, typically hexane, is then washed over the flakes to dissolve and extract the oil. Once the solvent is evaporated and recovered, the crude oil undergoes a multi-step refining process. This involves degumming to remove lecithin, neutralising with alkali to eliminate free fatty acids, bleaching with clay to remove natural pigments, and deodorising with high-temperature steam to strip away any remaining bean-like flavours, resulting in a highly stable, uniform product.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose soybean oil because it is incredibly cheap, abundant, and versatile. Its high smoke point makes it excellent for commercial frying, as it does not break down easily under intense heat. Because it has an entirely neutral flavour after refining, it carries other flavours beautifully without altering the taste of the final product. It also helps to emulsify dressings, keep baked goods moist, and extend the shelf life of packaged snacks by resisting rancidity, making it a highly cost-effective choice for large-scale food production.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID. While it is approved for use by food authorities worldwide, the high concentration of omega-six fatty acids in refined soybean oil is a point of concern for many modern shoppers. When consumed in large quantities, these fats can alter the balance of fatty acids in our cells. Because it is so cheap and functional, it is easy to consume this oil in large amounts without realising it, which is why Clara recommends choosing foods made with more balanced fats instead.

What the science says

Regulators have evaluated soybean oil as a standard food ingredient. The FDA has reviewed various soybean oils, including modified high-oleate varieties, under its GRAS notification program, concluding they are acceptable for use in food. EFSA has also assessed the safety of vegetable oils in various dietary contexts, establishing no upper limit for standard soybean oil consumption. Scientists continue to study how a high intake of refined seed oils affects the fatty acid composition of human tissues, particularly regarding the ratio of omega-six to omega-three fats in the modern diet.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in an enormous variety of supermarket products, ranging from margarine, mayonnaise, and creamy salad dressings to potato chips, crackers, and pre-packaged baked goods like cakes and biscuits. On packaging, it is rarely listed with an E-number because it is a food ingredient rather than an additive. Instead, look for it on the ingredient list under names like soybean oil, refined soybean oil, or simply grouped under the generic term vegetable oil, which frequently relies on soy as its primary source.
