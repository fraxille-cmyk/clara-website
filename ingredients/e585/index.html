Ferrous lactate (E585)

E585AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

An iron-based additive used to darken black olives, with a strict daily limit but no major safety concerns when consumed in normal amounts.

What it is

Ferrous lactate is an iron salt of lactic acid. It is a compound that combines iron, a mineral your body needs to carry oxygen in the blood, with lactic acid, a mild acid that occurs naturally in fermented foods and in your own muscles. In the food industry, it is used as a specialised additive rather than a direct nutrient, primarily helping to stabilise and deepen the dark colour of processed olives. It usually appears as a greenish-white powder or small crystals that dissolve easily in water.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers chemically synthesise it by reacting lactic acid with iron. The lactic acid is typically produced through the fermentation of carbohydrates, such as corn starch or beet sugar, by lactic acid bacteria. This acid is then neutralised with an iron source, such as iron carbonate or iron filings. The resulting mixture is filtered, crystallised, and dried to create a highly purified powder. While the starting materials are organic, the final substance is classified as a synthetic additive because of this chemical processing.

Why manufacturers use it

Food producers use this ingredient for a very specific cosmetic job: to turn green olives into a uniform, deep black colour. When olives are harvested, they are often green and bitter. During processing, they are treated with an alkaline solution and exposed to air to oxidise them, which darkens their skin. Manufacturers add ferrous lactate to the brine because the iron reacts with natural compounds in the olives to lock in that dark shade, preventing it from fading back to a dull brown or grey during storage. It is a highly efficient and cost-effective way to ensure a glossy, appealing appearance.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. While it is synthetic, it is not considered a high-risk substance, though regulators have set a clear limit on how much you should consume. EFSA has established a numeric acceptable daily intake of 0.8 mg per kg of body weight for iron from all food additive sources. Because it is only allowed in a very narrow category of foods, it is highly unlikely that a normal diet would lead you to exceed this safety threshold. There are no active concerns about its safety when it is consumed within these established limits.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this compound. EFSA re-evaluated the additive and confirmed its safety limits, noting that the iron is absorbed by the body in a similar way to other dietary iron sources. In the United States, the FDA recognises it as generally safe when used as a nutrient supplement. Because it simply breaks down into iron and lactic acid in your digestive system, scientists do not find any unusual accumulation or unexpected reactions in the body. It does not carry any warnings or classifications of concern from international bodies.

Where you'll find it

You will almost exclusively find this ingredient in canned or jarred black olives, particularly those labelled as ripe olives or olives darkened by oxidation. On ingredient lists in the UK and Europe, it is typically declared by its name, ferrous lactate, or by its E-number, E585. On US labels, you will see it listed as ferrous lactate, where it is sometimes also added to infant formulas or fortified foods as a source of dietary iron, though its primary role in European retail products remains colour retention.
