Potassium hydroxide (E525)

E525AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Potassium hydroxide is a synthetic acidity regulator used to adjust pH levels in food, and Clara rates it as a limited-concern additive.

What it is

Potassium hydroxide is a strong alkaline substance, often referred to as caustic potash. In its pure form, it is a white, odourless solid that readily absorbs moisture from the air. While it is highly corrosive in its concentrated industrial state, in the food industry it is used in tiny, carefully controlled amounts to neutralise acids and balance the pH levels of various products, making them more stable and pleasant to consume. It is the potassium-based counterpart to sodium hydroxide, which is commonly known as lye.

How it's made

This ingredient is produced synthetically through a process called the electrolysis of potassium chloride brine. An electrical current is passed through a saltwater solution containing potassium chloride, which causes the molecules to split and reform. This reaction yields potassium hydroxide along with chlorine gas and hydrogen gas as byproducts. The resulting solution is then purified, concentrated, and evaporated to create the food-grade flakes, pellets, or liquid solutions that manufacturers use in food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on potassium hydroxide primarily as an acidity regulator and processing aid. It is highly effective at adjusting and maintaining the pH of foods, which helps to stabilise colours, textures, and flavours during processing and storage. It is also used to peel fruits and vegetables chemically, to process cocoa and chocolate to reduce bitterness, and to help glaze baked goods like pretzels, giving them their characteristic dark, shiny crust and distinct flavour. It is often preferred over sodium-based alternatives when manufacturers want to keep the sodium content of a product low.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects its strong safety record in food production, where it is used in such small quantities that it poses no risk to consumers. Because it neutralises or reacts completely with other ingredients during processing, it is no longer present in its corrosive form by the time the food reaches the shelf. EFSA re-evaluated the substance and confirmed that no specific acceptable daily intake limit is required, as its use in food does not pose a safety concern.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated potassium hydroxide and agree on its safety. The European Food Safety Authority reviewed the additive and concluded that there is no need to establish a numerical acceptable daily intake, as it breaks down into potassium and hydroxide ions, which are naturally processed by the body without issue. The US Food and Drug Administration also recognises it as generally recognised as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practices. There are no open safety concerns or unresolved questions regarding its dietary consumption.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of processed foods, including cocoa powder, chocolate products, pretzels, olives, glazed baked goods, and infant formulas where precise pH control is essential. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its plain name as potassium hydroxide, or by its European food additive code, E525. On US labels, it is listed as potassium hydroxide in accordance with federal regulations, and it may sometimes be grouped under a general term like acidity regulator.
