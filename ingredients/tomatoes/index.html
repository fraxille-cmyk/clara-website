Tomatoes

Tomatoes are a nutritious whole food that Clara rates as GOOD, offering a rich source of natural vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants.

What it is

Tomatoes are the fleshy, juicy fruits of a plant in the nightshade family, though we almost always treat them as vegetables in the kitchen. Originating in South America, they have become a cornerstone of global cuisine. They are naturally rich in water, dietary fibre, vitamin C, and potassium. They also contain lycopene, a natural pigment that gives them their bright red colour and acts as a powerful antioxidant in your body, helping to protect your cells from everyday wear and tear.

How it's made

This is a whole food that comes straight from nature. Tomato plants are grown in fields or glasshouses, where they flower and produce green fruits that ripen under the sun. Once they reach their peak colour and sweetness, they are harvested by hand or machine. For packaged foods, they are simply washed, sorted, and either packed whole, chopped, or gently simmered and pureed. No complex chemical processing or synthetic additives are involved in bringing them to your table.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use tomatoes because they provide a rich, savoury depth of flavour known as umami, which comes from their natural glutamates. They offer a beautiful red colour, a thick texture, and a pleasant acidity that balances sweeter ingredients. Using whole or crushed tomatoes allows manufacturers to create rich sauces, soups, and stews without relying on artificial thickeners or synthetic flavourings. They are a cost-effective, highly popular ingredient that consumers recognise and trust on the label.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. Because this is a single-ingredient whole food with nothing added, it is a highly nutritious choice for your daily diet. There are no synthetic chemicals, artificial preservatives, or processing aids to worry about. For the vast majority of people, tomatoes are entirely beneficial and support overall well-being. A very small number of people might have an individual sensitivity or allergy to nightshade plants, but for everyone else, they are a wholesome and welcome ingredient.

What the science says

International food authorities view tomatoes as a highly beneficial dietary staple rather than an additive. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration recognise tomatoes as a standard whole food of excellent nutritional value. Scientific evaluations focus on the positive role of their natural compounds, particularly how cooking tomatoes actually increases the availability of lycopene, making it easier for your body to absorb. No regulatory body has established any upper limit or safety concern for consuming them.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a vast range of products, from canned chopped tomatoes and pasta sauces to ketchups, soups, ready meals, and savoury snacks. On ingredient lists, they can appear under several names depending on how they were prepared, including cherry tomatoes, chopped tomatoes, plum tomatoes, vine tomatoes, or simply tomato. On imported European products, you might also spot them listed under their Italian, German, or French names as pomodori, tomaten, or tomates.
