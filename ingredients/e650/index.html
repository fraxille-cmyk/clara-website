Zinc acetate (E650)

E650AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Zinc acetate is a synthetic mineral salt used in chewing gum to enhance flavour and freshen breath, rated neutral by Clara with a strict daily limit.

What it is

Zinc acetate is a synthetic salt formed by combining zinc, an essential mineral your body needs in small amounts, with acetic acid, the main component of household vinegar. In the food world, it is classified as a flavour enhancer. It is a white, crystalline powder that dissolves easily in water, leaving a slightly astringent or metallic taste that helps neutralise other strong odours in the mouth.

How it's made

To manufacture this ingredient, industrial producers react zinc oxide or zinc carbonate with acetic acid. Zinc oxide is typically obtained by heating zinc metal in air, while acetic acid is produced through the chemical synthesis of methanol or the fermentation of ethanol. The resulting liquid mixture is filtered, concentrated, and allowed to crystallise into high-purity zinc acetate crystals, which are then dried and ground into a fine powder.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this substance almost exclusively in chewing gum. It acts as a powerful flavour enhancer and breath freshener. The zinc ions in the salt bind with volatile sulphur compounds in the mouth, which are responsible for unpleasant breath, and neutralise them. This chemical reaction extends the clean, fresh sensation of the gum far longer than standard mint flavourings can achieve on their own.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. While zinc is a necessary nutrient, too much of it can interfere with how your body absorbs other essential minerals like copper. Because of this, JECFA established a numeric acceptable daily intake of 0.3 mg per kg of body weight in 1982. In the European Union, its use is strictly restricted to chewing gum up to a limit of 1000 mg per kg to ensure shoppers do not easily exceed this daily limit.

What the science says

Regulators have thoroughly evaluated zinc acetate to ensure it does not pose a risk when consumed in small quantities. JECFA assessed the substance and established the safe daily limit to protect consumers from excessive zinc intake. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration both permit its use, agreeing that the tiny amounts swallowed while chewing gum are processed safely by the digestive system without any toxicological concern.

Where you'll find it

You will almost exclusively find this ingredient in sugar-free chewing gums and breath-freshening mints. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically declared by its name, zinc acetate, or by its E-number, E650. On US labels, it is listed as zinc acetate, where it is also permitted as a nutrient supplement to fortify foods with essential zinc.
