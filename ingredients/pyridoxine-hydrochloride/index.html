Pyridoxine hydrochloride

FortificantNeutral · benign

Pyridoxine hydrochloride is a synthetic form of vitamin B6 used to fortify foods and drinks, helping to top up your daily intake of this essential nutrient.

What it is

Pyridoxine hydrochloride is a stable, water-soluble form of vitamin B6. In its natural state, vitamin B6 is found in many foods we eat every day, including chickpeas, bananas, potatoes, and fish. It is an essential nutrient, meaning our bodies cannot produce it on their own, so we must obtain it from our diet. This specific compound is a crystalline powder that is highly stable, making it an excellent choice for adding back the nutritional value that can sometimes be lost during food processing.

How it's made

While natural vitamin B6 is abundant in plants and animals, the pyridoxine hydrochloride found on food labels is created in a laboratory. Manufacturers synthesise it through a series of controlled chemical reactions, typically starting with simple organic compounds like alanine or oxazole derivatives. These compounds are combined and structured to mimic the exact molecular shape of natural pyridoxine. Finally, it is reacted with hydrochloric acid to form a salt, which crystallises into a highly stable, white powder that dissolves easily in water.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this ingredient to fortify products, restoring nutrients lost during milling or heating, or boosting the nutritional profile of everyday foods. It is highly favoured over other forms of vitamin B6 because the hydrochloride salt is incredibly stable against heat, light, and oxygen. This means it will not break down on the supermarket shelf or during cooking. It is also virtually odourless and tasteless, allowing manufacturers to enhance the nutritional value of a product without altering its original flavour, colour, or texture.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. This ingredient is simply a nutrient added to food, and it is entirely harmless at the amounts typically found on food labels. Because it is water-soluble, any excess that your body does not need is easily filtered by the kidneys and passed out of the body. The European Food Safety Authority has established a safe upper limit of twelve milligrams per day for adults, which is far higher than the small amounts used to fortify everyday food products.

What the science says

Regulators worldwide have thoroughly evaluated pyridoxine hydrochloride and recognise it as a safe source of vitamin B6. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated its safety and confirmed its daily upper limit in a scientific opinion. The United States Food and Drug Administration also classifies it as generally recognised as safe for use as a nutrient supplement in food. Scientific consensus shows that it is absorbed and metabolised by the body in the exact same way as the vitamin B6 naturally present in fresh whole foods.

Where you'll find it

You will commonly find this ingredient in breakfast cereals, energy bars, plant-based milk alternatives, infant formulas, and sports drinks. On the packaging, it might be listed under its chemical name pyridoxine hydrochloride, or simply as vitamin B6. On European or imported products, you may spot its French, Spanish, or Italian translations, such as chlorhydrate de pyridoxine, clorhidrato de piridoxina, or piridossina cloridrato. It does not have an E-number because it is classified as a nutrient rather than a food additive.
