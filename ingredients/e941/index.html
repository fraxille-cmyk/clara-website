Nitrogen (E941)

E941AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Nitrogen is a harmless, naturally occurring gas used to push oxygen out of food packaging, keeping your food fresh for longer without preservatives.

What it is

Nitrogen is a colourless, odourless, and tasteless gas that makes up about seventy-eight percent of the air we breathe every day. In the food industry, it is classified as a packaging gas. It is a completely natural substance of mineral origin that does not react with food, serving as an invisible, protective blanket inside packets to keep the contents fresh and intact until you open them.

How it's made

To obtain food-grade nitrogen, manufacturers cool normal atmospheric air until it liquefies. Since different gases in the air turn into liquids at different temperatures, producers can carefully separate the nitrogen from oxygen and other gases in a process called fractional distillation. This physical separation method requires no chemical modification, resulting in an extremely pure gas that is ready to be used directly in food packaging.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use nitrogen to displace oxygen inside food packaging. Oxygen is the main culprit behind food spoiling, as it allows mould to grow and causes fats to go rancid. By flushing a packet with nitrogen before sealing it, manufacturers create an oxygen-free environment that preserves the flavour, texture, and colour of the food. It also cushions delicate items like crisps, preventing them from crushing during transport.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because nitrogen is a natural component of the air we breathe and does not react with the food itself, it poses no health risks when used in packaging. EFSA re-evaluated the gas and determined that no limit is established for its daily intake, meaning it can be used as needed to keep food fresh. It is entirely non-toxic and leaves no chemical residues on your food.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated nitrogen and agree on its safety. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated it and confirmed its benign status, finding no safety concerns. Because it is an inert gas that we inhale with every breath, it passes through the packaging process without altering the nutritional value of the food or introducing any foreign substances to your body.

Where you'll find it

You will find nitrogen used in a wide variety of sealed products, including bags of crisps, pre-washed salad bags, coffee pods, and packets of grated cheese. On ingredient labels, it is usually listed simply as nitrogen or under its European code, E941. It is often mentioned on the packet with a phrase like packaged in a protective atmosphere, which tells you the air inside was replaced with this harmless gas.
