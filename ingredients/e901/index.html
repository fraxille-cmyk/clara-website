Beeswax (E901)

E901AdditiveNeutral · benign

Beeswax is a natural glazing agent made by honeybees, used to give food a protective, shiny coating and keep it fresh.

What it is

Beeswax is a natural wax produced by honeybees of the genus Apis. Young worker bees secrete this wax from special glands on their abdomens, using it to build the precise hexagonal cells of the honeycomb where they store honey and raise their brood. It is a complex mixture of natural compounds, including esters, fatty acids, and long-chain alcohols, which give it a unique structure that is solid at room temperature but softens easily when warmed.

How it's made

To obtain the wax, beekeepers harvest the honeycomb after extracting the honey. The empty combs and wax caps are melted in hot water or steam to separate the wax from impurities like pollen, honey residues, and propolis. Once melted, the liquid wax is filtered and cooled into solid blocks. For food use, the wax undergoes further physical filtration and gentle purification to remove any remaining impurities and neutralise its natural aroma, resulting in a clean, yellow or white substance.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use beeswax primarily as a glazing agent and surface treatment. It creates a thin, protective barrier that locks in moisture, which keeps fresh fruits from drying out and losing their firmness. On confectionery, it provides a beautiful, glossy finish that stops sweets from sticking together in the packet. It is a highly effective, natural alternative to synthetic, petroleum-derived waxes, offering excellent stability and a smooth texture that melts pleasantly in the mouth.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Beeswax has a long history of safe use in food and is not absorbed by the body, passing through the digestive system unchanged without causing any disruption. The European Food Safety Authority has evaluated its safety and confirmed that its use as a glazing agent does not raise safety concerns. Because it is a natural substance with no observed adverse effects at typical dietary levels, no daily intake limit has been established.

What the science says

Scientific evaluations show that beeswax is biologically inert when consumed in food. Joint FAO and WHO experts, along with European regulators, have reviewed the substance and concluded that it does not accumulate in the body or interfere with normal digestion. Because it is a complex natural mixture, researchers have analysed its components to ensure they contain no harmful contaminants, confirming that the purification process leaves only a clean, stable substance that is entirely suitable for human consumption.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient on labels listed as beeswax, white beeswax, yellow beeswax, or E901. It is commonly applied to the skins of citrus fruits, apples, and melons to preserve their freshness during transport. It is also a frequent ingredient in jelly sweets, chocolate-coated snacks, and chewing gum, where it provides a shiny outer shell. It even appears in some food supplements and pastry glazes to help hold their shape and texture.
