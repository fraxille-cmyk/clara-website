Calcium phosphates (E341)

E341AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Calcium phosphates are mineral salts used to control acidity, but EFSA warns that our total dietary intake of phosphates may exceed the safe daily limit.

What it is

Calcium phosphates are a family of mineral salts containing calcium and phosphorus. Phosphorus is an essential nutrient that your body uses to build bones and teeth, and it occurs naturally in many foods like milk, meat, and nuts. In the food industry, these salts are used as additives to manage acidity, help powders flow smoothly, and fortify products with calcium. They are closely related to the natural minerals found in our skeletal system, making them highly compatible with food formulations.

How it's made

These minerals are produced by reacting phosphoric acid with calcium hydroxide. The phosphoric acid is typically derived from mined phosphate rock, which is treated with sulphuric acid and purified to remove impurities. The calcium hydroxide comes from limestone that has been heated and mixed with water. When these two substances meet, they neutralise each other and form a fine, white powder that is washed, dried, and milled to the desired particle size.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on these salts because they are incredibly versatile. In powdered mixes like cake batters, gravies, and drink powders, they act as anti-caking agents by absorbing moisture and keeping the particles from sticking together. In baking, they work alongside raising agents to help doughs rise evenly. They also function as acidity regulators to stabilise the pH of processed foods, and they are a cheap, stable way to add calcium to breakfast cereals and plant-based milks.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. While phosphorus is a necessary nutrient, EFSA warns that estimated dietary exposure to phosphates may exceed the safe level for some population groups. Because phosphates are added to so many processed foods, it is easy to consume more than the established daily limit without realising it. High levels of phosphate in the blood can put extra strain on the kidneys and blood vessels, as the body must work harder to filter out the excess.

What the science says

The European Food Safety Authority conducted a major re-evaluation of these additives and established a group acceptable daily intake of 40 milligrams per kilogram of body weight for phosphates. EFSA noted that while the additives themselves do not pose an immediate risk, the cumulative intake from both natural food sources and food additives can easily push some consumers, especially children, over this safe threshold. The US Food and Drug Administration permits their use under several GRAS regulations, reflecting their long history in food production.

Where you'll find it

You will find these minerals in a wide range of processed foods, including baking mixes, processed cheeses, breakfast cereals, powdered drinks, and infant formulas. On ingredient lists, they are typically declared as calcium phosphates, monocalcium phosphate, dicalcium phosphate, tricalcium phosphate, or by the European code E341. On US labels, they are often listed simply as calcium phosphate or tricalcium phosphate to indicate their role as a nutrient supplement or anti-caking agent.
