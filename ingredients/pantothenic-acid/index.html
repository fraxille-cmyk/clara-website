Pantothenic acid

FortificantGood

Pantothenic acid is vitamin B5, an essential nutrient that helps your body turn food into energy and build hormones.

What it is

Pantothenic acid is the scientific name for vitamin B5, a water-soluble nutrient that your body needs every day. The name comes from the Greek word pantothen, which means from everywhere, because this vitamin is found in almost all whole foods, from vegetables to meats. In the body, it is a vital building block for coenzyme A, a helper molecule that allows cells to metabolise fats, proteins, and carbohydrates. Because your body cannot store water-soluble vitamins for long, any extra is simply flushed out in your urine.

How it's made

While plants and microbes synthesise this vitamin naturally, the version added to packaged foods is typically made in a laboratory to ensure purity and stability. Manufacturers usually produce it through a chemical synthesis process that combines beta-alanine, an amino acid, with a specific organic compound called pantolactone. The resulting substance is often converted into calcium pantothenate, a white, crystalline powder that is much more stable on the shelf and less sensitive to heat and air than the pure acid form.

Why manufacturers use it

Food companies add this ingredient to products primarily for fortification, restoring nutrients lost during processing or boosting the nutritional profile of everyday foods. It is a popular addition to breakfast cereals, energy bars, and infant formulas because it is highly stable and does not alter the flavour, colour, or texture of the food. By using this stable powder, manufacturers can guarantee that the vitamin content remains consistent from the factory floor to the moment you open the package.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As an essential nutrient that the body requires for daily survival, it is highly compatible with human biology. Because it is water-soluble, the body efficiently regulates its levels and excretes any excess, meaning there is very little risk of accumulation. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration both recognise it as a vital nutrient, and no upper limit of daily intake has been established because even very high amounts are processed smoothly by the body.

What the science says

Scientific bodies worldwide have thoroughly evaluated this vitamin and confirm its role in normal energy-yielding metabolism and the synthesis of steroid hormones. EFSA scientific opinions confirm that it contributes to normal mental performance and the reduction of tiredness. Because it is so abundant in a balanced diet, true deficiency is incredibly rare. Extensive reviews by the Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives and other regulators show no evidence of unwanted effects from the levels typically found in fortified foods and dietary supplements.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient listed on the back of breakfast cereals, meal replacement shakes, sports drinks, energy bars, and infant formulas. On the ingredient label, it is usually declared plainly as pantothenic acid, calcium pantothenate, d-calcium pantothenate, or simply vitamin B5. Because it is classified as a nutrient rather than a food additive, it does not have an E-number and is listed alongside other vitamins and minerals in the ingredient list or the nutritional information panel.
