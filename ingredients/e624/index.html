Monoammonium glutamate (E624)

E624AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Monoammonium glutamate is a synthetic flavour enhancer used to boost savoury taste, but high consumption can exceed the daily limit set by regulators.

What it is

Monoammonium glutamate is an ammonium salt of glutamic acid, an amino acid that occurs naturally in many protein-rich foods like aged cheeses, tomatoes, and mushrooms. It is a white, crystalline powder that dissolves easily in water. While it is chemically similar to monosodium glutamate, it swaps the sodium part for ammonium, making it a popular choice for products designed to deliver a rich, savoury taste without adding extra sodium to your diet.

How it's made

This additive is produced through a controlled fermentation process. Manufacturers use specific bacteria to ferment sugar sources, such as molasses, starch, or sugar beet syrup, which naturally yields glutamic acid. Once the glutamic acid is isolated and purified, it is neutralised using ammonia. This chemical reaction creates monoammonium glutamate, which is then crystallised, dried, and packaged as a fine powder ready for food manufacturing.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this ingredient as a highly effective flavour enhancer to intensify the savoury, umami taste of processed foods. It is particularly valuable in low-sodium formulations, such as diet soups, salt-free seasoning blends, and light snacks, because it provides the desired mouthwatering depth of flavour without the need for table salt. This allows brands to meet nutritional targets for sodium reduction while maintaining a taste that appeals to shoppers.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. EFSA established a group acceptable daily intake of 30 mg per kg of body weight for glutamates in 2017, and the regulator noted that this limit was exceeded by high consumers in several population groups. Because it is easy to consume more than the recommended limit when eating a diet rich in processed foods, Clara advises keeping your intake of this additive to a minimum.

What the science says

European and American authorities have thoroughly evaluated glutamic acid and its salts. EFSA conducted a major re-evaluation of these additives, grouping them together to set a single protective daily limit based on potential effects on the body at very high doses. While the human body metabolises ammonium glutamate in the same way as natural glutamates, researchers continue to monitor total dietary exposure to ensure that cumulative intake from multiple processed foods does not regularly exceed the established threshold.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of savoury processed foods, especially those marketed as low-sodium, reduced-salt, or diet options. It is common in instant noodle packets, savoury snack mixes, commercial spice blends, canned soups, and processed meat products. On ingredient labels, it is typically declared by its full name as monoammonium glutamate, or simply as E624, and on US labels it may be listed as monoammonium glutamate under general amino acid provisions.
