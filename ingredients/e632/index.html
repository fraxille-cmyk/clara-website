Dipotassium inosinate (E632)

E632AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Dipotassium inosinate is a savoury flavour enhancer of microbial origin, rated neutral by Clara because it has no specified acceptable daily intake limit.

What it is

Dipotassium inosinate is a potassium salt of inosinic acid, a compound found naturally in many foods like meat, fish, and mushrooms. It belongs to a family of ingredients known as nucleotides, which are the building blocks of genetic material in all living things. When used in food, it acts as a flavour enhancer, working in harmony with other savoury compounds to create a rich, satisfying taste. It is closely related to the more common disodium inosinate, but uses potassium instead of sodium to stabilise the molecule.

How it's made

This ingredient is produced through a natural fermentation process. Manufacturers use specific strains of microbes, such as yeasts or bacteria, which are grown in a nutrient-rich broth where they naturally produce guanosine monophosphate and inosine monophosphate. Once the fermentation is complete, the mixture is purified, and potassium hydroxide is added to neutralise the acid. This reaction crystallises the substance into a stable, dry powder that is easy to measure, blend, and store for food manufacturing.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this additive to boost the savoury, meaty flavour of foods, a taste profile often described as umami. It is incredibly efficient because it works synergistically with glutamates; when combined with ingredients like monosodium glutamate, the flavour-enhancing effect is multiplied several times over. This allows food companies to achieve a deep, savoury taste while using much less salt. Choosing the dipotassium version over the sodium version also helps developers formulate products with lower sodium levels without sacrificing the intense savoury profile consumers expect.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is based on its safe microbial origin and the fact that major food authorities have not found any reason to set a restrictive limit on its consumption. Because it is metabolised by the body in the same way as natural dietary nucleotides, it is considered of low concern. EFSA keeps its status as an authorised additive with no specified acceptable daily intake, meaning it can be used safely in foods at the levels needed to achieve the desired flavour effect.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated this compound and its close relatives. EFSA completed a full re-evaluation of inosinic acid and its salts, concluding there is no safety concern for the general population and confirming that no numerical acceptable daily intake is necessary. The human body processes this substance by breaking it down into uric acid, a normal metabolic byproduct that is easily excreted. Because it is used in very small quantities to enhance flavour, the amount added to food is considered negligible compared to the nucleotides we naturally consume in a standard diet.

Where you'll find it

You will typically find this ingredient in highly savoury, processed foods. It is a common component in instant noodles, commercial soups, stock cubes, savoury snack mixes, potato crisps, and pre-packaged sauces or gravies. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is listed either by its full name as dipotassium inosinate or by its European code, E632. On US labels, it is permitted as a flavouring substance and is typically declared by its common name in the ingredient deck.
