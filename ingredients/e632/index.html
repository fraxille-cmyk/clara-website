Dipotassium inosinate is a savoury flavour enhancer made by fermentation that Clara rates as a benign additive with no safety concerns.

What it is

Dipotassium inosinate is a potassium salt of inosinic acid, a compound found naturally in many animal tissues and mushrooms. It belongs to a family of substances called nucleotides, which are the basic building blocks of genetic material in all living things. In food, it acts as a powerful flavour enhancer, bringing out the deep, savoury taste known as umami. It is very similar to the compounds that make slow-cooked broths and aged cheeses taste so rich and satisfying.

How it's made

To make this ingredient on a commercial scale, manufacturers turn to natural fermentation. Microorganisms, usually specific strains of bacteria or yeast, are grown in a nutrient-rich broth where they produce guanosine monophosphate and inosine monophosphate. These compounds are harvested, purified, and reacted with potassium hydroxide to create the stable salt form. The final product is a white, crystalline powder that dissolves easily in water and can be added directly to food mixtures.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this ingredient because it is an incredibly efficient way to boost savoury flavours. It works in perfect harmony with glutamates, which are other flavour-enhancing compounds. When combined, they create a synergistic effect, making the savoury taste many times stronger than either ingredient could manage alone. This allows food companies to use less salt and fewer expensive meat extracts while still delivering a rich, satisfying taste in processed foods.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. It is processed by the body in the same way as the natural nucleotides found in everyday foods. Because it is a potassium salt, it does not add sodium to the diet, which can be helpful for those monitoring their salt intake. EFSA evaluated the additive in 2020 and determined that no limit is established for its daily intake, meaning it is considered safe for the general population at the levels typically found in food.

What the science says

The scientific consensus is that this substance is handled easily by our normal digestive processes. When we consume it, our bodies break it down into uric acid, a normal waste product that is easily filtered by the kidneys and excreted. European food safety experts reviewed all available data on inosinates and concluded there is no evidence of accumulation or harm. No safety concerns or open questions remain regarding its use as a flavour enhancer.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of savoury processed foods, including instant noodles, potato crisps, seasoned snack mixes, canned soups, gravy granules, and pre-packaged sauces. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically declared by its full name as dipotassium inosinate or by its code, E632. On US labels, it is listed as dipotassium inosinate and is permitted as a synthetic flavouring substance.
