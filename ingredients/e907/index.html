Hydrogenated poly-1-decene (E907)

E907AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Hydrogenated poly-1-decene is a synthetic glazing agent used to coat sweets and dried fruit, rated neutral with a daily limit of six milligrams per kilogram.

What it is

Hydrogenated poly-1-decene is a synthetic liquid hydrocarbon. It is a clear, odourless, and tasteless fluid that behaves much like mineral oil. Chemically, it consists of long chains of carbon and hydrogen atoms that have been saturated with hydrogen to make them highly stable. While it does not occur naturally in food, its inert nature makes it useful as a protective barrier on the surface of certain foods, preventing them from sticking together or losing moisture to the air.

How it's made

This ingredient is manufactured through a multi-step chemical process starting with decene, a hydrocarbon derived from petroleum. Manufacturers polymerise these decene molecules, linking them together into longer chains. These chains then undergo hydrogenation, a process where hydrogen gas is added under high pressure in the presence of a catalyst. This reaction saturates the chemical bonds, turning the mixture into a highly stable, purified liquid that is thoroughly filtered to meet strict food-grade purity standards.

Why manufacturers use it

Food producers use this substance primarily as a glazing agent and release agent. It creates a thin, glossy, protective coating on the surface of foods, which helps retain moisture and prevents items from drying out or clumping together in the packaging. It is highly valued because it is incredibly stable, does not turn rancid over time, and has no taste or smell that could alter the flavour profile of the food. It is a highly efficient and cost-effective alternative to natural waxes.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects its synthetic origin and the fact that it has a numeric acceptable daily intake of six milligrams per kilogram of body weight. Because it is not absorbed well by the human body, most of it simply passes through the digestive system unchanged. No toxicological concern has been identified at normal exposure levels, and regulators consider it safe when consumed within the established daily limit.

What the science says

The safety of this ingredient has been thoroughly evaluated by major food authorities. EFSA re-evaluated the substance and confirmed a numeric acceptable daily intake of six milligrams per kilogram of body weight. Studies show that the compound is poorly absorbed in the digestive tract and does not accumulate in organs. Because it is highly purified, it does not contain the impurities sometimes associated with cruder mineral oils, and no open safety concerns remain under current conditions of use.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a limited range of products, as regulations restrict its use to specific categories. It is primarily used for the surface treatment of sugar confectionery, such as gummy sweets and jelly beans, and on dried fruits like raisins to keep them shiny and free-flowing. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its full name, hydrogenated poly-1-decene, or by its European code, E907, and on US labels it is listed as hydrogenated poly-1-decene.
