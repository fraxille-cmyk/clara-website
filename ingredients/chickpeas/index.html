Chickpeas

Whole foodGood

Diets

✕ Not low-FODMAP
  • Not low-FODMAP — chickpeas is high in GOS.

A nutritious whole food legume that provides an excellent source of plant-based protein and dietary fibre in many savoury and sweet dishes.

What it is

Chickpeas are the edible seeds of a legume plant that belongs to the same botanical family as lentils, peas, and beans. Often called garbanzo beans, they have a round shape, a firm texture, and a mild, nutty flavour. They are a staple food in many traditional diets around the world, celebrated for their natural abundance of plant-based protein, complex carbohydrates, and dietary fibre. Unlike highly processed additives, they are a whole food that contributes both structure and nourishment to a wide variety of meals.

How it's made

These legumes grow in small pods on leafy green plants in warm, dry climates. Once the pods dry on the vine, the chickpeas are harvested, shelled, and cleaned. Depending on how they will be sold, they are either dried completely for long-term storage, or they are cooked in water and canned. Sometimes they are milled into a fine, pale yellow flour. The entire process is straightforward and mechanical, requiring no chemical synthesis or complex laboratory alterations to reach the final package.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers value these legumes for their versatility, mild taste, and excellent structural properties. In packaged foods, they act as a natural, protein-rich base for spreads, soups, and ready meals. When milled into flour, they provide a gluten-free alternative for baking, helping to bind ingredients and retain moisture. They are also highly cost-effective, allowing manufacturers to boost the protein and fibre content of a product without relying on isolated protein powders or synthetic starches.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As a wholesome, unprocessed legume, it is a highly nutritious ingredient rather than a chemical additive. There are no safety concerns or regulatory limits associated with eating them, as they are a traditional food source enjoyed globally for centuries. They are digested naturally by the body, providing slow-release energy and supporting a balanced digestive system. No regulator has established an upper intake limit for this natural ingredient.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities view these legumes as a standard, wholesome food ingredient rather than an additive requiring toxicological evaluation. The FDA recognises them as a traditional food substance, and EFSA includes them in general dietary guidelines as a beneficial source of plant proteins and dietary fibre. Scientific assessments focus on their positive nutritional profile, noting that their complex carbohydrates are fermented by friendly bacteria in the lower digestive tract, which supports overall bowel health and efficient nutrient absorption.

Where you'll find it

You will find them in the canned goods aisle, in ready-to-eat salads, and as the main ingredient in hummus and falafel. They are also increasingly used in grain-free pastas, crisps, and gluten-free baking mixes. On ingredient lists, they are typically declared simply as chickpeas, chick peas, or chick pea, and sometimes as garbanzo beans or chickpea flour depending on how they have been prepared.
