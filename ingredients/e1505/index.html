Triethyl citrate (E1505)

E1505AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Triethyl citrate is a synthetic carrier liquid used to dissolve and disperse food flavourings, rated neutral by Clara with a standard daily limit.

What it is

Triethyl citrate is a clear, odourless, and oily liquid that is created in a laboratory. While it is chemically related to citric acid, which is the natural compound that gives lemons and limes their sharp taste, this substance is modified so that it behaves more like an oil or a solvent than an acid. In the food industry, it does not function as a flavouring itself, but rather as a helper ingredient that allows other substances to mix together smoothly.

How it's made

To manufacture this ingredient, chemical producers combine citric acid with ethanol, which is a simple alcohol. This reaction, known as esterification, alters the chemical structure of the citric acid by attaching three ethyl groups to it. The resulting liquid is then purified, washed, and filtered to remove any leftover raw materials, leaving behind a highly stable, synthetic compound that is ready to be used in food processing.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on this ingredient because many complex flavourings and essential oils do not dissolve easily in water. It acts as an efficient carrier, holding these flavour molecules in a stable liquid form so they can be evenly distributed throughout a food product. Additionally, it serves as an excellent whipping agent, particularly in egg whites, where it helps to stabilise the delicate foam and prevent it from collapsing during industrial mixing.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Once inside your body, enzymes break it down into citric acid and ethanol, two substances that your system metabolises naturally and without issue. EFSA keeps its daily limit of 20 mg per kg of body weight, which is a standard safety margin that is very difficult to exceed through normal food consumption. There are no indications of accumulation or unwanted biological reactions at typical dietary levels.

What the science says

This ingredient has been thoroughly evaluated by major food safety authorities. EFSA re-evaluated the substance in 2014 and confirmed that it poses no safety concerns at current exposure levels, establishing a clear numeric limit for daily intake. The FDA also recognises it as safe for its intended uses in food. Because it is processed by the body using standard metabolic pathways, researchers have found no evidence of unexpected cellular activity or long-term accumulation.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in processed foods that contain complex added flavours, such as baked goods, confectionery, and soft drinks. It is also frequently used in dried egg white powders to assist with whipping. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its full name as triethyl citrate, or by its European code, E1505, and on US labels it is listed as triethyl citrate.
