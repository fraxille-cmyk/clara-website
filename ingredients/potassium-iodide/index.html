Potassium iodide

FortificantNeutral · benign

Diets

Potassium iodide is a mineral compound used to fortify foods with iodine, an essential nutrient that supports your body's natural hormone production.

What it is

Potassium iodide is a simple mineral salt made of potassium and iodine. In the kitchen, you might know it as the additive that makes table salt iodised. Iodine is a crucial micronutrient that our bodies cannot make on their own. It is vital for the thyroid gland, which sits in the neck and uses iodine to manufacture the hormones that control our metabolism, energy levels, and growth. Because soils in many parts of the world lack iodine, this salt is added to foods to ensure people get enough of this essential element.

How it's made

To make this mineral salt, manufacturers start with industrial iodine, which is often extracted from natural underground brine deposits or nitrate mineral ores. This iodine is reacted with potassium hydroxide, a strong alkaline compound. The chemical reaction binds the potassium and iodine atoms together, creating a highly stable, soluble white powder. This powder is then purified, crystallised, and dried so it can be easily blended into foods and salt in tiny, precise amounts.

Why manufacturers use it

Food producers use potassium iodide primarily for nutritional fortification. It is an incredibly stable source of iodine, meaning it does not easily break down or evaporate when exposed to heat during cooking or baking. This stability makes it far superior to other iodine sources for long-term shelf storage. By adding a tiny, controlled amount to staple foods like table salt, bread, or infant formula, manufacturers can reliably deliver this essential nutrient to consumers without altering the flavour, colour, or texture of the food.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. This rating reflects its role as a vital nutrient that supports healthy thyroid function when consumed in trace amounts. Because our bodies require only a tiny amount of iodine daily, regulators monitor its use to ensure foods are not over-fortified. The FDA regulates its use as a food additive under 21 CFR, and EFSA has established safe upper intake levels for daily iodine consumption to ensure it remains beneficial rather than excessive.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated potassium iodide because of its critical role in human nutrition. The WHO and JECFA have long supported the iodisation of salt as a safe and effective way to maintain adequate iodine levels across populations. EFSA re-evaluated iodine intakes and confirmed that while adequate intake is essential for metabolic health, excessive daily consumption should be avoided. No regulatory body has raised safety concerns regarding the trace amounts used in standard food fortification, as the substance is metabolised by the body just like naturally occurring iodine.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in iodised table salt, infant formulas, dietary supplements, and fortified foods like bread or milk substitutes. On ingredient labels, it may be listed plainly as potassium iodide, or under its regional names such as iodure de potassium, ioduro di potassio, kaliumiodid, kaliumjodid, or yoduro de potasio. It does not have an E-number, as it is classified as a nutrient rather than a technological food additive.
