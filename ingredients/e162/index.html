Beetroot red (betanin) (E162)

E162AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Beetroot red is a natural red food colour extracted from red beets, used to give foods a vibrant pink or deep red shade.

What it is

Beetroot red, also known as betanin, is a natural red food colour. It is the main pigment found in the red beetroot, the familiar root vegetable that stains your fingers when you slice it. Chemically, it belongs to a class of water-soluble pigments called betalains, which plants use to attract pollinators and protect themselves from environmental stress. It is not the same as anthocyanins, which are the pigments found in berries, though both provide beautiful red and purple hues to the plant world.

How it's made

To make this colour, manufacturers start with fresh, ripe red beetroots. The beets are crushed to extract their dark red juice, which is then concentrated by evaporating the water under a vacuum. To obtain a more purified form of betanin, the concentrated juice undergoes a separation process to remove most of the natural sugars, mineral salts, and proteins. The resulting liquid is then typically spray-dried into a fine, dark red powder that dissolves easily in water, ready to be added to food formulations.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use beetroot red to restore the natural pink or red colour of foods that might fade during high-heat processing or storage. It is a highly popular choice because it provides a warm, appealing strawberry-pink to deep purple-red shade without the need for synthetic coal-tar dyes. It is particularly favoured in products that are processed quickly or kept cold, as the pigment is sensitive to prolonged high heat and light, which can cause it to fade over time.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is a concentrated extract of a common vegetable that humans have eaten for centuries, it is processed by the body just like the pigments in fresh beets. Some people might notice a harmless pink tint to their urine after consuming large amounts, which is simply the pigment passing through the body unchanged. No limit is established for its daily intake because it is considered exceptionally safe for all age groups.

What the science says

The safety of beetroot red has been thoroughly evaluated by international food safety authorities. EFSA re-evaluated the colour and concluded that its use as a food additive is of no safety concern, noting that the pigment is naturally present in a standard diet. JECFA also reviewed the substance and did not set a numerical acceptable daily intake, meaning no limit is established because the substance does not present a hazard at the levels used in food.

Where you'll find it

You will find this colour in a wide variety of supermarket items, including strawberry yoghurts, ice creams, jelly sweets, pink marshmallow biscuits, gravy granules, and berry-flavoured soft drinks. On the ingredient list, it can appear under several names, such as beetroot red, betanin, beetroot red (betanin), or its European code, E162. It is often chosen for products aiming for a more natural ingredient profile.
