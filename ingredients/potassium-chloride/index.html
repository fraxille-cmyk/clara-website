Potassium chloride

IngredientNeutral

Potassium chloride is a mineral salt used to replace regular table salt, helping food makers lower the sodium content in processed foods.

What it is

Potassium chloride is a naturally occurring mineral salt that looks and tastes very similar to regular table salt. While standard table salt is made of sodium and chlorine, this version swaps the sodium for potassium. Potassium is an essential nutrient that our bodies need to keep our cells, nerves, and muscles functioning properly. In the kitchen and the food industry, it serves as a direct alternative to sodium chloride, helping to mimic the salty flavour we expect in savoury foods without adding extra sodium.

How it's made

This mineral is harvested directly from the earth. It is mined from ancient, dried-up lake beds and underground salt deposits, where it exists as a mineral called sylvite. Once extracted, the raw ore is crushed and dissolved in water. Manufacturers then use a process of crystallisation and flotation to separate the potassium chloride from other minerals like sodium chloride and clay. The resulting crystals are washed, dried, and refined into a clean, white, granular powder that is ready to be added to food.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use this ingredient primarily to reduce the sodium content in their products. It is the most effective substitute for regular salt because it provides a similar salty taste and performs the same technical roles. Beyond flavour, salt helps to preserve food, control the growth of microbes, and improve the texture of processed meats and baked goods. Potassium chloride can do all of these jobs, allowing manufacturers to meet nutritional guidelines for lower sodium without losing the structure or shelf life of their products.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. This mineral is a benign substance that helps lower sodium intake by providing essential potassium instead. For most people, consuming potassium through food is highly beneficial, as the body is excellent at balancing its mineral levels and flushing out any excess. No daily intake limit is established by food safety authorities for the general population. However, individuals with specific kidney conditions or those taking certain medications that affect how the body handles minerals should monitor their overall potassium intake from all sources.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this mineral and consider it a helpful tool for public health. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration both recognise it as a safe ingredient for the general population. Because it is a basic nutrient rather than a synthetic additive, it does not undergo the same type of restrictive testing as artificial preservatives. Scientific consensus supports its use as a straightforward way to reduce dietary sodium, which helps maintain a healthy fluid balance and normal blood pressure in the body.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of packaged foods, especially those labelled as low-sodium, reduced-salt, or light. It commonly appears in canned soups, savoury snacks, sliced breads, sports drinks, processed meats, and prepared frozen meals. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed plainly as potassium chloride. Because it is classified as a mineral nutrient rather than a technological food additive in many regions, it does not carry an E-number on European packaging.
