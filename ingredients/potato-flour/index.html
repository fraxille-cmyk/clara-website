Potato flour

Processed ingredientNeutral

Potato flour is a simple, gluten-free flour made from whole dehydrated potatoes, used to retain moisture and add a soft texture to baked goods.

What it is

Potato flour is a fine, pale powder made from whole potatoes that have been cooked, dried, and ground. Unlike potato starch, which is washed out of the tuber to isolate only the pure carbohydrate, this flour contains the entire potato, including its natural fibre, protein, and minerals. It has a distinct, mild potato flavour and a remarkable ability to absorb and hold water, which makes it a familiar and comforting ingredient for anyone who enjoys traditional baking.

How it's made

The journey from field to flour is straightforward and relies on physical processes rather than chemical alterations. Whole potatoes are first washed, peeled, and thoroughly cooked. These cooked potatoes are then mashed and dried on large, heated drums to create thin sheets of dehydrated potato. Finally, these dry sheets are milled and sifted into a fine, uniform flour. Because it uses the whole vegetable, the final powder retains the natural cream colour and nutritional profile of the original potato.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose this flour primarily for its exceptional water-binding properties. In gluten-free baking, it helps to mimic the structure and moisture-retaining qualities of wheat, keeping breads and cakes from becoming dry or crumbly. It also acts as a natural thickener in soups, sauces, and gravies, dispersing smoothly without clumping. Commercially, it extends the shelf life of baked goods by keeping them soft and fresh for longer, which is a cost-effective alternative to synthetic softeners.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. Because it is a whole-food ingredient rather than a chemical additive, it is digested just like a regular cooked potato, providing carbohydrates and a small amount of dietary fibre. No daily intake limit is established because it is classified as a food ingredient rather than an additive. It is generally well tolerated by the human digestive system, making it a reliable option for those who need to avoid gluten or wheat in their daily diet.

What the science says

Regulatory bodies view this ingredient as a standard food item rather than a substance requiring strict chemical limits. The FDA categorises potato products of this nature as generally recognised as safe under its standard food regulations. Because it is simply dehydrated potato, scientific evaluations focus on its nutritional value as a source of complex carbohydrates rather than any potential adverse effects. No international food safety authority has raised any safety concerns regarding its consumption in normal dietary amounts.

Where you'll find it

You will most often spot this ingredient on the labels of gluten-free breads, rolls, pancake mixes, and biscuits, where it helps create a tender crumb. It is also a common addition to packaged dry soups, ready-made gravies, and some extruded potato snacks. On packaging, it is listed simply as potato flour, with no E-number associated with it, as it is classified as a food ingredient rather than a food additive.
