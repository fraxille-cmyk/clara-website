Calcium propionate (E282)

E282AdditiveNeutral · benign

Calcium propionate is a synthetic salt used to prevent mould in bread and baked goods, and Clara rates it as a benign additive.

What it is

Calcium propionate is a synthetic calcium salt of propionic acid, an organic acid that occurs naturally in small amounts in some foods, like Swiss cheese. In the food industry, it is used primarily to keep baked goods fresh by stopping the growth of mould and wild yeasts. While propionic acid itself can have a sharp, pungent smell, this calcium salt form is much easier to handle and has a milder profile, making it highly compatible with flour-based recipes.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers chemically react calcium hydroxide, which is a mineral compound, with propionic acid. The propionic acid used in this process is typically synthesised from petrochemical starting materials. After the reaction takes place, the resulting liquid is crystallised, dried, and ground into a fine white powder or small granules that can be easily measured and mixed into large batches of dough.

Why manufacturers use it

Bread is highly susceptible to mould, especially in warm and humid conditions. Manufacturers use this ingredient because it is incredibly effective at preventing mould growth without interfering with the baking yeast that makes bread rise. It is a highly cost-effective preservative that extends the shelf life of packaged bread from a few days to several weeks, reducing food waste and allowing for efficient distribution across long distances.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Your body processes this compound by breaking it down into calcium and propionic acid, both of which are metabolised naturally just like the fatty acids already present in your diet. Because it is handled so efficiently by human metabolism, EFSA evaluated the substance in 2014 and determined that no acceptable daily intake limit is established, meaning it is considered safe for the general population at the levels used in food.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly reviewed this preservative and found no safety concerns. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated it in 2014 and concluded that it does not accumulate in the body or present any toxicological concern. In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration recognises it as generally recognised as safe, permitting its use in baked goods, cheeses, and confections with no open safety questions or restrictions on its standard commercial applications.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient most commonly in sliced bread, buns, rolls, tortillas, and other packaged baked goods. It also appears in some processed cheeses, pie fillings, and pre-made doughs. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its plain name as calcium propionate, but in Europe and other regions, it may also be declared by its additive number, E282.
