Calcium propionate (E282)

E282AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Calcium propionate is a synthetic preservative used to keep bread and baked goods free from mould, and regulators consider it a benign additive.

What it is

Calcium propionate is the calcium salt of propionic acid, an organic acid that occurs naturally in small amounts in some foods like Swiss cheese. In the food industry, it is used as an antimicrobial agent to keep baked goods fresh. It looks like a fine white powder and dissolves easily in water, releasing propionate ions that help protect food from spoilage without altering the texture of your bread.

How it's made

While propionic acid can be produced by bacteria during fermentation, the commercial ingredient is made synthetically to ensure purity and consistency. Manufacturers react propionic acid with calcium hydroxide, which is a mineral compound. This chemical reaction neutralises the acid and creates calcium propionate, which is then crystallised, dried, and ground into a fine powder ready for use in industrial baking.

Why manufacturers use it

Bakers rely on this ingredient because it is incredibly effective at stopping the growth of mould and certain bacteria that make bread spoil quickly. Unlike other preservatives, it does not interfere with yeast, meaning bread can rise fully and develop its proper texture during baking. It is a highly cost-effective way for manufacturers to extend the shelf life of packaged baked goods, reducing food waste and keeping products soft and fresh on supermarket shelves for days.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This synthetic calcium salt prevents mould in baked goods, and the EFSA has not specified an acceptable daily intake limit for it. Because the body metabolises propionate just like any other natural fatty acid, it does not accumulate in your system. Regulators have found no reason to set a strict daily limit, meaning it is considered safe for the general population in the amounts typically found in food.

What the science says

The safety of this preservative has been thoroughly evaluated by major food authorities. EFSA re-evaluated the substance in 2014 and concluded that there was no need to establish a numerical acceptable daily intake because it is rapidly metabolised and cleared by the body. The FDA also recognises it as generally recognised as safe under its federal regulations. There are no open safety concerns or outstanding questions regarding its use in human food.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in pre-packaged sliced bread, rolls, tortillas, pastries, and other baked goods that need to stay fresh on shelves. On ingredient labels, it is usually listed plainly as calcium propionate, but you might also see it written as E282 or E 282 on European packaging. It is occasionally used in processed cheese products and pre-made doughs to prevent early spoilage.
