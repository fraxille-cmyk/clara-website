Chlorophylls and chlorophyllins (E140)

E140AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Chlorophylls and chlorophyllins are natural green pigments extracted from plants, used to restore colour with no specified acceptable daily intake limit.

What it is

Chlorophylls are the natural green pigments that plants use to capture sunlight and turn it into energy. They are what make grass, spinach, and nettles look green. Chlorophyllins are closely related, water-soluble forms of these same pigments. When you see them on a food label, they are being used as a natural colouring agent to give food a vibrant, appealing green hue that might otherwise be lost during processing.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers harvest green plants such as grass, alfalfa, or nettles. They extract the natural pigments using solvents like acetone or ethanol, which are then carefully removed. The result is a concentrated green extract. For chlorophyllins, the extracted chlorophyll is treated with a mild alkali to make it dissolve easily in water, creating a stable green liquid or powder that is ready to be mixed into food.

Why manufacturers use it

Food processing, especially heating and canning, often destroys the natural green colour of vegetables and fruits, turning them a dull grey or brown. Manufacturers use these pigments to restore that fresh, natural green look. They choose chlorophylls because they are highly effective at low concentrations and provide a more natural appearance than synthetic green dyes, helping products look fresh and appealing on the shelf.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because these pigments are natural components of the green vegetables we eat every day, they are processed by the body in the same way as ordinary food. No limit is established for their daily intake because they are considered exceptionally safe. Regulators have found no reason to set a maximum daily limit, meaning you can consume them without concern about building up unwanted levels in your body.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated these green pigments and found no safety concerns. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated them and confirmed that they do not pose any risk to human health, deciding that setting a numerical daily limit was unnecessary. The US Food and Drug Administration also permits their use in food. Because they are simple plant extracts, they do not accumulate in the body or interfere with normal metabolic processes.

Where you'll find it

You will find these green pigments in a wide variety of products, including green confectionery, jellies, ice creams, liqueurs, and preserved green vegetables or fruits. On a label, they are typically listed as chlorophylls and chlorophyllins, or simply as E140. In some regions, you might see them listed as natural green colour, or under their specific US regulatory name when imported.
