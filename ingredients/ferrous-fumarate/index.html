Ferrous fumarate

FortificantNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Ferrous fumarate is a form of iron added to foods to boost their nutritional value, helping your body carry oxygen and maintain energy levels.

What it is

Ferrous fumarate is a mineral compound that combines iron with fumaric acid, an organic acid found naturally in many plants and fruits. It is a reddish-orange powder that serves as a source of dietary iron, which is an essential nutrient. Your body relies on iron to build red blood cells and transport oxygen from your lungs to the rest of your tissues. Because it is highly bioavailable, meaning your body can absorb and utilise it easily, it is a popular choice for enriching foods and supplements.

How it's made

To produce ferrous fumarate, manufacturers typically react hot aqueous solutions of ferrous sulphate and sodium fumarate. Ferrous sulphate is a mineral salt, while sodium fumarate is derived from fumaric acid. When these two solutions mix, a chemical reaction occurs, causing ferrous fumarate to precipitate out of the liquid as a fine powder. This powder is then carefully filtered, washed with water to remove any remaining salts, dried, and milled to a consistent particle size suitable for mixing into food products.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use ferrous fumarate to fortify products, replacing nutrients lost during processing or boosting the overall nutritional profile. It is particularly favoured over other iron sources because it is highly stable and does not easily oxidise or cause off-flavours in dry foods during storage. It also resists clumping, which helps it distribute evenly through large batches of flour or cereal. This stability ensures that the food retains its intended nutritional value and remains palatable throughout its entire shelf life.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. This mineral is a vital nutrient that is entirely harmless when consumed in the amounts typically found on food labels. The body regulates iron absorption based on its current needs, though extremely high doses from concentrated supplements can cause mild digestive discomfort. The European Food Safety Authority has evaluated its use as a source of iron in foods and food supplements, confirming its safety for the general population. No upper limit is established for its use as a standard food fortificant.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated ferrous fumarate to ensure it is an effective and safe way to deliver iron. The European Food Safety Authority published a scientific opinion confirming that ferrous fumarate is a highly bioavailable source of iron that is safely metabolised by the body. The Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives has also assessed iron compounds, establishing acceptable daily intakes for iron from all dietary sources. Research shows that when used to enrich staple foods, it successfully supports healthy blood mineral levels without causing adverse effects.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in fortified breakfast cereals, infant formulas, baby foods, and enriched flour or grain products. On ingredient labels, it may be listed under its main name, ferrous fumarate, or under its common aliases, which include iron fumarate and fumarato ferroso. Because it is classified as a nutrient fortificant rather than a technological food additive, it does not have an assigned E-number, but it is clearly declared so shoppers can see the nutritional boost it provides.
