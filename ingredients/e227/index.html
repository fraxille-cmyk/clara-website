Calcium hydrogen sulphite (E227)

E227AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Calcium hydrogen sulphite is a synthetic preservative that prevents browning, but EFSA warns that children can easily exceed the daily intake limit.

What it is

Calcium hydrogen sulphite is a synthetic chemical compound that belongs to the sulphite family. In food production, it is primarily used as a preservative and an antioxidant. It is a white, soluble powder that releases sulphur dioxide when it comes into contact with acidic environments, which helps to preserve the freshness and appearance of various foods. While some sulphites occur naturally in fermented foods, this particular version is entirely man-made for industrial use.

How it's made

This ingredient is manufactured through a chemical reaction involving calcium carbonate or calcium hydroxide. Manufacturers pass sulphur dioxide gas through an aqueous suspension of these calcium compounds. This chemical absorption process yields a solution of calcium hydrogen sulphite, which is then carefully purified, concentrated, and dried into a stable powder suitable for addition to food and beverage products.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this additive because it is an exceptionally effective preservative that performs multiple technical roles. It prevents browning in fruits and vegetables, which keeps products looking fresh and appealing on supermarket shelves. It also acts as an antimicrobial agent, stopping the growth of unwanted yeasts, moulds, and bacteria. This dual action extends the shelf life of products far beyond what is naturally possible, reducing waste and lowering distribution costs.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. The primary concern is that EFSA's daily limit of 0.7 mg per kg of body weight can be easily exceeded by toddlers and children who consume foods containing these additives. Additionally, sulphites are well-known triggers for sensitivity reactions, particularly in individuals with sensitive airways, which is why food laws require them to be clearly highlighted on ingredient labels when present above minimal levels.

What the science says

IARC classifies sulphur dioxide and sulphites, including calcium hydrogen sulphite, as Group 3, not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans. However, European safety assessors have raised concerns about cumulative exposure. In a comprehensive re-evaluation, EFSA concluded that high-consuming toddlers and children frequently exceed the group acceptable daily intake. Because sulphites are used across many common food categories, the total daily exposure can quickly add up to levels that exceed what regulators consider optimal for young bodies.

Where you'll find it

You will typically find this preservative in dried fruits, processed potato products, starches, and various soft drinks or alcoholic beverages. On ingredient labels, it is usually listed as calcium hydrogen sulphite, calcium bisulphite, or by its European code, E227. On US labels it is listed as calcium bisulphite. Because it is a sulphite, European regulations require it to be emphasised in the ingredients list, often in bold or capital letters, to alert sensitive shoppers.
