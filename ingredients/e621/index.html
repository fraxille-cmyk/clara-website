Monosodium glutamate (E621)

E621AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Monosodium glutamate is a common flavour enhancer that brings a savoury, umami taste to foods, rated neutral by Clara as it is safe at typical dietary levels.

What it is

Monosodium glutamate is the sodium salt of glutamic acid, which is one of the most abundant naturally occurring amino acids in our world. It is the very substance responsible for the savoury, rich taste known as umami. You already eat it naturally when you enjoy ripe tomatoes, aged cheeses, mushrooms, or soy sauce. In its isolated form, it looks like small white crystals and dissolves easily into liquid, ready to enrich the existing flavours of a dish.

How it's made

While it was originally extracted from seaweed, today monosodium glutamate is made through a modern fermentation process. Manufacturers feed carbohydrate sources, such as sugar beet, sugar cane, or starch from cassava and corn, to specific microbes. These tiny organisms ferment the sugars and produce glutamic acid as a natural byproduct. This acid is then neutralised with sodium, crystallised, washed, and dried into the pure white seasoning that is packaged for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use this ingredient because it is an incredibly efficient flavour enhancer. It intensifies the savoury notes in a recipe, making soups, sauces, and snacks taste much richer and more satisfying without the need for long simmering times or expensive ingredients. It also allows manufacturers to lower the overall sodium content of a product, because combining a small amount of this substance with a little salt creates a much stronger flavour impact than salt alone.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Regulators have evaluated this substance thoroughly and found it to be safe at the levels normally consumed in our daily diets. While some people have reported experiencing temporary physical sensitivities after eating foods containing it, these anecdotal accounts have not been confirmed in controlled clinical trials. No upper limit is established for daily intake in many regions, though some authorities suggest a conservative guidance level for general dietary comfort.

What the science says

The scientific consensus on this ingredient is highly stable. JECFA evaluated the substance and allocated an acceptable daily intake of not specified, which is their term for an additive of very low toxicity. EFSA re-evaluated the additive and established a group acceptable daily intake of thirty milligrams per kilogram of body weight for glutamic acid and its salts. These major scientific reviews conclude that the human body metabolises this substance in the exact same way as the natural glutamates found in everyday vegetables and meats.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of savoury processed foods, including potato crisps, instant noodles, canned soups, gravies, salad dressings, and seasoned meats. On the packaging, it is usually declared plainly by its full name as monosodium glutamate, or represented by its European code, E621. It is also a key component in many commercial seasoning blends and stock cubes where a quick boost of savoury depth is required.
