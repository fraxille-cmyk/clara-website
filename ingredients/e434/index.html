Polysorbate 40 (E434)

E434AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Polysorbate 40 is a synthetic emulsifier used to blend oil and water in processed foods, rated neutral by Clara with a standard daily intake limit.

What it is

Polysorbate 40 is a synthetic substance that acts as an emulsifier, which means it helps ingredients that normally separate, like oil and water, stay smoothly blended together. Chemically, it belongs to a family of compounds known as polyoxyethylene sorbitan esters. It is closely related to other common food additives like polysorbate 60 and polysorbate 80. While it is created in a laboratory, it is built from simple organic building blocks, including sorbitol, which is a sugar alcohol found naturally in fruits, and palmitic acid, a common fatty acid.

How it's made

To manufacture polysorbate 40, producers start with sorbitol and react it with palmitic acid, which is typically derived from vegetable oils. This reaction creates a compound called sorbitan monopalmitate. In the next step, this intermediate compound is chemically modified by reacting it with ethylene oxide. This process, known as ethoxylation, adds polyoxyethylene chains to the molecule, which gives the final ingredient its unique ability to dissolve in both water and oil. The resulting amber-coloured liquid is then purified and prepared for use in food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on polysorbate 40 because it is an exceptionally stable and efficient emulsifier. In processed foods, maintaining a consistent texture over a long shelf life is a major challenge, as fats and water naturally want to separate. This ingredient prevents that separation, ensuring that dressings, sauces, and spreads keep their creamy appearance and do not split. It also helps disperse flavours and colours evenly throughout a product, and it is favoured by companies because it performs reliably under different temperatures and processing conditions where natural emulsifiers might fail.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects the fact that while it is a highly processed, synthetic ingredient, it has been thoroughly evaluated by food safety authorities. EFSA keeps its daily limit of 25 mg per kg of body weight, a group acceptable daily intake established during a comprehensive review. No limit is established by some other bodies for specific individual uses, but the European limit ensures that total exposure across different foods remains within a moderate, well-studied range. There are no active safety concerns or warnings associated with its normal consumption.

What the science says

The safety of polysorbate 40 has been assessed by major international food safety bodies over several decades. When consumed, the body breaks down the ingredient, absorbing the fatty acid portion much like any other dietary fat, while the polyoxyethylene sorbitan part is mostly excreted unchanged. Scientific panels have reviewed animal studies to ensure that regular consumption does not disrupt the digestive system or affect nutrient absorption. The consensus among regulators is that the substance is of no toxicological concern when consumed within the established daily intake limit, and no major gaps in safety data remain.

Where you'll find it

You will find polysorbate 40 in a variety of processed foods that require stable emulsification, such as fine bakery wares, cake mixes, salad dressings, sauces, and whipped toppings. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically listed by its name, polysorbate 40, or by its European code, E 434. On US labels, it is commonly declared as polysorbate 40. It is often used in combination with other emulsifiers to achieve the perfect texture and consistency in packaged desserts and spreads.
