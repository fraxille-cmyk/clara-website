Sulphuric acid (E513)

E513AdditiveNeutral · benign

Sulphuric acid is a synthetic mineral acid used to control acidity in processed foods, and Clara rates it as a benign additive.

What it is

Sulphuric acid is a strong mineral acid composed of sulphur, oxygen, and hydrogen. While the raw chemical is famously corrosive in its concentrated industrial form, the food-grade version is highly diluted and used in tiny amounts. In the food industry, it serves primarily as an acidity regulator, helping to control the pH of a product. It is chemically identical to the acid that occurs naturally in some volcanic environments, though the version in your food is entirely man-made.

How it's made

To manufacture food-grade sulphuric acid, producers start with elemental sulphur, which is often recovered as a byproduct from natural gas and petroleum refining. This sulphur is burned in clean air to create sulphur dioxide gas. Through a catalytic process, this gas is converted into sulphur trioxide, which is then absorbed into water to form highly concentrated sulphuric acid. This acid is purified to remove impurities and diluted to precise, safe concentrations for food processing.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use sulphuric acid because it is an exceptionally efficient and cost-effective acidifier. It can adjust the pH of a large batch of food or beverage with just a few drops, which is much cheaper than using weaker organic acids like citric or malic acid. By lowering the pH, it helps control the growth of unwanted microbes, assists in the processing of starches and sugars, and ensures that other ingredients remain stable during manufacturing.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. When used in food, sulphuric acid is highly diluted and neutralised by other ingredients, turning into harmless sulphate salts that your body processes naturally. Because it poses no risk to human health at these levels, EFSA evaluated the additive in 2019 and determined that it is safe enough to not require a specified acceptable daily intake limit. It does not accumulate in the body and is broken down quickly.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated sulphuric acid and its salts. The European Food Safety Authority reviewed the additive and confirmed that its use in food does not raise safety concerns, meaning no numerical daily limit is necessary. JECFA, the joint FAO and WHO expert committee, also evaluated the substance and reached a similar conclusion, while the US FDA affirms it as generally recognised as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practices.

Where you'll find it

You will find sulphuric acid in a variety of processed foods, including modified starches, alcoholic beverages, cheese products, and some canned vegetables. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its plain name as sulphuric acid, or by its European code, E513. On US labels, it may be listed as sulfuric acid, reflecting the American spelling, but it performs the exact same acidity-regulating role in the product.
