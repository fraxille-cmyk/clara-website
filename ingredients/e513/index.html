Sulphuric acid (E513)

E513AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Sulphuric acid is a synthetic mineral acid used to control acidity; regulators consider it a benign additive with no established daily limit.

What it is

Sulphuric acid is a strong mineral acid made from sulphur, a natural element found in the earth. While the raw acid is highly corrosive in its concentrated form, in the food industry it is used in tiny, highly diluted amounts to adjust acidity. It behaves similarly to other food acids like citric or lactic acid, helping to manage the delicate balance of acidity in a recipe without adding any distinct flavour of its own.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, elemental sulphur is first burned in clean air to create sulphur dioxide gas. This gas is then converted into sulphur trioxide, which is absorbed into water to produce highly pure, concentrated sulphuric acid. This strong acid is then carefully diluted with purified water to precise, food-grade strengths before it is shipped to food manufacturers for use in processing.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this acid primarily as a processing aid and acidity regulator. It is highly efficient at adjusting the pH levels of water and food mixtures during production, which helps control the growth of unwanted microbes and ensures consistent texture. Because it is incredibly strong, only a tiny amount is needed to achieve the desired acidity, making it a highly cost-effective choice compared to milder organic acids.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Because it is highly diluted in finished foods, it poses no risk to your body. EFSA has not specified an acceptable daily intake limit for this substance because its strong acidic taste naturally limits how much can be added to food before it becomes unpalatable. No limit is established by other major regulators either, as the tiny amounts present in food are easily neutralised by the body's natural digestive processes.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this ingredient and found no safety concerns at the levels used in food. EFSA re-evaluated the substance and confirmed that it does not accumulate in the body or interfere with normal nutrition. JECFA and the FDA have also reviewed its use, concluding that the diluted acid behaves simply as a source of hydrogen and sulphate ions, both of which are normal parts of our daily diet and metabolism.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in processed foods, alcoholic beverages, cheese processing, and packaged waters where precise pH control is necessary. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its plain name as sulphuric acid, or by its European code, E513. On US labels it is listed as sulfuric acid, where it is affirmed as a generally recognised as safe substance.
