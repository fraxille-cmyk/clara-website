Calcium lactate (E327)

E327AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Calcium lactate is a benign food additive used to control acidity and firm up canned fruits and vegetables, with an excellent safety record.

What it is

Calcium lactate is a white, crystalline salt formed by combining lactic acid with calcium. While its name might make you think of dairy, it is actually a distinct mineral salt that does not contain lactose. In the kitchen and the food industry, it behaves as a gentle acidity regulator and a firming agent, helping to keep structured foods from turning mushy. It is a natural acid salt that our bodies can easily break down into calcium and lactic acid, both of which are completely familiar to our normal metabolic pathways.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers start by fermenting carbohydrates, such as corn starch or sugar beet syrup, using lactic acid bacteria. This fermentation process yields lactic acid. The resulting acid is then neutralised by adding calcium carbonate or calcium hydroxide, which are simple mineral compounds. The mixture is crystallised, purified, and dried into a fine powder. Although classified as synthetic because of this industrial processing, the raw materials and the fermentation steps mirror natural biological processes.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers rely on this salt for several practical reasons. It acts as an excellent firming agent, reacting with natural pectins in canned fruits and vegetables like tomatoes or apples to keep them crisp and intact during pasteurisation. It also regulates acidity, which helps preserve flavour and extends shelf life by creating an environment where spoilage organisms cannot thrive. Because it dissolves easily and has a neutral taste, it is a highly efficient way to fortify foods with calcium without altering their flavour profile.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. It has an exemplary safety record as a natural acid salt, and our bodies metabolise it just like the lactic acid naturally produced in our muscles during exercise. Because it is so well tolerated, EFSA has determined that no daily limit is established for its consumption. It is considered safe for the general population, including infants and young children, as it poses no toxicological concern when used in normal dietary amounts.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated this substance and found no reason for concern. EFSA re-evaluated lactic acid and its salts in 2018 and confirmed that they do not pose any safety risks at the levels currently used in food. The FDA also recognises it as generally recognised as safe. Because it is a simple mineral salt of a common organic acid, it does not accumulate in the body and is processed without any difficulty by our digestive system.

Where you'll find it

You will frequently spot this ingredient in canned vegetables, sliced packaged fruits, jams, jellies, and sports drinks. It is also used in baking powders and to fortify orange juice or milk alternatives with extra calcium. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its plain name as calcium lactate, but you might also see it listed under its European code, E327, or occasionally as E 327.
