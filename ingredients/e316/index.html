Sodium erythorbate (E316)

E316AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Sodium erythorbate is a synthetic antioxidant used to keep cured meats looking fresh and to stop oils from turning rancid.

What it is

Sodium erythorbate is a food additive that works as an antioxidant. Chemically, it is closely related to vitamin C, sharing a very similar structure, though it does not have the same vitamin activity in our bodies. Instead, it acts as a preservative that keeps foods from reacting with oxygen. It is a white, odourless, crystalline powder that dissolves easily in water, making it highly effective in liquid and wet food mixtures where oxygen can quickly spoil the quality.

How it's made

While it is chemically similar to natural vitamin C, sodium erythorbate is produced synthetically for industrial food use. Manufacturers start with simple sugars, usually derived from corn or sugar beets. These sugars undergo a fermentation process using specialised micro-organisms to produce an intermediate acid. This acid is then purified, chemically reacted with sodium hydroxide to neutralise it, and crystallised into the final sodium salt. This controlled process ensures a highly pure and consistent additive.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use this additive primarily to preserve the appealing pink colour of cured meats and to slow down the oxidation process that makes fats taste stale or rancid. It accelerates the curing process in meats, which allows factories to speed up production lines and lower costs. By reacting quickly with oxygen, it keeps the food looking fresh on supermarket shelves for much longer than untreated products, making it a highly efficient and cost-effective alternative to natural vitamin C.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. It is processed by the body in a similar way to ordinary sugars and organic acids, and it is quickly excreted without building up in our tissues. No strict upper limit is established for daily intake because evaluations show it is highly tolerated by the human body. It does not interfere with vitamin C absorption when consumed in normal dietary amounts, making it a benign addition to the modern food supply.

What the science says

The safety of this additive has been thoroughly evaluated by major international food authorities. JECFA has assessed its use and concluded that it poses no safety concerns at current dietary levels, establishing an acceptable daily intake as not specified. EFSA re-evaluated the substance and confirmed that it does not raise safety concerns, noting that it is rapidly metabolised and cleared from the body. Because it is not used as a vitamin, its evaluation focuses purely on its role as a stable and predictable preservative.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in cured and processed meats, such as bacon, ham, hot dogs, and sliced deli meats, where it keeps the pink colour stable. It also appears in canned fruits, frozen fish, and some soft drinks to stop browning and flavour loss. On packaging, it is listed as sodium erythorbate, E316, or occasionally under its alias e 316, helping shoppers identify its presence in processed foods.
