Sodium erythorbate (E316)

E316AdditiveNeutral · benign

Sodium erythorbate is a synthetic antioxidant used to preserve the colour and flavour of cured meats, and is considered safe with no daily limit.

What it is

Sodium erythorbate is a food additive that works as an antioxidant. Chemically, it is a sodium salt of erythorbic acid, which is a close relative of vitamin C. Although its structure is nearly identical to vitamin C, it has very little vitamin activity in the human body. Instead, it is used purely for its chemical ability to stop oxygen from reacting with food, helping to keep packaged foods looking and tasting fresh for much longer than they would on their own.

How it's made

While it is related to a natural vitamin, sodium erythorbate is produced synthetically for commercial use. Manufacturers start with sugars, typically derived from corn or starch, and ferment them using specialised microorganisms to produce an intermediate chemical called 2-keto-D-gluconic acid. This intermediate is then chemically converted into erythorbic acid. Finally, the acid is reacted with a sodium source, such as sodium hydroxide, to create the stable, highly soluble white crystalline powder that is added to foods.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on this ingredient primarily to preserve the appealing pink colour of cured meats. When meat is cured, oxygen can quickly turn it an unappetising grey or brown. Sodium erythorbate acts as a sacrificial antioxidant, reacting with oxygen before the meat can. It also speeds up the curing process, which allows factories to process meats much faster. It is highly favoured by the food industry because it is incredibly stable, dissolves easily in water, and is much cheaper to use than natural vitamin C.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is processed by the body in a similar way to ordinary dietary sugars and organic acids, it does not accumulate in your tissues. EFSA evaluated the additive in 2016 and determined that it is of no toxicological concern, meaning no numerical acceptable daily intake limit is established. It is widely accepted as a low-risk ingredient that performs a useful preservation role without presenting a hazard to human health.

What the science says

The scientific consensus on this additive is highly stable and consistent across major food authorities. Both European and American regulators have thoroughly assessed its safety profile. Because it is closely related to vitamin C, the body metabolises it easily and excretes any excess in urine. Extensive reviews have found no evidence of adverse effects even at high dietary concentrations, which is why the Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives and EFSA both concluded that a strict numerical limit on daily consumption is unnecessary.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in processed meat products, such as bacon, ham, hot dogs, sausages, and deli meats. It also appears occasionally in canned fruits, vegetables, and soft drinks to prevent browning and flavour loss. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its plain name as sodium erythorbate, but you might also see it written as sodium ascorbate or under its European designation, E316.
