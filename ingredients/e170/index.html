Calcium carbonate (E170)

E170AdditiveNeutral · benign

Calcium carbonate is a common mineral used as a natural white colour and firming agent, and is generally considered safe and benign.

What it is

Calcium carbonate is a naturally occurring mineral that makes up a large portion of the earth's crust. It is the very same substance that forms chalk, limestone, marble, and the shells of sea creatures like snails and pearls. In the food world, it serves as a mineral supplement, a natural white pigment, and a texturiser. It is an abundant compound that our bodies are already highly accustomed to processing, as calcium is a fundamental nutrient for building and maintaining our skeletal structure.

How it's made

To make the food-grade ingredient, manufacturers typically start with natural limestone deposits. The stone is mined, crushed, and washed to remove impurities. It then undergoes a purification process which can involve dissolving it and recrystallising it to ensure it is extremely clean and free from heavy metals. This refined powder is exceptionally fine and bright white, ready to be added to food products without altering their natural characteristics or introducing unwanted flavours.

Why manufacturers use it

Food producers rely on this mineral for several practical reasons. As a colour, it provides a clean, opaque white background that makes other colours pop, or simply gives a clean look to confectionery coatings and chewing gum. It also acts as an anti-caking agent, keeping powdered foods flowing freely instead of clumping together. In canned fruits and vegetables, it serves as a firming agent, reacting with natural pectins to keep the pieces from turning mushy during heat processing.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is a basic mineral that the human body uses daily, it does not pose the risks associated with synthetic chemical additives. The European Food Safety Authority has evaluated its safety as a food additive and concluded that it does not require a specific numerical daily limit, meaning it can be used responsibly in foods at the levels needed to achieve its technical effects. It is simply a source of calcium that the body metabolises normally.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly reviewed this mineral over many decades. The Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives established that no limit is established for its daily intake because it is of no toxicological concern at normal dietary levels. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated it as a food colour and confirmed its safety, noting that the calcium absorbed from it behaves exactly like calcium from any other dietary source. It is widely accepted as a straightforward, well-understood mineral.

Where you'll find it

You will spot this ingredient in a wide variety of everyday items, including chewing gum, white-coated sweets, baked goods, and fortified plant milks like oat or almond milk. On ingredient labels, it might be listed under its common name, calcium carbonate, or as E170. Depending on where the product was packaged, you might also see it written in other European languages as carbonate de calcium, carbonato de calcio, or kalsiumkarbonat.
