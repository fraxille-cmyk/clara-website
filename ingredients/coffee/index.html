Coffee is a natural, single-ingredient whole food made from roasted beans, with no added chemicals or artificial preservatives.

What it is

Coffee is a familiar beverage brewed from the roasted seeds of the coffee plant, which are commonly called beans. These seeds grow inside bright red or yellow fruits known as coffee cherries. It is a natural whole food that people have enjoyed for centuries, prized for its rich aroma, complex flavours, and the natural stimulating effect of its caffeine content. Because it is a single-ingredient food, it contains no added sugars, artificial colours, or synthetic preservatives in its pure form.

How it's made

The journey begins with harvesting the ripe cherries from coffee plants, which are grown in tropical regions around the equator. The outer fruit is removed to extract the green seeds inside, which are then dried. These green beans are roasted at high temperatures, a process that coaxes out their characteristic dark colour and rich aroma. Once roasted, the beans are either packaged whole or ground into a fine powder, ready to be steeped in hot water to release their soluble compounds.

Why manufacturers use it

Food and drink manufacturers use coffee as a primary ingredient in bottled beverages, ice creams, desserts, and baked goods to deliver its distinctive, robust flavour. It provides a natural depth of taste and a bitter balance to sweet recipes that synthetic flavourings struggle to replicate. Additionally, the natural caffeine in coffee is highly valued by manufacturers of energy drinks and pre-workout formulations who want to offer a plant-based source of stimulation instead of synthetic caffeine powder.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. This rating reflects that coffee is a single-ingredient whole food with nothing added to it. In its pure form, it is not an additive and carries no regulatory warnings or restrictive limits for the general population. While caffeine can make some people feel jittery or disrupt sleep if consumed in large amounts late in the day, the food itself is a wholesome, traditional ingredient that fits comfortably into a balanced diet.

What the science says

Major food authorities have evaluated coffee and its natural components extensively over many decades. EFSA established a scientific opinion on the safety of caffeine, advising that daily intakes up to 400 mg from all sources do not raise safety concerns for healthy adults, except for pregnant women who are advised to limit their intake to 200 mg. The consensus among global food safety bodies is that moderate consumption is entirely compatible with a healthy lifestyle, as the beverage naturally contains beneficial antioxidants and active plant compounds.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in bags of whole bean coffee, ground coffee, and instant granules, as well as in coffee-flavoured ice creams, yoghurts, liqueurs, and chocolate-covered beans. On ingredient lists, it is usually declared simply as coffee, roasted coffee, or ground coffee. On imported European products, you might spot it listed under its traditional regional names such as cafe, caffe, caffè, café, kaffe, kaffee, koffie, or even in Greek characters as καφές.

Sources
