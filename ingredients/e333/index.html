Calcium citrates (E333)

E333AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Calcium citrates are mineral salts used to control acidity and add calcium to food. Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive.

What it is

Calcium citrates are the calcium salts of citric acid, which is the natural compound that gives lemons and limes their sharp, sour bite. In the kitchen and the food industry, these white, odourless powders act as mineral supplements and acidity regulators. They are closely related to the calcium found naturally in dairy products and the citric acid found in citrus fruits, combining these two familiar elements into a single, stable ingredient that dissolves easily in liquids.

How it's made

To make calcium citrates on a commercial scale, manufacturers start with citric acid, which is typically produced by fermenting simple sugars using a common, food-grade microscopic fungus. This citric acid is then neutralised by adding calcium hydroxide or calcium carbonate, which are mineral compounds derived from natural limestone. The reaction causes calcium citrates to precipitate out of the liquid mixture as a fine white powder, which is then filtered, washed thoroughly with water, and dried for use in food.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose calcium citrates because they perform several helpful technical roles at a low cost. As acidity regulators, they help maintain a stable pH level, which keeps flavours consistent and stops ingredients from separating or spoiling. They also act as firming agents, helping to keep canned vegetables and processed fruits from turning mushy. Additionally, because they are highly bioavailable, they are a popular choice for fortifying plant-based milks and breakfast cereals with extra calcium without altering the texture or taste.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because calcium citrates are made of two substances that the human body is entirely familiar with, they are processed easily by our normal metabolic pathways. The calcium is absorbed to help maintain bones, while the citrate is converted into energy. No limit is established for daily intake because the body handles it so efficiently, and regulators consider it a very low-risk ingredient when used in normal dietary amounts.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated calcium citrates and found them to be of no safety concern. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the substance and confirmed that it does not pose any safety risks at the levels currently used in food. Because the components are natural parts of the human diet, regulators have not felt the need to set a restrictive acceptable daily intake. It is widely accepted as a reliable way to add calcium to foods and drinks.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of everyday foods, especially calcium-fortified plant milks, breakfast cereals, infant formulas, jams, jellies, and canned fruits. On food packaging, it might be listed by its specific chemical names, such as calcium citrate or calcium citrates, or under its European code, E333. On imported products, you might also spot its French, Spanish, or Italian names, such as citrate de calcium or citrato de calcio.
