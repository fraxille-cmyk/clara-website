Expeller pressed canola oil

Processed ingredientNeutral

Expeller pressed canola oil is a refined vegetable oil extracted mechanically from rapeseed, providing a source of fats for cooking and baking.

What it is

Expeller pressed canola oil is a refined vegetable oil that comes from the seeds of the canola plant, which is a specialised variety of rapeseed. It is a source of dietary fats, containing mostly unsaturated fats that remain liquid at room temperature. The term expeller pressed tells us about how the oil was extracted from the seed, indicating a mechanical process rather than one that relies on chemical solvents. It is a familiar, everyday cooking oil with a neutral flavour and a high smoke point, making it highly versatile in both home kitchens and commercial food production.

How it's made

To make this oil, harvested canola seeds are first cleaned and gently heated to help release the oil. They are then fed into a continuous screw press, known as an expeller. This machine physically squeezes the seeds under high pressure to force the oil out. Because this mechanical squeezing generates friction and heat, it is different from cold pressing, but it still avoids the use of chemical solvents like hexane which are common in standard oil extraction. After pressing, the crude oil is filtered and refined to remove free fatty acids, natural pigments, and strong odours, resulting in a clear, stable oil.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose expeller pressed canola oil because it is highly stable, has a neutral flavour, and offers a clean label appeal. Its lack of a distinct taste means it does not interfere with the intended flavours of snacks, baked goods, or dressings. It also has a high smoke point, which keeps it from breaking down during high-heat processing like frying or baking. Commercially, using a mechanically pressed oil allows brands to appeal to shoppers who prefer ingredients processed without chemical solvents, serving as a premium alternative to standard chemically extracted vegetable oils.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. As a refined vegetable oil, it is a source of fats and is processed to be stable for everyday consumption. Because it is an ingredient rather than a food additive, no daily intake limit is established by food authorities. It is digested by the body in the same way as other dietary fats, breaking down into fatty acids to be used for energy or cellular structure. While it is a refined fat and should be consumed as part of a balanced diet, the mechanical extraction method means it carries no residues of chemical solvents.

What the science says

Food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated canola oil and its extraction methods. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration recognise refined canola oil as a standard dietary fat. Assessments focus on the fatty acid profile, noting its high proportion of monounsaturated fats and omega-three fatty acids. Because expeller pressing is a physical extraction method, regulators do not raise any chemical safety concerns regarding solvent residues. No regulator has established an upper limit for its consumption, and it is treated as a standard food ingredient rather than an additive.

Where you'll find it

You will find expeller pressed canola oil in a wide variety of processed foods, including potato chips, crackers, baked goods, mayonnaise, salad dressings, and pre-made sauces. On a food label, it is typically listed plainly as expeller pressed canola oil, expeller pressed rapeseed oil, or sometimes simply as expeller pressed vegetable oil. Because it does not have an E-number, it is always declared by its common name, often highlighted by brands to show that the oil was extracted mechanically rather than with chemical solvents.
