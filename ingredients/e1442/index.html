Hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate (E1442)

E1442AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

A modified starch used to thicken and stabilise foods, digested by the body much like ordinary starch.

What it is

Hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate is a modified food starch. It starts as a natural plant starch, usually sourced from maize, tapioca, or wheat, which is then chemically altered to improve how it behaves when cooked, frozen, or stored. Unlike raw starch, which can lose its structure and weep water when cooled, this modified version maintains a smooth, uniform texture under demanding conditions. It behaves as a highly resilient thickening agent that keeps foods looking freshly made.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers treat natural plant starch with two chemical agents. First, propylene oxide is introduced to add hydroxypropyl groups to the starch molecules, which helps the starch hold onto water. Next, phosphorus oxychloride is used to cross-link the starch chains, reinforcing their physical structure. The resulting modified starch is thoroughly washed, purified, and dried into a fine white powder that is ready to be blended into food formulations.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this modified starch because it is incredibly robust. It resists high heat, survives acidic environments, and does not break down when subjected to mechanical stirring or pumping during factory processing. Crucially, it prevents water from separating out of gels and sauces during freeze-thaw cycles. This makes it an excellent, cost-effective choice for extending the shelf life of frozen meals and chilled desserts where ordinary flour or cornstarch would fail.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Once consumed, the body processes this modified starch in a very straightforward way. Enzymes in the digestive tract break it down into simpler sugars, much like they do with the natural starches found in everyday foods like potatoes or rice. Because it is digested so easily, it does not accumulate in the body and poses no safety concerns for the general population. No daily intake limit is established by regulatory authorities.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this ingredient and found no reason for concern. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated modified starches and concluded that they do not pose a safety risk at the levels currently used in food. Because it behaves so similarly to dietary starch, regulators have not felt the need to set a numerical acceptable daily intake. It is permitted for use in a wide variety of foods across the European Union, the United States, and globally.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in many processed foods that require a stable, creamy texture, such as yoghurts, frozen desserts, instant gravies, canned soups, and salad dressings. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its full name, hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate, or by its European code, E1442. On some labels, particularly those from North America, it may simply be listed as modified food starch or modified starch.
