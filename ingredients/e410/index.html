Locust bean gum (E410)

E410AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

A natural plant gum extracted from carob seeds that thickens foods and is widely regarded as safe with no specified daily intake limit.

What it is

Locust bean gum is a natural plant gum that comes from the seeds of the carob tree, a Mediterranean evergreen. It is a complex carbohydrate made of sugars called galactomannans, which are very similar to those found in other plant gums like guar gum. In the kitchen, it acts as a highly efficient thickener, helping to bind water and create smooth textures in liquid and semi-solid foods. People have used carob pods for thousands of years, and this gum is simply the soluble fibre extracted from the very centre of those seeds.

How it's made

The journey starts with the harvest of carob pods, which are gently crushed to separate the hard seeds from the sweet pulp. The seeds are then de-husked, often using a brief heat treatment or a mild acid wash to remove the tough outer skin. Once the outer layer is gone, the germ is separated from the endosperm, which is the part of the seed containing the valuable gum. This endosperm is milled into a fine, off-white powder. The entire process is physical and thermal, meaning the gum itself is not chemically modified during production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose locust bean gum because it is an exceptionally strong thickener that works beautifully in combination with other gums. When mixed with xanthan gum or carrageenan, it creates a much stronger, more elastic gel than any of those ingredients could manage alone. This synergy is incredibly useful in ice cream, where it prevents large ice crystals from forming during storage and ensures a slow, smooth melt. It also stabilises cream cheese, yoghurt, and sauces, keeping them from separating into liquid and solid parts on the shelf.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is a natural plant fibre that passes through the human digestive system largely unchanged, it is widely regarded as safe. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated it and confirmed there is no need to set a numerical daily limit, meaning it can be used in foods as needed. It does not accumulate in the body, and because it is not absorbed into the bloodstream, it has no chemical impact on our internal organs. Some people might experience mild bloating if they eat very large amounts of it, just as they would with any high-fibre food.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this ingredient and found no safety concerns. The European Food Safety Authority reviewed the gum and concluded that it does not raise any safety worries for the general population, even when consumed in large quantities. In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration has affirmed it as generally recognised as safe. Because it behaves like a soluble dietary fibre, scientific studies show it is fermented by beneficial bacteria in the large intestine, which is a normal and healthy part of digestion.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of dairy and plant-based alternatives, including ice cream, cream cheese, yoghurt, and dairy-free milks. It is also common in salad dressings, prepared sauces, and gluten-free baked goods, where it helps mimic the binding properties of gluten. On ingredient lists, it is usually declared by its common name as locust bean gum, carob bean gum, or under its European code, E410. On US labels, it is typically listed as locust bean gum or carob bean gum.
