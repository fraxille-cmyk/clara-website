Elderberry extract

Processed ingredientNeutral

Elderberry extract is a natural ingredient made from elderberries, used to add a rich purple colour and a sweet, tart berry flavour to foods.

What it is

Elderberry extract is a concentrated liquid or powder derived from the dark purple berries of the elder tree, known scientifically as Sambucus nigra. These berries have been gathered in European woodlands for centuries to make traditional cordials and preserves. The extract is rich in natural pigments called anthocyanins, which give the berries their deep, characteristic colour. In the food industry, it serves as a natural alternative to synthetic additives, bringing both a vibrant hue and a subtle, fruity taste to everyday products.

How it's made

The process begins with harvesting ripe elderberries, which are washed and crushed to release their juices. This juice is heated gently to neutralise naturally occurring compounds that are difficult to digest in raw berries. The liquid is then filtered to remove the seeds and skins. To create a concentrated extract, the water is evaporated under a vacuum, which preserves the delicate colours and flavours. For a powder version, the concentrated liquid is spray-dried onto a carrier, resulting in a fine, soluble powder ready for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers choose elderberry extract primarily for its dual ability to colour and flavour products naturally. It provides a beautiful, deep red to purple shade that consumers find appealing, especially in an era where synthetic dyes are increasingly unpopular. It also adds a pleasant, slightly tart berry flavour. Because it is stable under moderate heat and in acidic environments, it is an excellent choice for pasteurised foods and beverages, helping products maintain their appealing appearance and taste throughout their shelf life.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. As a natural food extract rather than a synthetic chemical, it is considered a benign ingredient with a long history of traditional use. No daily intake limit is established for elderberry extract when used as a natural colouring or flavouring foodstuff. It is processed to ensure it is entirely safe for consumption, as the gentle heating step during manufacturing removes any compounds from the raw berry that might otherwise cause mild stomach discomfort.

What the science says

The European Food Safety Authority and the Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives have evaluated elderberry concentrates and extracts. Because it is classified as a colouring foodstuff rather than an isolated food additive, it is regulated as a food ingredient. Scientific assessments confirm that the traditional preparation methods yield a stable, wholesome ingredient. Researchers have focused on its high concentration of natural antioxidants, which help protect cells from oxidative stress, though its primary role in packaged food remains sensory rather than therapeutic.

Where you'll find it

You will find elderberry extract in a variety of products, including fruit jellies, yoghurts, herbal teas, soft drinks, and fruit gums. On a food label, it is typically listed simply as elderberry extract, elderberry concentrate, or natural elderberry flavouring. Because it is a whole food concentrate rather than a purified additive, it does not have an E-number, making it a popular choice for brands aiming for a clean, simple ingredient list that shoppers can easily recognise.
