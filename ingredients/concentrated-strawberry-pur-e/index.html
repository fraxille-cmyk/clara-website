Concentrated strawberry purée

Processed ingredientNeutral

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Concentrated strawberry puree is real fruit with most of its water removed, used to add natural sweetness, flavour, and colour to foods.

What it is

Concentrated strawberry puree is simply fresh strawberries that have been crushed, strained to remove the seeds and tough fibrous parts, and then gently heated to evaporate a large portion of their natural water content. This process leaves behind a thick, deeply flavoured fruit paste. It is much more intense in flavour and sweetness than fresh fruit, but it retains the natural sugars, acids, and vibrant red pigments that make strawberries taste and look the way they do. It is a real food ingredient rather than a chemical additive.

How it's made

The journey begins with harvesting ripe strawberries, which are thoroughly washed and sorted to remove stems and leaves. The fruit is then crushed and passed through fine sieves to separate the smooth flesh from the seeds. To turn this standard puree into a concentrate, the mixture is heated under a vacuum. Using a vacuum allows the water to boil off at a much lower temperature, which helps preserve the delicate natural flavours, bright red colour, and heat-sensitive nutrients of the fresh strawberries. No artificial chemicals are used during this concentration process.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose this ingredient because it delivers a powerful burst of natural strawberry flavour and a rich red hue without adding excess liquid to a recipe. Excess water can ruin the texture of baked goods, yoghurts, and fruit bars, or make them spoil more quickly. By using a concentrate, makers can achieve the desired fruitiness and sweetness while keeping the recipe stable. It also allows them to sweeten products naturally, which appeals to shoppers looking to avoid refined white sugars on the ingredient list.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. Because this is a concentrated fruit product rather than a chemical additive, it does not carry the safety concerns associated with synthetic colourings or artificial sweeteners. It is digested just like the sugars and fibres found in whole fruit, though the concentration process means the natural sugars are more densely packed than they would be in fresh strawberries. No daily intake limit is established for this ingredient because it is classified as a food rather than a food additive.

What the science says

Regulatory bodies view concentrated fruit purees as standard food ingredients rather than additives, so they do not undergo the same chemical safety evaluations as preservatives or synthetic colourings. The European Commission and the FDA categorise concentrated fruit purees as safe food ingredients that can be used freely in accordance with good manufacturing practices. Scientific assessments of fruit concentrates generally focus on their nutritional profile, noting that while they retain beneficial plant compounds like polyphenols, the evaporation process concentrates the natural fructose and glucose.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of packaged foods, including fruit bars, fruit snacks, yoghurts, ice creams, smoothies, and baked goods like tarts or cereal bars. On the packaging, it is typically listed under plain names such as concentrated strawberry puree or concentrated strawberry purée. Because it is a whole food ingredient and not a chemical additive, it does not have an E-number and is never listed as a preservative or artificial flavouring.
