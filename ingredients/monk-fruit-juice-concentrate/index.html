Monk fruit juice concentrate

Processed ingredientNeutral

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Monk fruit juice concentrate is a natural, non-nutritive sweetener made from a small melon, used to sweeten foods without adding sugar.

What it is

Monk fruit juice concentrate is a highly concentrated liquid sweetener derived from a small, green melon native to southern China. It is also known as luo han guo. Unlike traditional fruit juices that contain high levels of fructose and glucose, this concentrate is prized for its intense sweetness, which comes from unique antioxidants called mogrosides. It provides a sweet taste without the calories or the rapid rise in blood sugar associated with standard sugars, making it a popular natural alternative to artificial sweeteners.

How it's made

The production begins with harvesting ripe monk fruits, which are then crushed to extract their raw juice. This juice is mixed with hot water to help dissolve the sweet compounds. The liquid undergoes a filtration process to remove the solid pulp, seeds, and bitter-tasting proteins. Next, the clarified juice is passed through a special carbon filter that concentrates the sweet mogrosides while removing unwanted off-flavours. Finally, the liquid is evaporated under gentle heat to create a stable, thick concentrate.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose monk fruit juice concentrate because it delivers intense sweetness, often up to two hundred times sweeter than sucrose, using only a tiny amount. This allows them to formulate low-calorie or sugar-free products while keeping a clean label that appeals to shoppers seeking natural ingredients. It is highly stable under heat and across different acidity levels, meaning it performs well in baked goods, pasteurised beverages, and dairy products where other natural sweeteners might break down or lose their flavour.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. As a natural, non-nutritive sweetener derived from monk fruit, it is generally well tolerated by the human body. Because it is a concentrated fruit juice rather than an isolated chemical additive, it is treated as a food ingredient rather than a synthetic sweetener. No limit is established for its daily intake in many regions, and it does not carry the digestive discomfort risks often associated with sugar alcohols or the chemical concerns linked to some artificial alternatives.

What the science says

Regulators have scrutinised the safety of monk fruit extracts and concentrates thoroughly. The FDA has evaluated several submissions and responded with no objections to its status as safe for use in foods. Because the sweet components, the mogrosides, pass through the upper digestive tract largely unabsorbed and are broken down by beneficial bacteria in the colon, scientific reviews show no adverse effects on human metabolism. It does not raise blood glucose levels, making it a helpful tool for managing dietary sugar intake.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of sugar-reduced and health-focused products, including yoghurts, protein powders, ready-to-drink teas, carbonated soft drinks, and keto-friendly baked goods. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared plainly as monk fruit juice concentrate, monk fruit concentrate, or sometimes by its traditional name, luo han guo juice concentrate. It does not have an E-number as it is classified as a food ingredient rather than a food additive.
