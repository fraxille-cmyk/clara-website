Stannous chloride (E512)

E512AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Stannous chloride is a mineral-based tin salt used in canned vegetables to preserve colour, with a strict daily limit set by regulators.

What it is

Stannous chloride is an inorganic salt made of tin and chlorine. In the world of food science, it is classified as a colour retention agent. While the word tin might make you think of the metal can itself, this substance is a specific chemical compound that dissolves easily in water. It acts as a powerful antioxidant, helping to keep pale vegetables looking bright and fresh rather than dull and grey when they are sealed away in jars or cans for long periods.

How it's made

This ingredient is produced through a direct chemical reaction. Manufacturers treat metallic tin with gaseous chlorine or hot hydrochloric acid. This process oxidises the tin, turning it into a soluble salt. The resulting solution is then cooled and crystallised to create high-purity stannous chloride crystals, which are ground into a fine white powder suitable for food processing. Although it is a manufactured chemical, its origin is entirely mineral.

Why manufacturers use it

When vegetables like white asparagus or green beans are processed and stored in cans, exposure to oxygen and heat normally causes them to turn an unappealing brown colour. Stannous chloride prevents this browning by reacting with oxygen before the food can oxidise. It also reacts with the natural pigments in the vegetables to stabilise their original colour. For manufacturers, it is a highly efficient and cost-effective solution that ensures canned goods look freshly packed even after months on the shelf.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is based on its mineral origin and the fact that it has a numeric acceptable daily intake of 0.5 mg per kg of body weight. EFSA established this limit to ensure consumers do not ingest too much tin, which in very high doses can cause temporary stomach irritation. Because it is only allowed in a few specific products, most people eat far less than this limit, making it a low risk in a balanced diet.

What the science says

Regulators have thoroughly evaluated the safety of this tin salt. EFSA completed a full re-evaluation of the additive and confirmed that it does not accumulate in the body, as most ingested tin is simply not absorbed and passes straight through. The FDA also recognises it as safe for its specific preservation roles. There are no outstanding safety concerns or open questions regarding its use, provided that manufacturers adhere to the strict maximum limits set for canned foods.

Where you'll find it

You will primarily find this ingredient in canned and bottled white or pale vegetables, with white asparagus being the most common example. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically listed by its name, stannous chloride, or by its European code, E512. On US labels it is listed as stannous chloride, where it is permitted as a preservative and colour stabiliser in a wider variety of canned foods.
