Adipic acid (E355)

E355AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Adipic acid is a synthetic acidity regulator used to add a tart flavour, with a daily limit established by regulators to ensure it remains safe.

What it is

Adipic acid is an organic acid that does not occur widely in nature, though tiny traces can be found in beetroots. In the food industry, it is used as a synthetic additive to control acidity and give foods a sharp, tart taste. It belongs to the same family of acidulants as citric acid and malic acid, which are often used to mimic the natural tanginess of fresh fruit. It is a white, crystalline powder that dissolves slowly in water, making it particularly useful in dry mixes.

How it's made

While some organic acids are brewed by fermenting sugars, adipic acid is produced through a purely synthetic chemical process. Manufacturers typically start with cyclohexane, a compound derived from petroleum. This raw material is oxidised using nitric acid under controlled industrial conditions. The resulting mixture is then cooled, crystallised, and purified to remove any chemical residues, leaving behind a highly pure, food-grade white powder that is ready to be added to food products.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose this ingredient because it is exceptionally good at maintaining a stable level of acidity over a long shelf life. It has a unique property of resisting moisture absorption, which prevents dry powder mixes from clumping together in their packaging. It also provides a smooth, lingering tartness rather than a sudden burst of sour flavour. This slow-release acidity makes it highly efficient and cost-effective compared to other natural acids that might absorb moisture and spoil the product texture.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is given because it is a synthetic ingredient with a numeric acceptable daily intake of 40 milligrams per kilogram of body weight. EFSA established this limit to ensure that even people who eat a lot of processed foods do not consume too much of it. Within this established daily limit, the additive is processed by the body without any known accumulation or adverse effects, and it is not considered a concern for the general population.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated this additive to ensure its safety in our diet. EFSA re-evaluated the substance in 2019 and confirmed the group acceptable daily intake of 40 milligrams per kilogram of body weight for adipic acid and its salts. The human body metabolises it easily, breaking it down and excreting it through urine. No safety concerns or adverse effects have been identified at the levels currently used in food, and no regulator has raised any open concerns regarding its everyday use.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in dry powdered products like gelatin desserts, instant puddings, cake mixes, and powdered drink mixes where moisture control is essential. It is also used in some processed cheeses, spreads, and bakery products to regulate acidity. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its plain name as adipic acid, or by its European code, E355, which helps shoppers identify it across different brands.
