Sodium propionate (E281)

E281AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Sodium propionate is a synthetic salt used to prevent mould in baked goods, and Clara rates it as a benign additive with no daily intake limit.

What it is

Sodium propionate is the sodium salt of propionic acid, a naturally occurring organic acid. In nature, propionic acid is produced in small amounts by bacteria during fermentation, such as in the ripening of Swiss cheese. The sodium salt version is a white, crystalline powder that dissolves easily in water. It acts as an effective preservative, particularly against moulds and certain bacteria, helping to keep food fresh for longer than it would naturally stay on the shelf.

How it's made

While propionic acid occurs naturally in some foods, the sodium propionate used in commercial food production is made synthetically in a factory. Manufacturers produce it by reacting propionic acid with sodium hydroxide or sodium carbonate. This chemical reaction neutralises the acid, resulting in a stable salt. The mixture is then purified, crystallised, and dried into a fine powder that is easy to measure, store, and blend into large batches of dough or batter.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use sodium propionate because it is highly effective at preventing the growth of mould and rope bacteria, which can ruin baked goods within days. Unlike some other preservatives, it does not interfere with the yeast used to make bread rise, allowing bakers to maintain the texture and volume of their loaves. It is also highly soluble and blends smoothly into doughs, making it a cost-effective way to extend the shelf life of pre-packed bakery items.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. The body metabolises sodium propionate in the same way it processes natural fatty acids, and it is considered to be of no toxicological concern. Because of its safety profile, EFSA did not specify an acceptable daily intake limit when they re-evaluated it. It is authorised for use in many countries, though the European Union restricts its use to specific categories like pre-packed sliced bread and certain cheeses to prevent excessive consumption.

What the science says

Scientific panels have thoroughly evaluated sodium propionate and found no reason for concern. EFSA reviewed the additive in 2014 and concluded that there was no need to establish a numerical acceptable daily intake because it is rapidly metabolised and cleared by the body. The US FDA also affirms it as generally recognised as safe. No regulator has raised safety concerns regarding its use at the levels typically found in food, and there are no reports of adverse reactions in the general population.

Where you'll find it

You will primarily find this preservative in pre-packed sliced bread, rolls, tortillas, pastries, and other baked goods that need to remain fresh on supermarket shelves. It is also sometimes used in processed cheese products. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is listed as sodium propionate or by its E-number, E281. On US labels, it is typically declared as sodium propionate, and it may occasionally appear on imported foods with the space as E 281.
