Sodium propionate (E281)

E281AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Sodium propionate is a synthetic salt used to keep bread and baked goods fresh by preventing mould growth, and it is considered safe with no established limit.

What it is

Sodium propionate is the sodium salt of propionic acid, an organic acid that occurs naturally in small amounts in some foods like Swiss cheese. In the food industry, it is used as a preservative to keep baked goods fresh. It looks like a white crystalline powder or transparent crystals and dissolves easily in water. While propionic acid itself has a very sharp, pungent smell, this salt form is much easier for bakers to handle and has a milder odour that does not interfere with the aroma of fresh bread.

How it's made

Although propionic acid is produced naturally by certain bacteria during fermentation, the sodium propionate used in food is made synthetically in a factory. Manufacturers produce it by reacting propionic acid with sodium hydroxide or sodium carbonate. This chemical reaction neutralises the acid, creating a stable salt. The resulting liquid is then purified, crystallised, and dried into a fine powder, ready to be mixed into commercial doughs and batters.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this ingredient primarily to prevent the growth of mould and certain bacteria in baked goods. Bread and cakes are warm and moist when they emerge from the oven, making them ideal breeding grounds for spores. Adding a tiny amount of this salt extends the shelf life of pre-packed bread by several days. It is highly favoured by industrial bakers because it does not interfere with the action of yeast, unlike some other common preservatives, allowing bread to rise perfectly while still protecting it from spoilage.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is based on the fact that the substance is processed by the body in the same way as natural fatty acids, showing no toxicological concern. Because it is handled so easily by human metabolism, EFSA has not specified an acceptable daily intake, meaning no limit is established for daily consumption. It is considered a benign additive that poses no known risk to health when consumed in normal dietary amounts.

What the science says

Scientific panels have thoroughly evaluated this preservative and found no reason for concern. EFSA re-evaluated the substance in 2014 and confirmed that it does not accumulate in the body, as it is quickly metabolised and excreted. The FDA also affirms its safety, designating it as generally recognised as safe. Because it has shown no adverse effects in safety studies, regulators have not felt the need to set a strict numerical limit on how much a person can safely consume each day.

Where you'll find it

You will find this preservative almost exclusively in bakery products, pre-packed sliced bread, tortillas, pastries, and some processed cheeses. On a food label, it is typically listed by its full name as sodium propionate or by its European code, E281. On US labels it is listed as sodium propionate, where it is restricted to specific technical uses in baking to ensure it is only used in the minimum quantity necessary to prevent spoilage.
