Propane-1,2-diol alginate (E405)

E405AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

A seaweed-derived thickener and stabiliser that is chemically modified, with an established daily limit of fifty-five milligrams per kilogram of body weight.

What it is

Propane-1,2-diol alginate is a food additive used to thicken liquids and keep ingredients from separating. It is a modified form of alginic acid, which is a natural substance found in the cell walls of brown seaweeds. While it starts in the ocean, it undergoes chemical changes to make it more stable and soluble in water than raw seaweed extracts. This allows it to perform reliably in acidic foods where ordinary seaweed thickeners would fail.

How it's made

The production process begins with harvesting brown seaweed, which is washed and treated to extract alginic acid. This extracted acid is then chemically reacted with propylene oxide under controlled conditions. This reaction attaches propylene glycol groups to the alginate structure, transforming it into propane-1,2-diol alginate. The resulting substance is washed, dried, and milled into a fine, cream-coloured powder that dissolves easily in water.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers choose this ingredient because it is an exceptionally resilient stabiliser. In acidic environments like salad dressings, fruit syrups, and carbonated drinks, other thickeners often clump or lose their strength. This additive keeps oils and waters beautifully emulsified and maintains a smooth texture over a long shelf life. It also stabilises the foam in beers and aerated dairy products, ensuring a creamy mouthfeel and a consistent appearance that shoppers expect.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. It is processed safely by the body, with the majority of it passing through the digestive tract without being absorbed. EFSA keeps its daily limit of fifty-five milligrams per kilogram of body weight. Because it has a set limit rather than an unlimited safety rating, Clara keeps a neutral eye on it, though it presents no safety concerns for the general population at the levels typically found in our food.

What the science says

This ingredient has been thoroughly evaluated by international food safety authorities. EFSA re-evaluated the substance in 2018 and confirmed that it does not raise safety concerns, establishing a clear acceptable daily intake. Animal studies show that it behaves much like dietary fibre in the gut, passing through largely intact, while the small amount that is broken down is metabolised without any adverse effects. No regulatory body has raised concerns regarding its use in food.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of processed foods, particularly salad dressings, ice creams, beer, cottage cheese, and fruit-flavoured beverages. On ingredient lists in the UK and Europe, it is typically written as propane-1,2-diol alginate or by its E-number, E405. On US labels, it is often listed as propylene glycol alginate, where the FDA permits its use as a multipurpose food additive.
