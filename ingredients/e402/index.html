Potassium alginate (E402)

E402AdditiveNeutral · benign

Potassium alginate is a natural thickener extracted from brown seaweed that is used to stabilise food textures, and Clara rates it as a benign additive.

What it is

Potassium alginate is a natural substance derived from the cell walls of brown seaweed, which grows abundantly in cold water oceans. Chemically, it is the potassium salt of alginic acid, a complex carbohydrate that helps seaweeds remain flexible in turbulent waters. In your food, it behaves much like other plant-based soluble fibres, absorbing water to form a thick, stable gel. It is closely related to sodium alginate, another common seaweed extract, but uses potassium to help build its gel structure.

How it's made

The production process begins with harvesting brown seaweed, which is washed and chopped into small pieces. An alkaline solution is used to dissolve the alginic acid from the plant material, separating it from the insoluble seaweed fibre. This mixture is filtered to remove any remaining plant debris. Potassium hydroxide is then added to the liquid, which reacts with the alginic acid to form potassium alginate. The resulting substance is precipitated, washed, dried, and milled into a fine, cream-coloured powder ready for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers value this ingredient for its exceptional ability to thicken liquids and stabilise emulsions without changing the flavour of the food. It creates a smooth, uniform texture in dairy alternatives, spreads, and sauces, preventing ingredients from separating on the shelf. Because it forms a gel in the presence of certain minerals, it is also used to create structured foods and to help retain moisture in baked goods. It is a highly efficient, plant-based alternative to animal-derived gelatin.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. This ingredient has an excellent safety record and is completely free from carrageenan, a different seaweed extract that some shoppers prefer to limit. Because it is not absorbed by the body and simply passes through the digestive system as dietary fibre, regulators have found no reason to establish a strict daily limit. No limit is established for its intake, and it is widely accepted as a gentle, non-toxic ingredient that poses no health concerns for the general population.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated this seaweed extract and confirmed its safety. EFSA re-evaluated alginic acid and its salts in 2017, concluding that there was no need to establish a numerical acceptable daily intake because the substance is not absorbed and shows no adverse effects. JECFA reached a similar conclusion during its evaluation, confirming its safety for use in food. The FDA also recognises it as generally safe for human consumption, treating it as a reliable, well-understood dietary fibre.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of processed foods, including ice cream, low-fat spreads, salad dressings, canned soups, and gluten-free bakery products. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its plain name as potassium alginate, but you may also see it written as E402 or e 402 on European packaging. It is popular in vegan and vegetarian products where a plant-based gelling agent is required to replace gelatin.
