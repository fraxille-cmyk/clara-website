Glyceryl diacetate (diacetin) (E1517)

E1517AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Glyceryl diacetate is a synthetic carrier used to dissolve and stabilise food flavourings, rated neutral by Clara with no established daily limit.

What it is

Glyceryl diacetate, often called diacetin, is a chemically modified compound derived from glycerol. Glycerol itself is a natural substance found in all fats, but in this case, it undergoes a chemical process to attach two acetic acid groups. Acetic acid is the very same compound that gives vinegar its characteristic sour taste and sharp smell. The resulting clear, colourless, and virtually odourless liquid is highly effective at dissolving other substances, making it a useful tool in modern food production.

How it's made

To manufacture this ingredient, producers react glycerol with acetic acid or acetic anhydride under controlled laboratory conditions. This chemical reaction, known as esterification, links the acetic acid molecules directly to the glycerol backbone. Because the reaction can produce a mixture of different compounds, the liquid is carefully distilled and purified to isolate the diacetate form. The final product is a highly pure, synthetic liquid that meets strict food-grade specifications before it is sold to food manufacturers.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers do not use this substance to change the texture or taste of the food itself, but rather as a carrier solvent. Many concentrated flavourings and essential oils are highly intense and do not mix easily into large batches of food. By dissolving these flavours in glyceryl diacetate first, manufacturers can distribute the taste evenly throughout a product. It is a highly stable liquid that does not break down easily, helping to preserve the strength and quality of delicate flavourings during storage.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects its long history of use in the food industry and its status as a chemically modified compound with no specified acceptable daily intake limit. Because it is used in tiny amounts solely to carry flavourings, it does not accumulate in the body. Regulators have evaluated its safety thoroughly and found no reason to set a restrictive daily limit, meaning it is considered safe for the general population when used in standard commercial quantities.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have evaluated this compound and found that the body processes it naturally. Once consumed, enzymes in the digestive system break it down into glycerol and acetic acid, two substances that the body routinely metabolises from regular food. JECFA evaluated the substance and established that no limit is necessary for its daily intake. No major regulatory body has raised safety concerns regarding its use as a carrier, and there are no active investigations into its biological effects.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient primarily in highly flavoured processed foods, such as baked goods, sweets, beverages, and chewing gum. Because it is classified as a carrier, it is often not listed on the main ingredient panel if it only enters the food as part of a flavouring mixture. When it is declared, it appears as glyceryl diacetate, diacetin, or by its European code E1517. On US labels it is listed as diacetin or glyceryl diacetate.
