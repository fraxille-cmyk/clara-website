Methyl p-hydroxybenzoate (E218)

E218AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

A synthetic preservative used to stop mould and yeast from growing in food, with a set daily limit that is easy to stay within.

What it is

Methyl p-hydroxybenzoate, often called methylparaben, is a synthetic preservative. It belongs to a family of compounds known as parabens, which are structurally similar to benzoic acid, a compound found naturally in many fruits and vegetables. While some parabens occur in nature, the version used in processed food is always made in a factory to ensure purity and consistency. It is a fine, white crystalline powder that has no odour and dissolves easily in water, making it highly effective at protecting liquid and moist foods.

How it's made

This ingredient is produced through a chemical process called esterification. Manufacturers take p-hydroxybenzoic acid, an organic compound, and react it with methanol, a simple alcohol. This reaction takes place under controlled heating in the presence of an acid catalyst, such as sulphuric acid, which helps the molecules bond. Once the reaction is complete, the resulting mixture is cooled, crystallised, washed thoroughly with water to remove any leftover chemicals, and then dried into the final powder that is added to food.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use this ingredient because it is an exceptionally reliable preservative that stops the growth of mould, yeast, and bacteria. It is particularly valued because it remains highly stable and active across a wide range of acidity levels, unlike many other preservatives that only work in highly acidic foods. By preventing spoilage, it extends the shelf life of products significantly. It is also highly cost-effective, requiring only tiny amounts to keep food fresh during transport and storage on supermarket shelves.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. It is processed efficiently by the human body, which breaks it down and excretes it in urine without any accumulation. EFSA keeps its daily limit of 10 mg per kg of body weight, a level established in 2004 that is very difficult to exceed through a normal diet. Because it has a numeric limit rather than an unlimited safety rating, Clara advises keeping an eye on how many preserved foods you consume, though it is considered safe for the general population.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this compound and found no reason for concern at normal dietary levels. EFSA reviewed the additive and confirmed its acceptable daily intake, noting that it does not accumulate in tissues. The FDA also recognises it as safe for use in food. Researchers have studied how the body metabolises the substance, showing that enzymes in the liver and kidneys quickly convert it into a harmless acid that is easily eliminated, meaning it does not linger in your system.

Where you'll find it

You will find this preservative in a variety of processed foods, particularly liquid-based products, baked goods, syrups, and confectionery. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its full chemical name, methyl p-hydroxybenzoate, or simply as E218. On US labels it is listed as methylparaben. It is also commonly found in cosmetics and personal care products like lotions and shampoos, where it performs the exact same protective role to keep the products free from microbial growth.
