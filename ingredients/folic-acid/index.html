Folic acid

FortificantNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Folic acid is a synthetic form of vitamin B9 used to fortify foods, helping the body build cells and maintain overall health.

What it is

Folic acid is the man-made version of folate, which is also known as vitamin B9. In nature, folate is found in leafy green vegetables, beans, and citrus fruits, where it plays a vital role in helping our cells divide and grow. Because natural folate is quite delicate and breaks down easily during cooking and storage, scientists created folic acid as a more stable alternative. This synthetic version is highly soluble and easily absorbed by the human body, making it an ideal choice for boosting the nutritional value of everyday foods.

How it's made

Folic acid is produced through a precise chemical synthesis in a controlled laboratory setting. The process combines specific organic compounds, including a derivative of pteridine, para-aminobenzoic acid, and glutamic acid, which is an amino acid. These components are reacted together, purified, and crystallised to create a highly stable, bright yellow-orange crystalline powder. This pure powder is then thoroughly tested to ensure it meets strict food-grade standards before being added to flour, cereals, and other everyday food products.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use folic acid primarily for nutritional fortification. It is an incredibly stable nutrient that can withstand the high heat of baking and the long shelf life of packaged goods without losing its potency. This stability makes it far superior to natural folate for commercial food production. By adding it to staple foods like wheat flour and breakfast cereals, manufacturers can help ensure that people receive an adequate daily intake of this essential vitamin, which is crucial for cellular health and development.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Folic acid is a vital nutrient that is harmless at the levels typically found on food labels. Because it is a water-soluble vitamin, any excess that your body does not need is simply flushed out in your urine. The European Food Safety Authority has established a tolerable upper intake level of one milligram per day for adults from fortified foods and supplements, which is well above the amount you would normally consume from a standard diet.

What the science says

Public health authorities worldwide have thoroughly evaluated folic acid and recognise its importance in human nutrition. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration have both assessed its safety and efficacy, leading to mandatory fortification programmes in many countries to support maternal health and early cellular development. Scientific consensus confirms that the synthetic form is highly bioavailable, meaning the body absorbs and utilises it very efficiently. No safety concerns exist when it is consumed within the established daily guidelines.

Where you'll find it

You will commonly find this ingredient in fortified breakfast cereals, white flour, bread, pasta, and infant formulas. On food labels, it may be listed under its English name or various European aliases such as vitamin b9, folate, acide folique, acido folico, folsäure, folsyra, folsyre, foliumzuur, or ácido fólico. Because it is classified as a nutrient rather than a technological food additive, it does not have an E-number.
