Egg white

Whole foodGood

Egg white is a natural, protein-rich whole food used to bind, thicken, and aerate foods without adding fat.

What it is

Egg white is the clear liquid found inside an egg, surrounding the yellow yolk. It is also known as albumen and consists of about ninety percent water and ten percent proteins. In nature, its job is to protect the yolk and provide nutrients for a growing embryo. In our kitchens and food factories, it is valued as a highly functional ingredient that delivers structure and protein without the fat and cholesterol found in the yolk. It is a familiar, traditional food that our bodies know how to digest easily.

How it's made

To make the egg white ingredients used in packaged foods, whole chicken eggs are first washed and sanitised. Mechanical crackers then split the shells and carefully separate the liquid albumen from the yolk. This liquid can be filtered and pasteurised to ensure it is clean and stable. Depending on how food manufacturers need to use it, the liquid is either packaged as is, frozen, or spray-dried into a fine, stable powder that can be stored on shelves for a long time and rehydrated when needed.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on egg white because of its incredible ability to change texture. When whipped, its proteins trap air to create stable foams for meringues and souffles. When heated, these proteins coagulate, turning from a liquid into a firm gel that binds other ingredients together in sausages, vegetarian meat alternatives, and baked goods. It is a highly efficient binder and texturiser that performs better than many starch alternatives, helping foods keep their shape and moisture during shipping and storage.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. It is a wholesome, nutrient-dense ingredient that provides a high-quality source of protein containing all the essential amino acids our bodies need to function. Because it is a whole food rather than an additive, no daily intake limits are established by regulatory bodies. The only safety consideration is for individuals with a specific allergy to egg proteins, who must read labels carefully to identify its presence in packaged foods. For everyone else, it is a nourishing and beneficial addition to a balanced diet.

What the science says

Food safety authorities worldwide recognise egg white as a standard, nutritious food ingredient. The FDA regulates its identity and pasteurisation standards under its food standards, ensuring that commercial egg products are processed to eliminate any unwanted micro-organisms. Because it is a traditional agricultural product rather than a chemical additive, it does not undergo the additive evaluation processes of EFSA or JECFA, as its safety as a staple food is well established through centuries of human consumption and extensive nutritional research.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of supermarket products, including baked goods, gluten-free breads, meringues, nougat, marshmallows, and vegetarian meat substitutes. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared simply as egg white, liquid egg white, dried egg white, or egg albumen. Because it is a whole food rather than a food additive, it does not have an E-number and is never listed with an additive function class.

Sources
