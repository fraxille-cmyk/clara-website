Acetylated distarch phosphate (E1414)

E1414AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Acetylated distarch phosphate is a modified food starch used to thicken and stabilise foods, which your body digests just like ordinary starch.

What it is

Acetylated distarch phosphate is a modified food starch. It starts as a natural starch, typically sourced from corn, tapioca, potato, or wheat, which is then chemically altered to improve how it behaves when cooked, frozen, or stored. Unlike ordinary flour or cornstarch, which can turn watery or clumpy under harsh conditions, this ingredient maintains its structure. It belongs to a family of modified starches designed to help processed foods keep their appealing texture from the factory floor to your kitchen table.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers treat natural starch with specific chemical agents. They use phosphorus oxychloride to link the starch molecules together, a process called cross-linking, which makes the starch highly resistant to heat and acid. They also treat it with acetic anhydride to introduce acetyl groups, which prevents the starch from turning into a firm gel when it cools down. After these chemical reactions are complete, the modified starch is thoroughly washed, purified, and dried into a fine white powder that is ready to be added to food formulations.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on this ingredient because it is an exceptionally robust thickener and stabiliser. In industrial food production, ingredients are subjected to intense heat, high-pressure pumping, and acidic environments, all of which would cause ordinary starch to break down and lose its thickness. This modified version keeps sauces smooth, prevents water from separating out of frozen meals when they thaw, and ensures that canned foods retain their desired thickness throughout their shelf life. It is a highly cost-effective way to maintain a consistent texture.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Once consumed, the body processes this ingredient in a very straightforward manner. Enzymes in your digestive tract break down the modified starch molecules, and the body metabolises the resulting components just like the starch found in everyday foods like potatoes or rice. There are no reports of adverse reactions or sensitivities associated with normal dietary intake. Because it behaves so similarly to ordinary dietary starch during digestion, regulators have not found any reason to restrict its use in everyday food products.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this modified starch and found no safety concerns. Because it is digested and absorbed in the same manner as natural starches, no acceptable daily intake limit is established, meaning it can be used freely in foods at the levels needed to achieve the desired texture. EFSA re-evaluated modified starches and confirmed they do not pose a safety concern for the general population. The chemical modifications are minimal enough that the substance does not accumulate in the body or alter normal digestive processes.

Where you'll find it

You will commonly find this ingredient in convenience foods that require a stable texture, such as frozen ready meals, canned soups, gravies, instant puddings, salad dressings, and mayonnaise. On ingredient labels, it is typically declared by its full name, acetylated distarch phosphate, or represented by its European additive code, E1414. It may also simply be grouped under the broader term of modified starch or modified food starch depending on local labelling regulations.
