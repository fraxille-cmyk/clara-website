Alginic acid (E400)

E400AdditiveNeutral · benign

Alginic acid is a natural thickener extracted from brown seaweed that Clara rates as a benign additive with no safety concerns.

What it is

Alginic acid is a natural carbohydrate found in the cell walls of brown seaweed. It is a structural substance that gives marine algae their strength and flexibility in turbulent ocean waters. When extracted, it behaves as a natural dietary fibre that can absorb many times its own weight in water. It is closely related to the alginate salts often used in cooking and medicine, and it behaves similarly to other plant-based gelling agents like pectin or agar.

How it's made

The production of this ingredient begins with the harvesting of wild brown seaweeds. These marine plants are washed and treated with an alkali solution, such as sodium carbonate, to dissolve the structural alginic acid from the plant tissue. The resulting thick liquid is filtered to remove any remaining seaweed solids. An acid is then added to the mixture, which causes the alginic acid to precipitate out as a solid. This solid is collected, washed, dried, and milled into a fine, cream-coloured powder.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers value this ingredient for its exceptional ability to thicken liquids and stabilise mixtures. It prevents ingredients from separating, ensuring that salad dressings stay blended and ice creams remain smooth without forming large ice crystals. It also acts as a gelling agent, helping to create a firm structure in jellies and fillings. Because it performs reliably at room temperature and does not require heat to set, it is often more convenient and cost-effective than other gelling agents.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. It is a natural seaweed extract that is processed without chemical modification of its core structure. Some shoppers worry about seaweed extracts because of concerns surrounding degraded carrageenan, but EFSA has confirmed that alginic acid is entirely free from this substance. It is not absorbed by the body and simply passes through the digestive system as a soluble dietary fibre, posing no safety concerns for the general population.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this ingredient and found it to be safe. Because it behaves as a simple dietary fibre and shows no adverse effects even at very high dietary levels, regulators have not felt the need to establish a numerical limit for daily intake. EFSA re-evaluated the substance and confirmed its safety, declaring that no numerical acceptable daily intake is necessary. The FDA also recognises it as safe for use in food, affirming its status as a generally recognised as safe substance.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of processed foods, including ice cream, puddings, salad dressings, pie fillings, and processed cheese. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically listed by its name as alginic acid or by its European code, E400. On US labels, it is simply declared as alginic acid. It is often grouped with other stabilisers and thickeners near the end of the ingredient list.
