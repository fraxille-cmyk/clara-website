Concentrated raspberry purée

Processed ingredientNeutral

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Concentrated raspberry puree is real raspberry fruit with most of its water removed, used to add natural flavour, colour, and sweetness to foods.

What it is

Concentrated raspberry puree is simply whole raspberries that have been crushed, sieved to remove the seeds, and then heated to evaporate a large portion of their natural water content. This process leaves behind a thick, deeply coloured, and intensely sweet paste. Unlike a simple juice concentrate, a puree retains the fine, pulpy flesh of the fruit, which gives it a more substantial texture and a richer, more authentic fruit character that closely resembles fresh raspberries.

How it's made

The journey begins with ripe raspberries which are harvested, washed, and sorted. These berries are gently heated to soften their structure before being passed through fine screens to separate the smooth pulp from the tough seeds and skins. This smooth pulp is then placed under a vacuum, which allows the water to evaporate at a lower temperature, preserving the bright colour and delicate flavour of the fruit. The resulting concentrated paste is pasteurised to ensure it stays fresh during transport and storage.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose this concentrate because it delivers a powerful burst of natural raspberry flavour and a vibrant red hue without adding artificial additives. It is much easier to store, transport, and blend into recipes than fresh, delicate berries which spoil quickly. By using a concentrate, manufacturers can keep the texture of their products consistent and appealing, while also using the natural sugars in the fruit to sweeten their recipes without relying solely on refined white sugar.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. Because it is a concentrated fruit product rather than an isolated chemical additive, it does not carry the risks associated with synthetic substances. It is a wholesome ingredient that provides natural fruit sugars alongside some of the original vitamins and minerals of the berry. However, because the water has been removed, the natural sugars are highly concentrated, meaning it should be enjoyed in moderation as part of a balanced diet.

What the science says

As a standard food ingredient rather than a food additive, this puree is not subject to the strict daily intake limits that regulators apply to synthetic chemicals. The European Commission and the FDA recognise concentrated fruit purees as safe, traditional food ingredients rather than additives. Scientific assessments focus on its nutritional profile, noting that while the concentration process removes some heat-sensitive vitamins, it retains many of the beneficial plant compounds and dietary fibres found in the original fruit.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in the dairy aisle in fruit yoghurts and desserts, as well as in smoothies, juices, ice creams, and fruit fillings for pastries. On packaging, it is typically listed under its plain name as concentrated raspberry puree or concentrated raspberry purée. Because it is a real food ingredient and not a chemical additive, it does not have an E-number and is never listed as one.

Sources
