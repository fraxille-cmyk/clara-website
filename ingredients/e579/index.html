Ferrous gluconate (E579)

E579AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

An iron-based additive used to keep black olives dark, with a strict daily limit but no major health concerns when consumed in normal amounts.

What it is

Ferrous gluconate is an iron salt of gluconic acid, a mild organic compound that occurs naturally in honey and fruit. In the food industry, it is used primarily as a colour retention agent to stabilise dark shades. It provides a highly bioavailable source of iron, meaning the human body can absorb it easily. While it can be used as a nutritional supplement to boost iron levels, in packaged food it serves a purely cosmetic purpose, ensuring that certain foods maintain a deep, uniform appearance.

How it's made

Although gluconic acid is found in nature, the food additive is produced synthetically on an industrial scale. Manufacturers typically prepare it by reacting barium gluconate or calcium gluconate with ferrous sulphate, or by directly combining hot gluconic acid with iron powder. The resulting solution is filtered, concentrated, and crystallised into a fine, pale greenish-yellow or grey powder. This synthetic process ensures a highly pure substance that is free from the organic impurities often found in natural extracts.

Why manufacturers use it

Food producers use this additive because it is incredibly effective at stabilising dark pigments, particularly in canned olives. Without it, green olives that are artificially oxidised to turn black would quickly fade back to an unappealing brown or greyish hue during storage. Ferrous gluconate reacts with the natural compounds in the fruit to lock in a rich, glossy black colour. It is preferred over other iron salts because it is highly soluble in water and does not impart a strong metallic taste to the brine.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. While it is synthetic, it is generally processed by the body in the same way as dietary iron. EFSA has established a numeric acceptable daily intake of 0.8 mg per kg of body weight for iron from all food additive sources. Consuming iron in very high quantities can cause mild digestive discomfort, but the tiny amounts used to colour food are well below any level that would cause concern for the general population.

What the science says

This ingredient has been thoroughly evaluated by major food safety authorities. EFSA re-evaluated the additive and confirmed its safety profile, maintaining the established daily limit of 0.8 mg per kg of body weight. JECFA has also reviewed the substance and found no toxicological concerns at typical exposure levels. Because it is a simple iron salt, the body metabolises it by separating the iron from the gluconate, absorbing what it needs and naturally excreting the rest, leaving no accumulation of synthetic residues.

Where you'll find it

You will almost exclusively find this additive in canned or jarred black olives, where it is used to maintain their dark colour. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared as ferrous gluconate, E579, or E 579. On US labels, it is listed as ferrous gluconate, where the FDA permits its use as a nutrient supplement and colouring aid under specific federal regulations. It is rarely found in other food categories, as its primary technological function is highly specific to olive processing.
