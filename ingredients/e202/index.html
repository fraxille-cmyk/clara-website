Potassium sorbate (E202)

E202AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Potassium sorbate is a widely used preservative that keeps food fresh by stopping the growth of mould and yeast, with a very strong safety record.

What it is

Potassium sorbate is the potassium salt of sorbic acid, a natural compound first found in the berries of the mountain ash tree. In the food world, it acts as a mild preservative that is highly effective at keeping yeast and mould from growing. It is a white, crystalline powder that dissolves easily in water, where it breaks down into sorbic acid, the active part that keeps food fresh. Because it does not alter the smell or flavour of food when used in tiny amounts, it is a favourite choice for preserving delicate recipes.

How it's made

While sorbic acid occurs naturally in some wild berries, the potassium sorbate used in food is made in a laboratory to ensure purity and consistency. Manufacturers produce it by neutralising synthetic sorbic acid with potassium hydroxide. Sorbic acid itself is typically made through a chemical process involving crotonaldehyde and ketene. The resulting mixture is purified, crystallised into a white powder or small pellets, and dried. This process creates an ingredient that is chemically identical to the natural compound but pure enough for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use this ingredient because it is incredibly effective at stopping the growth of mould, yeast, and some bacteria, which extends the shelf life of products. It is especially useful in foods with a slightly acidic pH, such as yoghurts, cheeses, and fruit sauces, where other preservatives might not work as well. It is highly stable, easy to mix into liquids, and does not change the texture of the food. By choosing this ingredient, manufacturers can distribute products over longer distances without the risk of spoilage before they reach your kitchen.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. It has a very strong safety record because our bodies metabolise it just like the natural fatty acids found in everyday foods, breaking it down into water and carbon dioxide. The only documented concern is a rare skin-contact sensitivity, which is mostly a consideration for cosmetics rather than food. EFSA has established a daily limit of eleven milligrams per kilogram of body weight, which is a generous threshold that most people never come close to reaching in their daily diet.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this preservative over many decades. JECFA and EFSA have both reviewed the safety data and confirmed that it is processed safely by the human body. In its re-evaluation, EFSA set a temporary group acceptable daily intake for sorbic acid and its salts, which was later finalised after new data confirmed no genetic safety concerns. The consensus among international regulators is that it is a highly predictable and well-understood ingredient that poses no concern for the vast majority of consumers.

Where you'll find it

You will find this preservative in a wide variety of supermarket items, including processed cheeses, yoghurts, dried fruits, wine, baked goods, and soft drinks. On food labels, it is usually listed plainly as potassium sorbate or by its European code, E202. It is also common in personal care products like shampoos and moisturisers to keep them fresh on bathroom shelves.
