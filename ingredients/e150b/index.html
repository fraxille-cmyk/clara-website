Caustic sulphite caramel (E150b)

E150bAdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

A chemically modified brown food colour made by heating sugars with sulphites, considered of limited concern with a set daily limit.

What it is

Caustic sulphite caramel is a dark brown food colour, one of four distinct classes of caramel colouring used in the food industry. Unlike the simple caramel you might make in your kitchen by heating sugar, this version is chemically modified. It is specifically designed to remain stable in acidic environments and to resist fading when exposed to light or heat, making it a highly reliable tool for keeping processed foods looking consistently appealing over a long shelf life.

How it's made

This colour is produced through a controlled chemical process of heating food-grade carbohydrates, such as glucose or sucrose, in the presence of sulphite compounds. These carbohydrates can come from corn, wheat, or starch. The addition of sulphites under high temperatures and pressures alters the chemical structure of the sugars, creating a complex mixture of dark compounds. No ammonium compounds are used in this specific class of caramel, which distinguishes it from other caramel colours.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose this additive because it provides a highly stable, rich brown hue that does not separate or fade during storage. It is particularly valued for its compatibility with acidic liquids, where other natural colours might clump or lose their intensity. Commercially, it is far more stable and cost-effective than using raw, unprocessed caramel, allowing factories to achieve the exact same shade of brown in every single batch of product.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This brown food colour is made by heating carbohydrates with sulphite compounds, and it has been assigned a numeric acceptable daily intake of 300 mg per kg of body weight. EFSA keeps this daily limit to ensure consumers do not ingest excessive amounts of the sulphite by-products created during manufacturing. While it is generally well tolerated, individuals who have a known sensitivity to sulphites may want to exercise caution when consuming products containing this colour.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this colour and found no toxicological concern at normal levels of consumption. EFSA re-evaluated the additive and established a group acceptable daily intake of 300 mg per kg of body weight for caramel colours, which was later refined to apply specifically to this class. The US FDA also permits its use, listing it as a safe colour additive. No regulator has identified any urgent safety concerns, and no limit is established beyond the standard daily intake guideline.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of packaged foods, including vinegar, whiskey, brandy, balsamic dressings, and certain sauces or gravies. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared as caustic sulphite caramel, caramel colour, or by its European code, E 150b. On US labels, it is often simply listed as caramel colour, though the specific manufacturing class is rarely distinguished on American packaging.
