Strawberries

Whole foodGood

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Strawberries are a whole fruit packed with natural vitamins, minerals, and dietary fibre, making them an excellent and nutritious addition to your food.

What it is

Strawberries are the bright red, juicy fruits of the Fragaria plant, which belongs to the rose family. Unlike most fruits, their seeds sit on the outside of their skin rather than the inside. They are loved for their sweet, slightly tart flavour and their vibrant colour. In the kitchen and on food labels, they are a whole food rather than an additive, prized for bringing natural sweetness, moisture, and a familiar, comforting taste to a wide variety of everyday products.

How it's made

These fruits grow on low-lying herbaceous plants in temperate regions across the globe. Farmers harvest them by hand when they reach peak ripeness, as they do not continue to sweeten after being picked. For use in packaged foods, fresh strawberries are washed, sorted, and either kept whole, sliced, pureed, or concentrated into a syrup. Some are freeze-dried to remove water while preserving their shape, intense flavour, and natural nutrients, ready to be added to breakfast cereals or snack bars.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use real strawberries to provide authentic flavour, natural colour, and appealing texture that synthetic alternatives cannot quite replicate. In products like yoghurts, jams, and baked goods, strawberries add a desirable fruitiness and natural sugars, which can help reduce the need for added artificial sweeteners. Using real fruit also allows manufacturers to list a recognisable, wholesome ingredient on the front of the pack, which appeals to shoppers looking for simple, minimally processed options.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As a whole fruit, it is a highly nutritious ingredient that provides essential vitamins, minerals, and dietary fibre to support your body. There are no regulatory limits or safety concerns associated with eating strawberries, as they are a natural food rather than a chemical additive. They are widely celebrated by nutritionists and food authorities alike for their positive contribution to a balanced, wholesome diet.

What the science says

Food authorities view strawberries as a beneficial component of a healthy diet. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration recognise them as a rich source of vitamin C, folate, and active plant compounds called anthocyanins, which give the fruit its red colour. These natural compounds help protect cells from oxidative stress and support normal blood vessel function. No regulator has established an upper limit for their consumption, and they are universally classified as a wholesome, nourishing food.

Where you'll find it

You will find strawberries in the bakery aisle, the dairy case, and the freezer section. They commonly appear in fruit yoghurts, jams, fruit spreads, breakfast cereals, muesli, cereal bars, and fruit smoothies. On the ingredient list, they are simply declared as strawberries, strawberry puree, strawberry juice concentrate, or freeze-dried strawberries, with no E-number since they are a whole food rather than a food additive.
