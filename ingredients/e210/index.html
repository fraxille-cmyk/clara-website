Benzoic acid (E210)

E210AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Benzoic acid is a synthetic preservative that stops mould and yeast growth, but EFSA warns that children can easily exceed the safe daily intake limit.

What it is

Benzoic acid is a preservative that occurs naturally in some fruits, such as cranberries, bilberries, and plums, as well as in spices like cinnamon and cloves. In the food industry, however, the version added to products is a synthetic compound. It is a simple organic acid that exists as a white, crystalline powder. It belongs to a family of preservatives called benzoates, which are widely used to keep food fresh and stop spoilage. It is chemically related to other common preservatives like sodium benzoate.

How it's made

While benzoic acid can be extracted from natural plant sources, this is not commercially viable for mass food production. Instead, manufacturers produce it synthetically on an industrial scale. The primary method involves the chemical oxidation of toluene, a liquid hydrocarbon derived from petroleum, using oxygen from the air. This reaction is typically assisted by a catalyst, such as cobalt or manganese salts, to speed up the process. The resulting mixture is then purified, crystallised, and dried to create a highly pure powder suitable for food use.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use benzoic acid because it is an exceptionally effective and inexpensive agent against yeasts, moulds, and some bacteria. It works best in acidic environments, where it can easily penetrate the cell walls of microorganisms and disrupt their internal chemistry, stopping them from multiplying. This makes it ideal for extending the shelf life of acidic foods and beverages. Without it, these products would spoil quickly, leading to waste and potential food safety issues. It is often chosen over other preservatives because it does not alter the appearance of the food.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. The primary issue is that the acceptable daily intake can be exceeded by young children. EFSA established a safe daily limit of 5 mg per kg of body weight in 2016. However, EFSA exposure models showed that toddlers and children who regularly consume products containing this preservative can easily go over this limit. Because younger children have a lower body weight, even small portions of foods containing this additive can push their intake past the recommended threshold, which is why Clara advises caution.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have evaluated benzoic acid multiple times to ensure its use does not compromise human health. EFSA completed a major re-evaluation of the additive and its salts in 2016, confirming the acceptable daily intake limit but highlighting the risk of overexposure in younger age groups. The FDA also reviews its use, maintaining its status as generally recognised as safe when used within strict limits. Researchers continue to monitor how the body metabolises benzoates, noting that while the liver normally filters them out efficiently, keeping overall exposure low is important for vulnerable populations.

Where you'll find it

You will find this preservative in a variety of acidic foods and drinks, including soft drinks, fruit juices, salad dressings, pickles, condiments, and jams. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared as benzoic acid or by its European code, E210. On US labels, it is listed as benzoic acid. It is often used alongside other benzoate salts, so you may see it grouped with sodium benzoate or potassium benzoate on the back of the pack.
