Calcium stearoyl-2-lactylate is a synthetic emulsifier used to improve the texture of baked goods, with a moderate daily intake limit.

What it is

Calcium stearoyl-2-lactylate is a synthetic food additive that helps blend ingredients that would normally separate, such as oil and water. It is a combination of lactic acid, stearic acid, and calcium. Lactic acid is a natural compound found in fermented foods, while stearic acid is a common fatty acid found in animal and vegetable fats. By combining these elements, chemists create a highly effective compound that behaves like a natural fat but has unique properties that help stabilise food mixtures.

How it's made

This ingredient is manufactured through a chemical synthesis process. Manufacturers react lactic acid with stearic acid in the presence of calcium hydroxide, which acts as a neutralising agent. The stearic acid used in this process is typically derived from vegetable oils like palm oil, though animal fats can also be used. The resulting mixture is chemically modified, purified, and then cooled and ground into a fine, off-white powder that can be easily mixed into commercial food formulations.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on this additive primarily as an emulsifier and dough strengthener. In commercial baking, it interacts with the proteins and starches in flour to make dough stronger, more elastic, and easier to handle in large machines. It also helps bread retain moisture, which keeps the crumb soft and extends the shelf life of packaged baked goods. By preventing the starch from crystallising too quickly, it keeps bread from going stale, offering a cheaper way to maintain freshness.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is based on its synthetic origin and the fact that it has a numeric limit on its daily consumption. EFSA established an acceptable daily intake of 22 mg per kg of body weight for this substance. When consumed, the body breaks it down into its individual components, calcium, lactic acid, and stearic acid, which are then metabolised just like the fats and acids naturally present in our regular diet.

What the science says

Food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this compound and found no safety concerns when it is consumed within the established limits. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated the additive and confirmed its safety profile, noting that the body processes its components efficiently. Because it breaks down into common dietary fats and acids, it does not accumulate in the body. No regulatory body has identified any adverse effects or open safety concerns at normal dietary exposure levels.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in packaged bakery products, including sliced bread, buns, rolls, tortillas, and pastries. It is also used in some dehydrated potato flakes, gravies, and creamers. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its full name, calcium stearoyl-2-lactylate, or by its European code, E482. On US labels, it is permitted as a direct food additive and is listed under its chemical name.
