Acetylated distarch adipate (E1422)

E1422AdditiveNeutral · benign

Acetylated distarch adipate is a modified food starch used to thicken and stabilise foods, digested by the body just like ordinary starch.

What it is

Acetylated distarch adipate is a modified food starch. It starts as a natural starch, usually sourced from maize, tapioca, or potatoes, which is then chemically altered to improve its physical properties. Unlike regular kitchen starch, which can become watery or clumpy when heated, cooled, or frozen, this modified version maintains its texture under extreme conditions. In the body, it is broken down and digested in much the same way as the natural starches found in everyday foods like bread or potatoes.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers start with raw starch extracted from crops. This starch is treated in a water slurry with two chemical agents: acetic anhydride, which is related to vinegar, and adipic acid. This process introduces specific chemical groups to the starch molecules, cross-linking them to create a stronger, more resilient molecular network. After the chemical reaction is complete, the modified starch is thoroughly washed, purified, and dried into a fine white powder, ready to be added to food formulations.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use this modified starch because it is an exceptionally robust thickener and stabiliser. It excels in products that undergo harsh processing, such as high-heat sterilisation, intense shearing, or acidic conditions. It keeps sauces from separating, stops water from weeping out of gels, and maintains a smooth, creamy texture in foods that go through freeze-thaw cycles. It beats natural starches because it does not break down or lose its thickening power during long storage periods on supermarket shelves.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is processed by the body similarly to ordinary dietary starch, it does not pose safety concerns when consumed as part of a normal diet. The chemical modifications are minimal, and the substances used to treat the starch are removed during purification, leaving only tiny, harmless residues. No daily intake limit is established by regulators because evaluations show it is processed safely by our normal digestive enzymes without causing unwanted effects.

What the science says

The safety of this modified starch has been thoroughly evaluated by major food authorities. The European Food Safety Authority re-evaluated it and concluded that its use in foods is of no safety concern, meaning no numerical daily limit is established. Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives evaluations also support its safety, noting that the starch is readily broken down by digestive enzymes into simple sugars. Scientists agree that it behaves like regular starch in the human digestive tract, providing a source of energy without any adverse effects.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of processed foods, particularly those that need to remain stable over time. It is common in jarred pasta sauces, gravies, frozen ready meals, yoghurts, salad dressings, and canned soups. On ingredient labels, it is typically declared by its full name, acetylated distarch adipate, or by its European code, E1422, often grouped under the general term of modified starch.
