Vitamin a palmitate

Processed ingredientGood

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Vitamin a palmitate is a stable, fat-soluble form of vitamin A used to fortify foods and restore nutrients lost during processing.

What it is

Vitamin a palmitate is a combination of retinol, which is active vitamin A, and palmitic acid, a common saturated fatty acid found in plants and animals. In its natural state, vitamin A is highly sensitive to air and light, which makes it break down quickly. By attaching it to a fatty acid, chemists create a stable, fat-soluble compound that your body can easily convert back into active vitamin A to support your vision, immune system, and skin.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers typically synthesise retinol in a controlled laboratory setting. This retinol is then chemically bonded to palmitic acid, which is usually derived from vegetable oils such as palm oil. The resulting compound is purified and often blended with a small amount of vegetable oil or an antioxidant to protect it from oxidation, ensuring it remains stable and active until you consume the food.

Why manufacturers use it

Food producers use this ingredient primarily for nutritional fortification. During processes like skimming milk or refining grains, natural vitamins are often lost. Adding this stable compound back into the food ensures that consumers still get their essential nutrients. It is highly favoured by manufacturers because its fat-soluble nature allows it to blend seamlessly into dairy products, spreads, and oils without altering the texture, appearance, or flavour of the final product.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. Because this is a highly beneficial nutrient used to fortify foods rather than a cosmetic or texturising additive, it is not classified under Clara's additive tiers. It is a well-understood source of vitamin A that the body metabolises naturally. While extremely high doses of vitamin A from supplements can accumulate in the body, the levels used to fortify everyday foods are carefully regulated and considered entirely safe for general consumption.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this compound as a source of vitamin A. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration recognise it as a highly bioavailable nutrient source. Scientific assessments focus on establishing safe upper limits for daily vitamin A intake from all sources to ensure public health, concluding that fortification at standard commercial levels poses no safety concerns for the general population.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in low-fat and skimmed milks, margarine, spreads, infant formulas, and fortified breakfast cereals. On ingredient labels, it does not have an E-number and is usually listed plainly as vitamin a palmitate, retinyl palmitate, or vitamin A. On US labels, it is often declared as vitamin A palmitate to highlight its role as an essential nutrient.
