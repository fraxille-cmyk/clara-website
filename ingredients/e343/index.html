Magnesium phosphates (E343)

E343AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Magnesium phosphates are mineral salts used to control acidity, but EFSA warns that our total intake of phosphates may exceed the safe daily limit.

What it is

Magnesium phosphates are mineral salts that combine magnesium, an essential nutrient, with phosphorus, another mineral found naturally throughout our bodies. In the food industry, they are used as a single ingredient to manage acidity and texture. While they contain minerals our bodies need to function, they are highly concentrated when used as food additives, which changes how we absorb them compared to the minerals naturally present in whole foods.

How it's made

These salts are produced by reacting phosphoric acid with magnesium oxide or magnesium carbonate. The phosphoric acid is typically derived from mined phosphate rock, which is purified through chemical processing. The resulting reaction yields a fine, white, odourless powder that is easily dissolved or mixed into food formulations. No complex organic synthesis is required, as it is a straightforward mineral reaction.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on this ingredient because it is an excellent acidity regulator and anti-caking agent. It keeps powdered foods flowing smoothly by absorbing excess moisture and prevents clumping during storage. It also helps stabilise the acidity of a product, which maintains a consistent flavour and texture over its shelf life. It is a cost-effective alternative to organic acids and is highly stable under heat.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. While magnesium itself is beneficial, the phosphate part of this additive is the main concern. EFSA established a safe daily limit of 40 milligrams of phosphorus per kilogram of body weight in 2019, but warned that estimated exposure to phosphates from all dietary sources may exceed this safe level for some population groups, particularly children and heavy consumers of processed foods.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have closely evaluated the impact of added phosphates on human biology. EFSA re-evaluated these additives in 2019 and raised concerns about the cumulative amount of phosphorus in modern diets, as high intake can affect how the body balances minerals. The FDA permits its use as a generally recognised as safe substance under its regulations. Researchers continue to monitor how efficiently our bodies absorb these inorganic phosphates compared to natural dietary phosphorus.

Where you'll find it

You will find this additive in powdered drinks, bakery mixes, processed cheeses, sports nutrition products, and food supplements. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared as magnesium phosphates, magnesium phosphate, or by its European code, E343. On US labels it is listed as magnesium phosphate, where it is often added to fortify products with magnesium or to keep dry mixes free-flowing.
