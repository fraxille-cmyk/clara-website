Karaya gum (E416)

E416AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Karaya gum is a natural plant-derived thickener and stabiliser that Clara rates as a benign additive with no safety concerns.

What it is

Karaya gum is a natural plant gum obtained from the dried sap of Sterculia trees, which are native to India and East Africa. It is a complex carbohydrate made of long chains of natural sugars, which means it behaves like a soluble dietary fibre in your body. In its raw state, it is a pale, tear-shaped droplet of dried resin. When ground into a fine powder, it has a remarkable ability to absorb water and swell up to many times its original volume, forming a thick, smooth gel.

How it's made

The production of this ingredient is straightforward and relies on traditional harvesting methods. Workers make small incisions in the bark of the Sterculia tree, allowing the natural sap to seep out and harden in the air. Once these tears of resin are dry, they are collected by hand. The raw gum is then sorted to remove any bits of bark, washed thoroughly to ensure purity, dried completely, and ground into a fine, off-white powder that is ready for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers value this gum because it is an exceptionally strong thickener that works well even in cold water and highly acidic environments. It prevents ice crystals from forming in frozen desserts, keeps oil and water from separating in dressings, and gives a stable, chewy texture to confectionery. Because it resists breaking down under heat or acid, it is a highly reliable stabiliser for packaged foods that need to maintain their texture and appearance over a long shelf life.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is a natural plant gum that passes through the human digestive system largely unchanged, it acts just like dietary fibre and is not absorbed by the body. EFSA re-evaluated the ingredient and concluded that there is no safety concern, meaning no numerical daily limit is established. It has a long history of use in food, and extensive reviews show that it does not cause adverse digestive effects when consumed in normal dietary amounts.

What the science says

Scientific panels have thoroughly evaluated this plant gum over several decades. JECFA established its safety in its evaluations, noting that the gum is not digested or absorbed by the body. In a comprehensive re-evaluation, EFSA confirmed that the gum does not raise any safety concerns and found no reason to set a numerical acceptable daily intake. The evidence shows it is non-toxic and behaves as a bulk-forming fibre, passing harmlessly through the digestive tract without altering nutrient absorption.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of packaged foods, particularly in egg-white desserts, cake fillings, toppings, salad dressings, and chewing gum. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its plain name as karaya gum, sterculia gum, or by its European code, E416. On US labels, it is often listed as karaya gum or sterculia gum under specific federal regulations that limit its maximum use levels to ensure it is used appropriately.
