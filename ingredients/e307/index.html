Alpha-tocopherol (E307)

E307AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Alpha-tocopherol is a synthetic form of vitamin E used as an antioxidant to keep oils fresh, and Clara rates it as a benign additive.

What it is

Alpha-tocopherol is a synthetic form of vitamin E, a nutrient that your body uses to protect cells from oxidative damage. While vitamin E occurs naturally in vegetable oils, nuts, and leafy greens, this particular version is created in a laboratory to match the molecular structure of the natural vitamin. It is a thick, pale yellow oil that does not dissolve in water but mixes easily with fats and oils, making it an excellent protector for fat-rich foods.

How it's made

To manufacture this ingredient, producers use a multi-step chemical synthesis. The process typically starts by reacting trimethylhydroquinone, a chemical compound, with isophytol, which is a synthetic alcohol. This reaction creates a mixture of different optical isomers of the vitamin, resulting in synthetic alpha-tocopherol. The final substance is carefully purified and refined to remove any unreacted starting materials, leaving a highly concentrated oil ready for food formulation.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use this ingredient primarily as an antioxidant to prevent fats and oils from turning rancid. When exposed to oxygen, light, and heat, unsaturated fats break down and develop unpleasant odours and flavours. Adding this substance stops this oxidation process, which significantly extends the shelf life of packaged foods. It is highly valued because it is stable during high-temperature processing and does not alter the colour or flavour of the final product.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is a synthetic form of an essential nutrient, your body metabolises it much like natural vitamin E. EFSA established a daily limit of 2 mg per kg of body weight in 2015, which is a conservative limit designed to ensure total intake from all food sources remains within a highly supportive range. There are no safety concerns or adverse effects associated with the amounts typically added to packaged foods.

What the science says

This ingredient has been thoroughly evaluated by major food safety authorities. EFSA reviewed its safety as a food additive in 2015 and concluded that it does not pose any safety concerns at typical exposure levels. In the United States, the FDA permits its use and classifies it as generally recognised as safe under its federal regulations. Because it functions as a nutrient in the body, scientific consensus views it as a highly predictable and well-understood substance.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of fat-based foods, including margarine, vegetable oils, salad dressings, breakfast cereals, and infant formula. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its name as alpha-tocopherol, or sometimes simply as vitamin E. On European labels, it may also be identified by its official additive code, which is E307, while on US labels it is often listed as tocopherols.
