Magnesium

FortificantGood

Magnesium is an essential mineral that your body needs to function, often added to foods to boost their nutritional value.

What it is

Magnesium is a vital mineral that your body cannot make on its own, meaning you must get it from what you eat. In the natural world, it is abundant in leafy green vegetables, nuts, seeds, and whole grains. In the food industry, it is used as a nutrient to fortify products, helping to ensure people get enough of this essential element in their daily diet. It is one of the most common and important minerals on Earth, playing a fundamental role in the biology of both plants and animals.

How it's made

To make the magnesium used in food fortification, manufacturers typically start with natural mineral deposits, such as dolomite or magnesium-rich salts from brine and seawater. These raw minerals undergo purification and chemical processing to create stable, highly soluble compounds that the human body can easily absorb, such as magnesium oxide, magnesium carbonate, or magnesium sulphate. These purified white powders are then carefully tested for quality and consistency before being supplied to food manufacturers for blending into recipes.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers add magnesium to products primarily to enhance their nutritional profile, turning everyday items into functional foods. It is an excellent choice for fortification because these mineral compounds are highly stable, meaning they do not break down during high-heat baking, pasteurisation, or long periods on the supermarket shelf. Additionally, magnesium compounds are chosen because they do not alter the final flavour, colour, or texture of the food, allowing brands to boost mineral content without changing the eating experience.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As an essential mineral, it is highly beneficial and necessary for human health, playing a key role in over three hundred enzymatic reactions in the body. It helps with energy production, muscle function, and maintaining strong bones. Because it is a vital nutrient rather than a cosmetic food additive, it does not carry the risks associated with synthetic colours or preservatives, and the body naturally regulates its levels by excreting any excess that it does not require.

What the science says

Scientific bodies worldwide have thoroughly evaluated magnesium and recognise its critical role in human biology. EFSA has established dietary reference values for daily intake, noting its contribution to normal muscle function, protein synthesis, and electrolyte balance. The FDA permits its use as a nutrient supplement in food, confirming its status as generally recognised as safe. Research shows that many people do not get enough magnesium from their standard diet, which is why regulators support the fortification of staple foods to help populations meet their daily nutritional requirements.

Where you'll find it

You will find magnesium added to fortified breakfast cereals, meal replacement shakes, protein powders, mineral waters, and plant-based milk alternatives. On the ingredient list, it rarely appears simply as magnesium; instead, you will see it listed by the specific compound used, such as magnesium oxide, magnesium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium sulphate, or magnesium chloride, which are all easily absorbed forms of this essential nutrient.
