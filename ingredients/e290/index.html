Carbon dioxide (E290)

E290AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Carbon dioxide is a natural gas used to create bubbles in drinks and keep packaged food fresh, with no safety limits or health concerns.

What it is

Carbon dioxide is the very same gas we breathe out and that plants absorb to grow. It is a natural compound made of carbon and oxygen that exists all around us in the atmosphere. In the food world, it is the magic ingredient that gives sparkling water its fizz and makes soda bubbly. When dissolved in water, it creates a mild acid that gives carbonated drinks their familiar, refreshing bite. It is also used as an invisible blanket to protect packaged foods from spoiling.

How it's made

While carbon dioxide is a natural gas, the version used in food is manufactured on an industrial scale. It is typically captured as a by-product from other industrial processes, such as the fermentation of alcohol or the manufacture of ammonia. The captured gas is thoroughly purified, washed, and filtered to remove any impurities until it reaches a high food-grade standard. It is then compressed under pressure into a liquid or solid state, or kept as a gas, ready to be added to food and drink.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on this gas for two main reasons. First, it provides the effervescence and sensory experience that consumers expect in carbonated soft drinks, beers, and sparkling waters. Second, it acts as a packaging gas. By flushing oxygen out of a food packet and replacing it with carbon dioxide, manufacturers can prevent the growth of bacteria and mould that need oxygen to survive. This natural preservation method keeps salads, meats, and baked goods fresh for much longer without changing their flavour.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. Because it is a normal part of human metabolism and is exhaled by our lungs every moment, our bodies are perfectly equipped to handle it. No limit is established for how much you can safely consume in a day. The Scientific Committee on Food evaluated it and did not specify an acceptable daily intake limit, meaning it is considered safe for general consumption. It does not accumulate in the body and is simply released as gas after we consume it.

What the science says

Major food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated carbon dioxide and found no reasons for concern. The Scientific Committee on Food established that it does not require a numerical daily limit due to its natural role in the body and lack of toxicity. The FDA permits its use as a substance that is generally recognised as safe under federal regulations. Because it is a simple gas that we naturally produce and eliminate, scientific assessments focus on its purity rather than any potential accumulation or harm in the human body.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in almost any carbonated beverage, including fizzy water, sodas, energy drinks, beers, and ciders. It is also used in the sealed packaging of fresh snacks, pre-washed salad leaves, crumpets, and fresh pasta to maintain freshness. On ingredient lists, it is usually declared simply as carbon dioxide, though it may occasionally appear under its European code, E 290, or be referenced on US labels as carbon dioxide under federal standards.
