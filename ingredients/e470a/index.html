Sodium, potassium and calcium salts of fatty acids (E470a)

E470aAdditiveNeutral · limited concern

A group of texture-improving additives derived from natural fats that help blend ingredients and prevent clumping, rated neutral with limited concern.

What it is

These substances are a group of food additives made by combining common minerals like sodium, potassium, or calcium with fatty acids. Fatty acids are the natural building blocks of fats and oils that we eat every day. By pairing these fatty acids with minerals, chemists create compounds that can interact with both water and oil. This unique chemical structure makes them highly effective at helping different ingredients mix together smoothly and stay blended without separating over time.

How it's made

Although fatty acids occur naturally in vegetable oils and animal fats, these additives are produced through a chemical manufacturing process. Manufacturers take these natural fats and treat them with strong alkaline mineral solutions, such as sodium hydroxide, potassium hydroxide, or calcium hydroxide. This chemical reaction, known as saponification, splits the fats and bonds the resulting fatty acids with the minerals. The mixture is then purified, dried, and ground into a fine, stable powder ready for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on these salts primarily as emulsifiers, stabilisers, and anticaking agents. In products that combine oil and water, they prevent the ingredients from separating, ensuring a uniform texture and appearance throughout the shelf life. They also coat dry particles to stop them from absorbing moisture and clumping together during storage. Using these salts is highly cost-effective because they are incredibly stable under heat and mechanical mixing, outperforming many delicate, unprocessed alternatives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. These texture-improving emulsifiers are derived from chemically modified natural fats, and EFSA has not specified a numerical acceptable daily intake. Because the body breaks them down into normal dietary fats and minerals that we already consume in large quantities, regulators do not see a need to limit their intake. There are no known safety concerns or adverse effects associated with consuming these compounds in the quantities typically found in packaged foods.

What the science says

These additives have been thoroughly evaluated by major food safety authorities. EFSA completed a full re-evaluation of these compounds and concluded that they do not pose a safety concern, which is why no numerical acceptable daily intake limit is established. The FDA also permits their use, recognising them as safe binders and emulsifiers. Because they are metabolised in the body just like ordinary dietary fats and minerals, scientists agree that they do not accumulate or cause unwanted biological reactions.

Where you'll find it

You will find these ingredients in a wide variety of processed foods, including cake mixes, powdered soups, dairy analogues, table-top sweeteners, and compressed tablets or sweets. On ingredient lists in the UK and Europe, they are typically declared as sodium, potassium and calcium salts of fatty acids, or simply by their collective E-number, E470a. On US labels, they are listed as salts of fatty acids, often specifying the individual mineral used, such as calcium salts of fatty acids.
