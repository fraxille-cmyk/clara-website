Triammonium citrate (E380)

E380AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Triammonium citrate is a benign acidity regulator used to balance flavours and control tartness, rated neutral by Clara.

What it is

Triammonium citrate is an ammonium salt of citric acid, the very same natural acid that gives lemons and limes their characteristic sour kick. In the food world, it acts as an acidity regulator, helping to manage the sharp or sour notes in a recipe. While citric acid is famous on its own, combining it with ammonia creates a salt that behaves differently in water, allowing food makers to fine-tune the acidity of a product without making it taste overly sour.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers start with citric acid, which is typically produced through the fermentation of simple sugars by a common mould. This citric acid is then chemically neutralised using ammonia. The reaction produces a white, crystalline powder that dissolves easily in water. Although it is synthetic in its production route, it is chemically identical to the natural acid salts found in various plants and fruits.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this ingredient primarily to control the pH, or acidity level, of foods and drinks. Maintaining a stable pH is crucial because it prevents unwanted changes in colour, texture, and flavour over time. It also acts as a stabiliser, helping other flavouring agents stay well-mixed and consistent throughout the shelf life of a product. It is often preferred over other salts because it dissolves quickly and does not leave a heavy, salty aftertaste.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is a simple salt of citric acid, the body processes it easily, breaking it down into components that are already part of our normal metabolic pathways. No upper limit is established for its daily intake because regulators have found no reason to restrict it. The Scientific Committee on Food established that no numerical acceptable daily intake is necessary, as it poses no safety concerns when used in typical food amounts.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated citric acid and its various salts, concluding that they are safe for general consumption. The European Scientific Committee on Food reviewed these substances and decided that their safety is well-established, requiring no specific daily limit. In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration recognises the closely related dibasic ammonium citrate as generally recognised as safe, confirming that these compounds do not accumulate in the body or cause adverse reactions when consumed in food.

Where you'll find it

You will typically find this ingredient in processed foods that require careful flavour balancing, such as soft drinks, confectionery, jams, jellies, and certain dairy products. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is usually declared by its name, triammonium citrate, or by its European code, E380. On products imported from or sold in the United States, you might see it listed under the name ammonium citrate.
