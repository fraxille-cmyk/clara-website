Acetylated oxidised starch (E1451)

E1451AdditiveNeutral · benign

Acetylated oxidised starch is a modified starch used to thicken foods, and your body digests it just like ordinary starch.

What it is

Acetylated oxidised starch is a modified food starch that has been chemically altered to improve its stability and texture. It starts as a natural starch, such as corn, potato, or wheat starch, which is then treated to change how it behaves when heated or cooled. This modification helps it resist breaking down under harsh processing conditions, making it a reliable helper in keeping foods smooth and consistent. To your body, it remains a carbohydrate, very similar to the natural starches you eat every day.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers start with natural starch granules suspended in water. They first treat the starch with an oxidising agent, usually sodium hypochlorite, which introduces carboxyl groups and slightly breaks down the starch chains. Next, they react this oxidised starch with acetic anhydride, a compound related to vinegar, to introduce acetyl groups. This dual process of oxidation and acetylation is carefully controlled, and the resulting modified starch is washed, dried, and ground into a fine white powder ready for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this modified starch because it is an excellent thickener, binder, and gelling agent that outperforms regular starch. Natural starches tend to lose their thickening power when heated, exposed to acids, or frozen and thawed. This modified version maintains its structure and keeps liquids thick and smooth even under high heat or acidic conditions. It also prevents water from separating out of gels, which extends the shelf life of packaged foods and ensures they look appealing when opened.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because it is chemically very similar to natural starch, your digestive system processes it in the same way, breaking it down into simple sugars for energy. No limit is established for its daily intake because evaluations show it behaves like a normal dietary component. It does not accumulate in the body, and there are no safety concerns associated with its normal consumption in food products.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated modified starches to ensure they are safe for consumption. EFSA re-evaluated acetylated oxidised starch and confirmed that it is digested and metabolised just like native starch, posing no safety concerns. JECFA has also assessed this ingredient and established that no daily intake limit is necessary. Because it behaves as a simple carbohydrate in the digestive tract, researchers agree that it does not present any unusual physiological effects or risks when used in food.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of processed foods that require a stable, thick texture, such as confectionery, jelly sweets, sauces, gravies, and some dairy desserts. On food labels, it is typically listed by its full name as acetylated oxidised starch, or by its European code, E1451. It is often grouped with other modified starches in the ingredient list, helping to maintain the structure of delicate fillings and toppings.
