Dimethyl polysiloxane (E900)

E900AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Dimethyl polysiloxane is a synthetic silicone-based additive used to stop liquids from foaming during frying and bottling, rated neutral by Clara.

What it is

Dimethyl polysiloxane is a synthetic silicone polymer, a fluid substance made of repeating silicon and oxygen atoms with carbon-based side groups. While that sounds highly industrial, it is essentially a liquid form of silicone, the same flexible material used to make kitchen spatulas and baking mats. In food, it acts as a surface-tension reducer, which means it stops bubbles from forming and holding their shape. It is completely odourless, tasteless, and does not dissolve in water.

How it's made

This substance is entirely synthetic and is manufactured in a chemical plant. It is produced by reacting silicon metal, which is derived from silica sand, with methyl chloride. This reaction creates chlorosilanes, which are then carefully mixed with water in a process called hydrolysis. The resulting molecules are polymerised, meaning they are linked together into long, stable chains. The final liquid is purified and filtered to ensure it meets strict food-grade standards before it is shipped to food manufacturers.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this additive primarily to control foam during food processing. When large batches of cooking oil are heated, or when sugary liquids are boiled and bottled, they can foam up violently, which slows down production lines and creates safety hazards. Adding a tiny amount of this silicone fluid pops the bubbles instantly. It is incredibly efficient, working at extremely low concentrations where other vegetable-based oils would fail, and it remains stable even at the very high temperatures used in commercial deep frying.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is based on its synthetic origin and its established limits. Because the body does not absorb this silicone polymer, it passes through the digestive tract virtually unchanged. EFSA has established a numeric acceptable daily intake of 17 mg per kg of body weight. At the tiny concentrations allowed in food, a shopper is highly unlikely to reach this limit, making it a low-concern ingredient for everyday consumption.

What the science says

Scientific panels have evaluated this substance thoroughly to ensure it does not accumulate in the body. Because of its large molecular size, it cannot easily cross the intestinal wall, so it is excreted without being metabolised. EFSA re-evaluated the additive in 2020 and confirmed its safety profile, maintaining the daily limit of 17 mg per kg of body weight. No regulatory body has raised concerns about its stability in food, and it is not classified as a hazard by any international food safety authority.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient most often in commercial frying oils, fast-food fried items like chicken nuggets and chips, and in some reconstituted fruit juices, soft drinks, and jams where foaming is a problem. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically declared by its name, dimethyl polysiloxane, or as E900. On US labels, it is often listed as dimethylpolysiloxane or simply referred to as a defoaming agent.
