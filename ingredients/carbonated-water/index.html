Carbonated water

IngredientGood

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Carbonated water is plain water infused with carbon dioxide gas under pressure, providing a refreshing fizz and a sharp taste with no health concerns.

What it is

Carbonated water is simply water that contains dissolved carbon dioxide gas. When you open the bottle, the pressure drops and the gas escapes as the familiar bubbles we see and feel on the tongue. This dissolved gas forms a very small amount of carbonic acid, which gives the water its characteristic sharp, refreshing taste. While some rare mineral waters emerge from underground springs naturally effervescent, most of the sparkling water we buy has its bubbles added during bottling.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers take purified water or natural spring water and chill it to a low temperature, which helps the gas dissolve more easily. They then pump carbon dioxide gas into the water under high pressure and seal it immediately in a bottle or can to keep the gas trapped inside. For naturally carbonated mineral water, the gas is captured directly at the source and either kept in the water or reintroduced during the bottling process to match its natural state.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use carbonated water as the essential base for sparkling drinks, sodas, and mixers. Because ingredients must be listed on a label in descending order of weight, seeing carbonated water at the very top of the list tells you that the drink is mostly water, with all the flavourings, colours, and sweeteners making up the rest. It provides the bubbly texture and crisp mouthfeel that consumers enjoy, which cannot be achieved with still water alone.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. Carbonated water is a completely benign ingredient that poses no health concerns for the general public. Water is essential for hydration and overall bodily functions, and the physical process of carbonation does not alter its hydrating qualities. No limit is established for its consumption, and it is a excellent choice for anyone looking to enjoy a fizzy beverage without the added sugars or artificial sweeteners found in traditional soft drinks.

What the science says

Food safety authorities, including the FDA and EFSA, view carbonated water as a simple, wholesome ingredient with no toxicological concerns. Scientific evaluations confirm that the mild acidity created by carbonic acid is easily neutralised by saliva in the mouth and does not harm the stomach or bones. It hydrates the body just as effectively as still water, making it a highly regarded option for daily fluid intake in dietary guidelines worldwide.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in sparkling mineral waters, club soda, seltzer, and as the first ingredient in almost all fizzy soft drinks. A bottle of plain sparkling water often has no ingredient list at all, because single-ingredient foods are exempt from printing one. On labels, it can appear under many names depending on where it was bottled, including carbonated spring water, club soda, seltzer, eau gazeuse, acqua frizzante, agua con gas, or kohlensäurehaltiges wasser.
