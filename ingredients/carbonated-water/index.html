Carbonated water

IngredientGood

Carbonated water is simply water with dissolved carbon dioxide gas, providing a refreshing fizz and excellent hydration with no added chemicals.

What it is

Carbonated water is water that contains dissolved carbon dioxide gas, which creates the familiar bubbles and fizz. It is chemically identical to regular water but with the addition of these tiny gas bubbles. You might know it as sparkling water, soda water, or seltzer. It behaves just like still water in your body, helping to transport nutrients and keep your temperature stable, while offering a lively texture on the tongue that many people find more appealing than flat water.

How it's made

To make this bubbly drink, manufacturers dissolve carbon dioxide gas into plain water under high pressure. This process is called carbonation. Some premium waters are naturally carbonated, drawing their bubbles from deep underground volcanic sources where gas and water mix under natural pressure. For most bottled varieties, purified municipal water or natural spring water is chilled, and then carbon dioxide gas is forced into the liquid just before the bottle is sealed, trapping the fizz inside.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use carbonation to transform plain water into an exciting, sensory experience. The bubbles provide a pleasant tingling sensation and a clean, refreshing mouthfeel that can make simple drinks feel premium. Commercially, it serves as the perfect base for unsweetened flavoured drinks, offering the satisfying fizz of traditional soft drinks without any of the sugar or artificial additives. The dissolved gas also creates a slightly acidic environment, which helps to preserve the freshness of the bottled water.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. This simple beverage is an excellent way to stay hydrated, as the carbonation process is a physical change that does not introduce any harmful substances to the water. The body processes carbonated water exactly like still water, absorbing it efficiently to support every cellular function. Because it is not classified as a food additive in the traditional sense, no daily intake limit is established, and it remains a wholesome choice for daily hydration.

What the science says

Major food authorities have thoroughly evaluated carbonated water and confirm its safety for regular consumption. The European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration view simple carbonated water as a benign beverage that poses no risk to bones or stomach lining, contrary to old myths. The mild acidity caused by the dissolved carbon dioxide is easily neutralised by saliva in the mouth, meaning it does not harm healthy teeth under normal drinking conditions.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in the drinks aisle, sold on its own or as the base for sparkling fruit drinks and mixers. On packaging, it is typically listed simply as carbonated water, sparkling water, or carbonated natural mineral water. Because it is a simple food rather than a chemical additive, it does not carry an E-number, making it easy to spot on clean labels that focus on simple, recognisable ingredients.
