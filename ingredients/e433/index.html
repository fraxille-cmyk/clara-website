Polysorbate 80 (E433)

E433AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Polysorbate 80 is a synthetic emulsifier used to blend oil and water, with a daily limit set by European regulators to ensure it is consumed at safe levels.

What it is

Polysorbate 80 is a synthetic food additive that belongs to a family of compounds known as polysorbates. It is a viscous, amber-coloured liquid that helps oil and water mix together smoothly. Chemically, it is made by combining a sugar alcohol called sorbitol with a fatty acid called oleic acid, which is commonly found in olive oil, and then treating the mixture with ethylene oxide. This structure allows one end of the molecule to cling to water while the other end clings to oil, bridging the gap between two substances that naturally repel each other.

How it's made

The production of polysorbate 80 is an entirely industrial process. It begins with sorbitol, which is derived from corn syrup, and oleic acid, which is sourced from vegetable oils or animal fats. These two ingredients are reacted to form a substance called sorbitan monooleate. Manufacturers then chemically modify this substance by reacting it with ethylene oxide, a synthetic gas. This process, known as ethoxylation, adds chains of polyoxyethylene to the molecule, which makes it highly soluble in water and ready for use as a food-grade emulsifier.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on polysorbate 80 because it is an exceptionally strong and stable emulsifier. It prevents ingredients from separating over long periods on supermarket shelves, which is a common challenge in creamy dressings, sauces, and spreads. In frozen desserts like ice cream, it plays a crucial role in keeping the texture smooth and creamy by preventing large ice crystals from forming as the product melts and refreezes. It also helps whipped toppings hold their airy shape and prevents essential oils from separating in flavoured drinks.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects the fact that while it is a highly processed synthetic substance, it has been thoroughly evaluated by food safety authorities. EFSA has established a numeric acceptable daily intake of 25 milligrams per kilogram of body weight for polysorbates. This limit is designed to ensure that even people who eat a lot of processed foods do not consume an amount that could disrupt the delicate lining of the digestive tract, which is the main area of focus for researchers studying these emulsifiers.

What the science says

European and American regulators have assessed polysorbate 80 several times to ensure its safety in the food supply. EFSA completed a major re-evaluation of the additive, concluding that it does not pose a safety concern at typical dietary levels, though they established a group limit to cover all polysorbates in the diet. Researchers continue to study how synthetic emulsifiers interact with the natural barrier of the human gut, but current evidence shows no toxicological concern when it is consumed within the established daily limit.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a variety of processed foods, particularly ice cream, frozen desserts, creamy salad dressings, mayonnaise, and whipped toppings. It is also used in cake mixes and instant puddings to help fats disperse evenly. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed as polysorbate 80 or by its European code, E 433. On US labels, it is listed as polysorbate 80, and it may occasionally appear under its full chemical name, polyoxyethylene sorbitan monooleate.
