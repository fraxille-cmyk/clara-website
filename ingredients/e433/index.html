Polysorbate 80 (E433)

E433AdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Polysorbate 80 is a synthetic emulsifier used to blend oil and water, which Clara recommends avoiding because children can easily exceed the daily limit.

What it is

Polysorbate 80 is a synthetic substance that belongs to a family of compounds called polyoxyethylene sorbitan esters. It is a viscous, amber-coloured liquid that acts as an emulsifier, helping ingredients that normally repel each other, such as oil and water, to mix smoothly. It is chemically modified from sorbitol, which is a sugar alcohol, fatty acids derived from vegetable oils, and ethylene oxide. This structure allows one end of the molecule to attract water while the other end binds to fats, creating a stable blend in processed foods.

How it's made

The production of polysorbate 80 is a multi-step chemical synthesis. It begins with sorbitol, a sugar alcohol often obtained from corn syrup. This sorbitol is dehydrated to form a compound called sorbitan. The sorbitan is then reacted with ethylene oxide, a synthetic gas, in a process called ethoxylation. Finally, this intermediate compound is chemically joined with oleic acid, a fatty acid typically sourced from olive oil or other vegetable fats. The resulting synthetic emulsifier is purified and filtered to ensure it meets food-grade standards before being shipped to food manufacturers.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers rely on polysorbate 80 because it is an exceptionally strong and versatile emulsifier. It prevents oil and water from separating in creamy products, ensuring a consistent texture and appearance throughout a long shelf life. In frozen desserts like ice cream, it helps create a smooth texture and prevents large ice crystals from forming as the product melts and refreezes. It also helps disperse flavours and colours evenly throughout a food product, ensuring that every bite tastes and looks the same, which is difficult to achieve with natural emulsifiers.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. The main issue is that during its re-evaluation, EFSA noted that the group acceptable daily intake of 25 mg per kg of body weight was exceeded in several exposure scenarios for toddlers and children. Because this synthetic emulsifier is used in many popular processed foods, young children can easily consume more than the recommended daily limit. While EFSA has established this limit to ensure consumer protection, the fact that children frequently exceed it makes it an ingredient that is best to bypass when shopping for your family.

What the science says

The safety of polysorbate 80 has been evaluated by major food authorities, including EFSA and the FDA. In 2015, EFSA conducted a comprehensive re-evaluation of the group of polyoxyethylene sorbitan esters and set a group acceptable daily intake of 25 mg per kg of body weight. The panel highlighted that while the substance itself does not raise immediate safety concerns at low doses, the cumulative exposure from various foods is too high for younger populations. Researchers continue to study how synthetic emulsifiers interact with the protective barrier in the human digestive system, though no new regulatory limits have been changed.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide range of processed foods, particularly ice cream, frozen desserts, whipped toppings, salad dressings, and cake mixes. It is also used in pickles and canned foods to help distribute flavours. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed by its full name, polysorbate 80, or by its European code, E433. On US labels, it is listed as polysorbate 80, and it may also appear on imported goods under its alternative spacing as e 433.
