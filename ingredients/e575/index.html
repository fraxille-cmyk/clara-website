Glucono-delta-lactone (E575)

E575AdditiveNeutral · benign

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Glucono-delta-lactone is a mild, naturally occurring food acid used to control acidity and raise dough, rated neutral and benign by Clara.

What it is

Glucono-delta-lactone is a naturally occurring organic acid that you can find in honey, fruit juices, and wine. It is a close relative of glucose, the simple sugar your body uses for energy. In its pure form, it is a white, odourless crystalline powder with a sweet taste that slowly turns pleasantly tart when it dissolves in water. Because it releases acid gradually rather than all at once, it behaves differently from other common food acids like citric acid or vinegar.

How it's made

Although it exists in nature, the ingredient on food labels is produced on a commercial scale through the fermentation of glucose. Manufacturers typically use a benign microbe to oxidise glucose, which is derived from corn or starch, into gluconic acid. This acid is then concentrated and crystallised to form glucono-delta-lactone. The entire process is a clean, controlled biochemical synthesis that does not chemically modify the underlying natural structure of the molecule.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers value this ingredient for its unique ability to release acid slowly. In baking, this slow release allows it to react gradually with baking soda, ensuring an even rise in doughs and batters without leaving a bitter aftertaste. In cheese and tofu production, it helps coagulate proteins gently, creating a smooth and uniform texture. It also functions as an acidity regulator, lowering the pH of foods to help preserve them and prevent spoilage without making the final product taste overly sour.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. It is exceptionally gentle on the body because it metabolises into gluconic acid, a substance your body produces naturally during normal carbohydrate metabolism. The Scientific Committee on Food established that no limit is established for its daily intake, reflecting its high safety profile. It carries no known health risks, does not accumulate in the body, and is processed just like any other simple carbohydrate you eat.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated this ingredient and found no toxicological concern. The Scientific Committee on Food assessed its safety and designated its acceptable daily intake as not specified, which is the safest category for food additives. The US Food and Drug Administration also recognises it as generally safe for use in food. Because it is a natural metabolite that the human body is already fully equipped to process, researchers have found no adverse effects even at high dietary levels.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of processed foods, including silken tofu, feta cheese, instant desserts, baking mixes, and cured meats. On ingredient lists, it is usually declared by its full name, glucono-delta-lactone, or simply as E575. On some labels, it may also appear as e 575 or be grouped under its functional class as an acidity regulator, helping to keep your food fresh and structurally sound.
