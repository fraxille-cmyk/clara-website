Sea salt

IngredientNeutral

Sea salt is a minimally processed salt harvested from evaporated seawater, used to season food and preserve freshness.

What it is

Sea salt is a natural seasoning composed mostly of sodium chloride, the same basic compound found in standard table salt. Unlike mined rock salt, it is harvested directly from modern oceans or saltwater lakes. Because it does not undergo heavy refining, it retains tiny amounts of natural minerals from the water, such as potassium, magnesium, and calcium. These trace elements can give it a slightly different texture, colour, and complex flavour profile compared to highly processed table salt, making it a favourite of home cooks and food producers alike.

How it's made

The production of sea salt is one of the oldest food technologies in human history. Seawater is channelled into shallow outdoor ponds or pans, where the sun and wind gradually evaporate the water. As the liquid disappears, salt crystals begin to form on the surface and settle at the bottom. Workers harvest these crystals by hand using traditional rakes, or with mechanical harvesters in larger operations. The gathered salt is then washed to remove impurities, dried naturally or in ovens, and crushed or sieved into various grain sizes, from delicate flakes to coarse crystals.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose sea salt primarily for its clean flavour and appealing texture. It enhances the natural taste of ingredients, balances sweetness, and suppresses bitter notes in processed foods. On a technical level, it acts as a natural preservative by drawing moisture out of food, which slows down the growth of spoilage organisms. It also helps control fermentation in bread and cheese making. Commercially, using sea salt on a label appeals to shoppers looking for simple, familiar ingredients, allowing brands to avoid highly refined additives.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. While it is a natural ingredient, sea salt is still sodium chloride, and the body processes it in exactly the same way as regular table salt. Consuming too much sodium can raise blood pressure, which puts extra strain on blood vessels and the heart over time. No specific daily limit is established for sea salt itself, but international health guidelines recommend limiting total daily sodium intake from all dietary sources. Choosing sea salt over table salt does not reduce your sodium intake, as they contain virtually the same amount of sodium by weight.

What the science says

Scientific assessments of salt focus on its sodium content rather than its origin. The World Health Organisation recommends that adults limit their daily salt intake to under five grams to help maintain healthy blood pressure. EFSA has also evaluated dietary sodium, concluding that current average intakes in Europe exceed what the body requires for normal physiological function. While some marketing claims suggest the trace minerals in sea salt offer distinct health benefits, researchers have found these minerals exist in such tiny quantities that they do not make a meaningful contribution to daily nutrition.

Where you'll find it

You will find sea salt in a vast range of savoury and sweet products, including potato crisps, roasted nuts, crackers, ready meals, canned soups, and salted caramels. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared simply as sea salt. Because it is a whole food ingredient rather than a food additive, it does not have an E-number. It is often highlighted on the front of packaging to signal a more traditional or premium recipe, though it performs the same chemical role as standard salt in the food.
