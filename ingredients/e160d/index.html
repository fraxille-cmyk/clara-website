Lycopene (E160d)

E160dAdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Lycopene is a red pigment used to colour food, rated neutral by Clara because synthetic versions have a strict daily intake limit.

What it is

Lycopene is a bright red carotenoid pigment that occurs naturally in red fruits and vegetables, most famously giving tomatoes their characteristic deep red hue. In the food industry, it is used as a food additive to restore or enhance colour in processed items. While you can eat plenty of lycopene naturally in fresh tomatoes, the version added to packaged foods is often produced synthetically in a laboratory to ensure a highly stable and consistent shade.

How it's made

To make the commercial additive, manufacturers can extract it from natural tomatoes using solvents, but they frequently synthesise it through chemical processes. Synthetic lycopene is created by reacting chemical precursors in a controlled laboratory environment to build the exact molecular structure found in nature. This synthetic route is highly efficient and produces a pure, concentrated powder that does not rely on seasonal tomato harvests, though it results in a product that regulators treat with slightly more caution than the direct fruit extract.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use lycopene to give foods an appealing, warm red or orange hue that might otherwise be lost during high-heat processing or long storage on supermarket shelves. It is highly valued because it is relatively stable under light and heat compared to some other natural red colours. Using this pigment allows companies to maintain a consistent appearance across different batches of products, ensuring that every package looks exactly as the shopper expects.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating is given because synthetic lycopene has a numeric acceptable daily intake of 0.5 mg per kg of body weight. While natural lycopene from tomatoes is widely eaten without restriction, the synthetic version is monitored more closely to ensure consumers do not exceed this daily limit through processed foods. No regulatory body has raised any urgent safety concerns, and no limit is established for the natural tomato extract.

What the science says

EFSA re-evaluated lycopene as a food additive and established the acceptable daily intake of 0.5 mg per kg of body weight for the synthetic form to ensure a large margin of safety. The FDA has also reviewed the substance, filing GRAS notices for both the synthetic version and the natural extract from tomatoes. Scientific panels agree that lycopene is metabolised safely by the human body, and their evaluations show no toxicological concern when it is consumed within the established guidelines.

Where you'll find it

You will find lycopene in a variety of colourful processed foods, including flavoured yoghurts, soft drinks, sweets, sauces, soups, and bakery decorations. On ingredient lists in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is often declared as colour lycopene or by its E-number, E160d. On US labels, it is typically listed simply as lycopene or tomato extract, depending on the specific source used by the manufacturer.
