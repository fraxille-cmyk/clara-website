Nisin (E234)

E234AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Nisin is a natural preservative made by bacteria that stops food spoiling, with a safe daily limit set by European regulators.

What it is

Nisin is a natural antibacterial substance, specifically a small protein known as a peptide, that is produced by friendly bacteria. It is the same kind of protective compound that occurs naturally in fermented foods like yoghurt and cheese. In the food industry, it serves as a highly effective preservative that targets and neutralises specific bacteria that cause spoilage, helping to keep food fresh and safe to eat for longer periods without changing the taste or texture of the product.

How it's made

To make this ingredient, manufacturers cultivate a specific strain of the food-grade bacterium Lactococcus lactis in a controlled liquid medium. The bacteria naturally produce nisin during their fermentation process. Once the fermentation is complete, the mixture is carefully filtered to separate the nisin from the bacterial cells. The resulting protein is then concentrated, purified, standardised with sodium chloride to ensure a consistent strength, and dried into a fine powder that is ready to be added to food products.

Why manufacturers use it

Food producers choose this ingredient because it is exceptionally good at preventing the growth of spore-forming bacteria that survive heat pasteurisation. It is particularly valued in the dairy industry because it works at a very low concentration and does not interfere with the natural flavours of delicate foods. By using it, manufacturers can extend the shelf life of chilled products, reduce the intensity of heat treatments needed during processing, and ensure that foods remain stable and free from spoilage during transport and storage.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This rating reflects its natural microbial origin and the fact that it is broken down easily by our digestive enzymes into simple amino acids, just like any other dietary protein. Because it is consumed in very small amounts, it is not expected to accumulate in the body. EFSA has established a numeric acceptable daily intake of 1 mg per kg of body weight, which provides a reassuring safety margin for everyday consumption.

What the science says

Independent scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated this preservative and found no toxicological concern at normal dietary levels. Because it is a peptide, the digestive system processes it quickly and completely, meaning it does not reach the lower gut in an active state where it could disrupt our beneficial resident bacteria. EFSA re-evaluated its safety in 2017 and confirmed its daily limit, while the FDA also maintains its long-standing status as a safe substance for specific food applications.

Where you'll find it

You will most commonly find this ingredient in dairy products, particularly processed cheese, clotted cream, and mascarpone, as well as in liquid egg products and certain traditional semolina or tapioca puddings. On ingredient labels in the United Kingdom and Europe, it is typically listed by its name as nisin or by its European code, E234. On US labels, it is often declared as nisin preparation, where it is permitted for use in specific pasteurised cheese spreads.
