High oleic sunflower oil

Processed ingredientNeutral

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

A refined vegetable oil rich in monounsaturated fats, offering excellent stability for cooking and a neutral flavour profile.

What it is

High oleic sunflower oil is a type of vegetable oil extracted from sunflower seeds that have been bred to contain a high concentration of oleic acid. Oleic acid is a monounsaturated fatty acid, the very same type of fat that gives olive oil its celebrated reputation. Unlike standard sunflower oil, which is high in polyunsaturated fats, this oil consists of at least eighty percent monounsaturated fats. This unique composition makes it much more stable when heated, while remaining a liquid at room temperature. It has a very light colour and a neutral taste.

How it's made

This oil begins with sunflower seeds from plants developed through traditional plant breeding techniques to select for high oleic acid content. Once harvested, the seeds are cleaned and mechanically pressed to extract the crude oil. To make it suitable for high-heat cooking and to ensure a long shelf life, the crude oil undergoes a refining process. This involves neutralising free fatty acids, bleaching with natural clays to remove pigments, and deodorising using steam. The result is a highly purified, clear oil that is free from seed residues and strong odours.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers choose this oil primarily for its outstanding stability and resistance to oxidation. Standard vegetable oils break down quickly when exposed to heat and air, but the high monounsaturated fat content in this oil keeps it from spoiling or developing off-flavours. This makes it an ideal choice for frying, baking, and roasting. It also extends the shelf life of packaged snacks like crisps and crackers without requiring artificial preservatives. Its neutral flavour ensures that it does not alter the intended taste of the final food product.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. As a refined vegetable oil high in monounsaturated fats, it is generally considered a positive addition to a balanced diet when used in moderation. Because it is a fat, it is energy-dense, but its fatty acid profile is highly favourable compared to saturated fats or partially hydrogenated oils. No limit is established for its consumption, as it is treated as a standard food ingredient rather than a regulated additive. It does not carry the risks associated with trans fats, making it a preferred choice for health-conscious food formulation.

What the science says

Regulators and health authorities worldwide view monounsaturated fats as a beneficial component of daily nutrition. The FDA has authorised a qualified health claim stating that supportive but not proven scientific evidence suggests that daily consumption of about one and a half tablespoons of oils high in oleic acid may reduce the risk of coronary heart disease when replacing fats and oils higher in saturated fat. EFSA has also evaluated unsaturated fatty acids, noting that replacing saturated fats with unsaturated fats like oleic acid helps maintain normal blood cholesterol levels.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of packaged foods, particularly crispy snacks, potato chips, granola bars, baked goods, and pre-made salad dressings. On food labels, it is typically declared plainly as high oleic sunflower oil. Because it is a whole food ingredient rather than a food additive, it does not have an E-number. It is often highlighted on packaging to signal that the product is free from hydrogenated oils and low in saturated fats.
