Eggs

Whole foodGood

✕ Not vegan

Eggs are a highly nutritious whole food that Clara rates as a good choice, though they are a major allergen for some people.

What it is

Eggs are a familiar whole food produced by female birds, most commonly chickens. They consist of a protective outer shell, a clear white rich in proteins, and a yellow yolk packed with fats, vitamins, and minerals. As a natural, single-ingredient food, they are a staple of human diets worldwide, prized for their culinary versatility and their dense concentration of essential nutrients.

How it's made

Eggs are a entirely natural product laid by hens. On modern farms, the eggs are collected, washed to remove surface dirt, and inspected using a bright light to check for internal quality and shell cracks. They are then graded by size, packed into cartons, and kept cool during transport to preserve their freshness before they reach the shop shelves.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers rely on eggs for their remarkable functional properties. The proteins in the egg white stretch and trap air when whipped, which helps cakes and souffles rise. The yolk contains natural emulsifiers that bind oil and water together, which is essential for making smooth mayonnaise and stable sauces. They also add a rich golden colour and a pleasant savoury flavour to baked goods.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this GOOD. As a single-ingredient whole food with nothing added, it is a highly nutritious option for most shoppers. The main consideration is that eggs are a major allergen, so individuals with an egg allergy must avoid them. For everyone else, they are a wholesome source of protein and healthy fats with no regulatory limits on consumption.

What the science says

Major food authorities, including the European Food Safety Authority and the US Food and Drug Administration, recognise eggs as a nutrient-dense whole food. Scientific evaluations focus primarily on their excellent protein quality and their profile of essential vitamins. While eggs contain dietary cholesterol, modern dietary guidelines from these regulators no longer set strict upper limits on egg consumption for healthy individuals, as research shows dietary cholesterol has a minimal impact on blood cholesterol for most people.

Where you'll find it

You will find eggs sold whole in cartons, or listed as an ingredient in a vast range of processed foods. They are common in fresh pasta, mayonnaise, cakes, biscuits, and quiches. On ingredient labels, they are typically declared clearly in bold to highlight them as an allergen, appearing under names such as egg, free-range eggs, hen eggs, or their European equivalents like oeufs, uova, or huevos.
