Calcium benzoate (E213)

E213AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Calcium benzoate is a synthetic preservative used to stop yeast and mould from growing, with a strict daily limit set by food safety regulators.

What it is

Calcium benzoate is a synthetic preservative that belongs to the benzoate family. It is the calcium salt of benzoic acid, a compound that occurs naturally in some fruits like cranberries and prunes, as well as in spices like cinnamon. In the food industry, however, the version you see on labels is always made in a factory to ensure it is pure and consistent. It works by making the environment inside food cells too acidic for unwanted microbes to survive, helping to keep packaged foods fresh for much longer.

How it's made

To manufacture this preservative, chemical producers start with benzoic acid, which is typically synthesised from toluene, a liquid derived from petroleum. This acid is then neutralised by reacting it with calcium hydroxide, which is commonly known as slaked lime. The chemical reaction causes calcium benzoate to crystallise out of the liquid mixture. These crystals are then filtered, thoroughly washed to remove any impurities, dried into a fine white powder, and packaged for use in food processing.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers choose this additive because it is highly effective at stopping the growth of yeasts, moulds, and certain bacteria in acidic foods. It is particularly valued because it dissolves well in liquid mixtures and does not alter the texture of the product. Compared to other preservatives, it is highly stable and works reliably over a long shelf life. It is also a cost-effective choice for keeping mass-produced foods stable during transport and storage, ensuring they do not spoil before they reach the kitchen cupboard.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. While it is synthetic, it is processed by the body and excreted without building up in your tissues. EFSA keeps its daily limit of 5 mg per kg of body weight for benzoic acid and its salts, including this one. This acceptable daily intake is a protective limit, and most people consume far less than this amount in their normal diet. There is no toxicological concern for the general population when it is consumed within these established regulatory limits.

What the science says

Food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this compound. EFSA completed a full re-evaluation of benzoic acid and its salts, confirming that the substance is metabolised safely by the liver and excreted in urine. The FDA also permits its use as an antimicrobial agent and recognises it as safe for its intended technical functions. Scientists have established a clear numeric limit to ensure that even people who consume many preserved foods stay well within a safe range, and no major regulatory body has raised any open safety concerns.

Where you'll find it

You will find this preservative in a variety of acidic foods and drinks, such as carbonated soft drinks, fruit juices, jams, pickles, salad dressings, and condiments. On a ingredient list, it is typically declared by its plain name as calcium benzoate, or by its European code, which is E213. On US labels it is listed as calcium benzoate, where it is often grouped with other preservatives that help maintain freshness in bottled and jarred goods.
