Annatto (E160b)

E160bAdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Annatto is a natural orange-yellow food colour extracted from achiote seeds, rated neutral by Clara with a daily limit set by European regulators.

What it is

Annatto is a natural food colour that gives food a rich yellow, orange, or peach hue. It comes from the seeds of the achiote shrub, a plant native to tropical regions of the Americas. People have used these seeds for centuries to colour food, textiles, and body paint. In modern food, the colour comes from two main natural pigments found in the seed coat, known as bixin and norbixin, which dissolve easily in oils or water to tint different kinds of food.

How it's made

To make this colour, manufacturers harvest the heart-shaped prickly pods of the achiote tree and remove the seeds. The seeds are then soaked in water or warm vegetable oil to dissolve the pigment coating. This mixture is filtered and purified to concentrate the colour. Depending on whether water or oil is used during extraction, the process yields either water-soluble norbixin or oil-soluble bixin, which are then standardised into liquid or powder forms for food production.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers use this ingredient to make products look more appealing and consistent. Natural dairy products can vary in colour depending on the season and what the cows eat, so adding a tiny amount of this extract ensures a cheese always has its familiar warm yellow or deep orange hue. It is highly valued because it resists fading under heat and light, and it is a cost-effective, plant-based alternative to synthetic petroleum-derived yellow dyes.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. This natural extract is generally well tolerated by the body, though regulators have set a clear limit on how much we should consume daily. EFSA keeps its daily limit of 6 mg per kg of body weight for these extracts. While some people report mild sensitivity or allergic-like reactions to the natural proteins in the seeds, it does not pose a general health risk for the vast majority of shoppers.

What the science says

European and American food safety authorities have thoroughly evaluated this plant extract. EFSA re-evaluated the colour and established a specific acceptable daily intake in 2016 to ensure consumers do not ingest too much from multiple food sources. The scientific consensus confirms that the pigments are metabolised and cleared by the body without accumulation. No major safety concerns or long-term adverse effects have been identified in modern scientific assessments.

Where you'll find it

You will find this colour in a wide variety of supermarket items, especially dairy products like Cheddar cheese, Red Leicester, butter, and spreads. It also appears in custard powders, ice cream, breakfast cereals, smoked fish, and bakery products. On ingredient lists, it is typically declared by its plain name as annatto, annatto extract, or under its European colour code E160b, and on US labels it is listed as annatto extract.
