Annatto (E160b)

E160bAdditiveAvoid · moderate concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Annatto is a natural orange-yellow colour from achiote seeds that Clara rates AVOID because children can easily consume more than the recommended daily limit.

What it is

Annatto is a natural food colour that imparts a warm, yellow-orange hue to foods. It is derived from the seeds of the achiote tree, which is native to tropical regions of the Americas. People have used these seeds for centuries as both a spice and a dye. In modern food production, the active colouring compounds inside the seeds, known as bixin and norbixin, are extracted to create a highly concentrated colouring agent that mimics the rich, golden look of traditional dairy products.

How it's made

The production process begins with the harvesting of achiote seeds. To extract the colour, the seeds are typically soaked in an alkaline water solution or an edible oil, which helps dissolve the pigment from the seed coats. This liquid is then filtered to remove the solid seed debris. Depending on the solvent used, the process yields either oil-soluble bixin or water-soluble norbixin. The resulting extract is purified, concentrated, and sometimes dried into a powder, ready to be added to food formulations.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers choose annatto because it provides a highly stable, vibrant yellow-to-orange colour at a very low cost. It is particularly valued in the dairy industry to ensure that cheeses and butters maintain a consistent, rich yellow colour throughout the year, regardless of seasonal changes in the cows' diet. Because it is derived from a plant, it allows brands to avoid synthetic petroleum-based dyes while still achieving the bright, appealing appearance that shoppers expect from processed foods.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this AVOID, a moderate-concern additive. While it is a natural plant extract, EFSA established an acceptable daily intake of 6 mg per kg of body weight for norbixin in 2016. The regulator later noted that refined exposure estimates for norbixin exceeded this acceptable daily intake in toddlers and children at the ninety-fifth percentile. Because younger children with lower body weights can easily consume more than the recommended safety limit through everyday foods, Clara advises avoiding this additive.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have evaluated the safety of this colour extract extensively. EFSA re-evaluated the substance and established specific acceptable daily intake limits for its individual pigment components, bixin and norbixin, to protect consumers. While the extract is not considered a safety concern for the general adult population at typical levels of consumption, the potential for young children to exceed the safety threshold remains an active area of regulatory monitoring. No other major toxicological concerns or limits have been established by international authorities.

Where you'll find it

You will frequently find this colour in dairy products like cheddar cheese, butter, spreads, and margarines, as well as in custard powders, ice creams, breakfast cereals, and bakery products. On ingredient labels, it is typically listed as annatto, annatto extract, or E160b. On US labels it is listed as annatto extract, where it is permitted as a colour additive exempt from certification.
