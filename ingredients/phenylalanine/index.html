Phenylalanine

IngredientNeutral

An essential amino acid and a natural building block of protein that our bodies need but cannot make on their own.

What it is

Phenylalanine is an essential amino acid, which means it is a fundamental building block of protein that our bodies require to function but cannot synthesise from scratch. We must obtain it through our diet. In nature, it is found abundantly in protein-rich foods such as eggs, chicken, beef, fish, milk, and nuts. It plays a vital role in the body by helping to produce other proteins, chemical messengers, and molecules that regulate our mood and alertness. On food labels, it is sometimes added directly to fortify products or appears as a component of certain artificial sweeteners.

How it's made

For commercial use, phenylalanine is typically produced through a fermentation process. Manufacturers use specific strains of micro-organisms, such as bacteria, which are fed simple sugars like glucose in large fermentation vats. These micro-organisms naturally synthesise the amino acid as they grow. Once the fermentation is complete, the mixture is filtered, purified, and crystallised to isolate the pure phenylalanine. The final product is a clean, white, crystalline powder that can be easily blended into foods, beverages, or dietary supplements.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use phenylalanine primarily for nutritional fortification and as a key component in food formulation. Because it is an essential amino acid, adding it to specialised foods, sports nutrition powders, and meal replacement shakes helps ensure a complete protein profile. It is also a critical raw material in the production of aspartame, a widely used intense sweetener. In this role, it helps deliver a sweet taste without the bulk or energy value of sugar, allowing manufacturers to create low-calorie versions of popular drinks and snacks that still appeal to consumers.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. As a natural amino acid, it is a normal and necessary part of the human diet. No daily intake limit is established for the general population because our bodies metabolise it routinely. However, individuals with a rare genetic variation cannot break down this amino acid properly, which is why products containing aspartame must carry a warning label. For everyone else, it is a standard dietary component that the body processes alongside other proteins without any safety concerns.

What the science says

The safety of phenylalanine has been thoroughly evaluated by global food authorities as part of their assessments of proteins and sweeteners. The FDA and EFSA have reviewed its metabolism and confirmed that it behaves identically to the phenylalanine found naturally in everyday foods. JECFA has also evaluated its safety and established that it poses no risk to the general public. Because it is a basic nutrient, regulators do not set an upper limit for healthy individuals, focusing instead on clear labelling to protect those who cannot metabolise it.

Where you'll find it

You will find phenylalanine listed on the labels of protein powders, meal replacement shakes, sports drinks, and fortified foods. It also appears indirectly in products sweetened with aspartame, such as diet sodas, sugar-free chewing gums, and light yoghurts, which carry the warning statement contains a source of phenylalanine. Because it is a nutrient rather than a food additive, it does not have an E-number and is simply declared by its common name on the ingredient list.
