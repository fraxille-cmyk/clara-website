Starch aluminium octenyl succinate (E1452)

E1452AdditiveNeutral · limited concern

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

Starch aluminium octenyl succinate is a modified starch used to carry nutrients in supplements, rated neutral because it is highly restricted.

What it is

Starch aluminium octenyl succinate is a chemically modified food starch. While regular starch comes from plants like corn, wheat, or potatoes, this version has been altered in a laboratory to attach octenyl succinate groups and aluminium ions. This chemical modification changes how the starch behaves in water and how it interacts with fats, turning it into a highly specialised ingredient that behaves very differently from the flour or cornflour you use in your kitchen.

How it's made

The production starts with natural food starch extracted from plant sources. Manufacturers treat this starch chemically with octenyl succinic anhydride to introduce hydrophobic properties, allowing it to bind with oils. It is then treated with aluminium sulphate to cross-link the starch chains. This chemical process alters the molecular structure of the starch, resulting in a synthetic powder that is washed, dried, and packaged for industrial use.

Why manufacturers use it

Manufacturers use this modified starch because it is an exceptionally good carrier for delicate nutrients. It has a unique ability to encapsulate fat-soluble substances, such as vitamins and carotenoids, protecting them from light, air, and moisture. This ensures that the nutrients do not degrade on the shelf. It also helps these oily nutrients disperse evenly in water, which is a difficult technical challenge that ordinary starches cannot solve.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a limited-concern additive. EFSA re-evaluated this substance and determined that no acceptable daily intake could be set due to insufficient data on its long-term effects. Because of this lack of comprehensive data, the regulator does not allow its general use in food. Instead, it is restricted to very specific roles where exposure is extremely low, meaning you are unlikely to consume it in significant quantities.

What the science says

The European Food Safety Authority evaluated the safety of this modified starch and noted that the available scientific database was too limited to establish a specific daily limit. Scientists have looked at how the body processes modified starches, which generally break down into glucose and organic acids, but the presence of aluminium requires extra caution. Because of the gaps in the scientific literature, regulators chose to limit its use strictly rather than ban it, ensuring consumer exposure remains minimal.

Where you'll find it

You will not find this ingredient in everyday foods like bread, sauces, or ready meals. It is authorised only as a carrier for vitamin preparations and carotenoids, which means it only appears on the label of food supplements. When it is present, it is listed by its full name as starch aluminium octenyl succinate or by its European code, E1452, usually in the fine print of the ingredient list on the back of a vitamin bottle.
