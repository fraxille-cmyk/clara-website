Paprika extract

Processed ingredientNeutral

Diets

✓ Suits every diet Clara checks — as an ingredient

A natural extract from red peppers used to give foods a warm, orange-red colour and a mild, savoury flavour.

What it is

Paprika extract is a concentrated liquid or powder derived from the sweet red pepper, a plant native to the Americas and now grown worldwide. It contains the natural pigments that give peppers their bright red hue, alongside the aromatic compounds that provide their characteristic warm, earthy taste. Unlike the spice powder you might keep in your kitchen cupboard, this extract is highly concentrated, allowing food makers to add precise amounts of colour and flavour to their recipes. It is a familiar, plant-derived ingredient that behaves much like a highly concentrated version of the kitchen spice.

How it's made

The journey starts with harvesting ripe, red sweet peppers, which are then dried and ground into a fine powder. To separate the rich pigments and flavours from the fibrous plant material, a solvent like hexane or carbon dioxide is passed through the powder. This solvent dissolves the colourful oils and is then completely evaporated away, leaving behind a thick, dark red resin. This resin is often mixed with vegetable oil to standardise its strength, ensuring that every batch has the exact same colouring power before it is bottled and sent to food makers.

Why manufacturers use it

Food makers choose this extract because it provides a vibrant, natural orange-red colour that makes packaged foods look appealing and fresh. It is highly stable under the heat of baking and processing, meaning the colour does not fade on the supermarket shelf. Commercially, it is a brilliant alternative to synthetic red dyes, allowing brands to keep their ingredient lists simple and appealing to shoppers who prefer plant-based colours. It also carries a subtle, warm flavour that enhances savoury recipes without the need for artificial flavourings.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL. Because it is a natural extract from a widely eaten vegetable, it is considered a benign ingredient rather than a synthetic additive. The body processes the natural pigments, mainly carotenoids, in the same way it metabolises the nutrients found in fresh red peppers. There are no known safety concerns associated with eating foods coloured with this extract, and no daily intake limit has been established by food safety authorities because it is treated as a normal, wholesome food component.

What the science says

Major food safety bodies have thoroughly evaluated this extract and confirmed its safety. The Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives (JECFA) and the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) have both reviewed the scientific data on its use. Their evaluations show that the natural pigments are absorbed and utilised by the body as beneficial antioxidants, with no undesirable effects even when consumed in large amounts. Because it is a simple plant extract, scientists view it as a straightforward food ingredient that poses no risk to human health.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of savoury foods, including potato crisps, tortilla chips, processed cheeses, orange-hued sauces, marinades, and seasoned meats like sausages. On food labels, it is usually listed simply as paprika extract, paprika oleoresin, or natural paprika colour. Because it is not classified as a synthetic food additive in many regions, it rarely carries an E-number, appearing instead under its plain, recognisable plant name to show that the colour comes from a natural source.
