Carotenes (E160a)

E160aAdditiveNeutral · benign

Carotenes are natural orange pigments from the carrot family used to give food a warm, golden colour, rated neutral by Clara.

What it is

Carotenes are natural pigments that belong to a family of compounds responsible for the bright orange, yellow, and red hues in many fruits and vegetables. If you have ever admired the deep orange of a fresh carrot or the warm yellow of autumn leaves, you have seen carotenes at work. In the human body, certain types of carotenes can be converted into vitamin A, which is essential for maintaining healthy vision and supporting a robust immune system.

How it's made

To make this ingredient for food manufacturing, carotenes are typically obtained in one of two ways. They can be extracted directly from natural plant sources, such as carrots, palm oil, or certain types of algae, using food-grade solvents that are later removed. Alternatively, they can be produced through a fermentation process using specific microscopic fungi, or synthesised in a laboratory to create a highly pure, identical match to the molecules found in nature.

Why manufacturers use it

Food manufacturers use carotenes primarily as a colouring agent to restore or enhance the natural appearance of processed foods. During heating, mixing, and storage, natural food colours often fade, leaving products looking pale or unappealing. Adding carotenes restores a rich, warm, golden-yellow or deep orange hue. It is highly valued because it is stable under heat and light, making it a reliable and natural-looking alternative to synthetic petroleum-derived dyes.

Is it harmful

Clara rates this NEUTRAL, a benign additive. Because carotenes are natural pigments of the same family found in carrots, they are processed easily by the body and are considered very safe. There is no evidence of adverse health effects from the levels typically used to colour food. The body simply regulates how much it converts into vitamin A, and any excess is stored harmlessly or cleared naturally through normal metabolic pathways.

What the science says

Scientific bodies have thoroughly evaluated carotenes and confirmed their safety for use in food. EFSA re-evaluated carotenes as a food colour and established a group acceptable daily intake of up to fifteen milligrams per kilogram of body weight for natural and synthetic carotenes. JECFA has also reviewed these pigments and concluded they present no safety concerns at typical dietary levels. Research shows that the body manages these compounds efficiently, just as it does when you eat fresh vegetables.

Where you'll find it

You will find this ingredient in a wide variety of everyday products, including margarines, spreads, cheeses, yoghurts, custards, cakes, and orange-flavoured soft drinks. On the packaging, it might be declared by its E-number, E160a, or under names like beta-carotene, carotene, or mixed carotenes. It is often chosen for products that want to highlight a natural or wholesome image while maintaining a bright, appealing appearance.
