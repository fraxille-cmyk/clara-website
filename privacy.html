Privacy Policy — Clara

Last updated: 20 October 2026

1. Who we are

Clara is a mobile app operated by Scandihouse Limited (registration number HE 493114), a company registered in Limassol, Cyprus. Scandihouse Limited is the data controller for the personal data described in this policy.

Contact: support@scandihouse.co

2. What Clara does

Clara lets you photograph a food label. The photo is analysed to read the product's ingredients and nutrition information, which Clara then presents to you along with scores and, if you have chosen to share health details, checks against those details.

Clara also has a food diary: you log what you eat — by scanning a label, photographing a plate, typing it or searching Clara's food table — and see your day's nutrition, set a goal, note your weight, keep a list of the supplements you take and tick them off each day, and save meals you eat often, including recipes you import from a link or paste in. You can browse Clara's recipes, keep a note of what's in your kitchen, and plan your week's meals. The diary, your supplements, Apple Health, plate photos, supplement label photos, typed food and recipe imports are handled differently from the rest of your data; sections 4 to 7 explain how.

3. What we collect, and why

Account information. Your email address, used to create and sign in to your account.

Profile information you choose to enter. Your name, age, gender, country and region, and your dietary goal. This is used to personalise what Clara shows you.

Health information you choose to enter. Your allergies, medical conditions, and ingredients you wish to avoid. This is used for one purpose only: to check what Clara shows you against those details — the products you scan, and, on your phone (this check never leaves it), the recipes you browse, import or save and your meal plan, where Clara hides or flags any recipe whose ingredients name something on your allergy list and keeps it out of the plan — and to tell you when something may be relevant to you. This is special-category data under the GDPR, and we process it only with your explicit consent, which you give during setup and can withdraw at any time (see section 11).

Photographs of labels. When you scan a product, the photograph is transmitted for analysis (see section 8) and the extracted result is saved to your scan history. The photograph itself is not stored — it is analysed and discarded, and only the extracted text-based result is kept.

Scan history. The products you have scanned and their results, so you can review them in the app.

Usage and diagnostic information. We collect events describing how the app is used (for example, that a scan was completed or a screen was viewed), linked to your account identifier, to understand and improve the app. If the app crashes or encounters an error, a technical report is sent to our crash-reporting service. We do not include your allergies, conditions or avoided ingredients in analytics events. When you open one of Clara's recipes or finish cooking one, the event records only where the recipe comes from (USDA's MyPlate Kitchen or Clara's own) — never which recipe; when you plan a week or add something to your kitchen, it records only how (planned or shuffled; from your list, a scan or typed in) — never a food, a recipe or an amount.

Shopping list. The items on your list, so they are there on each device you sign in on. For a line Clara adds from a recipe or your meal plan, the list also keeps its aisle, whether it came from a recipe or the plan, which food it is in Clara's food table and its amount, so the same food from two recipes becomes one line.

Subscription status. Whether you hold an active subscription. Payments are processed by Apple; we never receive or store your payment card details.

Plate photos, supplement label photos, typed food and recipe imports. Sent for analysis and not kept with your account; a free plate scan leaves only a record of who used it and when, and a recipe read from a public link is kept by its link alone, with nothing about who imported it — see sections 6 and 7. Your food diary — with your goal, your weights, your supplements and your saved meals — is not collected at all: it stays on your phone and in your own iCloud (section 4).

We do not sell your data. We do not use your data for advertising. We do not share your health information with anyone except the processors listed below, who act on our instructions.

4. Your food diary

The food diary holds what you log: each food, its amount, the meal and time, and its nutrition values — and, if you keep them, small photos of your plates (see section 6). Alongside it, Clara keeps your daily targets, the height, weight and activity you give for them, and your favourites.

Your goal, weights and saved meals. The goal you set, the weights you type in and the meals you save in My meals — including recipes you import, with their ingredients, method and the link they came from — are kept with the diary, in the same store and under the same rules as everything below.

Your supplements. The supplements you add — their name and brand, form, serving, the vitamins, minerals and other nutrients a serving contains, the ingredient line and how it sits with the dietary patterns Clara checks, when you take them and whether you want a reminder — and every dose you tick or skip are kept with the diary, in the same store and under the same rules as everything below. What a dose contains is added to the day's vitamins and minerals, and compared with reference intakes and EFSA's upper levels, on your phone. Reminders are notifications your phone schedules for itself; nothing about them is sent to us. Supplement doses are not written to Apple Health.

Your kitchen and meal plans. What you note as being in your kitchen (with when you bought it) and the weeks the meal planner plans for you are kept with the diary too, under the same rules. The planner uses your allergies, avoided ingredients and dietary patterns on your phone to leave out recipes that don't suit you; that check never leaves the phone.

Where it is stored. On your phone. If Sync with iCloud is on (Settings → Food diary; it is on by default when your phone is signed in to iCloud), your diary entries, goal, weights, supplements and the doses you tick, saved meals, kitchen and meal plans are also kept in your own iCloud account, in Clara's private CloudKit database, so your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account get them. Your targets, body details and favourites stay on the phone. Apple keeps the iCloud copy under your iCloud account and Apple's own terms.

Never on Clara's servers. The diary — with your goal, weights, supplements and saved meals — is not sent to us and is not part of your account on our servers — we cannot see it. Our usage analytics record only that the diary was used — for example that an entry was added, to which meal, and whether it came from a scan, a search or typed text, or that a supplement dose was ticked — never a food, a supplement, a nutrient, an amount, a calorie figure, a target or a body measurement.

Deleting it. Delete all diary data (Settings → Food diary) erases every entry, plate photo, goal, weight, supplement and dose, saved meal, kitchen item, meal plan, favourite and target from your phone and, while Sync with iCloud is on, from your iCloud, and cancels your supplement reminders. Deleting your account (section 12) does the same. Signing out does not: the diary belongs to the phone and stays on it until you delete it. If you turned Sync with iCloud off before deleting, the copy already in your iCloud stays there until you remove it in your iPhone's iCloud storage settings.

5. Apple Health

Clara works with Apple Health only if you turn it on, in Settings → Food diary, and only for what each switch says. Apple asks your permission for each kind of data before Clara can write or read it, and you can change your choices at any time in the Health app.

  • Write meals to Apple Health — Clara writes the nutrition of what you log to Health as nutrition samples: energy, protein, carbohydrates, fat, sugar, fibre, sodium, vitamins and minerals, caffeine and water. Editing an entry updates its samples, deleting it removes them, and Delete all diary data or deleting your account removes every sample Clara wrote.
  • Use Apple Health for height and weight — Clara reads your latest height and weight, to suggest your daily targets, and your weight history, to show it on the Goal page. It reads nothing else.

Nothing Clara reads from Health is stored in iCloud or sent to Clara's servers: your height and weight from Health stay in the app's settings on your phone, and the weights shown on the Goal page are read from Health each time you open it and never stored by Clara. We never use Health data for advertising, marketing or analytics, and never share it with anyone.

6. Plate photos, supplement labels, typed food and recipe imports

Plate photos. When you photograph a plate, a reduced-size copy of the photo is sent to Clara's server for a single analysis: our AI provider (section 8) estimates which foods are on the plate and how much of each. The photo is processed in memory and discarded once the answer is sent back — it is not stored and not logged. Our server's logs record only technical facts about the request, such as how many foods were found and how long it took — never the photo, a food name or an amount.

Supplement labels. When you scan a supplement's label (Clara Premium), a reduced-size copy of the photo is sent to Clara's server for a single read: our AI provider (section 8) reads the product's name, form, serving and the amounts it lists, and the text in the photo is used to check each amount. The photo is processed once, in memory, and discarded once the answer is sent back — it is not stored and not logged, and the answer is not cached. Our server's logs record only technical facts, such as how many amounts were read and how long it took — never the photo, the product, a nutrient or an amount. What was read is saved in your supplements on your phone (section 4), so a supplement is read once; ticking it off each day sends nothing.

Typed food. When you type what you ate, only that text is sent, the same way, for a single read into foods and amounts. It is processed in memory; the text is not stored and not logged.

Recipe imports. When you import a recipe, the link you paste or share to Clara — or the recipe text you paste or type — is sent to Clara's server for a single read. For a link, our server fetches that public page once (for a TikTok video, TikTok's public description of it; for an Instagram post, its public page) and reads the recipe from it; where the page doesn't say enough, the ingredient lines are sent to our AI provider (section 8) to read. A recipe read from a public link is kept in a cache keyed by that link, so the next import of the same page is quicker — the cache holds the public recipe only, with no account, no identity and nothing about who imported it. Pasted or typed text is not stored. Nothing else is kept: our server's logs record only the kind of source, whether the read worked and how many ingredients it found — never the link, the text, the recipe's name or an ingredient. When a link can't be read — a TikTok or Instagram post whose caption our server can't get, or a web page with no recipe in its markup — the server sends our error-reporting service (Sentry, section 8) a warning that names only the platform, or the website's domain (for example, bbcgoodfood.com), so we notice when a site changes: never the rest of the link, your account or anything you imported, and at most one for each platform or site an hour. The recipe you save goes into My meals on your phone (section 4).

All these requests are tied to your account only to check your subscription or your free plate scan (section 7). What comes back goes into your diary on your phone only when you add it. If Keep plate photos is on (Settings → Food diary), a small copy of the plate photo is saved with that diary entry — on your phone and, with Sync with iCloud, in your iCloud (section 4), never on our servers. Turning the setting off deletes the photos already kept.

7. The free daily plate scan

Without a subscription you can scan a plate free each day. To count it, our server records who used a free plate scan and when — your account identifier and the time — and nothing else: no photo, no food and no estimate. If the scan fails or finds no food, the record is removed and the scan doesn't count. Records older than 24 hours are deleted the next time you use a free plate scan, and all of them are deleted with your account. Subscribers' plate scans are not recorded.

8. Who processes your data on our behalf

  • Google (Gemini API) — label photographs and, where relevant to the check you have requested, your health details are sent to Google's Gemini service to extract and classify the label's contents; plate photos and the food you type are sent to it to estimate the foods and amounts, supplement label photos to read their amounts, and recipe text it is needed for to read the ingredients (section 6). Google processes this data as our processor and does not use it to train its models.
  • Supabase — hosts our database and authentication. Your account, profile, health details and scan history, and the free plate-scan record (section 7), are stored here, in Ireland (eu-west-1).
  • Adapty — manages subscription status.
  • Mixpanel — processes usage analytics events, hosted on Mixpanel's EU servers.
  • Sentry — receives crash and error reports from the app, and our server's warnings when a recipe link can't be read (section 6), hosted in Germany.
  • Apple — processes payments and, if you enable it, delivers the app and updates.

Your food diary's iCloud copy and your Apple Health data are held by Apple in your own account, under Apple's terms; Clara's servers have no access to either.

9. Legal bases

  • Explicit consent (GDPR Article 9(2)(a)) — for your allergies, conditions and avoided ingredients.
  • Performance of a contract (Article 6(1)(b)) — for your account, scans (including plate photos, supplement label photos and typed food), the free plate-scan record and your subscription, which are needed to provide the app.
  • Legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)) — for keeping the service secure and working, for diagnosing faults and crashes, and for understanding how the app is used so we can improve it.

10. How long we keep it

Your account, profile, health details, scan history and shopping list are kept until you delete your account or ask us to delete them. When you delete your account, this data is deleted immediately.

Your food diary, with your goal, weights, supplements and saved meals, is kept on your phone and in your iCloud until you delete it (section 4). Plate photos, supplement label photos, typed food and pasted recipes are not kept at all; free plate-scan records last 24 hours (section 7).

Recipes read from public links are kept in the recipe cache for up to 30 days and then read again (section 6); like the product cache below, it describes recipes, contains nothing about you, and is not deleted when an account is deleted.

Clara also keeps a cache of product information — for example, that a particular ingredient carries a particular additive number. This information describes food products, contains nothing about you, and cannot be linked back to you or any other person. It is not deleted when an account is deleted.

Usage analytics and crash reports are retained by the processors above under their own retention schedules, and are deleted or de-identified when your account is deleted or on request.

Records of your purchases are held independently by Apple and by our subscription provider under their own policies and retention obligations; we cannot delete those on your behalf.

11. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to:

  • access the personal data we hold about you;
  • correct it if it is wrong;
  • delete it (see section 12);
  • withdraw your consent to health-data processing at any time — you can remove your allergies, conditions and avoided ingredients in the app at any time, and Clara will stop checking products against them. Withdrawing consent does not affect processing carried out before you withdrew it;
  • object to or restrict processing;
  • receive a copy of your data in a portable format;
  • complain to a supervisory authority. In Cyprus this is the Office of the Commissioner for Personal Data Protection (www.dataprotection.gov.cy). You may also complain to the authority in your own country.

To exercise any of these, email support@scandihouse.co.

12. Deleting your data

You can delete your account from within the app, in Settings. This immediately deletes your profile — including all health details — your scan history, your shopping list and your free plate-scan records. On the phone you delete it from, it also erases your food diary (and its iCloud copy while Sync with iCloud is on) and removes the nutrition Clara wrote to Apple Health.

If you cannot access the app, email support@scandihouse.co and we will delete your account on request.

13. International transfers

Our processors may process data outside the European Economic Area. Where they do, transfers are covered by the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses or another lawful transfer mechanism.

14. Children

Clara is not intended for children under 13 — or under the minimum age of digital consent in your country, if that age is higher — and we do not knowingly collect data from them. If you believe a child has given us personal data, contact support@scandihouse.co and we will delete it.

15. Security

Access to your data requires authentication, and database rules restrict each account to its own records. Data is encrypted in transit. No system is perfectly secure, but we take reasonable measures to protect your information.

16. Clara is not medical advice

Clara is an informational tool. It is not a medical device, and it does not diagnose, treat, or provide medical advice. Clara reads labels using AI, which can misread or miss information, and the foods and amounts it gives for plates, typed food and imported recipes are estimates. Where Clara shows that a day's vitamin or mineral is above an upper level, it quotes EFSA's figures for healthy adults (the NIH's for iron) — a fact about the number, not advice about what to take. Always check the physical packaging yourself, particularly for allergens, and consult a qualified professional for medical decisions.

17. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy we will update the date at the top and, where the change is significant, tell you in the app.
